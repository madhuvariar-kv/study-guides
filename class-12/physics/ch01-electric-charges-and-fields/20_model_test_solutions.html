2.
When a glass rod is rubbed with silk, the rod becomes positively charged because
(a) protons move from the silk to the rod(b) electrons move from the rod to the silk(c) electrons move from the silk to the rod(d) positive charge is created on the rod
(1 mark)

💡 Only the outer electrons are loosely held.

✅ Answer: (b) electrons move from the rod to the silk

Rubbing only transfers electrons. Glass loses some electrons to silk, so the rod is left with a deficit of electrons (positive) and the silk gains them (negative). Protons are bound in the nuclei and do not move (a); (c) would make the rod negative; (d) is wrong because charge is never created – conservation of charge (ചാർജ് സംരക്ഷണം).

State Coulomb’s law in electrostatics and write it in vector form.

25.
Why do two electric field lines never cross each other? Why can electrostatic field lines not form closed loops?
(2 marks)

💡 What does the tangent to a field line tell you?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: one direction of E at a point; lines start on + and end on −

29.
With the help of labelled diagrams, explain how two identical metal spheres on insulating stands can be charged equally and oppositely by induction.
(3 marks)

💡 Touch, bring the rod, separate, then remove the rod.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: touch → bring rod → separate → remove rod: A +, B −

(1) Place the two metal spheres A and B on insulating stands, touching each other (one conductor).

(2) Bring a negatively charged rod near A, without touching. Free electrons are repelled to the far side of B: A gets a positive induced charge, B a negative one.

(3) Keeping the rod in place, separate the spheres slightly.

(4) Remove the rod. The charges spread uniformly over each sphere: A is positive and B negative, with equal magnitudes. The rod loses no charge.

Marking: Diagrams 1 mark; steps 1½ marks; result (equal and opposite charges) ½ mark.

30.
Explain the three basic properties of electric charge.
(3 marks)

💡 Add, keep, count in packets.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: additivity, conservation, quantisation

(1) Additivity (ചാർജിന്റെ സങ്കലനീയത): the total charge of a system is the algebraic sum of the individual charges, e.g. +2 + 5 − 3 = +4 units. Charge is a scalar.

31.
Derive an expression for the torque acting on an electric dipole placed in a uniform electric field.
(3 marks)

💡 Couple = force × perpendicular distance.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: τ = pE sin θ, τ = p × E

A dipole (charges ±q, separation 2a) makes an angle θ with a uniform field E. Force on +q = qE (along E), on −q = −qE – equal and opposite, so the net force is zero.

They act at different points and form a couple. Torque = magnitude of one force × perpendicular distance between their lines of action = qE × 2a sin θ.

Since p = q × 2a: τ = pE sin θ; in vector form τ = p × E. It tends to turn p towards E (maximum pE at θ = 90°, zero at 0° and 180°).

Marking: Diagram 1 mark; forces and couple ½ + ½; τ = pE sin θ 1 mark.

33.
Define electric field at a point. Obtain an expression for the electric field due to a point charge.
(3 marks)

💡 Divide Coulomb’s force by the test charge.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: E = F/q0; E = 14πε0·Q/r2 r̂

The electric field (വൈദ്യുത മണ്ഡലം) at a point is the force experienced by a unit positive test charge (പരീക്ഷണ ചാർജ്) placed at that point: E = F/q0 (q0 → 0, so it does not disturb the source).

Derive an expression for the electric field at a point on the axial line of an electric dipole.

💡 Subtract the two fields – they point in opposite directions.

✅ Answer: E = 14πε0·2pr/(r2 − a2)2 ≈ 14πε0·2p/r3, along p

Dipole: −q at A, +q at B, AB = 2a, centre O; P on the axis at OP = r (on the side of +q).

Field at P due to −q (distance r + a): E−q = 14πε0 q(r + a)2, towards A (along −p̂).

Field at P due to +q (distance r − a): E+q = 14πε0 q(r − a)2, away from B (along +p̂).

E+q > E−q, so E = 14πε0 q [1(r − a)2 − 1(r + a)2] = 14πε0 4qar(r2 − a2)2.

With p = 2qa: E = 14πε0 2pr(r2 − a2)2 p̂. For r ≫ a: E = 14πε0 2pr3, directed along p (from −q to +q).

Marking: Diagram 1 mark; E due to each charge 1 + 1 marks; net E 1 mark; r ≫ a form with direction 1 mark.

38.
Derive an expression for the electric field at a point on the equatorial line of an electric dipole.
(5 marks)

💡 Vertical components cancel; horizontal ones add.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: E = −14πε0·p/(r2 + a2)3/2 ≈ −14πε0·p/r3

Dipole: −q at A, +q at B, AB = 2a, centre O; P on the perpendicular bisector with OP = r. Each charge is √(r2 + a2) from P.

Magnitudes: E+q = E−q = 14πε0 qr2 + a2; E+q along BP (away from B), E−q along PA (towards A).

Resolve: the components perpendicular to the axis are equal and opposite and cancel; the components parallel to the axis add, both pointing opposite to p.

E = 2E+q cos θ with cos θ = a√(r2 + a2) ⇒ E = 14πε0 2qa(r2 + a2)3/2.

E = −14πε0 p(r2 + a2)3/2 p̂; for r ≫ a, E = −14πε0 pr3 – opposite to p. (Half the axial value at the same distance.)

Marking: Diagram 1 mark; magnitudes 1 mark; resolution 1 mark; net E 1 mark; r ≫ a form with direction 1 mark.

39.
State Gauss’s law. Using it, derive an expression for the electric field due to an infinitely long, straight, uniformly charged wire.
(5 marks)

💡 Cylindrical Gaussian surface; only the curved surface has flux.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: E = λ/(2πε0r), radial

Gauss’s law: the total flux through a closed surface = qenclosed/ε0.

Wire (അനന്തമായ നീളമുള്ള നേർകമ്പി) with linear charge density (രേഖീയ ചാർജ് സാന്ദ്രത) λ. By symmetry E is radial and has the same magnitude at all points at distance r. Take a Gaussian cylinder of radius r and length l, coaxial with the wire.

Flat ends: E is parallel to the end faces (perpendicular to dS) – no flux. Curved surface: E ∥ dS, so flux = E × 2πrl.

Charge enclosed = λl. Gauss: E × 2πrl = λl/ε0.

E = λ2πε0r n̂, radially outward for λ > 0 (E ∝ 1/r).

Marking: Statement 1 mark; diagram 1 mark; flux 1 mark; charge enclosed and Gauss 1 mark; result 1 mark.

40.
Using Gauss’s law, derive an expression for the electric field due to a uniformly charged infinite plane sheet.
(5 marks)

💡 A pillbox with two flat faces.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: E = σ/(2ε0), independent of distance

Infinite plane sheet (അനന്തമായ സമതല ഷീറ്റ്) with surface charge density (ഉപരിതല ചാർജ് സാന്ദ്രത) σ. By symmetry E is normal to the sheet, pointing away on both sides (σ > 0) and equal in magnitude at equal distances.

Gaussian surface: a cylinder (pillbox) of cross-section A crossing the sheet, its axis normal to the sheet.

Curved surface: E ⊥ dS – no flux. Each flat face: flux EA. Total φ = 2EA.

Charge enclosed = σA. Gauss: 2EA = σA/ε0.

E = σ2ε0 n̂ – independent of the distance from the sheet.

Marking: Diagram 1 mark; symmetry and Gaussian surface 1 mark; flux 1 mark; charge enclosed and Gauss 1 mark; result 1 mark.

41.
Using Gauss’s law, obtain the expression for the electric field due to a uniformly charged thin spherical shell at a point (i) outside the shell, (ii) inside the shell.
(5 marks)

💡 Concentric Gaussian spheres outside and inside.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: outside E = 14πε0·q/r2; inside E = 0

Thin spherical shell (നേർത്ത ഗോളീയ കവചം) of radius R, total charge q = 4πR2σ. By symmetry E is radial and the same at every point at a given distance r from the centre.

(i) r > R: Gaussian sphere of radius r: flux = E × 4πr2; charge enclosed = q.

E × 4πr2 = q/ε0 ⇒ E = 14πε0 qr2 r̂ – as if all the charge were at the centre.

(ii) r < R: a Gaussian sphere inside encloses no charge: E × 4πr2 = 0 ⇒ E = 0 everywhere inside.

Marking: Diagram 1 mark; outside: flux and charge 1 mark, result 1 mark; inside: argument 1 mark, result 1 mark.

42.
Three point charges are placed in air as shown: +2 μC at O, +3 μC at A, 30 cm from O, and −4 μC at B, 40 cm from O, with OA ⊥ OB. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant force on the charge at O.
(5 marks)

💡 Find each force, then add them as perpendicular vectors.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: F = 0.75 N at 36.9° to AO produced, towards B

Given: Q = 2 × 10−6 C; qA = 3 × 10−6 C, OA = 0.30 m; qB = −4 × 10−6 C, OB = 0.40 m; OA ⊥ OB.

Formula: F = 14πε0 |q1q2|r2; superposition: F = F1 + F2.

Step 1: F1 (due to A) = 9 × 109 × 2 × 10−6 × 3 × 10−6(0.30)2 = 0.6 N, repulsive – along AO produced (away from A).

Step 2: F2 (due to B) = 9 × 109 × 2 × 10−6 × 4 × 10−6(0.40)2 = 0.45 N, attractive – along OB (towards B).

Step 3: F1 ⊥ F2: F = √(0.62 + 0.452) = √0.5625 = 0.75 N.

Step 4: tan θ = F2/F1 = 0.45/0.6 = 0.75 ⇒ θ = 36.9° with AO produced, on the side of B.

Marking: F₁ 1 mark; F₂ 1 mark; directions/diagram 1 mark; resultant 1 mark; direction 1 mark.

43.
Two point charges +4 μC and +9 μC are placed 50 cm apart in air. (a) Find the point on the line joining them where the resultant electric field is zero. (b) Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant electric field at the midpoint of the line joining them.
(5 marks)

💡 At the null point the two fields are equal and opposite.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 20 cm from the +4 μC charge (b) 7.2 × 105 N C−1 towards the +4 μC charge

Given: q1 = 4 × 10−6 C at A, q2 = 9 × 10−6 C at B, AB = 0.50 m.

Formula: E = 14πε0 qr2; for like charges the null point lies between them, nearer the smaller charge.

(a) At N, AN = x: q1x2 = q2(0.5 − x)2 ⇒ 0.5 − xx = √(9/4) = 1.5 ⇒ 0.5 = 2.5x ⇒ x = 0.2 m = 20 cm from the +4 μC charge (there each field is 9 × 105 N C−1, opposite).

(b) At the midpoint M (r = 0.25 m): E1 = 9 × 109 × 4 × 10−6(0.25)2 = 5.76 × 105 N C−1 towards B; E2 = 9 × 109 × 9 × 10−6(0.25)2 = 1.296 × 106 N C−1 towards A.

Resultant E = E2 − E1 = 7.2 × 105 N C−1, towards A (the +4 μC charge).

Marking: (a) condition 1 mark, x 1 mark; (b) E₁ 1 mark, E₂ 1 mark, resultant with direction 1 mark.

An electric dipole consists of charges ±8 nC separated by 5 mm. Find (a) its dipole moment, (b) the electric field at a point on its axis 20 cm from its centre, (c) the electric field at a point on its equatorial line 20 cm from its centre. (d) If the dipole is placed in a uniform field of 5 × 104 N C−1 at 30° to it, find the torque on it. (e) What is the maximum torque?
(5 marks)

💡 p first; r ≫ a, so use the 1/r³ forms.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 4 × 10−11 C m (b) 90 N C−1 (c) 45 N C−1 (d) 1 × 10−6 N m (e) 2 × 10−6 N m

Given: q = 8 × 10−9 C, 2a = 5 × 10−3 m, r = 0.20 m (r ≫ a), E = 5 × 104 N C−1, θ = 30°.

(d) τ = pE sin θ = 4 × 10−11 × 5 × 104 × 0.5 = 1 × 10−6 N m.

(e) τmax = pE (at θ = 90°) = 2 × 10−6 N m.
