2. Summary – the textbook list as cards

1Forces that build matter

Electric and magnetic forces determine the properties of atoms, molecules and bulk matter. – page 4

2Two kinds of charge

Electric charge (വൈദ്യുത ചാർജ്): like charges repel, unlike attract. Glass rubbed with silk: +; plastic rubbed with fur: − (Franklin’s convention). – page 4

3Conductors and insulators

Conductors (ചാലകം) let charge move; insulators (ഇൻസുലേറ്റർ (കുചാലകം)) do not. In metals electrons move; in electrolytes both + and − ions. – page 5

4Three properties of charge
q = ne,  e = 1.6 × 10−19 C

Quantisation (ചാർജിന്റെ ക്വാണ്ടീകരണം), additivity (ചാർജിന്റെ സങ്കലനീയത) – algebraic sum, and conservation (ചാർജ് സംരക്ഷണം) – charge is transferred, never created or destroyed. – page 6

5Coulomb’s law
F21 = k q1q2 r̂21/r212,  k = 1/4πε0 ≈ 9 × 109 N m2 C−2

Coulomb’s law (കൂളോം നിയമം); ε0 = 8.854 × 10−12 C2 N−1 m−2. – page 7

6Electric vs gravitational
ke2/(G memp) ≅ 2.4 × 1039

Between an electron and a proton the electric force is about 1039 times stronger. – page 7

7Superposition
F1 = F12 + F13 + … + F1n

Superposition principle (അധ്യാരോപണ തത്വം): each pair force is unaffected by other charges; add the forces as vectors. – page 8

8Electric field
E = F/q0;  point charge E = |q|/(4πε0r2)

Electric field (വൈദ്യുത മണ്ഡലം): radially outward for +q, inward for −q; obeys superposition. – page 9

9Field lines

Field lines (വൈദ്യുത മണ്ഡലരേഖകൾ): the tangent gives the direction of E; crowded where E is strong; uniform field – parallel, equally spaced lines. – page 10

10Properties of field lines

(i) continuous, no breaks; (ii) never cross; (iii) start on + and end on − charges – no closed loops. – page 10

11Electric dipole
p = q × 2a, from −q to +q

Electric dipole (വൈദ്യുത ദ്വിധ്രുവം) and dipole moment (ദ്വിധ്രുവ മൊമെന്റ്). – page 11

12Dipole fields
axial E = 2pr/[4πε0(r2 − a2)2] ≅ 2p/4πε0r3
equatorial E = −p/[4πε0(r2 + a2)3/2] ≅ −p/4πε0r3

Note the 1/r3 fall-off (a point charge: 1/r2). – page 11

13Dipole in a uniform field
τ = p × E,  τ = pE sin θ

Torque (ടോർക്ക് (ബലാഘൂർണം)) but no net force in a uniform field (ഏകീകൃത വൈദ്യുത മണ്ഡലം). – page 11

14Electric flux
Δφ = E·ΔS,  ΔS = ΔS n̂

Electric flux (വൈദ്യുത ഫ്ലക്സ്); for a closed surface n̂ is the outward normal. – page 10

15Gauss’s law and its uses
φ = qenc/ε0
wire λ/(2πε0r) · sheet σ/(2ε0) · shell q/(4πε0r2) outside, 0 inside

Gauss’s law (ഗോസ് നിയമം), easiest with symmetry. – page 12, page 13

3. Points to Ponder

  1. Why does the nucleus hold together? The protons repel, but the strong force (range about 10−14 m – the size of a nucleus) holds them; quantum mechanics keeps electrons out of the nucleus.
  2. Gravity vs electricity: both follow the inverse square law (വിപരീത വർഗ നിയമം), but gravity is only attractive while electric forces of + and − charges can cancel – so the weaker gravity dominates on large scales.
  3. k is not arbitrary in SI: the ampere is defined magnetically and 1 C = 1 A s, so k ≈ 9 × 109 N m2 C−2.
  4. Why is 1 C so large? Because it is set by magnetic forces, which are much weaker than electric ones; 1 C is too big a unit for electrostatics (we use μC, nC).
  5. Additivity is not obvious – it holds because charge is a scalar (അദിശം), with no direction.
  6. Charge is invariant – the same in frames moving relative to each other (kinetic energy, for example, is not). go further
  7. Conservation is a separate property: invariance in time. A scalar need not be conserved (kinetic energy in an inelastic collision); a vector can be (angular momentum).
  8. Quantisation of charge is a basic, unexplained law of nature – there is no such law for mass.
  9. Superposition is not just vector addition: a pair force is not changed by other charges, and there are no extra three-body forces.
  10. The field of discrete charges is not defined at the charges; for a volume distribution it is defined everywhere; across a charged surface it jumps.
  11. A configuration with zero total charge (a dipole) still has a field – it just falls faster than 1/r2 far away.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Forgetting to convert cm → m and μC → C, or squaring the distance wrongly.
  • Treating forces and fields as scalars – adding magnitudes instead of adding vectors.
  • Wrong direction of the field of a negative charge (it points towards the charge) and of the force on a negative test charge (opposite to E).
  • Dipole moment direction is from −q to +q; the axial field is 2p/r3 along p, the equatorial field p/r3 opposite to p.
  • A net force on a dipole in a uniform field? No – only a torque, τ = pE sin θ with θ between p and E.
  • Using only the enclosed charges for E in Gauss’s law. E is due to all charges; only the flux depends on the enclosed charge.
  • Flux through a closed surface depends on its shape or size? No – only on the enclosed charge.
  • Field inside a charged conducting shell is not zero? It is zero. Field of a plane sheet depends on distance? It does not.
  • Field lines crossing or forming closed loops in electrostatics.
