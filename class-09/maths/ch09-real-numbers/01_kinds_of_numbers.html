Kinds of Numbers സംഖ്യകൾ പലതരം

Before putting every number on one line, let us meet the whole family: counting numbers, zero, fractions, negatives and integers. You will see that one family sits inside the next, like boxes inside boxes – and that even a whole number like 5 is secretly a fraction.

📖 What you already know
  • Counting numbers 1, 2, 3, … and the number 0.
  • Fractions like ½, ¾, 1½ name parts of a whole.
  • Negative numbers like −3 name temperatures below zero or positions to the left (chapter 7, ന്യൂനസംഖ്യകൾ).

2. Every whole number is a fraction

5 is five wholes. Cut each whole into 2 equal halves and you have 10 halves: 5 = 10/2. Into 3 thirds: 15/3. Even 5 = 5/1.

🎚️ Cut the wholes into equal parts

Negatives work the same way: −3 = −3/1 = −6/2. And 0 = 0/1 = 0/5. So every integer is also a rational number.

⭐ Key idea

A rational number (ഭിന്നകസംഖ്യ) is any number that can be written as a fraction x/y, where x and y are whole numbers or their negatives and y is not 0. Natural numbers, integers and ordinary fractions are all rational.

3. Fractions as decimals

Divide the top by the bottom and a fraction becomes a decimal. Some decimals stop; others repeat a block of digits for ever.

🎚️ Long division, digit by digit

Try 1/4 (stops), 1/3 (repeats 3), 2/7 (repeats 285714), 5/12 (4166…). A fraction's decimal always either stops or repeats. Irrational numbers like √2 = 1.41421356… never stop and never repeat.

🌍 Real life

A cricket score is a natural number, a bank balance can be negative (an integer), a recipe uses ¾ cup (rational), and the diagonal of a square tile is √2 times its side (irrational). Each kind of number was invented because people needed it.

5. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • Natural numbers ⊂ integers ⊂ rational numbers ⊂ real numbers. Irrational numbers are the real numbers that are not rational.
  • Every integer is a fraction: 5 = 5/1 = 10/2, −3 = −3/1, 0 = 0/1.
  • Fractions have decimals that stop or repeat; irrational numbers have decimals that neither stop nor repeat.
