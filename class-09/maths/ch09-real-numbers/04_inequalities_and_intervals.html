Inequalities and Intervals ചെറുതും വലുതും

This chapter asks questions like "which numbers are less than 3 away from 1?". The answers are not single numbers but whole stretches of the number line. Here we learn to read and draw them: the signs < and >, "between", "or", and equations that have two answers.

📖 What you already know
  • On the number line the bigger number is to the right (page 2).
  • Solving x + 2 = 5 means finding the x that makes it true: x = 3.

💡 Memory trick

The sign always opens towards the bigger number, like a hungry mouth: 3 < 8, 8 > 3.

2. One-sided: x < a and x > a

🎚️ A ray of numbers

An open circle means the end point is not included (<, >); a filled dot means it is (≤, ≥).

3. Between: a < x < b

"x is between −2 and 4" means x is bigger than −2 and smaller than 4. We write −2 < x < 4 and shade the stretch between them.

🎚️ Shade an interval and test points

4. "Or": x < a or x > b

"x is less than −2 or greater than 8" allows two separate stretches: everything to the left of −2 and everything to the right of 8 – two rays pointing away from each other.

🎚️ Two rays
⭐ Key idea

"and" (between) gives one stretch with two ends: a < x < b. "or" gives two rays going outwards: x < a or x > b. Open circles at the ends when the end numbers are not included.

5. Equations with two cases

Later in this chapter, equations like |x − 1| = 3 have two answers. The trick is "two cases": the answer can be on one side or the other. Practise with ordinary equations first.

🎬 Animation: x − 1 = 3 and 1 − x = 3
🌍 Real life

"Children between 5 and 12 years" (5 < age < 12), "speed at most 50 km/h" (v ≤ 50), "temperature below −2 °C or above 40 °C triggers an alarm" (t < −2 or t > 40) – rules everywhere are intervals and rays.

6. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • <, > leave the end out (open circle); ≤, ≥ include it (filled dot).
  • a < x < b: one shaded stretch between a and b. x < a or x > b: two rays going outwards.
  • Some equations have two answers – think in two cases, one on each side.
