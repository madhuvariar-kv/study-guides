Area and Volume as Rules പരപ്പളവും വ്യാപ്തവും

On the last page the perimeter of a stretched rectangle became the rule p(x) = 4x + 10. Now we do the same for its area, and then for the volume of a stretched block. This time squares x² and cubes x³ appear – and the rules start to look like real polynomials.

📖 What you need (pages 2, 3 and 5 of this guide)
  • x² = x × x and x³ = x × x × x.
  • (x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab – the multiplication identities (ഗുണനസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ, chapter 4).
  • p(3) means “the value of p when x is 3”.

1. The area of the stretched rectangle

Back to the 3 cm × 2 cm rectangle. Instead of trying one added length after another, the textbook starts straight away with a general added length of x cm and cuts the new rectangle into pieces.

🎬 Cutting the new rectangle into four pieces
⭐ Area rule (textbook page 179)

If every side of the 3 cm × 2 cm rectangle is lengthened by x cm and the new area (പരപ്പളവ്) is a(x) square centimetres, then

a(x) = (x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 5x + 6

In algebra we usually write the letters first: x² + 5x + 6 rather than 6 + 5x + x² – the same thing.

🎚️ Area for any added length

Four pieces: the old 6, two strips 3x and 2x, and a little square x². The strips together are 5x.

2. Using the rule: a(1), a(1½), a(2)

🎬 Putting numbers into a(x) = x² + 5x + 6
🗣️ The same results in ordinary language (page 179)
  • If all sides are lengthened by 1 cm, the area is 12 square centimetres.
  • If all sides are lengthened by 1½ cm, the area is 15¾ square centimetres.
  • If all sides are lengthened by 2 cm, the area is 20 square centimetres.
⚠️ Careful

The area does not grow by the same amount each time: a(1) = 12, a(2) = 20, a(3) = 30, a(4) = 42 – the jumps are 8, 10, 12. That is the x² piece at work. The perimeter p(x) = 4x + 10 grew by exactly 4 each time; the area does not.

3. Stretching a block: volume

Now a rectangular block (ചതുരക്കട്ട) with edges 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm. Every edge is lengthened by the same x cm. What happens to the volume (വ്യാപ്തം)?

🎚️ The growing block

The new edges are x + 1, x + 2 and x + 3, so the new volume is (x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3). To write it out as a sum we multiply step by step.

🎬 Multiplying out (x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3)
⭐ Volume rule (textbook page 180)

If every edge of a block with edges 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm is lengthened by x cm, and the volume of the bigger block is v(x) cubic centimetres, then

v(x) = (x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3) = x³ + 6x² + 11x + 6

To multiply two sums, multiply each term of the first by each term of the second, and add (ആദ്യത്തെ തുകയിലെ സംഖ്യകളോരോന്നിനെയും, രണ്ടാമത്തെ തുകയിലെ ഓരോന്നുകൊണ്ടും ഗുണിച്ച്, കൂട്ടണം).

🔎 Quick checks

x = 0 means nothing was added: v(0) = 6 = 1 × 2 × 3 ✓. x = 1: the block is 2 × 3 × 4 = 24, and 1 + 6 + 11 + 6 = 24 ✓. Checking with a simple number catches most multiplication slips.

🌍 Real life

A tailor adds the same seam allowance x to every side of a 30 cm × 20 cm piece: cloth needed (30 + 2x)(20 + 2x) = 4x² + 100x + 600. A baker's cake tin grows by x cm each way: the batter needed is a volume rule like v(x). Shopkeepers, builders and packers use such rules to see quickly how cost changes with size.

4. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • Area of the stretched 3 × 2 rectangle: a(x) = (x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 5x + 6; a(1) = 12, a(1½) = 15¾, a(2) = 20.
  • Volume of the stretched 1 × 2 × 3 block: v(x) = (x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3) = x³ + 6x² + 11x + 6.
  • To multiply sums: each term by each term, then collect like terms.
  • Perimeter rules have x; area rules have x²; volume rules have x³.
