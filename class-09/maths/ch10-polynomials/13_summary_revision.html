1. The chapter in one picture

𝑥² One rectangle, three rules

A 3 cm × 2 cm rectangle with every side increased by x. Its perimeter is p(x) = 4x + 10 (degree 1) and its area is a(x) = x² + 5x + 6 (degree 2). The block 1 × 2 × 3 grown the same way has v(x) = x³ + 6x² + 11x + 6 (degree 3).

2. The key ideas

1Measures as rules (pages 177–178)
p(x) = 4x + 10

When every side of the 3 × 2 rectangle grows by x cm, the perimeter is p(x) cm. Writing p(x) instead of p shows that p changes with x; p(3) = 22 means “x = 3 gives p = 22”. (അളവുകളുടെ ബീജഗണിതം)

2Area and volume (pages 178–180)
a(x) = x² + 5x + 6
v(x) = x³ + 6x² + 11x + 6

Multiply out the brackets (ഗുണനസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ), letters first: (x + 3)(x + 2) = x² + 5x + 6; a(1½) = 15¾.

3Polynomial (page 181)

ഇത്തരം ക്രിയകൾ മാത്രം ഉൾപ്പെടുന്ന ബീജഗണിത വാചകത്തിന്റെ പൊതുവായ പേരാണ് ബഹുപദം.
An algebraic expression in which powers of the changing number x are multiplied by fixed numbers and added (a fixed number may be added too) is a polynomial (ബഹുപദം).

4Not polynomials (pages 181–182)
√(2x² + 2x + 1)    2x + 50/x

Square root of x (വർഗമൂലം) or reciprocal of x (വ്യുൽക്രമം) → not a polynomial. But roots of fixed numbers are fine: ½x² + √2 x is a polynomial.

5Degree (page 182)

ഇങ്ങനെ വരുന്ന ഏറ്റവും വലിയ കൃത്യങ്കത്തെ ബഹുപദത്തിന്റെ കൃത്യങ്കം എന്നാണ് പറയുന്നത്.
The highest power of x is the degree (കൃത്യങ്കം): 4x + 10 → 1, x² + 5x + 6 → 2, x³ + 6x² + 11x + 6 → 3.

6General forms and coefficients (page 183)
ax + b  ·  ax² + bx + c  ·  ax³ + bx² + cx + d

a, b, c, d are fixed numbers, the coefficients (ഗുണകങ്ങൾ) of the powers; they can be any real numbers (a ≠ 0 for the stated degree).

7Special values (exercise 2)
p(0) = d    p(1) = a + b + c + d
p(−1) = −a + b − c + d

p(0) is the constant term, p(1) the sum of the coefficients, and p(10) writes single-digit coefficients as digits: 4x³ + 2x² + 3x + 7 → 4237.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • p(2) is not p × 2. p(2) means “the value of p when x is 2”: for p(x) = 4x + 10, p(2) = 18.
  • Forgetting brackets with negatives. For p(x) = 3x² + 6x + 1, p(−1) = 3(−1)² + 6(−1) + 1 = 3 − 6 + 1 = −2; writing −1² as −1 gives a wrong answer.
  • Thinking any √ makes it “not a polynomial”. √2 x is a polynomial – √2 is a fixed number. Only roots of the changing number spoil it.
  • Calling x + 1/x a polynomial. 1/x is the reciprocal of x – not a power of x we allow. Not a polynomial.
  • Losing the sign of a coefficient. In 2x² − 5x + 3 the coefficient of x is −5, not 5.
  • Degree = number of terms? No: x³ + 1 has two terms but degree 3.
  • (x + 3)(x + 2) ≠ x² + 6. Multiply every term by every term: x² + 2x + 3x + 6 = x² + 5x + 6.
