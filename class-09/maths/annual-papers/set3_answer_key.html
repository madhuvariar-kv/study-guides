1.
The sum of two numbers is 36 and their difference is 10. The smaller number is
(a) 13(b) 23(c) 18(d) 26
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Sum (തുക) − difference (വ്യത്യാസം) = twice the smaller number: 36 − 10 = 26.
  2. Smaller number = 26 ÷ 2 = 13. (Larger = (36 + 10) ÷ 2 = 23. Check: 23 + 13 = 36, 23 − 13 = 10 ✓.)
✅ Answer: (a) 13

Marking: 1 mark for (a).

The orthocentre (the point where the altitudes meet) of a right triangle is
(a) the midpoint of the hypotenuse(b) the vertex of the right angle(c) the centroid(d) a point outside the triangle
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. In a right triangle each of the two perpendicular sides is perpendicular to the other side through the opposite vertex, so each of them is an altitude.
  2. These two altitudes meet at the right-angle vertex, so the orthocentre (ലംബകേന്ദ്രം) is the vertex of the right angle. (The midpoint of the hypotenuse is the circumcentre, not the orthocentre.)
✅ Answer: (b) the vertex of the right angle

11.
If 1 is added to the numerator of a fraction, it becomes ½. If 3 is added to its denominator, it becomes ⅓. Find the fraction.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the numerator (അംശം) be x and the denominator (ഛേദം) be y.
  2. x + 1y = ½ means y is twice x + 1:   y = 2x + 2.
  3. xy + 3 = ⅓ means y + 3 is three times x:   y + 3 = 3x.
  4. Put y = 2x + 2 in the second equation: 2x + 2 + 3 = 3x ⇒ 2x + 5 = 3x ⇒ x = 5.
  5. y = 2 × 5 + 2 = 12. The fraction is 512.
  6. Check: 612 = ½ ✓ and 515 = ⅓ ✓.
✅ Answer: 512

Marking: Two equations: 1 mark (½ each). Numerator ½, denominator ½.

12.
State whether each statement is true or false, with the reason.   (a) √8 + √2 is bigger than √10.   (b) (√5 + 2) − (√5 − 1) is not a fraction (rational number).
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Draw a right triangle with perpendicular sides √8 and √2. Its hypotenuse² = 8 + 2 = 10, so the hypotenuse is √10.
  2. In any triangle two sides together are longer than the third side, so √8 + √2 > √10: true. (Decimals: 2.828 + 1.414 = 4.242 > 3.162 ✓.)
  3. (b) (√5 + 2) − (√5 − 1) = √5 + 2 − √5 + 1 = 3.
  4. The √5 parts cancel and the result 3 is a natural number (so also a fraction): the statement is false.
✅ Answer: (a) True   (b) False – it equals 3

Marking: (a) “true” ½, reason ½. (b) “false” ½, working showing 3 ½.

13.
In triangle ABC, AD is the median to BC and G is the centroid (figure). Prove that the area of triangle BGD is ⅙ of the area of triangle ABC.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. D is the midpoint of BC, so BD = DC. Triangles ABD and ACD have equal bases on BC and the same height from A, so area ABD = ½ × area ABC.
  2. The centroid (മധ്യമകേന്ദ്രം) divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1, so AG = 2 GD, i.e. GD = ⅓ AD.
  3. Triangles BGD and BAD have their bases GD and AD on the same line AD and the same vertex B, so they have the same height; their areas are in the ratio of the bases: area BGD = ⅓ × area BAD.
  4. So area BGD = ⅓ × ½ × area ABC = ⅙ × area ABC. ∎
✅ Answer: area BGD = ⅓ × area ABD = ⅓ × ½ × area ABC = ⅙ area ABC

Marking: Area ABD = ½ ABC: ½. AG : GD = 2 : 1: ½. Same height ⇒ ratio of bases: ½. Conclusion: ½.

14.
Numbers that are 1 less than a multiple of 5 (4, 9, 14, 19, …) can be written as 5n − 1. Prove that the product of two such numbers is always 1 more than a multiple of 5. Check with 9 × 14.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Take two such numbers 5m − 1 and 5n − 1 (m, n natural numbers).
  2. (x − y)(u − v) = xu − xv − yu + yv, so (5m − 1)(5n − 1) = 25mn − 5m − 5n + 1.
  3. = 5(5mn − m − n) + 1, which is 1 more than a multiple of 5. ∎
  4. Check: 9 × 14 = 126 = 125 + 1 = 5 × 25 + 1 ✓ (here m = 2, n = 3 and 5mn − m − n = 30 − 2 − 3 = 25).
✅ Answer: (5m − 1)(5n − 1) = 5(5mn − m − n) + 1

Marking: Expansion 1, writing as 5(…) + 1 ½, check ½.

15.
In the roof truss ABC (figure), the rafters meet at a right angle at C and the post CD is perpendicular to the beam AB. AD = 2 m and DB = 8 m. (a) Why do triangles ADC and CDB have the same angles? (b) Find the length of the post CD and the area of triangle ABC.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Let ∠A = x. In triangle ADC, ∠ADC = 90°, so ∠ACD = 90° − x.
  2. ∠ACB = 90°, so ∠BCD = 90° − (90° − x) = x; then in triangle CDB, ∠B = 90° − x.
  3. So both triangles have the angles x, 90° − x and 90°: the same angles.
  4. (b) In triangles with the same angles (സദൃശത്രികോണങ്ങൾ), sides opposite equal angles are in the same ratio. Opposite x: CD (in ADC) and DB (in CDB). Opposite 90° − x: AD (in ADC) and CD (in CDB).
  5. So CDDB = ADCD, i.e. CD8 = 2CD ⇒ CD² = 16 ⇒ CD = 4 m.
  6. AB = 2 + 8 = 10 m. Area = ½ × AB × CD = ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 m².
✅ Answer: (a) both have angles x, 90° − x, 90°   (b) CD = 4 m; area = 20 m²

Marking: (a) ½. (b) ratio equation ½, CD ½, area ½.

17.
Solve:   (a) |x − 7| = |x + 2|   (b) |x + 2| = 5
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) |x − 7| is the distance between x and 7; |x + 2| = |x − (−2)| is the distance between x and −2.
  2. Equal distances from 7 and −2 means x is their midpoint (മധ്യബിന്ദു): x = ½(7 + (−2)) = ½ × 5 = 2½.
  3. (b) |x + 2| = |x − (−2)| = 5: x is at distance 5 from −2.
  4. To the right: −2 + 5 = 3; to the left: −2 − 5 = −7.
✅ Answer: (a) x = 2½   (b) x = 3 or x = −7

18.
A rectangle has breadth x cm and length 2x cm. Write as expressions in x: (a) the length of its diagonal, (b) its perimeter divided by its area. Is each a polynomial? Give the reason, and the degree where it is one.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) By Pythagoras, diagonal² = x² + (2x)² = x² + 4x² = 5x², so diagonal = √5 × x = √5 x cm.
  2. Here x is only multiplied by the fixed number √5, so it is a polynomial (ബഹുപദം) of degree 1. (√5 is a coefficient; it does not change with x.)
  3. (b) Perimeter = 2(2x + x) = 6x; area = 2x × x = 2x². Perimeter ÷ area = 6x2x² = 3x.
  4. This takes the reciprocal of the changing number x, so it is not a polynomial.
✅ Answer: (a) √5 x – polynomial of degree 1   (b) 3/x – not a polynomial

Marking: (a) expression ½, “polynomial, degree 1” with reason ½. (b) expression ½, “not a polynomial” with reason ½.

19.
Two positive numbers have sum s and difference d. (a) Show that the larger number is ½(s + d) and the smaller is ½(s − d). (b) A 7.5 m rope is cut into two pieces whose lengths differ by 2.5 m. Use (a) to find the pieces. (c) Someone says: “A rectangle has perimeter 18 cm and its length is 10 cm more than its breadth.” Show that this is impossible.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Let the numbers be x (larger) and y: x + y = s and x − y = d.
  2. Adding: 2x = s + d ⇒ x = ½(s + d). Subtracting: 2y = s − d ⇒ y = ½(s − d).
  3. (b) s = 7.5, d = 2.5: longer piece = ½(7.5 + 2.5) = ½ × 10 = 5 m; shorter piece = ½(7.5 − 2.5) = ½ × 5 = 2.5 m. (Check 5 + 2.5 = 7.5, 5 − 2.5 = 2.5 ✓.)
  4. (c) Length + breadth = half the perimeter = 9 cm, and length − breadth = 10 cm.
  5. Breadth = ½(9 − 10) = −½ cm. A length cannot be negative: the difference of two positive numbers can never be more than their sum. So such a rectangle is impossible.
✅ Answer: (a) add / subtract the equations   (b) 5 m and 2.5 m   (c) breadth would be −½ cm

Marking: (a) 1. (b) both pieces 1 (½ each). (c) equations ½, conclusion with reason ½.

20.
Two right-triangular flower beds are made in the corners of a courtyard. Bed P has perpendicular sides 1 m and 3 m; bed Q has perpendicular sides 2 m and 2 m. (a) Find the length of the third side (hypotenuse) of each bed. (b) Without adding up either perimeter, show that the perimeter of P is longer than that of Q by √10 − √8 m. (c) Using √10 ≈ 3.162 and √8 ≈ 2.828, find this difference in centimetres.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) By Pythagoras: bed P, hypotenuse² = 1² + 3² = 10 ⇒ hypotenuse √10 m. Bed Q, hypotenuse² = 2² + 2² = 8 ⇒ hypotenuse √8 m.
  2. (b) Perimeter of P = 1 + 3 + √10 = 4 + √10; perimeter of Q = 2 + 2 + √8 = 4 + √8.
  3. Difference = (4 + √10) − (4 + √8) = 4 + √10 − 4 − √8 = √10 − √8 m: the two perpendicular sides add to 4 m in both beds, so only the hypotenuses differ.
  4. (c) √10 − √8 ≈ 3.162 − 2.828 = 0.334 m = 33.4 cm.
✅ Answer: (a) √10 m and √8 m   (b) (4 + √10) − (4 + √8) = √10 − √8   (c) 33.4 cm

Marking: (a) ½ + ½. (b) both perimeters and the subtraction 1. (c) 1.

21.
Draw triangle ABC with BC = 7 cm, CA = 5 cm and AB = 6 cm. Find the midpoints D of BC and E of CA by drawing their perpendicular bisectors. Draw the medians AD and BE and mark their meeting point G. Measure AD and AG, and check that AG = 2 GD.
[3]

  1. Draw BC = 7 cm. With centre B and radius 6 cm draw an arc; with centre C and radius 5 cm draw an arc cutting it at A. Join AB and AC.

  3. In the same way draw the perpendicular bisector of CA (radius say 3.5 cm); it cuts CA at its midpoint E.

✅ Answer: G is the centroid; AD ≈ 4.3 cm, AG ≈ 2.8 cm, GD ≈ 1.4 cm, so AG = 2 GD

22.
A rectangular plot has length x m and breadth y m (x > 3). It is reshaped: the length is decreased by 3 m and the breadth is increased by 3 m (figure). (a) Use an identity to show that the new area is xy + 3(x − y) − 9. (b) Show that the new area is more than the old area when x − y > 3, the same when x − y = 3 and less when x − y < 3. (c) For a plot 20 m long and 12 m broad, find the new area using (a), and check by direct multiplication.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) New sides: x − 3 and y + 3. Using (x + y)(u − v) = xu − xv + yu − yv:
  2. (y + 3)(x − 3) = yx − 3y + 3x − 9 = xy + 3(x − y) − 9.
  3. (b) New area − old area = 3(x − y) − 9 = 3(x − y − 3).

  5. (c) xy = 20 × 12 = 240 and x − y = 8: new area = 240 + 3 × 8 − 9 = 240 + 24 − 9 = 255 m².
  6. Check: the new plot is 17 m × 15 m = 255 m² ✓ (bigger than 240, since x − y = 8 > 3).
✅ Answer: (a) (y + 3)(x − 3) = xy + 3(x − y) − 9   (b) compare 3(x − y) with 9   (c) 255 m²

A straight road is taken as a number line; the bus stand is at 0 and distances are in km, east positive (figure). Bus stop A is at −7 and bus stop B is at 5. (a) Find the distance between A and B. (b) A new stop M is to be equally far from A and B. Write this condition with absolute values and find M. (c) A school bus must always stay less than 4 km from M. Write this with absolute value, then as an inequality, and show it on the number line.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Distance = bigger − smaller = 5 − (−7) = 5 + 7 = 12 km (= |−7 − 5|).

  5. So x lies between −1 − 4 = −5 and −1 + 4 = 3: −5 < x < 3.
  6. On the number line (open circles: −5 and 3 themselves are not allowed):
✅ Answer: (a) 12 km   (b) |x + 7| = |x − 5|, M = −1   (c) |x + 1| < 4, i.e. −5 < x < 3

24.

  4. (c) The diagonal of a square of side a is √2 a, so the outer diagonal = √2 (x + 4) = √2 x + 4√2 cm.
  5. x is only multiplied by the fixed number √2 and the fixed number 4√2 is added, so it is a polynomial (degree 1, coefficients √2 and 4√2 – ഗുണകങ്ങൾ).
✅ Answer: (a) f(x) = 8x + 16   (b) degree 1; f(10) = 96 cm²   (c) √2 x + 4√2 – a polynomial

25.
Meera makes a paper kite ABDC for Onam (figure). Triangle ABC is equilateral with side 12 cm. Triangle BCD on the other side of BC has BD = 9 cm and CD = 15 cm. (a) Find the height of triangle ABC from A. (b) Find the area of triangle ABC. (c) Find the area of triangle BCD using Heron’s formula. (d) Find the total area of the kite correct to two decimal places, taking √3 ≈ 1.732.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) The height of an equilateral triangle bisects the base: (half side)² + h² = side², so h² = 12² − 6² = 144 − 36 = 108.
  2. h = √108 = √(36 × 3) = 6√3 cm (= √3 × half the side).
  3. (b) Area = ½ × 12 × 6√3 = 36√3 cm² (= √3 × 6²).
  4. (c) Sides 12, 9, 15: s = ½(12 + 9 + 15) = 18; s − 12 = 6, s − 9 = 9, s − 15 = 3.
  5. Area = √(18 × 6 × 9 × 3) = √2916 = 54 cm². (Check: 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225 = 15², so BCD is right-angled at B and its area is ½ × 9 × 12 = 54 ✓.)
  6. (d) Total = 36√3 + 54 ≈ 36 × 1.732 + 54 = 62.352 + 54 = 116.352 ≈ 116.35 cm².
✅ Answer: (a) 6√3 cm   (b) 36√3 cm²   (c) 54 cm²   (d) ≈ 116.35 cm²

Marking: (a) 1. (b) 1. (c) s ½, area ½. (d) 1.

26.
Children are decorating the temple ground for the festival. (a) At 4 p.m. a vertical stick 1.2 m tall casts a shadow 0.8 m long, and at the same time the flagstaff (കൊടിമരം) casts a shadow 6 m long. (b) For the pandal, two bamboo poles start from a point O (figure): OA = 2 m, AC = 1 m, OB = 2.4 m, BD = 1.2 m, and the cross piece AB = 1.8 m. [4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) The stick and the flagstaff are vertical, so each makes a right angle with the ground.
  2. At the same moment the sun’s rays are parallel, so they make equal angles with the level ground. The two triangles (object, shadow, ray) therefore have the same angles.
  3. Sides opposite equal angles are in the same ratio: heightshadow is the same: h6 = 1.20.8 = 1.5 ⇒ h = 6 × 1.5 = 9 m.
  4. (b)(i) OC = 2 + 1 = 3 m and OD = 2.4 + 1.2 = 3.6 m. OC/OA = 3/2 = 1½ and OD/OB = 3.6/2.4 = 1½. ∠COD = ∠AOB (the same angle at O).
  5. (ii) Two sides change by the same scale 1½ and the angles between them are equal, so by the third way (മൂന്നാംവഴി) the third side also changes by 1½: CD = 1½ × 1.8 = 2.7 m.
  6. (iii) The triangles also have the other angles equal: ∠OCD = ∠OAB. These are corresponding angles made by the line OC with AB and CD, so CD ∥ AB.
✅ Answer: (a) 9 m   (b) (i) 1½ times (ii) CD = 2.7 m (iii) yes

Marking: (a) reason for equal angles 1, height 1. (b)(i) ½ (ii) rule named ½, CD ½ (iii) ½.

27.
In a Vallam Kali race the straight canal is taken as a number line in metres: the finishing post is O (zero) and east is positive. Two boats, Karichal and Chundan, pass O at the same moment; this moment is time 0, times after it are positive and times before it are negative. Position s = vt. (a) Karichal moves west at 8 m/s. Write its v and find its position 3 s after and 3 s before passing O. (b) With the distributive law (x + y)z = xz + yz, taking x = 10, y = −8, z = −3, explain why (−8) × (−3) must be 24. (c) 4 s before passing O, Chundan was at 36. Find its v. Which boat is faster? (d) Find both positions 5 s after passing O. Which boat is ahead, and by how much?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) West is the negative direction, so v = −8. After 3 s: s = (−8) × 3 = −24 (24 m west of O).
  2. 3 s before: t = −3, s = (−8) × (−3) = 24 (24 m east of O – the boat was coming from the east).
  3. (b) Left side: (10 + (−8)) × (−3) = 2 × (−3) = −6.
  4. Right side: 10 × (−3) + (−8) × (−3) = −30 + (−8) × (−3). For both sides to be equal, −30 + (−8) × (−3) = −6, and −30 + 24 = −6, so (−8) × (−3) = 24.
  5. (c) s = vt gives v = s ÷ t = 36 ÷ (−4) = −9 (ന്യൂനഹരണം: positive ÷ negative is negative). Chundan also moves west, at 9 m/s.
  6. Chundan’s speed 9 m/s is more than Karichal’s 8 m/s, so Chundan is faster.
  7. (d) t = 5: Karichal s = (−8) × 5 = −40; Chundan s = (−9) × 5 = −45.
  8. Both go west and −45 is further west, so Chundan is ahead by −40 − (−45) = −40 + 45 = 5 m.
✅ Answer: (a) v = −8; −24 and 24   (b) −30 + ? = −6 ⇒ 24   (c) v = −9; Chundan   (d) −40 and −45; Chundan by 5 m

Marking: (a) v ½, two positions ½. (b) 1. (c) v ½, faster boat ½. (d) positions ½, lead ½.

28.
A kolam is painted on a square tile of side 20 cm (figure). At each corner there is a quarter circle of radius 5 cm (shaded grey), painted red. At the centre there is a ring between circles of radii 7 cm and 5 cm (shaded blue), painted yellow; the inner circle and the rest of the tile are left unpainted. (a) Find the total red area, as a multiple of π. (b) Find the yellow area, as a multiple of π. (c) Taking π ≈ 3.14, find the unpainted area. (d) A circle is to be painted with the same area as the yellow ring. Find its radius in simplest form.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Four quarter circles of radius 5 make one full circle: red area = π × 5² = 25π cm².
  2. (b) Ring (വൃത്തവലയം) = big circle − small circle = π × 7² − π × 5² = 49π − 25π = 24π cm².
  3. (c) Square = 20 × 20 = 400 cm². Painted = 25π + 24π = 49π ≈ 49 × 3.14 = 153.86 cm².
  4. Unpainted = 400 − 153.86 = 246.14 cm².
  5. (d) πr² = 24π ⇒ r² = 24 ⇒ r = √24 = √(4 × 6) = 2√6 cm (≈ 4.9 cm).
  6. This is the third side of a right triangle with hypotenuse 7 and one side 5: 7² − 5² = 24 – the textbook’s “ring = circle” idea.
✅ Answer: (a) 25π cm²   (b) 24π cm²   (c) 246.14 cm²   (d) 2√6 cm

Marking: (a) 1. (b) 1. (c) painted area ½, answer ½. (d) r² = 24 ½, 2√6 ½.
