1.
The height of an equilateral triangle of side 2 cm is
(a) 1 cm(b) √2 cm(c) √3 cm(d) 3 cm
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. The height cuts the triangle into two right triangles with hypotenuse 2 cm and base 1 cm (half the side).
  2. By Pythagoras, height² = 2² − 1² = 4 − 1 = 3, so the height is √3 cm.
✅ Answer: (c) √3 cm

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

To divide a line AB into 5 equal parts using parallel lines, how many equal steps should be marked on the slanted line drawn from A?
(a) 4(b) 5(c) 6(d) 10
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Mark 5 equal steps A1, …, A5 on the slanted line and join A5 to B.
  2. Lines through A1, A2, A3, A4 parallel to A5B cut AB into 5 equal parts, because parallel lines that cut one line into equal parts cut every line into equal parts.
✅ Answer: (b) 5

True or false? Give the reason:   10½ × 4½ = 40¼
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  2. 10½ × 4½ = (10 × 4) + ½(10 + 4) + ¼ = 40 + 7 + ¼ = 47¼, not 40¼. So the statement is false. (40¼ leaves out the two products 10 × ½ and ½ × 4.)
✅ Answer: False; 10½ × 4½ = 47¼

11.
Five years ago a mother was 7 times as old as her daughter. Five years from now she will be 3 times as old as her daughter. Find their present ages.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the daughter’s present age be d and the mother’s m years.
  2. Five years ago: m − 5 = 7(d − 5), so m = 7d − 30.
  3. Five years from now: m + 5 = 3(d + 5), so m = 3d + 10.
  4. So 7d − 30 = 3d + 10 ⇒ 4d = 40 ⇒ d = 10; m = 3 × 10 + 10 = 40.
  5. Check: 5 years ago 35 = 7 × 5 ✓; in 5 years 45 = 3 × 15 ✓.
✅ Answer: Daughter 10 years, mother 40 years

Marking: Two equations 1 mark; daughter’s age ½, mother’s age ½.

[2]

  3. Correct to a centimetre (two decimal places): 7.16 m.
✅ Answer: (a) √10 m (b) 7.16 m

13.
In the figure, the lines l, m, n are parallel. AB = BC = 2.5 cm and PQ = 3.2 cm. (a) Find QR. Give the reason. (b) Are the lines l, m, n equally spaced? Why?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

✅ Answer: (a) QR = 3.2 cm (b) Yes

15.
In the triangles ABC and PQR, ∠A = ∠P and ∠B = ∠Q (figure). AB = 4 cm, BC = 6 cm, CA = 5 cm and PQ = 6 cm. Find QR and PR.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Two pairs of angles are equal, so the third angles are equal too: ∠C = ∠R. The triangles have the same angles.
  2. So sides opposite equal angles are in the same ratio. AB (opposite ∠C) and PQ (opposite ∠R): 6 ÷ 4 = 1½. Every side of PQR is 1½ times the matching side of ABC.
  3. QR is opposite ∠P, matching BC (opposite ∠A): QR = 1½ × 6 = 9 cm.
  4. PR is opposite ∠Q, matching CA (opposite ∠B): PR = 1½ × 5 = 7.5 cm.
✅ Answer: QR = 9 cm, PR = 7.5 cm

Marking: Same angles and scale factor 1½: 1 mark; QR ½, PR ½.

16.
Take x = −3, y = 5, z = −4. (a) Show that x − (y − z) = (x − y) + z. (b) Is x − (y − z) equal to x − y − z for these numbers?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) y − z = 5 − (−4) = 5 + 4 = 9 (since x − (−y) = x + y). So x − (y − z) = −3 − 9 = −(3 + 9) = −12.
  2. x − y = −3 − 5 = −8, and (−8) + (−4) = −(8 + 4) = −12. Both sides are equal.
  3. (b) x − y − z = −3 − 5 − (−4) = −8 + 4 = −4, which is not −12. So no: subtracting (y − z) is the same as subtracting y and then adding z.
✅ Answer: (a) both sides are −12 (b) No: x − y − z = −4

Marking: (a) each side ½; (b) value −4 ½, conclusion ½.

18.
Each edge of a cube of edge 2 cm is increased by x cm. Write the volume v(x) of the new cube as a polynomial. What is its degree?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. The new edge is x + 2, so v(x) = (x + 2)(x + 2)(x + 2).
  2. (x + 2)(x + 2) = x² + 2x + 2x + 4 = x² + 4x + 4.
  3. (x + 2)(x² + 4x + 4) = (x³ + 4x² + 4x) + (2x² + 8x + 8) = x³ + 6x² + 12x + 8.
  4. The highest power of x is 3, so the degree is 3. (Check: v(1) = 1 + 6 + 12 + 8 = 27 = 3³ ✓.)
✅ Answer: v(x) = x³ + 6x² + 12x + 8; degree 3

Marking: (x + 2)² ½, the polynomial 1 mark, degree ½.

19.
(a) Show that (3½)² − (2½)² = 6. (b) Use this to draw a line of length √6 cm with ruler and compass, and draw a square of area 6 sq cm on it. Write the steps. (c) 2.4² = 5.76 and 2.5² = 6.25. Between which two tenths does √6 lie?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) (3½)² = 12¼ and (2½)² = 6¼, so (3½)² − (2½)² = 12¼ − 6¼ = 6.
  2. (b) Draw AB = 2.5 cm. Draw the perpendicular to AB at B.
  3. With A as centre and radius 3.5 cm, draw an arc cutting the perpendicular at C. Join AC.
  4. ABC is a right triangle with hypotenuse AC = 3.5 cm, so BC² = 3.5² − 2.5² = 6 (part (a)): BC = √6 cm.
  5. On BC draw the square BCDE (perpendiculars at B and C, arcs of radius BC). Its area is (√6)² = 6 sq cm.
  6. (c) 2.4² = 5.76 < 6 < 6.25 = 2.5², so 2.4 < √6 < 2.5.
✅ Answer: (a) 12¼ − 6¼ = 6 (b) right triangle with hypotenuse 3½ cm and one side 2½ cm; the other side is √6 cm (c) between 2.4 and 2.5

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) right triangle with the correct sides ½, BC = √6 with reason ½, square ½; (c) ½ mark.

20.
Construct a square of perimeter 13 cm. Write the steps of construction and say why the method gives equal parts. What is the length of a side of the square?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Side of the square = 13 ÷ 4 = 3¼ cm – so we divide a 13 cm line into 4 equal parts.
  2. Draw AB = 13 cm. From A draw a slanted ray AX. With the compass mark 4 equal steps of 3 cm on it: A1, A2, A3, A4.
  3. Join A4B. Through A1, A2, A3 draw lines parallel to A4B (slide a set square). They meet AB at P, Q, R. Then AP = PQ = QR = RB = 3¼ cm.
  4. Draw perpendiculars at the two ends of AP, cut them with arcs of radius AP, and join: this is the square of side 3¼ cm, perimeter 4 × 3¼ = 13 cm.
  5. Why: the parallel lines through A1, A2, A3, A4 (and one through A) cut AX into equal parts. Parallel lines that cut one line into equal parts cut every line into equal parts, so they cut AB into 4 equal parts.
✅ Answer: Side = 13 ÷ 4 = 3¼ cm; divide a 13 cm line into 4 equal parts with parallel lines and draw the square on one part

Marking: Side 3¼ cm ½; dividing the line (ray, equal steps, parallels) 1½; square drawn ½; reason ½.

22.
A KSRTC bus runs on a straight east–west road. Positions are measured in kilometres from the bus stand O, east as positive and west as negative. The bus starts 4 km west of O. It goes 7 km east, then 10 km west, then 2 km east. (a) Write each move as an addition and find the position after the second move. (b) Find the final position and say where it is. (c) What single change of position would take the bus from its start straight to the same place?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Start: −4. First move: −4 + 7 = 7 − 4 = 3.
  2. Second move (10 km west = −10): 3 + (−10) = 3 − 10 = −(10 − 3) = −7.
  3. (b) Third move: −7 + 2 = −(7 − 2) = −5. The bus ends 5 km west of the bus stand.
  4. (c) Change = 7 + (−10) + 2 = −1: one move of 1 km west. Check: −4 + (−1) = −(4 + 1) = −5 ✓.
✅ Answer: (a) −7 (b) −5: 5 km west of O (c) −1, i.e. 1 km west

Marking: (a) additions ½, −7 ½; (b) −5 ½, meaning ½; (c) 1 mark.

23.
Consider the numbers −3,  2/5,  √7,  0,  −√16,  π,  1.6. (a) Which of them are integers? Which are rational and which are irrational? (b) Arrange them in increasing order. [3]

Step-by-step solution

25.
At the Nehru Trophy boat race in Alappuzha, the gallery sells adult tickets and child tickets. One family paid ₹1300 for 3 adult and 2 child tickets. Another family paid ₹1100 for 2 adult and 3 child tickets. (a) Write two equations, taking the prices of an adult ticket and a child ticket as ₹x and ₹y. (b) Find the price of each ticket. (c) A school group bought 50 tickets in all and paid ₹10000. How many adult and how many child tickets did they buy?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) 3x + 2y = 1300 … (1)   2x + 3y = 1100 … (2)
  2. (b) Add (1) and (2): 5x + 5y = 2400, so x + y = 480.
  3. Subtract (2) from (1): x − y = 200.
  4. Sum + difference = twice the larger: 2x = 680 ⇒ x = 340; y = 480 − 340 = 140. Adult ticket ₹340, child ticket ₹140. (Check: 1020 + 280 = 1300 ✓, 680 + 420 = 1100 ✓.)
  5. (c) Let the group buy a adult and c child tickets: a + c = 50 and 340a + 140c = 10000.
  6. Multiply the first by 140: 140a + 140c = 7000. Subtract it from the second: 200a = 3000 ⇒ a = 15, c = 50 − 15 = 35.
  7. Check: 15 × 340 + 35 × 140 = 5100 + 4900 = 10000 ✓.
✅ Answer: (b) adult ₹340, child ₹140 (c) 15 adult tickets and 35 child tickets

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) method ½, each price ½; (c) equations ½, the two numbers ½.

26.
Anu’s family has a rectangular vegetable plot in the courtyard, x m long and y m wide. Its area is 120 sq m and its perimeter is 46 m. Without finding x and y first: (a) Using (x + 1)(y + 1) = xy + x + y + 1, find the area if the length and the breadth are each increased by 1 m (figure). (b) Find the area if each is increased by ½ m instead. (c) Using (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy, find the length and the breadth. (d) Check your answers to (a) and (b) with these sides.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  6. (d) 16 × 9 = 144 ✓ and 15½ × 8½ = 120 + ½(23) + ¼ = 131¾ ✓ (or 15½ × 8½ = 131.75).
✅ Answer: (a) 144 sq m (b) 131¾ sq m (c) 15 m and 8 m (d) 16 × 9 = 144, 15½ × 8½ = 131¾

27.
Before a Vallam Kali boat race, the organisers want the width of the river. T is a tree on the far bank and A is the point on the near bank just opposite it (TA is perpendicular to the bank). Rahul walks 50 m along the bank from A to B, puts a stick at B, and walks 10 m more to C. From C he walks at right angles to the bank, away from the river, until the stick B and the tree T are in one line with him; this point is D, and CD = 12 m (figure). (a) Show that the triangles TAB and DCB have the same angles. (b) Write the ratios of the sides opposite the equal angles. (c) Find the width TA of the river. (d) If Rahul had walked only 5 m beyond B, how far from C would D have been?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) ∠TAB = ∠DCB = 90°. ∠ABT = ∠CBD, since they are vertically opposite angles (TD and AC are straight lines).
  2. Two angles are equal, so the third angles ∠ATB and ∠CDB are equal too (angles of a triangle add up to 180°). So the triangles have the same angles.
  3. (b) Sides opposite equal angles are in the same ratio: opposite ∠B: TA and DC; opposite ∠T and ∠D: AB and CB; opposite the right angles: TB and DB. So TA/DC = AB/CB = TB/DB.
  4. (c) AB/CB = 50/10 = 5, so TA = 5 × DC = 5 × 12 = 60 m.
  5. (d) The river is still 60 m wide and now AB/CB = 50/5 = 10, so CD = TA ÷ 10 = 60 ÷ 10 = 6 m.
✅ Answer: (a) right angles at A and C, vertically opposite angles at B (b) TA/DC = AB/CB = TB/DB (c) 60 m (d) 6 m

Marking: (a) right angles and vertically opposite angles ½, third angle ½; (b) 1 mark; (c) 1 mark; (d) 1 mark.

28.
The walking track round a panchayat playground has two straight parts of 66 m each, joined at the ends by two semicircles of diameter 28 m (figure). Take π = 227. (a) Find the length of one semicircular end. (b) Find the length of one round of the track. (c) Manu rides a bicycle once round the track. The diameter of its wheel is 70 cm. How many times does the wheel turn? (d) A second track runs 1 m outside the first all round (straight parts still 66 m, semicircles of diameter 30 m). How much longer is one round of it?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Circumference of a circle of diameter 28 m = π × 28 = 227 × 28 = 88 m. A semicircle is half: 44 m.
  2. (b) One round = 66 + 66 + 44 + 44 = 220 m.
  3. (c) One turn of the wheel covers its circumference: π × 70 = 227 × 70 = 220 cm = 2.2 m.
  4. Number of turns = 220 ÷ 2.2 = 100.
  5. (d) The two semicircles of the outer track make a full circle of diameter 30 m: π × 30 = 6607 = 9427 m. One round = 132 + 9427 = 22627 m.
  6. Extra length = 22627 − 220 = 627 m. (In general the difference is π × 30 − π × 28 = π × 2 = 2π, because the diameter grows by 2 m; 2π ≈ 6.28 m.)
✅ Answer: (a) 44 m (b) 220 m (c) 100 times (d) 627 m (that is 2π m)

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark; (c) circumference of the wheel ½, 100 turns ½; (d) outer round ½, difference ½.
