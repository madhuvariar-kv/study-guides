Multiplying Differences വ്യത്യാസ ഗുണനം

Section 1 multiplied sums. But 26 × 17 is much easier to think of as (30 − 4)(20 − 3): numbers just below round numbers. This page finds the identity for a difference times a difference, (x − y)(u − v) = xu − xv − yu + yv, then a sum times a difference, (x + y)(u − v) = xu − xv + yu − yv, and uses them to find two numbers from the products of their neighbours.

📖 What you already know (from pages 1–5 of this guide)
  • (x + y)(u + v) = xu + xv + yu + yv, and (x + 1)(y + 1) = xy + x + y + 1 (page 5).
  • Numbers can be written near round numbers: 26 = 30 − 4, 17 = 20 − 3, 99 = 100 − 1 (page 4).
  • Subtracting a bracket: a − (b − c) = a − b + c; the trick 612 − 170 = 612 − 200 + 30 (page 4).
  • (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy, and from a sum and a difference you can find the two numbers (page 3).

1. One difference times a number: 18 × 5

18 × 5 can be done as (10 + 8) × 5 = 50 + 40 = 90. But 18 is also 20 − 2, and 20 × 5 is easier than 18 × 5.

🎬 Animation: 18 × 5 = (20 − 2) × 5

The same trick: 38 × 5 = (40 − 2) × 5 = 200 − 10 = 190 and 45 × 8 = 45 × (10 − 2) = 450 − 90 = 360.

🎚️ Round up, then subtract

Pick a number just below a multiple of ten (38, 47, 69, …). Multiplying the round number is easy; then take away the small extra piece.

3. In algebra: (x − y)(u − v)

Replace the numbers with letters: the big rectangle is x by u, the piece we want is (x − y) by (u − v). The textbook reads the equation off the picture and then rearranges it.

🎬 Animation: from the picture to the identity (textbook pages 76–77)
⭐ Key idea 3 (textbook page 77)

x > y, u > v ആയ ഏതു അധിസംഖ്യകളെടുത്താലും

(x − y)(u − v) = xu − xv − yu + yv

For any positive numbers with x > y and u > v. Notice the signs: the two "mixed" products xv and yu are subtracted, the product of the two small numbers yv is added back (it was subtracted twice).

Now 26 × 17 in one line: (30 − 4)(20 − 3) = 600 − 90 − 80 + 12 = 612 − 170. And 612 − 170 is easiest as 612 − 200 + 30 = 442.

4. The "previous numbers" rule: (x − 1)(y − 1)

29 × 49 = (30 − 1)(50 − 1) = 1500 − 30 − 50 + 1 = 1421. The identity behind it:

(x − 1)(y − 1) = xy − x − y + 1 = xy − (x + y) + 1

In words: the product of the numbers one before = product − sum + 1. (Compare page 5: the product of the numbers one after = product + sum + 1.)

5. Finding two numbers from their neighbours' products

Textbook page 78: the product of the numbers one after two numbers is 525, and the product of the numbers one before them is 437. What are the numbers?

🎬 Animation: add, subtract, then use a Class 8 identity
💡 The pattern of the method

Two equations with the same unknown parts xy and (x + y): adding them kills (x + y) and gives xy; subtracting kills xy and gives x + y. Then (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy (Class 8, page 3 of this guide) gives the difference, and sum ± difference gives the two numbers. Exercise 4 uses exactly this method.

6. A sum times a difference: (x + y)(u − v)

What if one number is just above a round number and the other just below? 14 × 59 = (10 + 4)(60 − 1). Here is the picture: a tall rectangle with a strip cut off the top.

🎬 Animation: (x + y)(u − v) (textbook page 80)
⭐ Key idea 4 (textbook page 80)

x, y, u, v എന്ന ഏതു നാലു അധിസംഖ്യകളിലും, u > v ആണെങ്കിൽ

(x + y)(u − v) = xu − xv + yu − yv

The algebra: (x + y)(u − v) = x(u − v) + y(u − v) = xu − xv + yu − yv. Each term of the sum multiplies the whole difference.

So 14 × 59 = (10 + 4)(60 − 1) = 600 − 10 + 240 − 4 = 590 + 236 = 826.

7. One up, one down

8 × 5 = 40. Add 1 to the bigger number and take 1 from the smaller: 9 × 4 = 36 – smaller. The other way: 7 × 6 = 42 – bigger. Why? With the bigger number x and the smaller y:

(x + 1)(y − 1) = xy − x + y − 1 = xy − (x − y) − 1      (x − 1)(y + 1) = xy + x − y − 1 = xy + (x − y) − 1
🎚️ Move one up and one down

Spreading the numbers apart (bigger up, smaller down) always lowers the product; bringing them together raises it – except for consecutive numbers (x − y = 1), where (x − 1)(y + 1) = xy + 1 − 1 = xy: no change at all. Try x = 8, y = 7.

🌍 Real life

A shop sells 99 notebooks at ₹47: 100 × 47 − 47 = ₹4653, no calculator. A customer returns one of 19 items priced ₹52: the bill is 52 × 18 = 52 × 19 − 52. A tailor trims 4 cm off a 30 cm width and 3 cm off a 20 cm length of cloth: the remaining piece is (30 − 4)(20 − 3) = 600 − 90 − 80 + 12 = 442 sq cm. Whenever something is "a little less than a round amount", the difference identities turn the sum into mental arithmetic.

🧭 Summary of Sections 4 and 5
  • A number times a difference: 18 × 5 = (20 − 2) × 5 = 100 − 10.
  • (x − y)(u − v) = xu − xv − yu + yv: 26 × 17 = (30 − 4)(20 − 3) = 600 − 90 − 80 + 12 = 442.
  • (x − 1)(y − 1) = xy − (x + y) + 1: product of the previous numbers = product − sum + 1.
  • Given (x + 1)(y + 1) and (x − 1)(y − 1): add → xy, subtract → x + y, then (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy.
  • (x + y)(u − v) = xu − xv + yu − yv: 14 × 59 = (10 + 4)(60 − 1) = 826.
  • (x + 1)(y − 1) = xy − (x − y) − 1 and (x − 1)(y + 1) = xy + (x − y) − 1 (x the bigger number).
