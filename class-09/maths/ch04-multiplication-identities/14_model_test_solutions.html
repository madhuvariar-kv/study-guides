📖 How marks are usually given

1-mark questions: the correct answer only. 2- and 3-mark questions: about one mark for writing the right identity, the rest for the working and the final answer. 5-mark questions: marks for each part as shown. In a proof, using a general form (2n + 1, x, x + 1, 10m + n, …) earns a mark by itself; examples alone earn nothing. A correct method with an arithmetic slip usually loses only one mark.

The general form of a natural number that leaves remainder 2 on division by 3 is ______.
(1)
Answer: 3n + 2
Multiples of 3 are 3n; two more than a multiple of 3 is 3n + 2 (n = 0, 1, 2, … gives 2, 5, 8, 11, …).

In any 2 × 2 square of dates on a calendar, the difference of the two diagonal products is (a) 1 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 8
(1)
Answer: (c) 7
(x + 1)(x + 7) − x(x + 8) = x² + 8x + 7 − x² − 8x = 7.

(1)

9.
Compute 32 × 52 using the identity (x + y)(u + v) = xu + xv + yu + yv. Show the four products.
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: 1664
32 × 52 = (30 + 2)(50 + 2) = 30 × 50 + 30 × 2 + 2 × 50 + 2 × 2 = 1500 + 60 + 100 + 4 = 1664.
Marks: 1 for splitting into (30 + 2)(50 + 2) and writing the four products, 1 for 1664.

10.
The product of two natural numbers is 600 and their sum is 50. Find the product of the two numbers that come just after them.
(2)
Answer: 651
(x + 1)(y + 1) = xy + (x + y) + 1 = 600 + 50 + 1 = 651. (The numbers are 20 and 30; 21 × 31 = 651 ✔.)
Marks: 1 for the identity (x + 1)(y + 1) = xy + x + y + 1, 1 for 651.

11.
The product of two odd numbers is 195 and their sum is 28. Find the product of the odd numbers that come just after each of them.
(2)
Answer: 255
The next odd numbers are x + 2 and y + 2: (x + 2)(y + 2) = xy + 2(x + y) + 4 = 195 + 56 + 4 = 255. (The numbers are 13 and 15; 15 × 17 = 255 ✔.)
Marks: 1 for using x + 2 and y + 2 with the identity xy + 2(x + y) + 4, 1 for 255. (Using x + 1 loses both marks – the next odd number is 2 more.)

12.
Compute 38 × 49 by writing each number as a difference and using the identity for (x − y)(u − v).
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: 1862
38 × 49 = (40 − 2)(50 − 1) = 40 × 50 − 40 × 1 − 2 × 50 + 2 × 1 = 2000 − 40 − 100 + 2 = 1862.
Marks: 1 for (40 − 2)(50 − 1) with the four signed products, 1 for 1862. A sign error in the last term (− 2 instead of + 2) loses 1 mark.

13.
Compute mentally, showing the identity used: (i) 6½ × 8½ (ii) 9½ × 19½
(2)
Answer: (i) 55¼ (ii) 185¼

SECTION C4 × 3 = 12 marks
14.
Numbers of the form 4n + 1 (such as 5, 9, 13, …) leave remainder 1 on division by 4. Prove that the product of any two such numbers is also of the form 4n + 1.
(3)
Proof
Take two such numbers 4m + 1 and 4n + 1 (m, n = 0, 1, 2, …).
(4m + 1)(4n + 1) = 16mn + 4m + 4n + 1 = 4(4mn + m + n) + 1.
4mn + m + n is a whole number, so the product is 4 × (a whole number) + 1: it leaves remainder 1 on division by 4. Check: 5 × 9 = 45 = 4 × 11 + 1 ✔.
Marks: 1 for writing the numbers as 4m + 1 and 4n + 1 (two different letters!), 1 for expanding with the identity, 1 for regrouping as 4(…) + 1 and the conclusion.
Review: Page 7 · Properties of operations · Page 8 · Exercise 2
15.
Four dates on a calendar form a 2 × 2 square. (i) Taking the first date as x, write the other three dates. (ii) Prove that the difference of the two diagonal products is always 7. (iii) What would the difference be in a number grid with 5 columns?
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) x + 1, x + 7, x + 8 (ii) 7 (iii) 5
(i) The date to the right is x + 1; a week later is x + 7, and x + 8.
(ii) x(x + 8) = x² + 8x and (x + 1)(x + 7) = x² + 7x + x + 7 = x² + 8x + 7. Difference = 7, whatever x is.
(iii) With 5 columns the square is x, x + 1, x + 5, x + 6: (x + 1)(x + 5) − x(x + 6) = x² + 6x + 5 − x² − 6x = 5.
Marks: 1 for the four dates, 1 for the two products and the difference 7, 1 for 5 (with the products or a clear reason).
Review: Page 7 · Calendar squares · Page 8 · Exercise 3, Q1
16.
For two natural numbers x and y, (x + 1)(y + 1) = 1056 and (x − 1)(y − 1) = 930. (i) Find xy and x + y. (ii) Find x − y. (iii) Find the two numbers.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: xy = 992, x + y = 63, x − y = 1; the numbers are 32 and 31
(i) xy + (x + y) + 1 = 1056 and xy − (x + y) + 1 = 930. Adding: 2xy + 2 = 1986 ⇒ xy = 992. Subtracting: 2(x + y) = 126 ⇒ x + y = 63.
(ii) (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy = 3969 − 3968 = 1 ⇒ x − y = 1.
(iii) x = ½(63 + 1) = 32, y = ½(63 − 1) = 31. Check: 33 × 32 = 1056 ✔, 31 × 30 = 930 ✔.
Marks: 1 for xy = 992 and x + y = 63 (adding and subtracting the identities), 1 for x − y = 1 via (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy, 1 for 32 and 31.
Review: Page 9 · Finding the numbers · Page 10 · Exercise 4
17.
A rectangle has perimeter 50 cm and area 150 sq cm. Each side is increased by 2 cm. Find the area of the new rectangle without finding the sides.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: 204 sq cm
Let the sides be x and y: 2(x + y) = 50 ⇒ x + y = 25, and xy = 150.
New area = (x + 2)(y + 2) = xy + 2x + 2y + 4 = xy + 2(x + y) + 4 = 150 + 50 + 4 = 204 sq cm. (The sides are 15 and 10; 17 × 12 = 204 ✔.)
Marks: 1 for x + y = 25 (from the perimeter), 1 for the identity (x + 2)(y + 2) = xy + 2(x + y) + 4, 1 for 204 sq cm.

SECTION D2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
The product of two natural numbers is 925 and their difference is 12. Let x be the larger number and y the smaller. (i) Find (x + 1)(y − 1). (ii) Find (x − 1)(y + 1). (iii) Find x + y, and hence the two numbers.
(5)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) 912 (ii) 936 (iii) x + y = 62; the numbers are 37 and 25
(i) (x + 1)(y − 1) = xy − x + y − 1 = xy − (x − y) − 1 = 925 − 12 − 1 = 912.
(ii) (x − 1)(y + 1) = xy + x − y − 1 = xy + (x − y) − 1 = 925 + 12 − 1 = 936.
(iii) (x + y)² = (x − y)² + 4xy = 144 + 3700 = 3844 = 62² ⇒ x + y = 62. Then x = ½(62 + 12) = 37, y = ½(62 − 12) = 25. Check: 37 × 25 = 925 ✔, 38 × 24 = 912 ✔, 36 × 26 = 936 ✔.
Marks: 1 for each identity with its value in (i) and (ii) (2 marks), 1 for (x + y)² = (x − y)² + 4xy = 3844, 1 for x + y = 62, 1 for 37 and 25.

19.
The figure shows part of the multiplication table (1 to 9). (i) Take the 2 × 2 square whose top-left number is mn (row m, column n). Write the other three numbers and prove that the two diagonal sums differ by 1. (ii) In a 3 × 3 square of the table, by how much do the diagonal sums of the four corner numbers differ? Justify. (iii) 12 × 63 = 21 × 36. Writing the numbers as 10m + n and 10p + q, explain why reversing the digits of both numbers keeps the product exactly when mp = nq, and give one more such pair.
(5)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) differ by 1 (ii) by 4 (iii) mp = nq; e.g. 13 × 62 = 31 × 26 = 806
(i) Row m holds m, 2m, 3m, …; so the square is mn, m(n + 1) = mn + m (to the right), (m + 1)n = mn + n (below) and (m + 1)(n + 1) = mn + m + n + 1. Diagonal sums: mn + (mn + m + n + 1) = 2mn + m + n + 1 and (mn + m) + (mn + n) = 2mn + m + n. Difference 1.
(ii) Corners mn, m(n + 2) = mn + 2m, (m + 2)n = mn + 2n, (m + 2)(n + 2) = mn + 2m + 2n + 4. Sums 2mn + 2m + 2n + 4 and 2mn + 2m + 2n: difference 4. (Example: 35 + 63 = 98, 45 + 49 = 94.)
(iii) (10m + n)(10p + q) = 100mp + 10mq + 10np + nq and (10n + m)(10q + p) = 100nq + 10np + 10mq + mp. The middle terms are the same, so the products are equal exactly when 100mp + nq = 100nq + mp, i.e. 99mp = 99nq, i.e. mp = nq. For 12 and 63: 1 × 6 = 2 × 3 ✔. Another pair: 13 and 62 (1 × 6 = 3 × 2): 13 × 62 = 806 = 31 × 26.
Marks: 1 for the four cells mn, mn + m, mn + n, mn + m + n + 1, 1 for the two sums and the difference 1, 1 for the 3 × 3 case (difference 4 with reason), 1 for the expansion showing 99(mp − nq), 1 for a correct new pair.
