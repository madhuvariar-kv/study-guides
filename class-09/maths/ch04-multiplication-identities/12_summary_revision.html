2. Number facts the identities prove

  • Odd × odd = odd. (2m + 1)(2n + 1) = 2(2mn + m + n) + 1.
  • Remainder 1 × remainder 1 (÷ 3) leaves remainder 1. (3m + 1)(3n + 1) = 3(3mn + m + n) + 1. Likewise remainder 1 × remainder 2 leaves 2: (3m + 1)(3n + 2) = 3(3mn + 2m + n) + 2.
  • Four consecutive numbers: middles' product − ends' product = 2. (x + 1)(x + 2) − x(x + 3) = 2. Six consecutive numbers: difference 6.
  • Calendar 2 × 2 square: diagonal products differ by 7. (x + 1)(x + 7) − x(x + 8) = 7. In a grid with 5 columns the difference is 5.
  • Multiplication-table squares: diagonal sums differ by 1 (2 × 2), 4 (3 × 3), 9 (4 × 4). Cells mn, mn + n, mn + m, mn + m + n + 1.
  • Digit reversal 12 × 63 = 21 × 36 happens exactly when mp = nq (tens × tens = units × units), because the two products differ by 99(mp − nq).

3. Recipe: finding two numbers from their neighbours' products

  1. Write the two facts as identities: e.g. (x + 1)(y + 1) = xy + (x + y) + 1 and (x − 1)(y − 1) = xy − (x + y) + 1.
  2. Add them: 2xy + 2 = (sum of the two products) ⇒ xy.
  3. Subtract them: 2(x + y) = (difference of the two products) ⇒ x + y. (For one-up-one-down products, subtracting gives x − y instead.)
  4. Use the Class 8 bridge: (x − y)² = (x + y)² − 4xy (or (x + y)² = (x − y)² + 4xy) to get the other one.
  5. Finish: x = ½(sum + difference), y = ½(sum − difference).

Textbook example: 525 and 437 ⇒ xy = 480, x + y = 44, (x − y)² = 1936 − 1920 = 16 ⇒ x − y = 4 ⇒ 24 and 20.

5. Common mistakes

⚠️ Watch out for these
  • Forgetting the cross terms. (20 + 6)(10 + 5) is NOT 20 × 10 + 6 × 5. There are four products, not two.
  • Sign slips in (x − y)(u − v). The last term is plus yv: (30 − 4)(20 − 3) = 600 − 90 − 80 + 12. "Minus times minus is plus" – the corner was removed twice, so it is added back.
  • Previous numbers use − sum + 1. (x − 1)(y − 1) = xy − (x + y) + 1, not xy − (x + y) − 1. 29 × 49 = 1500 − 80 + 1 = 1421.
  • Which number is bigger? In (x + 1)(y − 1) = xy − (x − y) − 1 the x is the number that went up. Decide first which one you call x.
  • Rounding the wrong way. 47 is 50 − 3, not 40 + 7, if you want small corrections; both are right, but one is far easier.
  • Mixed numbers. 6½ × 8½ is not 6 × 8 + ½ × ½. The middle terms ½ × 8 and 6 × ½ are needed: 48 + 4 + 3 + ¼ = 55¼.
  • "It worked for three examples" is not a proof. Use a general form (2n + 1, x, x + 1, …) and the identity to prove a pattern for all numbers.
