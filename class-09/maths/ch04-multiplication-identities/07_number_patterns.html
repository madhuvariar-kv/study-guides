Number Properties & Curiosities ക്രിയാവിശേഷങ്ങൾ · ക്രിയാകൗതുകങ്ങൾ

Checking a few examples can make you suspect that something is always true. Algebra lets you prove it. In this part of the chapter the identity (x + y)(u + v) = xu + xv + yu + yv proves facts about odd numbers and remainders, and then explains three number tricks: reversed digits, calendar squares and multiplication-table squares.

📖 What you already know
  • (x + y)(u + v) = xu + xv + yu + yv – every term of the first sum times every term of the second (page 5).
  • General forms: even numbers 2n, odd numbers 2n + 1, numbers leaving remainder 1 on division by 3 are 3n + 1, two-digit numbers are 10m + n (page 2).
  • An identity (സർവസമവാക്യം) is true for every value of the letters, so one algebraic calculation covers infinitely many examples.

1. From examples to proof

7 × 9 = 63, 11 × 13 = 143, 25 × 31 = 775 – odd times odd gave an odd number every time. But "every time I tried" is not "always". The textbook asks: how can we prove this for all odd numbers? The answer is to write a general odd number with a letter and multiply with the identity.

2. Odd × odd is always odd

🎬 Proof animation: (2m + 1)(2n + 1)
🎚️ Try any m and n

Whatever m and n you choose, the product is 2 × (something) + 1 – an odd number. The algebra guarantees it; the slider only illustrates it.

⭐ Key fact (textbook page 65)

(2m + 1)(2n + 1) = 4mn + 2m + 2n + 1 = 2(2mn + m + n) + 1. The product of two odd numbers is one more than an even number, so it is odd. രണ്ട് ഒറ്റസംഖ്യകളുടെ ഗുണനഫലവും ഒറ്റസംഖ്യ തന്നെയാണ്.

3. Remainders: numbers of the form 3n + 1

4. Four consecutive numbers

Take 1, 2, 3, 4: the ends multiply to 1 × 4 = 4, the middle pair to 2 × 3 = 6. Take 2, 3, 4, 5: 2 × 5 = 10 and 3 × 4 = 12. The difference is 2 both times. Always?

For any four consecutive natural numbers, (product of the middle two) − (product of the ends) = 2, because (x + 1)(x + 2) = x² + 3x + 2 and x(x + 3) = x² + 3x.

5. Curiosity 1: reverse the digits, keep the product

12 × 63 = 756 and 21 × 36 = 756. Also 32 × 46 = 1472 and 23 × 64 = 1472. Reverse both numbers and the product does not change! Usually that fails: 12 × 34 = 408 but 21 × 43 = 903. So when does it work?

🎬 Finding the rule with 10m + n
🎚️ Test any two two-digit numbers

The rule: reversing both numbers keeps the product exactly when m × p = n × q (tens digit × tens digit = units digit × units digit). Pairs where the second number is just the first reversed, like 12 × 21, are left out of the list because they are not interesting.

6. Curiosity 2: the calendar square

In any month, mark a 2 × 2 square of dates, for example 6, 7, 13, 14. Multiply diagonally: 14 × 6 = 84 and 13 × 7 = 91. Difference 7. Try 22, 23, 29, 30: 22 × 30 = 660, 23 × 29 = 667. Difference 7 again. Click any square below.

7. Curiosity 3: squares in the multiplication table

Here the trick uses sums, not products. In the table of 1 to 9, take any 2 × 2 square, say 12, 16, 15, 20. Add diagonally: 12 + 20 = 32 and 16 + 15 = 31. Difference 1. For 35, 40, 42, 48: 35 + 48 = 83 and 40 + 42 = 82. Click any square.

🖐️ Click a cell of the table (ഗുണിതപ്പട്ടിക)

Row n contains n, 2n, 3n, …, 9n. The square beginning at the m-th entry of row n holds mn, (m + 1)n, m(n + 1) and (m + 1)(n + 1).

🌍 Real life: tricks and checks

Magicians use these facts: ask a friend to circle any 2 × 2 square on a calendar and tell you only the top-left date; you instantly announce both diagonal products (x(x + 8) and that number plus 7). Bank clerks use a similar idea for a quick check: if a total is off by a multiple of 9, two digits were probably swapped – because 10m + n and 10n + m differ by 9(m − n). Algebra turns a curiosity into a tool.

🧭 Summary of Sections 2 & 3
  • To prove a fact about all numbers of a kind, write the general form (2n + 1, 3n + 1, 10m + n, x, x + 1, …) and multiply with the identity.
  • (2m + 1)(2n + 1) = 2(2mn + m + n) + 1: odd × odd is odd. (3m + 1)(3n + 1) = 3(3mn + m + n) + 1: remainder 1 stays remainder 1.
  • Four consecutive numbers: (x + 1)(x + 2) − x(x + 3) = 2.
  • Reversing both two-digit numbers keeps the product exactly when mp = nq (12 × 63 = 21 × 36).
  • Calendar 2 × 2 square: (x + 1)(x + 7) − x(x + 8) = 7. Multiplication-table 2 × 2 square: diagonal sums differ by 1.
