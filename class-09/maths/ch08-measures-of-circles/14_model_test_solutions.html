10.
A chord of length 8 cm is 3 cm from the centre of a circle. Find the circumference and the area of the circle.
(2)
🎬 Step-by-step
Answer: circumference 10π cm, area 25π sq cm

The perpendicular from the centre halves the chord: half-chord 4 cm. Radius² = 3² + 4² = 25, so r = 5 cm. Circumference 2π × 5 = 10π ≈ 31.42 cm; area π × 25 = 25π ≈ 78.54 sq cm.

Marks: 1 mark for r = 5; ½ mark each for 10π and 25π.

14.
A regular hexagon of side 3 cm is drawn inside a circle with its corners on the circle. Find the difference between the areas of the circle and the hexagon, correct to two decimal places.
(3)

15.
An isosceles triangle with base 6 cm and height 9 cm has its three corners on a circle. Find the circumference of the circle.
(3)
🎬 Step-by-step
Answer: 10π cm

The centre lies on the height. Let it be k cm above the base; then R = 9 − k and, from the right triangle with half-base 3, R² = 3² + k². So (9 − k)² = 9 + k² ⇒ 81 − 18k = 9 ⇒ k = 4, R = 5. Circumference 2π × 5 = 10π ≈ 31.42 cm.

Marks: 1 mark for the equation; 1 mark for R = 5; 1 mark for 10π.

16.

(3)

17.
Three circles of diameters 2 cm, 4 cm and 6 cm have their centres on a diameter of a circle of diameter 12 cm and together fill that diameter. Show that the sum of their circumferences equals the circumference of the big circle.
(3)
🎬 Step-by-step
Answer: 2π + 4π + 6π = 12π

Circumferences of the small circles: 2π, 4π, 6π; sum 12π. Big circle: π × 12 = 12π. In general π(d₁ + d₂ + d₃) = π × (sum of diameters) = π × D.

Marks: 1 mark each small circumference; 1 mark the sum and conclusion.

SECTION D2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
  1. Using regular polygons drawn inside a circle, explain why the area of a circle is ½ × circumference × radius.
  2. Hence show that the area is πr².
  3. Show that the ring between circles of radii 5 cm and 4 cm (same centre) has the same area as a circle of radius 3 cm.
  4. Explain why this happens.
(5)
🎬 Step-by-step
Answer: ½cr; πr²; 25π − 16π = 9π

(i) Join the centre to the corners of a regular polygon: n triangles of area ½sh, total ½ph. With more sides p → c and h → r, while the polygon’s area → the circle’s area, so a = ½cr. (ii) c = 2πr ⇒ a = ½ × 2πr × r = πr². (iii) Ring = π(5² − 4²) = 9π = π × 3². (iv) 5² − 4² = 3²: a right triangle with hypotenuse 5 and side 4 has third side 3, so the ring equals the circle on that side.

Marks: 2 marks (i), 1 mark (ii), 1 mark (iii), 1 mark (iv).

Review: Page 9 · Area of a circle

19.
A regular hexagon has sides 4 cm. Arcs of radius 2 cm are drawn inside it with each corner as centre, and the parts cut off are removed.
  1. Find the area of the hexagon.
  2. Find the total area of the six parts cut off.
  3. Find the area of the star-shaped part left.
  4. Find the total length of the six arcs.
(5)
🎬 Step-by-step
Answer: 24√3, 8π, 24√3 − 8π, 8π cm

(i) Six equilateral triangles of side 4: 6 × (√3/4) × 16 = 24√3 ≈ 41.57 sq cm. (ii) Each interior angle is 120° = ⅓ of a full turn, so each cut-off part is ⅓ of a circle of radius 2: (4π)/3; six give 8π ≈ 25.13 sq cm. (iii) Star = 24√3 − 8π ≈ 16.44 sq cm. (iv) Each arc is ⅓ × 2π × 2 = 4π/3; six give 8π ≈ 25.13 cm.

Marks: 1 + 1 + 1 marks for (i)–(iii); 2 marks for (iv) with the 120° reasoning.
