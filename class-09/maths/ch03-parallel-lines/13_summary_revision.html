1. The key results

Keys 1–3 · pp. 34–36 ഒരേ അകലം ഇടവിട്ടുള്ള സമാന്തരവരകൾ മറ്റ് ഏതു വരയേയും മുറിക്കുന്നത് ഒരേ നീളമുള്ള ഭാഗങ്ങളായാണ്. ഒരു വരയെ ഒരേ നീളമുള്ള ഭാഗങ്ങളായി മുറിക്കുന്ന സമാന്തരവരകൾ, ഏതു വരയേയും മുറിക്കുന്നത് ഒരേ നീളമുള്ള ഭാഗങ്ങളായിട്ടാണ്.

Equal division: equally spaced parallels cut every line into equal parts; parallels that cut one line equally are equally spaced, so they cut every line equally.

Key 4 · p. 43 മൂന്നോ അതിലധികമോ സമാന്തരവരകൾ, ഏതു രണ്ടു വരകളെയും മുറിക്കുന്നത് ഒരേ അംശബന്ധത്തിലാണ്.

Ratio division: three or more parallels cut any two lines in the same ratio: a/b = p/q. Proof by areas of triangles.

Keys 5–7 · pp. 46–49 ഏതു ത്രികോണത്തിലും ഒരു വശത്തിനു സമാന്തരമായി വരയ്ക്കുന്ന വര, മറ്റു രണ്ടു വശങ്ങളെ ഒരേ അംശബന്ധത്തിലാണ് മുറിക്കുന്നത് – അവയുടെ ഒരേ ഭാഗങ്ങളായിട്ടാണ്. മറിച്ച്, രണ്ടു വശങ്ങളെ ഒരേ അംശബന്ധത്തിൽ ഭാഗിക്കുന്ന വര മൂന്നാമത്തെ വശത്തിനു സമാന്തരമാണ്.

Dividing a triangle: a line parallel to one side cuts the other two in the same ratio (the same fractions of themselves); and a line that cuts two sides in the same ratio is parallel to the third.

Keys 8–10 · pp. 51–52 ഏതു ത്രികോണത്തിലും രണ്ടു വശങ്ങളുടെ മധ്യബിന്ദുക്കൾ യോജിപ്പിക്കുന്ന വര, മൂന്നാമത്തെ വശത്തിനു സമാന്തരവും അതിന്റെ പകുതിയുമാണ്.

Midpoint theorem: the line through the midpoint of one side parallel to another bisects the third; the line joining two midpoints is parallel to the third side and half of it. Four congruent triangles.

2. Construction recipes

  1. Divide AB into n equal parts: ray from A; n equal compass steps A₁ … Aₙ; join AₙB; parallels through the other marks (set squares). Each part = AB ÷ n.
  2. Divide AB in the ratio m : n: same ray with m + n equal steps; join the last mark to B; one parallel through mark m. Parts = AB·m/(m + n) and AB·n/(m + n).
  3. Triangle / square / hexagon of perimeter p: divide a line of length p into 3 / 4 / 6 equal parts; build the figure on one part (arcs for the triangle; perpendiculars for the square; a circle of radius = side for the hexagon).
  4. Rectangle with perimeter p and sides m : n: divide a line of length p/2 in m : n; the two parts are the sides.
  5. Triangle with perimeter p and sides a : b : c: divide a line of length p in a : b : c; swing arcs of the other two parts from the ends of one part.
  6. 7 cm into 3 equal parts (textbook): four parallels 2 cm apart; a 7 cm compass arc from a point on the first cuts the fourth; join. Each part 2⅓ cm.

3. Proof toolbox

Congruent right triangles

Between parallels, the little right triangles on a transversal have equal angles (corresponding) and either equal vertical sides (equal spacing) or equal hypotenuses (equal pieces). ASA / AAS makes them congruent – the engine of Keys 1–3.

Areas with the same height

Triangles with the same apex and bases on one line have areas in the ratio of their bases; triangles on the same base with apexes on a parallel have equal areas. Together they prove a/b = p/q (Key 4).

The ratio theorem twice

Most Exercise 3 proofs apply "a parallel cuts the other two sides in the same ratio" in two triangles and compare: AP/PB = AQ/QC and AQ/QC = AR/RD ⇒ AP/PB = AR/RD.

One pair equal and parallel

A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides equal and parallel is a parallelogram; its diagonals bisect each other. Used for the medians (PQDE) and for Varignon's parallelogram.

Euler line: circumcentre, centroid and orthocentre are collinear; the centroid divides the segment from the circumcentre to the orthocentre in 1 : 2. In an equilateral triangle all three coincide.

6. Common mistakes

⚠️ Watch out for these
  • Joining the wrong mark. It is the last mark on the ray that is joined to the far end B; the parallels go through the other marks.
  • Unequal compass steps. The steps on the ray can be any length, but they must all be the same – do not change the compass opening.
  • Wrong pair of sides. In "a parallel cuts the other two sides in the same ratio", compare top piece to bottom piece on each side: AP/PB = AQ/QC, not AP/AQ = PB/QC (that is also true, but mixing them up causes slips).
  • "Half" is only for midpoints. A parallel through a point at 1 : 2 gives a third, not a half. Only the midline is half the third side.
  • Median vs altitude. A median goes to the midpoint of the opposite side; an altitude is perpendicular to it. They coincide only in isosceles/equilateral cases.
  • 2 : 1 from the vertex. AG = 2 GD, so AG is two-thirds of the median, GD one-third – not the other way round.
  • Ratio vs lengths. Three parallels 2 cm and 3 cm apart cut a slanted line in the ratio 2 : 3 – the pieces are not 2 cm and 3 cm.
