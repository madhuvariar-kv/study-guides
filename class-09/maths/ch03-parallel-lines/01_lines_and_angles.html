Lines, Angles & Parallel Lines വരകളും കോണുകളും

Before we can talk about what parallel lines do, we need to agree on what they are – and on the angles they make with any line that crosses them. This page collects every fact about lines and angles that Chapter 3 uses. Nothing is assumed: play with each figure until it feels obvious.

📖 How to use pages 1–4

These four pages are the toolbox. Each idea here is used later in the chapter, and the chapter pages say "page 1 of this guide" when they lean on one. If a figure is already familiar, slide it once and move on; if not, read the step list under the animation slowly.

1. Points, lines, segments and rays

A point has a position but no size. A line (വര) goes on for ever in both directions. The part of a line between two points A and B is the line segment AB (we usually just say "the line AB" when the meaning is clear, as the textbook does). A ray starts at a point and goes on for ever one way.

👀 Four things that look alike but are different

In this chapter "a 7 cm line" always means a segment 7 cm long, and "a line through P" means a full line. Arrowheads on a drawing mean "this goes on".

2. Angles കോണുകൾ

Two rays from the same point make an angle. We measure it in degrees with a protractor: a full turn is 360°, a straight line is 180°, a quarter turn is a right angle, 90°.

🎚️ Turn the ray and read the protractor

Less than 90° is acute; exactly 90° is a right angle; between 90° and 180° is obtuse; exactly 180° is a straight angle – the two rays form one straight line.

3. Perpendicular lines ലംബം

Two lines that meet at a right angle are perpendicular. In figures a small square at the corner is the sign for 90°. The distance from a point to a line is measured along the perpendicular – because it is the shortest way to the line.

🖐️ Drag Q and R: the perpendicular is the shortest path to the line

F is the foot of the perpendicular from Q. Whatever point R you choose on the line, QR is never shorter than QF (QR is the hypotenuse of the right triangle QFR). So "the distance from Q to the line" means QF.

4. Parallel lines സമാന്തരവരകൾ

Two lines in a plane that never meet, however far they are extended, are parallel. Another way to say it: they are the same distance apart everywhere. Railway tracks, the two long edges of a ruler, the lines of a notebook. The sign ∥ means "is parallel to".

🎚️ Measure the gap at different places

Left: the perpendicular distance between the two red lines is the same at every position – they are parallel. Right: the blue lines get closer as you move right – they are not parallel, and if extended they would meet.

5. A line crossing two parallels: the angle pairs

A line that crosses two other lines is called a transversal (ഛേദകം). It makes eight angles, four at each crossing. When the two lines are parallel these angles come in equal pairs with special names. Click a name to see the pairs.

🖐️ Name the pairs

Click the same name again to see its next pair. Corresponding angles sit in the same position at the two crossings (an F shape); alternate angles are on opposite sides of the transversal between the lines (a Z shape); co-interior angles are on the same side between the lines (a C shape) and add up to 180°; vertically opposite angles are across a crossing from each other.

⭐ The rule the whole chapter leans on

If two lines are parallel, every pair of corresponding angles is equal (and so is every pair of alternate angles, and co-interior angles add to 180°). The converse is also true: if a transversal makes equal corresponding (or alternate) angles with two lines, the lines are parallel. Chapter 3 uses this in the form: "the slanted line makes the same angle with every one of the parallel lines".

🧭 Summary
  • A segment has two ends; a line goes on both ways. Angles are measured in degrees; 90° is a right angle, 180° a straight line.
  • Perpendicular (ലംബം) = at right angles. The distance from a point to a line is along the perpendicular, the shortest path.
  • Parallel lines (സമാന്തരവരകൾ) never meet and stay the same distance apart.
  • With a transversal: corresponding angles equal, alternate angles equal, co-interior angles add to 180° – if and only if the lines are parallel.
  • Angles of a triangle add to 180°; an exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
