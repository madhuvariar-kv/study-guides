Equal Division സമഭാഗം

You have drawn parallel lines before. Now we discover something they do for us: a set of equally spaced parallel lines cuts every line that crosses them into equal parts. From this one fact comes a neat way to divide any line – 7 cm into 3 equal parts, say – using only a ruler, a compass and set squares.

📖 What you already know (from pages 1–4 of this guide)
  • Parallel lines (സമാന്തരവരകൾ) stay the same distance apart; a line crossing them makes equal corresponding angles with each of them.
  • Two right triangles with one equal side and one equal acute angle are congruent (ASA / AAS, തുല്യത്രികോണങ്ങൾ).
  • A compass copies a length; set squares slide to draw parallels; arcs locate the third vertex of a triangle.

Parallel lines that cut some line into equal parts are equally spaced.

Put the two ideas together: ഒരു വരയെ ഒരേ നീളമുള്ള ഭാഗങ്ങളായി മുറിക്കുന്ന സമാന്തരവരകൾ, ഏതു വരയേയും മുറിക്കുന്നത് ഒരേ നീളമുള്ള ഭാഗങ്ങളായിട്ടാണ്.
Parallel lines that cut one line into equal parts cut every line into equal parts.

🌍 Real life

Ruled notebook paper, the rungs of a ladder, railway sleepers, the steps of a staircase – all are equally spaced parallels. A carpenter who wants five equal strips from a board lays a ruler diagonally across it so that a convenient length (say 25 cm) spans the board, marks every 5 cm, and draws parallels through the marks. No division sums, no awkward decimals.

4. Dividing a 7 cm line into 3 equal parts (page 36)

7 ÷ 3 = 2.333… cannot be measured with a ruler. But 6 cm can be divided into three 2 cm pieces easily. Here is the textbook's trick.

🎬 Animation: four parallels 2 cm apart and a 7 cm compass arc
💡 Why it works

The four perpendiculars are parallel and 2 cm apart, so they cut any line – including the slanted 7 cm line – into three equal parts (Key idea 1). Each part is 7 ÷ 3 = 2⅓ cm. The lines need not even be perpendicular to the base: any four parallels spaced 2 cm along the base line will do.

5. The slanted-ray method: 10 cm into 3 equal parts (pages 37–38)

A second method needs no perpendiculars at all. Start the helping line from one end of the line to be divided.

💡 Why it works

Imagine the three parallel lines extended, and a fourth one through A. These four parallels cut the slanted ray into equal 2 cm pieces, so (Key idea 3) they cut AB into equal pieces too: 3⅓ cm each.

7. An equilateral triangle with perimeter 10 cm (page 38)

Perimeter 10 cm means each side is 10 ÷ 3 = 3⅓ cm. Divide a 10 cm line into three equal parts, then use one part as the side.

🎬 Animation: from the divided line to the triangle

A square with perimeter 10 cm is done the same way: divide 10 cm into four equal parts (2½ cm each) and draw the square on one part. Exercise 1 asks you for a triangle, a square and a hexagon like this – see page 7 of this guide for the animated solutions.

8. Ruled paper does the work for you സമാന്തര സമഭാഗം

📏 A stick across the lines of a notebook (textbook page 39)

To cut a stick into 7 equal parts, lay it slanting so that one end is on line 1 and the other on line 8 (7 gaps). Mark where the lines cross it. The lines of the notebook are equally spaced parallels – Key idea 1 does the rest.

9. Check yourself

🧭 Summary of Section 1
  • Equally spaced parallel lines cut every line into equal parts (congruent little right triangles prove it).
  • Conversely, parallels that cut one line into equal parts are equally spaced, so they cut every line into equal parts.
  • To divide a line into n equal parts: draw a ray from one end, step off n equal lengths with the compass, join the last mark to the other end, and draw parallels through the other marks.
  • A triangle or square of given perimeter: divide the perimeter length into 3 or 4 equal parts first, then construct on one part.
