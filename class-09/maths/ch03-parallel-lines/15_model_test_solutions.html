Model Test Paper – Solved ഉത്തരങ്ങൾ

Every question of the model paper, with a full solution, an animated figure wherever a construction or a proof helps, and a marking hint so you can score your own paper honestly.

📖 How marks are usually given

1-mark questions: the correct answer only. 2- and 3-mark questions: about one mark for naming the right idea (which theorem, which construction step), the rest for the working and the final answer. 5-mark questions: marks for each part as shown. In a proof, every step must give its reason ("parallel lines cut any two lines in the same ratio", "midpoint theorem", …) – a correct figure with reasons earns marks even if the last line is missing. In a construction question a neat rough sketch with the steps in order earns the first mark.

SECTION A8 × 1 = 8 marks
1.
Equally spaced parallel lines cut any line into ______ parts.
(1)
Answer: equal
Key idea 1 of the chapter: the little right triangles between the parallels are congruent, so every piece of the crossing line is the same length.
Review: Page 5 · Equal division
2.
Three parallel lines cut one line into pieces of 3 cm and 5 cm. They cut a second line into pieces of 6 cm and ______ cm.
(1)
Answer: 10 cm
Three parallels cut any two lines in the same ratio: 3 : 5 = 6 : x, so x = 5 × 6 ÷ 3 = 10.
Review: Page 6 · Division in a ratio
3.
In triangle ABC, a line parallel to BC meets AB at P and AC at Q. If AP : PB = 2 : 3 and AC = 10 cm, then AQ = ______ cm.
(1)
Answer: 4 cm
The parallel cuts both sides into the same fractions: AP is 2/5 of AB, so AQ is 2/5 of AC = 2/5 × 10 = 4 cm (and QC = 6 cm; 4 : 6 = 2 : 3 ✔).
Review: Page 8 · Dividing a triangle
4.
The line joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is ______ to the third side and ______ of it.
(1)
Answer: parallel; half
The midpoint theorem (മധ്യഭാഗം): parallel to the third side and half its length.
Review: Page 8 · The midpoint theorem
5.
The point where the three medians of a triangle meet is called the (a) orthocentre (b) centroid (c) circumcentre (d) incentre
(1)
Answer: (b) centroid (മധ്യമകേന്ദ്രം)
Altitudes meet at the orthocentre, perpendicular bisectors at the circumcentre, angle bisectors at the incentre; medians meet at the centroid.
Review: Page 10 · Centres of a triangle
6.
The centroid divides each median, measured from the vertex, in the ratio (a) 1 : 1 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 1 : 2
(1)
Answer: (b) 2 : 1
The distance from the vertex to the centroid is twice the distance from the centroid to the midpoint of the opposite side.
Review: Page 10 · Medians and the centroid
7.
The circumcentre of a right triangle is (a) the right-angle vertex (b) the midpoint of the hypotenuse (c) the centroid (d) outside the triangle
(1)
Answer: (b) the midpoint of the hypotenuse
The midpoint of the hypotenuse is equidistant from all three vertices (Exercise 5, Q1), so it is the circumcentre; the circumradius is half the hypotenuse.

8.
The triangle formed by joining the midpoints of the sides of a triangle has perimeter 9 cm. The perimeter of the original triangle is ______ cm.
(1)
Answer: 18 cm
Each side of the midpoint triangle is half a side of the original, so the original perimeter is twice 9 cm.

SECTION B5 × 2 = 10 marks
9.
Describe, with a rough sketch, how to divide a 7 cm line into 3 equal parts using parallel lines. What is the length of each part?
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: each part is 7 ÷ 3 = 2⅓ cm (≈ 2.33 cm)

Draw AB = 7 cm. From A draw a ray at any convenient angle and step off three equal lengths on it with the compass (A₁, A₂, A₃). Join A₃ to B. Through A₂ and A₁ draw lines parallel to A₃B with set squares; they cut AB at Q and P. Four parallel lines (through A, A₁, A₂, A₃) cut the ray into equal parts, so they cut AB into equal parts too: AP = PQ = QB = 2⅓ cm.

Marks: 1 for the construction steps with a sketch (equal steps on the ray, join the last mark, parallels), 1 for the length 2⅓ cm.
Review: Page 5 · The slanted-ray method
10.
A 12 cm line is to be divided in the ratio 1 : 3. Describe the construction and write the lengths of the two parts.
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: 3 cm and 9 cm

Draw AB = 12 cm and a ray from A. Mark four equal steps on the ray: A₁ after one step and A₄ after four steps (1 + 3). Join A₄ to B and draw the line through A₁ parallel to A₄B; it meets AB at P. The parallels cut AB in the same ratio as the ray: AP : PB = 1 : 3, so AP = 12 × ¼ = 3 cm and PB = 12 × ¾ = 9 cm.

Marks: 1 for the construction (1 step and 3 steps on the ray, join, one parallel), 1 for 3 cm and 9 cm.
Review: Page 6 · Dividing a line in a ratio
11.
In triangle ABC, AB = 8 cm and AC = 6 cm. A line parallel to BC meets AB at P and AC at Q. If AP = 2 cm, find AQ and QC.
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: AQ = 1.5 cm, QC = 4.5 cm

A line parallel to one side cuts the other two sides into the same fractions. AP is 2/8 = ¼ of AB, so AQ is ¼ of AC: AQ = ¼ × 6 = 1.5 cm, and QC = 6 − 1.5 = 4.5 cm. Check with the ratio form: AP : PB = 2 : 6 = 1 : 3 and AQ : QC = 1.5 : 4.5 = 1 : 3 ✔.

Marks: 1 for using "same fraction / same ratio" (AP/AB = AQ/AC or AP/PB = AQ/QC), 1 for AQ = 1.5 and QC = 4.5.
Review: Page 8 · Dividing a triangle
12.
The diagonals of a quadrilateral are 10 cm and 6 cm long. The midpoints of its sides are joined in order. What are the lengths of the sides of the quadrilateral so formed, and what is its perimeter?
(2)
Answer: sides 5 cm, 3 cm, 5 cm, 3 cm; perimeter 16 cm

Each side of the midpoint quadrilateral joins the midpoints of two sides of a triangle whose third side is a diagonal, so it is half that diagonal (midpoint theorem). Two sides are ½ × 10 = 5 cm and two are ½ × 6 = 3 cm. Perimeter = 5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 cm.

Marks: 1 for "half the diagonal" with the midpoint theorem, 1 for the four sides and 16 cm.

13.
  1. In triangle ABC the median AD is 12 cm long and G is the centroid. Find AG and GD.
  2. The hypotenuse of a right triangle is 10 cm. How far is the circumcentre from each vertex?
(2)
Answer: (i) AG = 8 cm, GD = 4 cm (ii) 5 cm

(i) The centroid divides the median 2 : 1 from the vertex: AG = ⅔ × 12 = 8 cm, GD = ⅓ × 12 = 4 cm.
(ii) The circumcentre of a right triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, so its distance from each vertex is half the hypotenuse: 5 cm.

Marks: 1 for 8 cm and 4 cm, 1 for 5 cm (with the reason "midpoint of the hypotenuse").

SECTION C4 × 3 = 12 marks
14.
Prove that in any triangle, the line drawn through the midpoint of one side parallel to a second side bisects the third side.
(3)
🎬 Proof
Proof

Let D be the midpoint of AB in triangle ABC, and let the line through D parallel to BC meet AC at E. Draw the line through A parallel to BC as well. Now three parallel lines (through A, through D, and BC) cut the two lines AB and AC, so they cut them in the same ratio: AD/DB = AE/EC. Since AD = DB, the ratio is 1, so AE = EC: the line bisects AC. ∎

Marks: 1 for a correct labelled figure with the third parallel through A, 1 for "three parallels cut any two lines in the same ratio" (AD/DB = AE/EC), 1 for AD = DB ⇒ AE = EC.
Review: Page 8 · Sections 3–4
15.
In the trapezium ABCD, AB ∥ DC and the diagonals AC and BD meet at O.
  1. Prove that AO/OC = BO/OD.
  2. If AO = 6 cm, OC = 4 cm and BD = 15 cm, find BO and OD.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) proof below (ii) BO = 9 cm, OD = 6 cm

(i) Draw the line through O parallel to AB (it is also parallel to DC). Then AB, this line and DC are three parallel lines cutting the two lines AC and BD. Three parallels cut any two lines in the same ratio, so AO/OC = BO/OD. ∎
(ii) BO/OD = AO/OC = 6/4 = 3/2, so BO and OD are in the ratio 3 : 2 and add up to 15 cm: BO = 15 × 3/5 = 9 cm, OD = 15 × 2/5 = 6 cm.

Marks: 1 for drawing the parallel through O, 1 for the ratio statement with its reason, 1 for BO = 9 and OD = 6.
Review: Page 7 · Exercise 2, Q3
16.
A triangle has perimeter 12 cm and its sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
  1. Explain how to divide a 12 cm line in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 using parallel lines, and why the construction gives the correct ratio.
  2. Write the lengths of the three sides, draw a rough sketch of the triangle, and say what kind of triangle it is.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm – a right triangle

(i) Draw AB = 12 cm and a ray from A. Choose any unit and mark 3 units, then 4 units, then 5 units along the ray (points X, Y, Z). Join Z to B and draw lines through X and Y parallel to ZB; they cut AB at P and Q. The parallels through A, X, Y, Z are four parallel lines; parallel lines cut any two lines in the same ratio, so AP : PQ : QB = AX : XY : YZ = 3 : 4 : 5.
(ii) The sides are 12 × 3/12 = 3 cm, 12 × 4/12 = 4 cm and 12 × 5/12 = 5 cm. Taking PQ = 4 cm as the base, arcs of 3 cm from P and 5 cm from Q meet at the third vertex R. Since 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5², it is a right triangle (right angle between the 3 cm and 4 cm sides).

Marks: 1 for the construction steps, 1 for the reason (parallels cut any two lines in the same ratio), 1 for the sides 3, 4, 5 and "right triangle".
Review: Page 6 · Triangle with sides in a ratio · Page 7 · Exercise 2, Q2
17.
In a right triangle the perpendicular sides are 6 cm and 8 cm. The right-angle vertex and the midpoints of the three sides are joined to form a quadrilateral, as in the figure.
  1. Prove that this quadrilateral is a rectangle.
  2. Find its area and compare it with the area of the triangle.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) a rectangle (ii) area 12 sq cm – exactly half the triangle's 24 sq cm

(i) Let the right angle be at C, with CA = 6 cm and CB = 8 cm; M, N, L are the midpoints of CA, CB, AB. ML joins the midpoints of CA and AB, so ML ∥ CB and ML = ½CB = 4 cm. NL joins the midpoints of CB and AB, so NL ∥ CA and NL = ½CA = 3 cm. Both pairs of opposite sides of CMLN are parallel, so it is a parallelogram; its angle at C is 90°, so it is a rectangle.
(ii) Area of the rectangle = 3 × 4 = 12 sq cm. Area of the triangle = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 sq cm. The rectangle is half the triangle.

Marks: 1 for ML ∥ CB and NL ∥ CA by the midpoint theorem, 1 for "parallelogram with a right angle ⇒ rectangle", 1 for 12 sq cm = half of 24 sq cm.

SECTION D2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
Prove that the three medians of a triangle pass through one point, and that this point divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1 from the vertex.
(5)
🎬 Proof
Proof

In triangle ABC let D and E be the midpoints of BC and CA, and let the medians AD and BE meet at G. By the midpoint theorem, ED ∥ AB and ED = ½AB. Let P and Q be the midpoints of AG and BG; in triangle ABG the midpoint theorem gives PQ ∥ AB and PQ = ½AB. So PQ and ED are equal and parallel: PQDE is a parallelogram, and its diagonals PD and QE bisect each other: PG = GD and QG = GE. But AP = PG (P is the midpoint of AG), so AG = AP + PG = 2GD; likewise BG = 2GE. Thus the point where two medians meet divides each of them in the ratio 2 : 1 from the vertex.

Now take the medians BE and CF (F the midpoint of AB). By the same argument their meeting point divides BE in the ratio 2 : 1 from B – but there is only one such point on BE, namely G. So CF also passes through G: all three medians pass through G (the centroid, മധ്യമകേന്ദ്രം), and G divides each of them 2 : 1. ∎

Marks: 1 for the figure with two medians, D, E, G; 1 for ED ∥ AB, ED = ½AB; 1 for PQ ∥ AB, PQ = ½AB ⇒ PQDE is a parallelogram; 1 for PG = GD, QG = GE ⇒ AG = 2GD, BG = 2GE; 1 for the third median passing through the same point.
Review: Page 10 · Medians and the centroid
19.
  1. Prove that the quadrilateral formed by joining the midpoints of the sides of any quadrilateral, in order, is a parallelogram.
  2. If the diagonals of the original quadrilateral are equal and perpendicular to each other, what special kind of parallelogram is formed? Give reasons.
  3. The diagonals of a quadrilateral are perpendicular and each is 8 cm long. Find the area of the quadrilateral formed by joining the midpoints of its sides.
(5)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) proof below (ii) a square (iii) 16 sq cm

(i) In quadrilateral ABCD let P, Q, R, S be the midpoints of AB, BC, CD, DA. Draw the diagonal AC. In triangle ABC, PQ joins the midpoints of two sides, so PQ ∥ AC and PQ = ½AC. In triangle ADC, SR ∥ AC and SR = ½AC. So PQ and SR are equal and parallel, and PQRS is a parallelogram. ∎ (Using the diagonal BD in the same way, QR and SP are each ½BD.)
(ii) The sides of PQRS are ½AC and ½BD. If the diagonals are equal, all four sides are equal – a rhombus. If the diagonals are perpendicular, then PQ (∥ AC) is perpendicular to QR (∥ BD), so the angles are right angles – a rectangle. Equal AND perpendicular diagonals give both: a square.
(iii) Each side of the midpoint quadrilateral is ½ × 8 = 4 cm and the angles are right angles, so it is a square of side 4 cm: area = 4 × 4 = 16 sq cm.

Marks: 1 for PQ ∥ AC, PQ = ½AC (midpoint theorem in ΔABC); 1 for SR ∥ AC, SR = ½AC ⇒ parallelogram; 1 for "equal diagonals ⇒ rhombus" and "perpendicular ⇒ rectangle" ⇒ square; 1 for side 4 cm; 1 for area 16 sq cm.
