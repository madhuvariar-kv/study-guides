📖 What you already know (page 1 of this guide)
  • Angles are measured in degrees; the angles of a triangle add to 180°.
  • Between parallel lines, alternate and corresponding angles are equal.

1. Naming a triangle

👀 ΔABC: vertices, sides, angles

Vertices are capital letters A, B, C. The side opposite vertex A is BC (it does not touch A). ∠A means the angle at A. "ΔABC ≅ ΔPQR" is read "triangle ABC is congruent to triangle PQR", and the order of the letters tells you which vertex matches which.

2. Congruent triangles തുല്യത്രികോണങ്ങൾ

Two triangles are congruent when one fits exactly on the other – same side lengths and same angles. You do not need to check all six: any one of four short tests is enough.

TestWhat must matchRemember
SSSthree sidessides fix a triangle completely
SAStwo sides and the angle between themthe angle must be the included one
ASA (or AAS)two angles and one sidetwo angles fix the third, so any side works
RHSright angle, hypotenuse, one sideonly for right triangles

Two warnings: SSA (two sides and a non-included angle) is not a test, and AAA (three angles) gives the same shape but any size – that is Chapter 6, not congruence.

🎮 Which test applies? Pick a pair, then answer
Equal sides carry the same number of tick marks; equal angles carry the same arc. Choose the test.

4. Isosceles triangles സമപാർശ്വത്രികോണം

A triangle with two equal sides is isosceles. Its two base angles are equal, and the line from the apex to the midpoint of the base does three jobs at once.

🎬 Animation: the median from the apex is also the altitude and the perpendicular bisector
⭐ Isosceles facts

If AB = AC then ∠B = ∠C, and the line from A to the midpoint D of BC is perpendicular to BC and bisects ∠A. Conversely, if ∠B = ∠C then AB = AC. An equilateral triangle (all sides equal) is isosceles three times over – every median is also an altitude. Exercise 5 of the chapter uses exactly this.

5. Parallelograms സാമാന്തരികം

A quadrilateral whose opposite sides are parallel is a parallelogram. Everything else about it follows from alternate angles and congruent triangles.

🎬 Animation: opposite sides equal, diagonals bisect each other
🎬 Animation: one pair of opposite sides equal AND parallel ⇒ parallelogram
⭐ Parallelogram facts used in this chapter
  • Opposite sides are equal and parallel; opposite angles are equal.
  • The diagonals bisect each other (cut each other in half).
  • If a quadrilateral has one pair of opposite sides that are both equal and parallel, it is a parallelogram. (Used for the centroid proof and in Exercise 4.)

6. Rectangle, rhombus, square, trapezium

🖐️ Compare the special quadrilaterals

A rectangle (ചതുരം) is a parallelogram with a right angle – its diagonals are equal. A rhombus (സമഭുജസാമാന്തരികം) is a parallelogram with all sides equal – its diagonals are perpendicular. A square (സമചതുരം) is both. A trapezium (ലംബകം) has just one pair of parallel sides. Exercise 4 asks when a figure made from midpoints becomes each of these – the diagonals are the key.

7. The perpendicular bisector and the circumcentre പരിവൃത്തകേന്ദ്രം

The perpendicular bisector of a segment AB is the line through its midpoint at right angles to it. Every point on it is the same distance from A and from B (congruent triangles again: SAS). In a triangle the three perpendicular bisectors meet at one point, the circumcentre, which is equally far from all three vertices – so a circle centred there passes through A, B and C.

🖐️ Drag the vertices: three perpendicular bisectors, one circumcentre

Make the triangle right-angled and watch where the circumcentre goes – it lands on the hypotenuse. Make an angle obtuse and it moves outside. Section 5 of the chapter uses the circumcentre to prove that the altitudes of a triangle meet at one point.

8. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • Congruence tests: SSS, SAS, ASA/AAS, RHS. Two right triangles with an equal side and an equal acute angle are congruent.
  • Isosceles: equal sides ⇔ equal base angles; the median from the apex is also the altitude and the perpendicular bisector.
  • Parallelogram: opposite sides equal and parallel, diagonals bisect each other; one pair of opposite sides equal and parallel is enough to make a parallelogram.
  • Rectangle: equal diagonals. Rhombus: perpendicular diagonals. Square: both. Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides.
  • The perpendicular bisectors of the sides of a triangle meet at the circumcentre, equidistant from the vertices.
