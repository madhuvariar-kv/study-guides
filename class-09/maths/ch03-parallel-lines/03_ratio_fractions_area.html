📖 What you already know (pages 1–2 of this guide)
  • Parallel lines are the same distance apart everywhere.
  • Area of a rectangle = length × breadth; a diagonal cuts it into two equal triangles.

1. Ratio അംശബന്ധം

"The sides are in the ratio 5 : 3" means: for every 5 units of one there are 3 units of the other – the sizes can be anything, only the comparison is fixed. To split a length L in the ratio m : n, think of it as m + n equal shares: one piece gets m shares, the other gets n.

first piece = L × mm + n, second piece = L × nm + n

2. Mixed numbers and decimals

Dividing lengths produces fractions. 10 ÷ 3 = 3⅓ ("three and a third"): the whole-number part 3 and the leftover ⅓. As a decimal it is 3.333…, which never ends – that is exactly why the chapter divides lines with parallels instead of with a ruler.

3. "The same ratio" and "the same fraction"

Saying a : b = p : q (the same ratio) is the same as saying a/b = p/q. It is also the same as saying that a is the same fraction of (a + b) as p is of (p + q). The chapter switches between these forms freely, so let us see why they agree.

🎬 Animation: 2 : 3 and 4 : 6 are the same ratio
⭐ Three ways to say one thing

For positive lengths: a : b = p : q ⇔ a/b = p/q ⇔ a/(a + b) = p/(p + q). In words: "the pieces are in the same ratio" ⇔ "each piece is the same fraction of its whole". Section 3 of the chapter uses the fraction form: "the line cuts both sides into the same fractions – one third and two thirds".

4. Area of a triangle: ½ × base × height

A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and height, so its area is ½ × base × height. The height is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the base line – and it does not matter where along that line the vertex sits. Two consequences follow, and Section 2 of the chapter is built on both.

🖐️ Fact A: same base, apex on a parallel line ⇒ same area

Drag the apex C left and right. It stays on the green line, which is parallel to AB, so the height never changes – and neither does the area. Triangles with the same base and their apexes on a line parallel to the base have equal areas.

🖐️ Fact B: same apex, bases along one line ⇒ areas in the ratio of the bases

Drag D along the base line. Both triangles have the same height h, so area(ADC) : area(DBC) = ½·AD·h : ½·DB·h = AD : DB. The areas are in the same ratio as the bases.

💡 Why these two facts matter

In Section 2 the textbook proves that three parallel lines cut any two lines in the same ratio. It turns lengths into areas with Fact B (a/b = A/B), then shows the areas are equal across the two lines with Fact A (A = P, B = Q). Keep both facts in mind when you get there.

🧭 Summary
  • To split L in the ratio m : n: pieces L·m/(m+n) and L·n/(m+n). Write awkward answers as mixed numbers (3⅓, 9⅜).
  • a : b = p : q ⇔ a/b = p/q ⇔ a/(a+b) = p/(p+q) – same ratio, same fraction.
  • Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height. Same base, apex on a parallel ⇒ equal areas. Same apex, bases on one line ⇒ areas in the ratio of the bases.
  • Area of a parallelogram = base × height. Halving all sides of a triangle quarters its area.
