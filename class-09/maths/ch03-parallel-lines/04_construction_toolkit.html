Half of Chapter 3 is drawing: dividing lines, drawing parallels through given points, building triangles and squares with a compass. Every construction in the chapter is made from the ten small moves on this page. Watch each one, then do it on paper with a sharp pencil.

📖 Your tools
  • Ruler (scale): to draw straight lines and measure. Compass: to copy a length and to draw arcs. Set squares (two triangles, 45° and 30°–60°): to draw right angles and, by sliding, parallel lines. Protractor: angles.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Changing the compass opening half-way through a set of equal steps or arcs. Tighten the screw and hold the compass by its top.
  • Letting the set square drift while sliding: keep the ruler pressed firmly and move only the set square.
  • Measuring from the ruler's edge instead of from the 0 mark.
  • Arcs that are too short to meet – draw generous arcs, and let construction lines stay (lightly) on the page; examiners want to see them.

7. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • Compass: copy lengths, mark equal steps, draw arcs that meet to locate a point.
  • Perpendicular at or from a point: equal arcs on the line, then arcs that cross; or use a set square.
  • Parallel through a point: slide a set square along a ruler, or copy a corresponding angle.
  • Triangle from three sides, equilateral triangle, square, hexagon (side = radius) – all from arcs.
  • Perpendicular bisector: two pairs of arcs of the same radius from the two ends.
