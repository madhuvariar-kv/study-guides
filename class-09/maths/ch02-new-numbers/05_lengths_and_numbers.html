Lengths and Numbers നീളങ്ങളും സംഖ്യകളും

Every length you have measured so far was a number you could write: 3 m, 2½ m, 1.75 m – natural numbers or fractions. This section finds a length that is none of these: the diagonal of a square whose sides are 1 metre. It is a real length you can draw with a ruler, yet no fraction, however clever, is exactly right.

📖 What you already know (from pages 1–4 of this guide)
  • The area of a square is side × side; a square of area 4 sq m has side √4 = 2 m (വർഗം, വർഗമൂലം).
  • A mixed number like 1½ is a fraction (ഭിന്നസംഖ്യ), and (1½)² = 1 + 1 + ¼ = 2¼ – the square can be seen as four pieces.
  • Pythagoras: the square on the hypotenuse of a right triangle equals the two squares on the perpendicular sides together.
  • Any side of a triangle is shorter than the other two sides added together.

💡 So, with sides of 1 metre…

The small square has area 1 × 1 = 1 sq m, and the big square has area 2 sq m. What is the length of its side? It is the diagonal of the small square – longer than 1 m. It is also the third side of a triangle whose other two sides are 1 m and 1 m, so it is shorter than 1 + 1 = 2 m. Somewhere between 1 and 2, then. Which number between 1 and 2 has square 2?

2. Hunting for the fraction

Let us do what the textbook does: try fractions between 1 and 2 and square them. The picture shows why (1½)² = 1 + 1 + ¼: the square of side 1½ is a 1 × 1 square, two 1 × ½ strips and a ½ × ½ corner.

Whatever n and d you choose, the square comes out a little below 2 or a little above 2 – never exactly 2. Some fractions get very close (try 7/17: 1 7/17 squared is 1.99…), but close is not equal.

⭐ Key idea 1 (textbook page 20)

ഒരു ഭിന്നസംഖ്യയുടെയും വർഗം 2 അല്ല.

No fraction has square 2.

⭐ Key idea 2 (textbook page 20)

വശങ്ങളുടെയെല്ലാം നീളം 1 ആയ സമചതുരത്തിന്റെ വികർണ്ണത്തിന്റെ നീളം ഒരു ഭിന്നസംഖ്യയായി പറയാൻ കഴിയില്ല.

If the diagonal were some fraction of a metre, that fraction would have square 2 (because the side of a square of area 2 must square to 2). No fraction does. So the diagonal of a square with sides 1 cannot be written as a fraction – a length that is not a number we know!

🌍 Real life

Lay a measuring tape across a square tile from corner to corner. The tape's marks are all fractions of a metre (centimetres, millimetres), and the corner never lands exactly on a mark – it is always a little past 1.41 and a little short of 1.42. Carpenters, tilers and architects simply use a rounded value, 1.414 for example, and that is why the next section learns to find such decimals.

4. And a cube of volume 2

🧊 Which side gives a cube of volume 2 cubic cm?

A cube (ഘനം) of side s has volume (വ്യാപ്തം) s × s × s. 1.2³ = 1.728 is too small and 1.3³ = 2.197 is too big; and – you guessed it – no fraction has cube exactly 2. Lengths that are not fractions turn up everywhere once you start looking.

5. Where do numbers come from? സംഖ്യകൾ ഉണ്ടാകുന്നത്

📜 A little history (textbook side box, page 22)

Numbers were invented as people needed them. Counting sheep, days or people needs only the natural numbers 1, 2, 3, … Measuring – land in the Nile valley after the yearly floods (around 3000 BC), cloth, grain – needs parts of a unit, so fractions appeared. Now we have met lengths that even fractions cannot name, so new numbers are needed; that is this chapter. Much later, physics needed negative numbers and even "complex numbers". Every kind of number was born from a problem that the old numbers could not handle.

💡 What exactly is the problem?

Nothing is wrong with the length: you can draw the diagonal and cut a stick to match it. What is missing is a number for it. The next section fixes that by giving the length a name, √2, and then finding out how big √2 is.

6. Check yourself

🧭 Summary of Section 1
  • The square drawn on the diagonal of a square has twice the area (four half-squares).
  • For a square of side 1 m the diagonal square has area 2 sq m, so the diagonal is a number between 1 and 2 whose square is 2.
  • Trying fractions – 1½, 1¼, 1⅓, … – gives squares close to 2 but never 2. No fraction has square 2, so the diagonal is not a fraction.
  • The height of an equilateral triangle of side 2 (its square is 3) and the side of a cube of volume 2 are more such lengths.
  • New numbers are needed to name these lengths – that is the job of Section 2.
