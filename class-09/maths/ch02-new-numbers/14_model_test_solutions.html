Every question of the model paper, with a full solution, an animated figure wherever a picture or a chain of steps helps, and a marking hint so you can score your own paper honestly.

📖 How marks are usually given

1-mark questions: the correct answer only. 2- and 3-mark questions: about one mark for the right idea (which fact, which picture), the rest for the working and the final answer with the correct rounding. 5-mark questions: marks for each part as shown. In a proof, every step must give its reason ("odd × odd is odd", "lowest terms means no common factor"); a correct chain of reasons earns marks even if the last line is missing.

SECTION A8 × 1 = 8 marks
1.
The square drawn on the diagonal of a square of side 1 m has area ______ sq m.
(1)
Answer: 2 sq m
The diagonal cuts the unit square into two half-squares, and the big square is made of four such halves: 4 × ½ = 2.

2.
√(2¼) = ______
(1)
Answer: 1½
(1½)² = 1 + 1 + ¼ = 2¼, so the side of a square of area 2¼ is 1½ – a fraction, because 2¼ = 9/4 and both 9 and 4 are perfect squares.

√10 lies between the natural numbers ______ and ______.
(1)
Answer: 3 and 4
3² = 9 < 10 < 16 = 4², so the side of a square of area 10 is between 3 and 4 (√10 = 3.162…).

4.
A right triangle has perpendicular sides 1 m and √2 m. Its hypotenuse is (a) 2 m (b) √3 m (c) 3 m (d) (√2 + 1) m
(1)
Answer: (b) √3 m
hypotenuse² = 1² + (√2)² = 1 + 2 = 3, so the hypotenuse is √3 m. (Not √2 + 1: the hypotenuse is always less than the two sides added together.)

Which of these is NOT a fraction? (a) 1.5 (b) √9 (c) √5 (d) 2¼
(1)
Answer: (c) √5
1.5 = 1½ and 2¼ are fractions; √9 = 3 is a natural number. No fraction squares to 5 (2.2² = 4.84, 2.3² = 5.29, and the appendix argument works for 5 as it does for 2).

1.41² = 1.9881 and 1.42² = 2.0164. So √2 lies between ______ and ______.
(1)
Answer: 1.41 and 1.42
1.9881 < 2 < 2.0164, so the number whose square is 2 lies between 1.41 and 1.42. To 2 decimal places √2 ≈ 1.41.

The perimeter of a right triangle with perpendicular sides 1 m and 1 m is (a) (2 + √2) m (b) 3 m (c) 2√2 m (d) (1 + √2) m
(1)

The hypotenuse is √(1 + 1) = √2 m; perimeter = 1 + 1 + √2 = 2 + √2 = 3.4142… m.

For positive numbers x and y, √x + √y ______ √(x + y). (Write >, < or =.)
(1)
Answer: >
√x, √y and √(x + y) are the sides of a right triangle (squares of areas x and y corner to corner), and two sides of a triangle together exceed the third. Check: √9 + √16 = 7 > 5 = √25.

SECTION B5 × 2 = 10 marks
9.
Describe how to draw a square of area 2 sq cm and a square of area 3 sq cm using right triangles. Write the lengths of their sides.
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: sides √2 cm ≈ 1.41 cm and √3 cm ≈ 1.73 cm

Area 2: draw a right triangle with perpendicular sides 1 cm and 1 cm. By Pythagoras the square on its hypotenuse has area 1 + 1 = 2 sq cm; draw that square. Its side is the hypotenuse, √2 cm.

Area 3: draw a right triangle with perpendicular sides 1 cm and √2 cm (use the hypotenuse just drawn as one side). The square on its hypotenuse has area 1 + 2 = 3 sq cm; its side is √3 cm. (Or: a right triangle with hypotenuse 2 cm and one side 1 cm – the square on the other side has area 4 − 1 = 3.)

Marks: 1 for each square – the right triangle used (with Pythagoras as the reason) and the side length written as √2 or √3.

10.
Show that (1⅓)² < 2 < (1½)². What does this tell you about √2?
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: 1 7/9 < 2 < 2¼, so 1⅓ < √2 < 1½

(1⅓)² = (4/3)² = 16/9 = 1 7/9, which is less than 2 (by 2/9). (1½)² = (3/2)² = 9/4 = 2¼, which is more than 2 (by ¼). Picture: (1⅓)² = 1 + ⅓ + ⅓ + 1/9 and (1½)² = 1 + ½ + ½ + ¼.

√2 is the number whose square is 2. Since squares grow with the number, √2 lies between 1⅓ and 1½ – so √2 is between 1.33… and 1.5, and neither of these fractions is √2.

Marks: 1 for the two squares correctly computed (as improper fractions, mixed numbers or pictures), 1 for the conclusion 1⅓ < √2 < 1½.

11.
A right triangle has hypotenuse 3 cm and one perpendicular side 2 cm. Find the third side, and the perimeter correct to 2 decimal places.
(2)
🎬 Solution
Answer: third side √5 cm ≈ 2.24 cm; perimeter 5 + √5 ≈ 7.24 cm

By Pythagoras, 3² = 2² + (third side)², so (third side)² = 9 − 4 = 5: the third side is √5 cm. 2.2² = 4.84 and 2.3² = 5.29, and 2.23² = 4.9729, 2.24² = 5.0176, so √5 ≈ 2.236 ≈ 2.24.

Perimeter = 3 + 2 + √5 = 5 + √5 = 5 + 2.236… = 7.236… ≈ 7.24 cm.

Marks: 1 for the third side √5 (with 9 − 4 = 5), 1 for the perimeter 5 + √5 ≈ 7.24 cm.

12.
Using √2 ≈ 1.414 and √3 ≈ 1.732, find correct to 2 decimal places:
  1. 1 + √2 + √3
  2. (2 + √3) − (1 + √2)
(2)
Answer: (i) 4.15 (ii) 1.32

(i) 1 + 1.414 + 1.732 = 4.146 → 4.15 (the third decimal is 6, so round up).

(ii) (2 + √3) − (1 + √2) = 2 − 1 + √3 − √2 = 1 + √3 − √2 = 1 + 1.732 − 1.414 = 1.318 → 1.32. (Keeping the roots until the end avoids rounding twice.)

Marks: 1 for each part; the rounding to 2 places must be correct (4.146 → 4.15, 1.318 → 1.32).
Review: Page 8 · Adding and subtracting
13.
Write the decimal forms of ⅓, ⅙ and √2. Which of them have a repeating block of digits?
(2)
Answer: ⅓ = 0.333…, ⅙ = 0.1666…, √2 = 1.41421…; ⅓ and ⅙ repeat, √2 does not

⅓ = 0.333… (the digit 3 repeats for ever); ⅙ = 0.1666… (6 repeats). √2 = 1.41421356… – the digits go on without end, but no block of digits ever repeats. That is the difference between a fraction and a new number like √2: every fraction's decimal terminates or repeats.

Marks: 1 for the three decimal forms, 1 for saying that ⅓ and ⅙ repeat while √2 has no repeating block.

14.
1.7² = 2.89, 1.8² = 3.24, 1.73² = 2.9929 and 1.74² = 3.0276.
  1. Between which tenths does √3 lie?
  2. Between which hundredths?
  3. Write √3 correct to 2 decimal places.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) 1.7 and 1.8 (ii) 1.73 and 1.74 (iii) 1.73

(i) 2.89 < 3 < 3.24, i.e. 1.7² < 3 < 1.8², so 1.7 < √3 < 1.8. (ii) 2.9929 < 3 < 3.0276, i.e. 1.73² < 3 < 1.74², so 1.73 < √3 < 1.74. (iii) √3 is between 1.73 and 1.74; since 1.735² = 3.010225 > 3, √3 is below 1.735, so correct to 2 decimal places √3 ≈ 1.73. (In fact √3 = 1.73205…)

Marks: 1 for each part; in (i) and (ii) the comparison of the squares with 3 must be written.

15.
A right triangle has perpendicular sides 1 m and 2 m.
  1. Find its hypotenuse.
  2. Find its perimeter correct to a centimetre.
  3. A second right triangle has perpendicular sides √5 m and 2 m. Find its hypotenuse, and how much longer its perimeter is than that of the first triangle.
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) √5 m (ii) 5.24 m (iii) hypotenuse 3 m; longer by exactly 2 m

(i) hypotenuse² = 1² + 2² = 5, so the hypotenuse is √5 m (≈ 2.236 m).

(ii) perimeter = 1 + 2 + √5 = 3 + √5 = 3 + 2.236… = 5.236… → 5.24 m correct to a centimetre.

(iii) hypotenuse² = (√5)² + 2² = 5 + 4 = 9, so the hypotenuse is √9 = 3 m – a natural number. Its perimeter is √5 + 2 + 3 = 5 + √5. Difference: (5 + √5) − (3 + √5) = 2 m exactly – no decimals needed, because √5 cancels.

Marks: 1 for √5, 1 for 3 + √5 ≈ 5.24 m, 1 for the hypotenuse 3 m and the difference 2 m.

16.
An equilateral triangle of side 4 m is cut along its height into two halves.
  1. Find the height.
  2. Find the perimeter of one half, correct to 2 decimal places.
  3. How much less is this than the perimeter of the whole triangle?
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) √12 m ≈ 3.46 m (ii) 6 + √12 ≈ 9.46 m (iii) 6 − √12 ≈ 2.54 m

(i) The height meets the base at its midpoint, so each half is a right triangle with hypotenuse 4 m and base 2 m. height² = 4² − 2² = 16 − 4 = 12, height = √12 m. 3.4² = 11.56, 3.5² = 12.25; 3.46² = 11.9716, 3.47² = 12.0409, so √12 ≈ 3.464 ≈ 3.46 m.

(ii) perimeter of one half = 2 + 4 + √12 = 6 + √12 = 6 + 3.464 = 9.464 → 9.46 m.

(iii) whole triangle: 3 × 4 = 12 m. Less by 12 − (6 + √12) = 6 − √12 = 6 − 3.464 = 2.536 → ≈ 2.54 m.

Marks: 1 for height² = 16 − 4 = 12 and √12 ≈ 3.46; 1 for 6 + √12 ≈ 9.46 m; 1 for 6 − √12 ≈ 2.54 m.

17.
Right triangles are drawn one after another as in the figure: each has one perpendicular side 1 m, the hypotenuse of each becomes a perpendicular side of the next, and the first has both perpendicular sides 1 m.
  1. Write the sides of the 5th triangle.
  2. How much longer is the perimeter of the 5th triangle than that of the 4th?
  3. After the 3rd triangle, which is the next triangle whose hypotenuse is a natural number?
(3)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) 1, √5, √6 m (ii) √6 − 2 ≈ 0.45 m (iii) the 8th triangle (hypotenuse √9 = 3 m)

The hypotenuses are √2, √3, √4, √5, …: the nth triangle has sides 1, √n and √(n + 1). (i) 5th triangle: 1 m, √5 m, √6 m.

(ii) 4th triangle: 1, √4 = 2, √5. Perimeters: 5th − 4th = (1 + √5 + √6) − (1 + 2 + √5) = √6 − 2 = 2.449 − 2 = 0.449 m ≈ 0.45 m.

(iii) The hypotenuse of the nth triangle is √(n + 1); it is a natural number when n + 1 is a perfect square: n + 1 = 4 (3rd triangle), then n + 1 = 9, i.e. the 8th triangle, with hypotenuse 3 m (then the 15th, with hypotenuse 4 m).

Marks: 1 for the sides 1, √5, √6 (with the pattern 1, √n, √(n + 1)); 1 for √6 − 2 ≈ 0.45 m; 1 for the 8th triangle with the reason n + 1 = 9.

SECTION D2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
Prove that the square of no fraction is 2.
(5)
🎬 Proof, one step at a time
Proof

Suppose some fraction has square 2, and write it in lowest terms as x/y, so that x and y have no common factor. Then (x/y)² = 2 gives x²/y² = 2, so x² = 2y². Thus x² is even. The square of an odd number is odd (odd × odd = odd), so x cannot be odd: x is even.

Since x and y have no common factor and x is even, y cannot be even too (2 would be a common factor): y is odd.

Now x is even, so x = 2z for some natural number z. Substituting: (2z)² = 2y², i.e. 4z² = 2y², so y² = 2z². Thus y² is even, and as before y is even.

So y is odd and y is even – impossible. Our supposition must be wrong: no fraction has square 2. ∎

Marks: 1 for starting with a fraction x/y in lowest terms and x² = 2y²; 1 for "x² even ⇒ x even" with the reason (odd² is odd); 1 for y odd (no common factor); 1 for x = 2z ⇒ y² = 2z² ⇒ y even; 1 for stating the contradiction and the conclusion.

19.
Two squares of areas x and y are placed corner to corner as in the figure, and the triangle between them is completed.
  1. Explain why the square drawn on the third side of this triangle has area x + y.
  2. Write the lengths of the three sides of the triangle.
  3. Why is √x + √y > √(x + y)?
  4. Check this with x = 9 and y = 16.
  5. For x = 3 and y = 6, compute √x + √y − √(x + y) correct to 3 decimal places.
(5)
🎬 Solution
Answer: (i) Pythagoras (ii) √x, √y, √(x + y) (iii) triangle inequality (iv) 7 > 5 (v) 1.181

(i) The two squares meet at a right angle, so the triangle between them is a right triangle whose perpendicular sides are the sides of the squares. By Pythagoras, the square on the hypotenuse equals the two squares on the perpendicular sides together: area x + y.

(ii) The sides of the squares are √x and √y (a square of area x has side √x), and the hypotenuse is the side of the square of area x + y: √x, √y, √(x + y).

(iii) In any triangle, two sides together are longer than the third side. Here that gives √x + √y > √(x + y).

(iv) x = 9, y = 16: √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7 and √25 = 5; indeed 7 > 5 (a 3-4-5 triangle).

(v) x = 3, y = 6: √3 + √6 − √9 = 1.732 + 2.449 − 3 = 1.181.

Marks: 1 for each part: (i) Pythagoras with the right angle explained; (ii) the three sides; (iii) "two sides of a triangle exceed the third"; (iv) 7 > 5; (v) 1.181.
