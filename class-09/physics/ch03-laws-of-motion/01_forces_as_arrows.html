Forces, Units and Arrows ബലം

This whole chapter is about forces and what they do to moving things. Before Newton's laws, we need to be completely sure what a force is, what it can do, how we measure it and how we draw it. This page starts from zero: pushes and pulls, the effects of a force, the common kinds of forces, the unit newton, drawing forces as arrows, using + and − for opposite directions, and adding forces that act along one line.

📖 What you need
  • Nothing from earlier classes is assumed – every idea is explained here.
  • Only simple addition and subtraction, including negative numbers (explained below).

1. What is a force?

A force (ബലം) is a push or a pull on an object. You push a stalled autorickshaw to start it. You pull a bucket of water up from the well. The rowers in a vallam kali snake boat push the water back with their oars. Every force has two things: how strong it is (its size) and which way it acts (its direction).

A force always acts on an object and is given by something else: your hand, a rope, the Earth, a magnet, the ground. We can't see a force itself, only what it does.

2. What can a force do?

Watch the five effects. In every one of them, something about the object changes. That is the big idea of this chapter: forces change motion (and sometimes shape).

🎬 Animation: five things a force can do
⭐ Key idea

A force can (1) make a resting object move, (2) change the speed of a moving object (faster or slower), (3) stop a moving object, (4) change the direction of motion and (5) change the shape of an object. Effects 1–4 are all changes in motion, and they are what Newton's laws are about.

🌍 Real life in Kerala

On the school ground, one football shows all five. A kick starts it, a second kick makes it go faster, the goalkeeper's hands stop it, a header turns it, and a hard kick even squashes it for a moment.

3. Kinds of forces

Forces come from different sources. Four kinds appear again and again in this chapter:

💪 Muscular force

The force of our muscles: pushing a cart, pulling a rope in a tug of war, rowing a boat, kicking a ball.

🛑 Friction (ഘർഷണം)

The force between two surfaces that opposes motion. It slowly stops a ball rolling on the ground (page 4 explains more).

🌍 Gravity

The Earth's pull on everything, always downwards. It brings a mango or a coconut down from the tree.

🧲 Magnetic force

A magnet pulls iron pins and nails, even from a small distance, without touching them.

Muscular force and friction need touching: they are contact forces. Gravity and magnetic force act across a gap: they are non-contact forces.

4. The unit of force: the newton

The SI unit of force is the newton, written N, after Isaac Newton. On page 9 you will see its exact meaning: 1 N is the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s². For now, get a feel for it:

  • The Earth pulls a 100 g packet (a small biscuit packet or a bar of soap) down with about 1 N (0.98 N, to be exact).
  • Holding a 1 kg packet of rice in your hand, you feel a pull of about 10 N (9.8 N).
  • A strong adult can push with a few hundred newtons; a tug-of-war team pulls with well over a thousand.
🧪 Feel a newton: the spring balance

A spring balance measures force: the bigger the pull, the more the spring stretches. The pull of the Earth on a mass is mass (in kg) × 9.8, in newtons. Try 100 g (about 1 N), 500 g and 1000 g.

5. Forces are drawn as arrows

“Push the box with 200 N” is not enough information: push it which way? Because a force has both size and direction, we draw it as an arrow:

  • the arrowhead shows the direction of the force;
  • the length of the arrow shows the size of the force, drawn to a scale, for example 1 cm for 50 N;
  • the tail starts at the object the force acts on.
🧪 Draw a force arrow to scale

Positive values point to the right, negative values to the left. With 1 cm = 50 N, a 250 N force is a 5 cm arrow; with 1 cm = 100 N the same force is only 2.5 cm long. The force is the same, only the drawing scale changed.

6. + and − for opposite directions

For forces along one straight line (left–right, or up–down) there are only two possible directions. So we choose one direction as positive (+) and the opposite direction is then negative (−). Always say which one you chose. For example, taking east (right) as +:

  • 200 N towards the east is written +200 N (or just 200 N);
  • 150 N towards the west is written −150 N.

The minus sign does not mean “less than nothing”. It only means “the opposite direction”. A force of −150 N is just as strong as +150 N; it pulls the other way. Your textbook uses this idea all the time, for example in Table 3.1 and for braking forces.

⚠️ Careful

Once you have chosen a positive direction, keep it for the whole problem. And when the answer comes out negative, say what it means in words: “the force is towards the west” or “the force is opposite to the motion”.

7. Adding forces along a line

When two or more forces act on one object at the same time along a line, their total effect is one single force. To find it, add the forces with their signs:

total force = F₁ + F₂ + …   (each with its + or − sign)
  • Same direction: 120 N + 90 N = 210 N, the forces help each other.
  • Opposite directions: 250 N + (−400 N) = −150 N, i.e. 150 N in the negative direction. The bigger force wins by the difference.
  • Equal and opposite: 300 N + (−300 N) = 0. The forces cancel; the total is zero.

On a drawing, add arrows tip to tail: start the second arrow where the first one ends. The total runs from the tail of the first to the tip of the last. Page 5 gives this total its textbook name, the resultant force (സഫലബലം).

🧪 Add two forces on a crate (tip to tail)

Right is +. Red: F₁ starts at 0. Blue: F₂ starts where F₁ ends. Purple: the total, from 0 to the tip of F₂. Make F₁ = 200 and F₂ = −200: the total is 0, and the crate stays still.

Worked example 1 · Pushing a stalled autorickshaw

Q. Two friends push a stalled autorickshaw forward. One pushes with 120 N and the other with 90 N, both in the same direction. What is the total force on the autorickshaw from the two friends?

Take forward as +. Both forces are forward, so both are positive. Add them.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: F = 120 N + 90 N = 210 N forward.

Worked example 2 · Pulled both ways

Q. A cart is pulled towards the east with a force of 250 N and towards the west with a force of 400 N at the same time. Find the total force on the cart and its direction.

Take east as +. Then the westward pull is −400 N. Add with signs, and explain the sign of the answer in words.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: F = 250 + (−400) = −150 N, i.e. 150 N towards the west. The minus sign means the total force is towards the west.
