📐 How the figures are drawn

Air particles are small blue dots; where they crowd together is a compression (C, മർദം കൂടിയ മേഖല), where they spread out a rarefaction (R, മർദം കുറഞ്ഞ മേഖല). Each particle only moves to and fro about its own place – the disturbance travels. Blue arcs are sound spreading out, yellow lines are light. Every number in the animations is worked out with distance = speed × time, the speeds of Table 8.3, 350 m/s for air in the textbook’s examples, and 3 × 108 m/s for light.

SECTION AAnswer all · 8 × 1 = 8 marks
1.
Sound is produced by the of objects.
(1 mark)

💡 Touch a ringing bell or a struck tuning fork – what do you feel?

✅ Answer: vibration (കമ്പനം)

Every source of sound (ശബ്ദസ്രോതസ്സ്) – a bell, a tuning fork, a veena string, our vocal cords – makes sound only while it vibrates, moving rapidly to and fro. Hold the prongs of a ringing tuning fork tightly: the vibration stops and so does the sound.

Marking: 1 mark.

2.
Sound cannot travel through
(a) steel(b) water(c) air(d) vacuum
(1 mark)

💡 Which one has no particles at all?

✅ Answer: (d) vacuum (ശൂന്യത)

Sound needs a medium (മാധ്യമം) – a solid, a liquid or a gas – to travel. A vacuum has no particles to pass the vibrations on, so sound cannot travel through it. (Steel, water and air all carry sound.)

Marking: 1 mark for (d).

✅ Answer: (d) aluminium

💡 What is inside a flute that can vibrate?

5.
When we speak, the in the larynx (voice box) vibrate.
(1 mark)

💡 Touch your throat while you speak.

✅ Answer: vocal cords (സ്വനതന്തുക്കൾ)

Our voice is produced when the vocal cords in the larynx (സ്വനപേടകം) at the top of the windpipe vibrate. Touch your throat while speaking loudly – you can feel it.

6.
The thin membrane in our ear that vibrates when sound reaches it is the
(a) outer ear (pinna)(b) ear canal(c) eardrum(d) inner ear
(1 mark)

💡 Which part works like the balloon diaphragm in the bottle activity?

✅ Answer: (c) eardrum (കർണ്ണപുടം)

The pressure variations of sound travel down the ear canal (കർണ്ണനാളം) and make the eardrum (tympanum) vibrate, like the balloon diaphragm with the mustard seeds. It passes the vibrations on to the inner ear.

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

9.
At night Arun sees the flash of a firecracker and hears its sound 4 s later. How far away did it burst? (Take the speed of sound in air as 350 m/s.)
(2 marks)

💡 distance = speed × time; the light arrives almost instantly.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: 1400 m (1.4 km)

Given: speed of sound v = 350 m/s; time t = 4 s

Formula: distance = speed × time

Substitution: 350 m/s × 4 s

Answer: distance = 1400 m = 1.4 km

The light of the flash (3 × 108 m/s) reaches Arun almost at once, so the 4 s is the time the sound took.

Marking: Formula ½, substitution ½, answer with unit 1.

10.
Astronauts standing on the Moon cannot hear each other speak directly, even when they stand close together. (a) Why? (b) How do they talk to each other?
(2 marks)

💡 What is there between two astronauts on the Moon?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) no air – no medium on the Moon   (b) by radio

(a) The Moon has no atmosphere – no air. Sound needs a medium to travel, so the vibrations of one astronaut’s voice cannot reach the other (it is a vacuum).

(b) They use radio sets in their helmets. Radio waves are electromagnetic waves, like light, and can travel through vacuum.

Marking: (a) 1 mark (no air / vacuum, sound needs a medium); (b) 1 mark.

11.
Music and noise are both sounds. (a) What is the difference between them? (b) Give one example of each.
(2 marks)

💡 Think of the shape of the vibration – orderly or jumbled?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) regular vs irregular vibrations   (b) e.g. veena / traffic horns

(a) Music (സംഗീതം) is produced by regular (orderly) vibrations and is pleasant to hear. Noise (ഒച്ച) is produced by irregular vibrations and is unpleasant.

(b) Music: a veena, a flute, a song in the music class. Noise: horns at a traffic junction, a construction drill, rubbing a coconut shell on a stone.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) ½ + ½.

12.
Lightning and thunder happen at the same moment, but we usually see the lightning first and hear the thunder later. Why?
(2 marks)

💡 Compare 3 × 10⁸ m/s with 350 m/s.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: light is far faster than sound

Light travels at about 3 × 108 m/s, so the flash reaches us almost instantly. Sound travels in air at only about 350 m/s, so the thunder takes several seconds to cover the same distance (about 3 s for every kilometre).

Marking: 1 mark for comparing the two speeds, 1 mark for the conclusion.

13.
Write two difficulties faced by people with hearing loss, and two ways in which we can support them.
(2 marks)

💡 Think of the classroom, the road and the playground.

✅ Answer: any two difficulties + any two ways

Difficulties: problems in communicating with others; risk of accidents (they may not hear horns or alarms); difficulty in learning in class; loneliness.

Support: treat them with sympathy and kindness; include them in every activity; speak facing them, clearly and a little slowly; learn some sign language; use written notes.

14.
An electric bell is ringing inside a glass bell jar placed on a vacuum pump (figure). The pump slowly removes the air from the jar. (a) What happens to the sound we hear? (b) When almost all the air has been removed, can we still see the hammer striking the gong? Can we hear it? (c) What do you conclude?
(3 marks)

💡 What carries the sound from the bell to the glass and to us?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) it becomes fainter   (b) seen, but almost not heard   (c) sound needs a medium

(a) The sound becomes fainter and fainter as the air is removed.

(b) We can still see the hammer striking (light travels through vacuum), but we can hardly hear anything. Let the air back in and the sound returns.

(c) Sound needs a medium (മാധ്യമം) to travel; it cannot travel through vacuum (ശൂന്യത).

Marking: 1 mark for each part.

16.
A worker strikes one end of a long, empty steel pipeline with a hammer. Meera, 2972 m away at the other end, has her ear against the pipe. (Speed of sound: steel 5944 m/s, air 347 m/s – Table 8.3.) (a) How long does the sound take to reach her through the steel? (b) How long does it take through the air? (c) How many sounds does she hear? Which one comes first?
(3 marks)

💡 time = distance ÷ speed, once for each medium.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 0.5 s   (b) about 8.6 s   (c) two; the one through steel, about 8.1 s earlier

Given: distance d = 2972 m; speed in steel = 5944 m/s

Formula: time = distance ÷ speed

Substitution: 2972 ÷ 5944

Answer: (a) t = 0.5 s

Given: d = 2972 m; speed in air = 347 m/s

Formula: time = distance ÷ speed

Substitution: 2972 ÷ 347

Answer: (b) t ≈ 8.6 s

(c) She hears two sounds: first the one through the steel (solids carry sound fastest), and about 8.6 − 0.5 ≈ 8.1 s later the one through the air.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark; (c) ½ for “two”, ½ for “through steel first”.

17.
The figure shows how the sound of a tuning fork reaches our ear. (a) What do the letters C and R stand for? (b) Name the parts labelled 1, 2 and 3. (c) Name the three things needed for us to experience sound.
(3 marks)

💡 Where are the air particles crowded, and where are they spread out?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) compression, rarefaction   (b) outer ear, ear canal, eardrum   (c) source, medium, organ of hearing

(a) C – compression, a region of higher pressure (മർദം കൂടിയ മേഖല) where the air particles are crowded; R – rarefaction, a region of lower pressure (മർദം കുറഞ്ഞ മേഖല) where they are spread out.

(b) 1 – outer ear (pinna, ചെവിക്കുട); 2 – ear canal (കർണ്ണനാളം); 3 – eardrum (കർണ്ണപുടം).

(c) A source of sound, a medium and an organ of hearing (the ear).

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) 1 mark for all three (two correct: ½); (c) 1 mark.

SECTION DAnswer all · 2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
During a monsoon storm Anu sees a flash of lightning and hears the thunder 6 s later. (Speed of sound in air 350 m/s; speed of light 3 × 108 m/s.) (a) Why does she hear the thunder after seeing the flash? (b) How far away did the lightning strike? (c) How long did the light of the flash take to reach her? (d) A few minutes later the gap is only 3 s. Is the storm coming nearer or moving away? Find its new distance. (e) Write two safety steps to take when thunder is heard.
(5 marks)

💡 distance = speed × time for the sound; time = distance ÷ speed for the light.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) light is much faster   (b) 2100 m   (c) 7 × 10−6 s   (d) nearer, 1050 m   (e) e.g. go indoors; keep away from trees and water

(a) Both happen together, but light (3 × 108 m/s) reaches her almost at once while sound (350 m/s) takes 6 s.

Given: v = 350 m/s; t = 6 s

Formula: distance = speed × time

Substitution: 350 × 6

Answer: (b) 2100 m = 2.1 km

Given: d = 2100 m; speed of light = 3 × 108 m/s

Formula: time = distance ÷ speed

Substitution: 2100 ÷ (3 × 108)

Answer: (c) 7 × 10−6 s (7 millionths of a second – “at once”)

(d) A shorter gap means a shorter distance: the storm is coming nearer. New distance = 350 × 3 = 1050 m.

(e) Go indoors at once (a building or a closed vehicle); keep away from tall trees, open grounds, terraces and water; do not use wired phones or touch electrical appliances; stay inside for 30 minutes after the last thunder (any two).

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((b), (c): formula ½, answer with unit ½; (d): ½ + ½; (e): ½ + ½).

19.
“Noise pollution is a serious public-health problem.” (a) What is noise pollution? (b) Write two causes of noise pollution in our towns. (c) Write two harmful effects of noise on our health. (d) Name two groups of people who are affected the most. (e) Suggest two ways to reduce noise pollution.
(5 marks)

💡 Think of a busy junction, a festival loudspeaker and a hospital next to a road.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) unwanted, harmful sound   (b)–(e) any two each

(a) Noise pollution (ശബ്ദമലിനീകരണം) is unnecessary sound that is unbearable and uncomfortable and that harms humans and other living things.

(b) The growing number of vehicles and their horns; loudspeakers and public-address systems; construction machines; factories; firecrackers; aircraft and trains.

(c) Headache, sleeplessness, stress and anger, high blood pressure, hearing loss, poor concentration in studies.

(d) Traffic police; workers in factories, mines and construction sites; people living near airports, highways and railway lines; patients in hospitals; babies, old people and students.

(e) No air horns, and silencers kept in good order; box-type loudspeakers instead of horn-type; no loudspeakers in public places before 6 am and after 10 pm; silence near hospitals, schools and courts; plant trees; ear plugs or ear muffs for workers near loud machines.

Marking: 1 mark for each part (½ for each point in (b)–(e)).
