How Sound Is Produced ശബ്ദം എങ്ങനെ ഉണ്ടാകുന്നു?

A school bell, a koel singing in the mango tree, thunder in the monsoon, the chenda at a temple festival – our world is full of sound. On this page you find out what every one of these sounds has in common: something is vibrating. You pluck a rubber band, strike a tuning fork, splash water with it, take six musical instruments apart and look inside your own throat.

📖 What you need (pages 1–3 of this guide)
  • Vibration (കമ്പനം) = a rapid to-and-fro motion about one rest position – a plucked string, a swing, a ruler twanged on a desk.
  • Straight-line motion (നേർരേഖാചലനം) = moving along a line to new places – a ball rolling along the floor.
  • Air is made of tiny particles that bump into their neighbours (page 2). A vibrating object pushes and pulls these particles (page 3).

1. The bell that made no sound

The chapter opens in a physics lab. A bell is fixed inside a glass bell jar (ബെൽജാർ) that stands on the plate of a vacuum pump (വാക്വം പമ്പ്). The teacher presses the switch…

ബെൽ പ്രവർത്തിച്ചിട്ടും ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്നില്ലല്ലോ!The bell is working, but I can't hear any sound!
🎬 The opening picture – a bell inside a bell jar

Blue dots = air particles (a sketch – real air has far more). The loudness bar is a simple model: the less air, the fainter the sound outside. A real pump can never remove all the air, so a very faint sound may remain. The bell-jar experiment is a teacher's demonstration.

Q (page 157). ബെൽജാറിനുള്ളിലെ അലാം പ്രവർത്തിച്ചിട്ടും കുട്ടിക്ക് ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയാത്തതിനു കാരണം എന്തായിരിക്കും? – The alarm inside the bell jar is working, yet she cannot hear it. What could be the reason?

The vacuum pump has removed the air from the bell jar. Sound needs a medium (like air) to travel; with (almost) no air between the bell and the glass, the sound cannot get out to her ear – even though she can see the hammer striking. Why we can still see it: light does not need air. You will prove this step by step on page 6 ▶.

പ്രകൃതിയിൽ ഉണ്ടാകുന്നതും മനുഷ്യനിർമ്മിതവുമായ നിരവധി ശബ്ദങ്ങൾ നാം ശ്രവിക്കാറുണ്ടല്ലോ. ശബ്ദമില്ലാത്ത ലോകത്തെക്കുറിച്ച് ചിന്തിക്കാനാകുമോ? എന്താണ് ശബ്ദം? – We hear many sounds, some made by nature and some made by people. Can you imagine a world without sound? What is sound?

Q (page 158). നിത്യജീവിതത്തിൽ ശബ്ദം പ്രയോജനപ്പെടുന്ന സന്ദർഭങ്ങൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യൂ. – List situations in daily life where sound is useful. The textbook gives the first: സംഗീതാസ്വാദനത്തിന് – for enjoying music.

Talking to each other and learning in class · alarms and bells (school bell, alarm clock, fire alarm) · horns that warn people on the road · announcements at bus stands and railway stations · a doctor listening to the heart with a stethoscope · a mechanic finding a fault by the sound of an engine · animals communicating (a mother hen calling her chicks, a dog barking a warning) · going further: ultrasound scanning in hospitals and SONAR on ships (page 12). Why it matters: sound carries information and warnings to us without our having to look.

3. How is sound produced? (Fig 8.1, 8.2)

Fig 8.1 shows a man striking the hanging iron school bell with a hammer. A boy wonders: ഈ ബെല്ലിൽനിന്ന് ശബ്ദമുണ്ടാകുന്നതിന് കാരണമെന്തായിരിക്കും? – What makes the sound come from this bell?

When the hammer strikes it, the metal bell vibrates – it moves to and fro very fast. Touch a bell just after it is struck and you can feel it tingle; hold it tightly and the sound stops. Why: the vibrating metal pushes and pulls the air around it, and this disturbance reaches our ears. The next two activities prove it.

Activity – a rubber-band "instrument" (Fig 8.2)

Activity – the tuning fork (Fig 8.4, 8.5)

A tuning fork (ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്ക്) is a U-shaped steel fork with a stem. ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്കിന്റെ ഒരു ഭുജത്തിൽ റബ്ബർ ചുറ്റിക കൊണ്ട് അടിച്ച് ഉത്തേജിപ്പിക്കൂ. ഉത്തേജിപ്പിച്ച ഫോർക്കിനെ ചെവിയുടെ സമീപത്തേക്ക് കൊണ്ടുവരൂ. – Strike one prong with a rubber hammer and bring the fork near your ear. Then touch the prongs gently, dip them into water, and finally grip both prongs tightly.

⚠ Strike the fork only with a rubber hammer or on a rubber pad – never on a desk, a glass or a stone. Use a plastic or steel bowl of water (no glassware), and do not push the fork into your ear: hold it a few centimetres away.

Q. നിരീക്ഷണങ്ങളിൽ നിന്ന് എന്ത് നിഗമനത്തിലാണ് എത്തിച്ചേർന്നത്? – What conclusion do you reach from these observations?

While the fork vibrates, we hear a sound (and the water splashes). When the vibration is stopped by gripping, the sound stops at once. So sound is produced by the vibration of objects. Why the gripping test matters: it shows the link both ways – no vibration, no sound.
⭐ Key idea (textbook page 159)

വസ്തുക്കളുടെ കമ്പനം മൂലമാണ് ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാകുന്നത്. ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാക്കുന്ന വസ്തുക്കളാണ് ശബ്ദസ്രോതസ്സുകൾ.

Sound is produced by the vibration (കമ്പനം) of objects. Objects that produce sound are called sources of sound (ശബ്ദസ്രോതസ്സുകൾ).

  • Every source of sound has a main part that vibrates.
  • The sound we hear is the combined effect of the vibrations of several parts of the source and of the surrounding air.
  • In musical instruments, the vibrations of the main part and the associated parts make the sound sweeter and louder.
💡 Why does a veena need its big round kudam?

A string on its own is thin – it pushes very little air, so its sound is weak. Fixed to a hollow wooden body, its vibrations make the whole body and the air inside vibrate too. This large vibrating surface pushes much more air, so the sound becomes louder, and the body adds its own rich tone, so it becomes sweeter. Try it: pluck a rubber band held between two fingers, then the same band stretched over an open box.

🌴 Kerala connection – chenda melam and panchavadyam

At Thrissur Pooram and other temple festivals, rows of chenda players perform the chenda melam. Each stick-stroke sets the stretched skin vibrating; the wooden frame and the air inside the long body join in, which is why a single chenda can be heard far across the temple ground. In panchavadyam the five instruments are timila, maddalam and idakka (skin), elathalam (metal plates) and kombu (a horn – the air column inside vibrates). The mridangam of a Carnatic concert works like the tabla: a skin at each end, a hollow wooden body.

Q (page 161). നമ്മൾ സംസാരിക്കുമ്പോൾ സ്വനതന്തുക്കൾ മാത്രമായിരിക്കുമോ കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്നത്? കമ്പനം ചെയ്യാൻ സാധ്യതയുള്ള അനുബന്ധ ഭാഗങ്ങൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യൂ. – When we speak, do only the vocal cords vibrate? List the associated parts that may vibrate. (Textbook: പേശികൾ – muscles.)

No. The vocal cords are the main vibrating part, but many associated parts vibrate with them: the muscles of the throat (given), the air in the windpipe, throat, mouth and nose, the walls of the throat and the chest, and the lips, tongue, teeth and cheeks, which also shape the sound into words. Why we think so: you can feel vibration on your chest and nose while humming; and when you have a cold (blocked nose) your voice changes even though your vocal cords are the same.

6. Three textbook questions (page 161, Table 8.2)

Q1. ചുവടെ നൽകിയിരിക്കുന്ന സംഗീതോപകരണങ്ങൾ വാദനം ചെയ്യുമ്പോൾ കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്ന പ്രധാനഭാഗം ഏത്? (a) മൃദംഗം (b) ഓടക്കുഴൽ (c) ഗിറ്റാർ – Which is the main vibrating part when these are played: (a) mridangam (b) odakkuzhal (flute) (c) guitar?

(a) Mridangam → the stretched skin (membrane) at its ends. (b) Odakkuzhal → the air column inside the bamboo tube. (c) Guitar → the strings. Why: in each case this is the part we strike, blow into or pluck – it starts vibrating first; the body and the air inside are associated parts.

Q2. പ്രധാനഭാഗമായോ അനുബന്ധഭാഗമായോ വായു കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്ന സംഗീതോപകരണങ്ങളുടെ പേരുകൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യുക. – List musical instruments in which air vibrates as the main part or as an associated part.

Air is the main part: odakkuzhal (flute), nadaswaram, shehnai, kombu, conch (shankhu), trumpet and bugle, mouth organ and harmonium (with their reeds), whistle. Air is an associated part: veena, violin, guitar, chenda, tabla, mridangam, idakka, ghatam – the air inside the hollow body vibrates along with the strings or the skin. Why so many: almost every instrument has a hollow body, because the vibrating air inside makes the sound louder.

🧭 Summary
  • Sound is a form of energy that gives us the sensation of hearing.
  • Sound is produced by vibration – a rapid to-and-fro motion (rubber band, tuning fork, bell). Stop the vibration and the sound stops.
  • Objects that produce sound are sources of sound.
  • Every source has a main vibrating part (strings of a veena, skin of a chenda, reeds of a harmonium, plates of an elathalam) and associated parts (body, air inside) that make the sound louder and sweeter.
  • Our voice comes from the vocal cords in the larynx (voice box); the throat, mouth, nose and the air in them vibrate too.
