📖 What you need (pages 1–9 of this guide)
  • Sound ശബ്ദം is a form of energy ഊർജരൂപം that produces the sensation of hearing ശ്രവണബോധം. It is produced by vibration കമ്പനം, which is a quick to-and-fro motion (page 1, page 5).
  • Sound travels through a medium മാധ്യമം (solid, liquid or gas) as compressions and rarefactions. The particles only move to and fro; they do not travel with the sound. It cannot travel through vacuum ശൂന്യത (page 3, page 6).
  • Speed of sound ശബ്ദവേഗം: fastest in solids, slowest in gases (Table 8.3). It increases with temperature. In air it is about 350 m/s, while light travels at 3 × 108 m/s. distance = speed × time (page 4, page 7).
  • To hear a sound we need a source, a medium and an organ of hearing. Music comes from regular vibrations and noise from irregular ones (page 8, page 9).

1 · The bell that cannot be heard (p 157)

ബെൽജാറിനുള്ളിലെ അലാം പ്രവർത്തിച്ചിട്ടും കുട്ടിക്ക് ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയാത്തതിനു കാരണം എന്തായിരിക്കും? The alarm inside the bell jar ബെൽജാർ is working, yet she cannot hear any sound. What could be the reason? (Thought bubble: “ബെൽ പ്രവർത്തിച്ചിട്ടും ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്നില്ലല്ലോ!” – “The bell is working, but I can't hear any sound!”)

നിങ്ങൾക്ക് പരിചിതമായ ശബ്ദങ്ങൾ ഏതൊക്കെയാണ്? എഴുതി നോക്കൂ. Which sounds are familiar to you? Write them down. (Given: the school bell ringing, the song of the koel കുയിൽ, thunder ഇടിനാദം.)

Look at the picture on p 157. The bell jar stands on a vacuum pump വാക്വം പമ്പ്. What does a vacuum pump take out of the jar? Step through the animation and watch the blue dots, which stand for air particles.

🎬 Solution animation – a ringing bell in a bell jar on a vacuum pump
Why no sound? The vacuum pump has removed the air from the bell jar. The bell is still vibrating, and we can see the hammer striking. But sound needs a medium മാധ്യമം to travel, and there is almost no air (nearly a vacuum ശൂന്യത) around the bell to carry the vibrations to the glass and out to her ear. Sound cannot travel through vacuum. The textbook answers this on p 162; see section 10.
Familiar sounds: the school bell, the koel's song and thunder (given). Others: rain on the roof, temple drums (chenda), a vehicle horn, a pressure-cooker whistle, a dog barking, the ceiling fan, waves on the beach, friends talking.
Key idea ശ്രവണബോധം ഉളവാക്കുന്ന ഊർജരൂപമാണ് ശബ്ദം. Sound is a form of energy that produces the sensation of hearing.
⚠️ Common trap

“The bell stopped working.” No. You can still see the hammer hitting the gong, so the bell is fine. Only the medium is missing. Let the air back in and the sound returns at once.

2 · Uses of sound, and why the school bell rings (p 158, Fig 8.1)

നിത്യജീവിതത്തിൽ ശബ്ദം പ്രയോജനപ്പെടുന്ന സന്ദർഭങ്ങൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യൂ. List situations in daily life where sound is useful. (Given: to enjoy music സംഗീതാസ്വാദനത്തിന്.)

ഈ ബെല്ലിൽനിന്ന് ശബ്ദമുണ്ടാകുന്നതിന് കാരണമെന്തായിരിക്കും? (Fig 8.1: a man strikes the hanging school bell.) What could be the reason this bell makes a sound?

Strike the bell in the lab and watch its outline closely. Then hold it with your hand. What stops when the sound stops?

🧪 Try it – strike the school bell (Fig 8.1)

The vibration is drawn much bigger and slower than in real life. A real bell's rim moves only a tiny fraction of a millimetre, hundreds of times a second.

Uses of sound: enjoying music (given); talking and listening to each other; teaching and learning in class; the school bell, alarm clocks and fire alarms; vehicle horns, ambulance sirens and railway-crossing bells that warn us; announcements at bus and railway stations; the doctor's stethoscope; animals calling to each other; the radio, TV and phone calls. (Go further: ultrasound scanning and SONAR, see page 12.)
The bell: when the rod strikes it, the metal bell vibrates. Its rim moves in and out very quickly. These vibrations push and pull on the air around it, and that is the sound we hear. Hold the bell and the vibration stops, so the sound stops too.

3 · The rubber-band instrument (p 158, Fig 8.2)

Activity. പെൻസിൽ ബോക്സ്, റബ്ബർ ബാൻഡ്, പെൻസിലുകൾ ഇവ ഉപയോഗിച്ച് ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാക്കുന്ന ഒരു ഉപകരണം നിർമ്മിക്കൂ (ചിത്രം 8.2). റബ്ബർ ബാൻഡിന്റെ മധ്യഭാഗം അല്പം വലിച്ചു വിടൂ. Make a sound-making device with a pencil box, a rubber band and two pencils (Fig 8.2). Pull the middle of the rubber band a little and let it go.

  • ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കുന്നുണ്ടോ? Do you hear a sound?
  • റബ്ബർ ബാൻഡ് അങ്ങോട്ടുമിങ്ങോട്ടും ദ്രുതഗതിയിൽ ചലിക്കുന്നതായി അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്നുണ്ടോ? Does the rubber band seem to move to and fro rapidly?
  • റബ്ബർ ബാൻഡ് ഏതുതരം ചലനത്തിലാണ്? (കമ്പനം/നേർരേഖാചലനം) What kind of motion is the rubber band in? (vibration കമ്പനം / straight-line motion നേർരേഖാചലനം)

Pluck the band in the lab and watch the graph on the right. Does the middle of the band keep going in one direction, or does it come back again and again?

🧪 Try it – pluck the band and watch its motion
• Yes, we hear a sound (a twang) as long as the band moves.
• Yes, the band moves to and fro rapidly. It goes up, down, up, down, many times a second, and looks blurred.
• This motion is vibration കമ്പനം, not straight-line motion നേർരേഖാചലനം. The band keeps returning through the same middle position. It does not travel off in one direction.
⚠️ Common trap

“The band moves a long way, so it is straight-line motion.” Straight-line motion means going along a line from one place to another, like a ball rolling across the floor. A vibrating band stays in the same place and only swings to and fro about its rest position.

4 · Toys that make sound (p 158, Fig 8.3)

ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്ന കളിപ്പാട്ടങ്ങൾ നിങ്ങൾക്ക് പരിചിതമാണല്ലോ. അവ ഏതൊക്കെയെന്ന് കുറിക്കൂ. You know toys that can make sound. Note down which ones. (Fig 8.3: (a) ola-peepi ഓലപ്പീപ്പി, (b) paper whistle പേപ്പർവിസിൽ, (c) chiratta-veena ചിരട്ടവീണ.)

ഇഷ്ടമുള്ള ഒരു കളിപ്പാട്ടം നിർമ്മിച്ച് അതിന്റെ ശബ്ദം ശ്രവിക്കൂ. അതിൽ എങ്ങനെയാണ് ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാകുന്നത് എന്ന് ചർച്ച ചെയ്തു കണ്ടെത്തി സയൻസ് ഡയറിയിൽ എഴുതൂ. Make a toy you like and listen to its sound. Discuss how it makes the sound and write it in your science diary.

For every toy, ask one question: which part vibrates? It may be a leaf, a strip of paper, a string, a stretched skin, or the air inside a tube.

Sound-making toys: ola-peepi, paper whistle, chiratta-veena (given); also a balloon whistle, a toy drum (thudi), a party whistle, a rattle, a straw flute (pipi), a rubber-band guitar, a toy bell, a spinning top that hums, and a whistle made by blowing across a bottle.
How they make sound (one each):
• Ola-peepi: blowing into the narrow end makes the thin, pressed leaf there vibrate very fast. The cone of rolled palm leaf makes the sound louder.
• Paper whistle: the thin paper strip or flap held between the fingers vibrates in the stream of air we blow.
• Chiratta-veena: the bow makes the string vibrate. The coconut shell, the thin cover on it and the air inside vibrate along with it and make the sound louder.
In every toy, the sound comes from something that vibrates.
🛠️ Make a toy: a straw whistle (pipi) in 3 minutes
  1. Flatten one end of a paper or plastic drinking straw (about 3 cm) by pressing it with your fingernail.
  2. Cut the flattened end into a V point with scissors, with an adult's help.
  3. Put the V end just inside your lips, press lightly and blow hard.
  4. What vibrates? The two flaps of the V vibrate against each other, and they make the air in the straw vibrate. Cut the straw shorter while blowing and listen to how the sound changes.
⚠ Use scissors only with an adult. Do not blow whistles close to anyone's ear.

5 · The tuning fork (p 159, Figs 8.4, 8.5)

Activity. ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്കിന്റെ ഒരു ഭുജത്തിൽ റബ്ബർ ചുറ്റിക കൊണ്ട് അടിച്ച് ഉത്തേജിപ്പിക്കൂ. ഉത്തേജിപ്പിച്ച ഫോർക്കിനെ ചെവിയുടെ സമീപത്തേക്ക് കൊണ്ടുവരൂ. Strike one prong of a tuning fork ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്ക് with a rubber hammer to set it vibrating, and bring it near your ear (Fig 8.4).

  • ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്നുണ്ടോ? Can you hear a sound?
  • ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്കിന്റെ ഭുജങ്ങൾ കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്നതായി അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്നില്ലേ? (Touch the prongs gently.) Don't you feel the prongs vibrating?
  • ജലകണികകൾ തെറിച്ചു പോകുന്നതിനു കാരണം ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്കിന്റെ കമ്പനമല്ലേ? (Touch the prongs of the struck fork to the water surface in a vessel, Fig 8.5.) Isn't the vibration of the tuning fork the reason why the water drops splash?
  • ശബ്ദം കേൾക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്നുണ്ടോ? ഇപ്പോൾ ട്യൂണിങ് ഫോർക്ക് കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്നുണ്ടോ? (Strike it again and hold both prongs tightly in your hand.) Can you hear a sound? Is the fork vibrating now?
  • നിരീക്ഷണങ്ങളിൽ നിന്ന് എന്ത് നിഗമനത്തിലാണ് എത്തിച്ചേർന്നത്? What conclusion did you reach from these observations?

Do the four steps in the lab in order: strike, bring it to the ear, dip it in water, then grip it. Each time, ask: is it vibrating, and is there a sound?

🧪 Try it – the tuning-fork bench

Prong movement is magnified and slowed down. A real fork vibrates hundreds of times a second.

⚠ Strike a tuning fork only on a rubber pad or a rubber hammer, never on a table or a glass. Use a plastic or steel bowl for the water test, not glassware, and wipe up spills.
• Yes, a sound is heard when the struck fork is brought near the ear.
• Yes, a gentle touch on the prongs shows that they are vibrating. They tickle the finger.
• Yes, the water drops splash because the vibrating prongs hit the water surface again and again.
• When both prongs are held tightly there is no sound, and the fork is not vibrating any more.
• Conclusion വസ്തുക്കളുടെ കമ്പനം മൂലമാണ് ശബ്ദം ഉണ്ടാകുന്നത്. Sound is produced by the vibration of objects. Objects that produce sound are called sources of sound ശബ്ദസ്രോതസ്സുകൾ.
Examples of sources of sound ശബ്ദസ്രോതസ്സുകൾക്ക് ഉദാഹരണങ്ങൾ എഴുതുക. the tuning fork and musical instruments (given); bells, our vocal cords, loudspeakers, a buzzing bee's wings, a ticking clock, a whistling pressure cooker.
Musical instruments you know നിങ്ങൾക്കു പരിചിതമായ സംഗീതോപകരണങ്ങളുടെ പേരുകൾ സയൻസ് ഡയറിയിൽ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യൂ. odakkuzhal (flute) and mridangam (given); veena, violin, chenda, tabla, harmonium, elathalam, idakka, nadaswaram, guitar, maddalam, keyboard.
⚠️ Common trap

“Holding the fork blocks the sound from reaching the ear.” Not quite. Gripping the prongs stops the vibration itself. No vibration means no sound is produced at all. That is the whole point of this step.

⚠️ Common trap

Writing only the main part, or only the body. A full answer names both. Example: the violin's strings vibrate (main part), and the wooden body, the bridge and the air inside vibrate along with them (associated parts). Without the body a violin string alone sounds very weak.

7 · Our voice (p 161, Fig 8.7)

ഞാൻ സംസാരിക്കുമ്പോൾ പ്രധാനമായും കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്നത് ഏത് ഭാഗമായിരിക്കും? Which part mainly vibrates when I speak?

ഉച്ചത്തിൽ സംസാരിച്ചുകൊണ്ട് തൊണ്ടയിൽ സ്പർശിച്ചുനോക്കൂ. കമ്പനം അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്നില്ലേ? Touch your throat while speaking loudly. Don't you feel a vibration?

  • നമ്മൾ സംസാരിക്കുമ്പോൾ സ്വനതന്തുക്കൾ മാത്രമായിരിക്കുമോ കമ്പനം ചെയ്യുന്നത്? When we speak, do only the vocal cords vibrate?
  • കമ്പനം ചെയ്യാൻ സാധ്യതയുള്ള അനുബന്ധ ഭാഗങ്ങൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യൂ. List the associated parts that may vibrate. (Given: muscles പേശികൾ.)

The book tells you that sound is produced when the vocal cords സ്വനതന്തുക്കൾ in the larynx (voice box) സ്വനപേടകം, at the top of the windpipe, vibrate. Press “Say aaa” and watch which other parts start moving.

🧪 Try it – the voice box (Fig 8.7)
• Main part: the vocal cords സ്വനതന്തുക്കൾ in the larynx (voice box) സ്വനപേടകം. Air from the lungs makes them vibrate. That is the vibration you feel on your throat.
• No, the vocal cords are not the only parts that vibrate.
• Associated parts: the muscles of the throat (given); the air in the throat, mouth and nose; the walls of the windpipe and the throat; the cheeks and the chest. The lips, tongue, teeth and palate shape the sound into words.
💡 Try it on yourself

Put your fingers on your throat and say “aaa”, then whisper “aaa”. When you whisper the vocal cords hardly vibrate, so the buzz under your fingers almost disappears. Hum with your lips closed and touch your nose: you can feel the air in the nose vibrating too.

13 · Why do astronauts talk by radio? (p 163, Fig 8.12)

ബഹിരാകാശ സഞ്ചാരികൾ പരസ്പരം സംസാരിക്കാൻ റേഡിയോ സംവിധാനം ഉപയോഗിക്കേണ്ടിവരുന്നത് എന്തുകൊണ്ടാണ്? Why do astronauts have to use a radio system to talk to each other?

Outside the spacecraft, is there any air between the two helmets? What does sound need in order to travel?

🎬 Solution animation – sound stops at the helmet, radio goes through
Space is almost a vacuum: there is no air outside the astronauts' helmets. Sound needs a medium, so the sound of one astronaut's voice cannot travel across the empty space to the other. The voice is picked up by a microphone inside the helmet and sent as radio waves, which can travel through vacuum. The other astronaut's radio receives them and turns them back into sound inside the helmet, where there is air.
⚠️ Common trap

“Radio waves are a kind of sound.” They are not. Radio waves are electromagnetic waves, the same family as light, and they need no medium. That is why we can see the Sun and talk to spacecraft, but we cannot hear the Sun.

15 · The firecracker heard 3 s later (p 164)

രാത്രിയിൽ പടക്കം പൊട്ടുമ്പോഴുണ്ടായ വെളിച്ചം കണ്ട് 3 s നു ശേഷം അതിന്റെ ശബ്ദം കേട്ടു എങ്കിൽ സ്ഫോടനം നടന്നത് എത്ര അകലെയായിരിക്കും? (ശബ്ദവേഗം 350 m/s എന്ന് പരിഗണിക്കുക). At night, the sound of a firecracker was heard 3 s after its flash was seen. How far away did the explosion happen? (Take the speed of sound as 350 m/s.)

Light reaches you almost instantly, so the 3 s is the time the sound took. Use distance = speed × time.

🎬 Solution in exam layout – Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer
🧪 Try other numbers
Given: time taken by the sound t = 3 s; speed of sound v = 350 m/s.
Formula: distance travelled by sound = speed of sound × time ശബ്ദം സഞ്ചരിച്ച ദൂരം = ശബ്ദവേഗം × സമയം.
Substitution: d = 350 m/s × 3 s.
Answer: d = 1050 m (about 1.05 km). Light (3 × 108 m/s) covers this distance in only 1050 ÷ (3 × 108) s = 0.0000035 s, so we can treat the flash as seen at the instant of the explosion.
⚠️ Common trap

Using the speed of light with the 3 s. The 3 s is the extra time the sound needs, so it goes with 350 m/s. Also always write the unit: “1050 m”, not just “1050”.

16 · Why is thunder heard after lightning? (p 164)

പല സന്ദർഭങ്ങളിലും മിന്നൽ കണ്ട് അല്പസമയം കഴിഞ്ഞ് മാത്രം ഇടിനാദം കേൾക്കുന്നതിന്റെ കാരണം വിശദമാക്കുക. Explain why, in many cases, we hear thunder only some time after seeing the lightning.

Both start together in the cloud. Compare how long light and sound each take to cover the same distance.

🎬 Solution animation – the race between light and sound
Lightning and thunder are produced at the same moment. But light travels at 3 × 108 m/s, so it reaches us almost instantly. Sound travels through air at only about 350 m/s, so it needs several seconds to cover the same distance, about 3 s for every kilometre. So we see the flash first and hear the thunder later. The farther away the lightning, the longer the gap.

Worked example – how far away is the storm?

Same method as the firecracker: the 5 s belongs to the sound. Multiply speed by time, then change metres to kilometres (1 km = 1000 m).

🎬 Solution in exam layout – Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer
🧪 Try other numbers – the thunder timer
Given: time taken by the sound t = 5 s; speed of sound v = 350 m/s.
Formula: distance = speed × time.
Substitution: d = 350 m/s × 5 s.
Answer: d = 1750 m = 1.75 km. (The light took only 1750 ÷ (3 × 108) s ≈ 0.0000058 s.)

• No. A source and a medium are not enough. We also need an organ of hearing ശ്രവണേന്ദ്രിയം, the ear, in working order. The grandmother in Fig 8.13 has the source (the boy's voice) and the medium (air), but her ears have become weak with age.
• Yes, the seeds jump up and down, because the diaphragm vibrates when the sound (pressure changes in the air) hits it.
• Our ear has a similar part that can vibrate: the eardrum (tympanum) കർണ്ണപുടം.
How we hear (p 165): a vibrating object makes rapid pressure variations മർദവ്യതിയാനങ്ങൾ in the air around it: compressions C മർദം കൂടിയ മേഖല and rarefactions R മർദം കുറഞ്ഞ മേഖല. They travel through the medium, enter the outer ear ചെവിക്കുട and the ear canal കർണ്ണനാളം, and make the eardrum vibrate. The eardrum sends the vibrations to the inner ear ആന്തരകർണ്ണം. From there signals reach the brain, and only then do we perceive the sound.
Three things are needed to hear a sound: a source, a medium and an organ of hearing.
⚠️ Common trap

“The air from the tuning fork travels into the ear.” No. The air particles only move to and fro near their own places. It is the pattern of compressions and rarefactions, the disturbance, that travels to the ear.

🧪 Try it – the shape of a sound (picture only, no audio)
• The traffic-junction sound is unpleasant because it is a mixture of many irregular sounds (horns, engines, brakes) and it is very loud.
• The music class is pleasant to hear. Its sound comes from regular, orderly vibrations.
• The chiratta-veena gives sweet music, but rubbing the coconut shell on a stone gives a harsh, unpleasant sound. Its vibrations are irregular.
• Key idea സംഗീതം (Music) … ഒച്ച (noise): sound produced by regular (orderly) vibrations that is pleasant to hear is music. Sound produced by irregular vibrations that is unpleasant is noise.
• Yes, even music becomes noise when it is played too loud (beyond a limit), for example festival loudspeakers late at night.
• Yes, loud sound harms the ear. Very loud sounds, or loud sounds heard for a long time, can damage the inner ear and cause permanent hearing loss.
⚠️ Common trap

“Noise = loud sound and music = soft sound.” The real difference is regular vs irregular vibration. A soft scrape of a shell on a stone is still noise. Loud music is still made of regular vibrations, but because it is unpleasant and harmful it becomes noise for the listener.
