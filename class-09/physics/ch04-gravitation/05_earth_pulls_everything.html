Chapter 4 · Section 1Textbook pages 73–75

The Earth Pulls Everything ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണബലം

Mangoes drop, a stone thrown up comes back, a feather drifts down. Something pulls all of them towards the ground. On this page you find out what pulls them, in which direction, whether we can measure that pull, and what it depends on.

📖 What you need (pages 1 and 2 of this guide)
  • A force (ബലം) is a push or a pull; its unit is the newton (N).
  • A spring balance (സ്പ്രിങ് ബാലൻസ്) measures force: the bigger the force, the more the spring stretches.
  • 100 g = 0.1 kg, 200 g = 0.2 kg.

1. Chandrayaan-3 and a classroom full of questions

The chapter opens with a class watching India's Chandrayaan-3 lander on the Moon (textbook page 73). The children say:

എത്ര കൃത്യതയോടെയാണ് നമ്മുടെ ശാസ്ത്രജ്ഞർ ഈ പേടകം ചന്ദ്രനിലിറക്കിയത്!How precisely our scientists landed this craft on the Moon!
ചന്ദ്രനിലുമുണ്ടോ ഗുരുത്വാകർഷണം?Is there gravity on the Moon too?
ഭൂമിയുടെ ഗുരുത്വാകർഷണബലത്തെയും ചന്ദ്രന്റെ ഗുരുത്വാകർഷണബലത്തെയും അതിജീവിച്ച് ചന്ദ്രനിൽ ഭാരതം!Overcoming the gravity of the Earth and of the Moon – Bharat on the Moon!

By the end of this chapter you will be able to answer all three. We start with something you see every day.

🎬 Everything comes back down

The mango and the stone speed up as they fall; the leaf and the feather drift slowly because the air holds them back (more about that on page 7). But all of them come down.

Q. കല്ലിനും തൂവലിനും താഴേക്ക് പതിക്കാൻ ആവശ്യമായ ബലം എവിടെ നിന്നാണ് ലഭിച്ചത്? – Where does the force that makes the stone and the feather fall come from?

From the Earth. The Earth pulls (attracts) every object towards itself. Why: nothing is touching the falling stone, yet it speeds up downwards – so an unseen pull must act on it. That pull is the Earth's attraction, called gravity.

🎬 Stones dropped into wells everywhere (Fig 4.1, 4.2)

Q. കല്ലുകൾ ആകർഷിക്കപ്പെടുന്നത് കിണറുകളുടെ അടിത്തട്ടിലേക്കല്ലേ? – In wells all over the Earth, are the stones not pulled to the bottom of each well?

Yes. Every stone lands at the bottom of its own well. Since every well points towards the middle of the Earth, every stone is pulled towards the centre of the Earth, whichever side the well is on.

Q. ഭൂമിയുടെ മറുവശത്തുള്ളവർ ഭൂമിയിൽ നിന്നു വീണുപോകുന്നില്ലല്ലോ! ഭൂമിയുടെ ആകർഷണമല്ലേ ഇതിനു കാരണം? – People on the other side of the Earth don't fall off. Isn't the Earth's attraction the reason?

Yes. For a person in Brazil, “down” is towards the Earth's centre just as it is for us in Kerala. The Earth pulls each of them towards its centre, so their feet stay pressed to the ground. There is no “bottom” of the Earth to fall off.

The thinking boy in the textbook asks: ഈ ആകർഷണബലം അളക്കുവാൻ കഴിയുമോ? – Can this attractive force be measured? First, the textbook's key idea:

⭐ Key idea (textbook page 73)

എല്ലാ വസ്തുക്കളെയും ഭൂമി ആകർഷിക്കുന്നു. ഇതിന്റെ ദിശ ഭൂമിയുടെ കേന്ദ്രത്തിലേക്കാണ്. ഈ ആകർഷണബലമാണ് ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണ ബലം.

The Earth attracts every object. The direction of this pull is towards the centre of the Earth (ഭൂകേന്ദ്രം). This attractive force (ആകർഷണബലം) is the Earth's gravitational force – gravity.

3. Activity 4.1 – Measuring the Earth's pull with a spring balance

The textbook's activity (പ്രവർത്തനം, page 74, Fig 4.3): fix a spring balance to a window bar, pull its hook with your hand, then hang 100 g and 200 g masses on it.

⚠ Real experiment: tie the balance firmly to the window bar, pull gently and let the hook go back slowly – a spring that snaps back can hurt your fingers. Use the slotted masses from the school lab with your teacher or an adult.
🎬 Virtual lab: the activity step by step

Textbook questions (page 74) – try them, then check.

  1. എന്തുകൊണ്ടാണ് സ്പ്രിങ് വലിഞ്ഞത്? Why did the spring stretch?
  2. സ്പ്രിങ് ബാലൻസ് റീഡിങ് എത്രയാണ്? ഇതല്ലേ നാം പ്രയോഗിച്ച ബലം? What is the reading? Isn't this the force we applied?
  3. ബലത്തിന്റെ യൂണിറ്റ് ഏതാണ്? What is the unit of force?
  4. With 100 g hanging: why did the spring stretch? Which force pulled the 100 g down? What is the reading?
  5. With 200 g: why more stretch? മാസ് കൂടുമ്പോൾ ആകർഷണബലത്തിന് എന്ത് സംഭവിക്കുന്നു? (കൂടുന്നു / കുറയുന്നു) What happens to the attractive force when the mass increases? Write one factor that affects it.
1. Because of the force of my hand pulling the hook. Why: a spring stretches only when a force pulls it.
2. Whatever the pointer shows (in our lab, 3 N). Yes – the reading is the force we applied, because the balance is made to show the pull on its hook.
3. The newton (N).
4. Nobody is pulling, yet it stretches: the Earth pulls the 100 g mass downwards (gravity). Reading ≈ 0.98 N ≈ 1 N (0.1 kg × 9.8 N per kg).
5. The Earth pulls the 200 g mass harder: reading ≈ 1.96 N ≈ 2 N. The attractive force increases (കൂടുന്നു) when the mass increases. One factor: the mass of the object.

Q. 100 kg മാസുള്ള വസ്തുവിൽ ആകർഷണബലം കൂടുതൽ അനുഭവപ്പെട്ടത് എവിടെ വച്ചാണ്? – Where did the 100 kg object feel the greatest force? (at the surface / at 1,00,000 m / at 10,00,000 m)

At the surface – 980 N, the largest number in the table. Why: there it is closest to the Earth's centre (6400 km away instead of 6500 km or 7400 km).

Q. ഭൂമിയിൽ നിന്ന് വസ്തുവിലേക്കുള്ള അകലം കൂടുമ്പോൾ ഭൂമി പ്രയോഗിക്കുന്ന ആകർഷണബലം (കൂടുന്നു / കുറയുന്നു) – As the distance from the Earth increases, the attractive force …?

Decreases (കുറയുന്നു): 980 N → 950 N → 730 N. Why: the same object, farther away, is pulled less. So distance is a second factor.

📒 Science diary (സയൻസ് ഡയറി) – what we found

  • ഭൂമി എല്ലാ വസ്തുക്കളെയും അതിന്റെ കേന്ദ്രത്തിലേക്ക് ആകർഷിക്കുന്നു. – The Earth attracts all objects towards its centre.
  • വസ്തുവിന്റെ മാസ് കൂടുന്നതിനനുസരിച്ച് ആകർഷണബലം കൂടുന്നു. – The attractive force increases as the mass of the object increases.
  • വസ്തുവിലേക്കുള്ള അകലം കൂടുന്നതിനനുസരിച്ച് ആകർഷണബലം കുറയുന്നു. – The attractive force decreases as the distance to the object increases.

5. Is the Earth the only one that pulls? – Tides

The thinking boy asks: ഭൂമി മാത്രമാണോ ഇത്തരത്തിൽ ആകർഷണബലം പ്രയോഗിക്കുന്നത്? – Is the Earth the only body that pulls like this? No! The sea at Kochi rises and falls twice a day: high tide (വേലിയേറ്റം) and low tide (വേലിയിറക്കം). These happen because the Moon and the Sun pull on the Earth – mostly on its oceans, which can move.

🌊 Tide lab – turn the Earth through one day

The bulges (exaggerated hugely) point towards and away from the Moon. As the Earth spins, Kochi passes through both bulges, so there are two high tides and two low tides in about 24 h 50 min.

Q. സൂര്യനും ചന്ദ്രനും ഭൂമിയിൽ ബലം പ്രയോഗിക്കുമെങ്കിൽ പ്രപഞ്ചത്തിലെ മറ്റു ഗോളങ്ങൾ തമ്മിലും പരസ്പരാകർഷണബലം ഉണ്ടാകില്ലേ? – If the Sun and the Moon exert a force on the Earth, won't all other bodies in the universe also attract each other?

Yes. If the Earth pulls stones, the Moon pulls the Earth's oceans and the Sun pulls the Earth, there is nothing special about any of them: every object in the universe attracts every other object. This mutual attractive force (പരസ്പരാകർഷണബലം) is described by Newton's universal law of gravitation – the next page.
🌴 Kerala connection

Fishermen at Kochi, Munambam and Vizhinjam plan their trips with the tide tables; big ships enter Kochi port and the Vizhinjam harbour more easily at high tide. In Kerala the tide range is small (well under a metre) compared with places like the Gulf of Khambhat in Gujarat, where it can be several metres – but the cause is the same: the Moon's and Sun's gravity.

📚 Scientist from Kerala – Thanu Padmanabhan (താണു പത്മനാഭൻ), 1957–2021
  • Born in Thiruvananthapuram. BSc and MSc from University College, Thiruvananthapuram, with gold medals.
  • Made fundamental contributions to cosmology (the study of the universe as a whole) and gravitation. He worked for many years at IUCAA (Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics), Pune.
  • He presented a new way of looking at gravity, different from those of Newton and Einstein – gravity as an “emergent” effect, the way temperature emerges from the motion of countless molecules.
  • Honours: Padma Shri, the Kerala Sasthra Puraskaram and many more.

A boy from Thiruvananthapuram became one of the world's leading thinkers on gravity – the very topic of this chapter.

🧭 Summary
  • The Earth attracts every object towards its centre: this is the Earth's gravitational force (gravity).
  • A spring balance measures it in newtons: 100 g → 0.98 N ≈ 1 N; 200 g → 1.96 N ≈ 2 N.
  • The pull increases with mass and decreases with distance (Table 4.1: 980 N → 950 N → 730 N).
  • The Moon and the Sun pull on the Earth too (tides) – every body attracts every other body.
