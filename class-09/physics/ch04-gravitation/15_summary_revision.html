2. The key boxes of the textbook

1The Earth pulls everything (p. 73)എല്ലാ വസ്തുക്കളെയും ഭൂമി ആകർഷിക്കുന്നു. ഇതിന്റെ ദിശ ഭൂമിയുടെ കേന്ദ്രത്തിലേക്കാണ്. ഈ ആകർഷണബലമാണ് ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണ ബലം.

The Earth attracts every object; the direction is towards the centre of the Earth (ഭൂകേന്ദ്രം). This attractive force is the Earth’s gravitational force. – page 5

2Universal law of gravitation (p. 75)സാർവിക ഗുരുത്വാകർഷണനിയമം
F = G m1m2 / d2

Every object in the universe attracts every other object. The force is directly proportional to the product of the masses (ഗുണനഫലം) and inversely proportional to the square of the distance (അകലത്തിന്റെ വർഗം) between them. – page 6

3Acceleration due to gravity (p. 78)ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണബലത്താൽ വസ്തുക്കൾക്കുണ്ടാവുന്ന ത്വരണം ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണ ത്വരണം.
g = GM / R2 ≈ 9.8 m/s2

The acceleration produced in objects by the Earth’s gravitational force is g. The object’s own mass is not in the formula, so all objects fall together (without air). – page 7

4One kilogram weight (p. 82)ഒരു കിലോഗ്രാം ഭാരം (1 kgwt)
W = mg;  1 kgwt = 1 kg × 9.8 m/s2 = 9.8 N

1 kgwt is the force with which the Earth attracts a 1 kg mass. Weight (ഭാരം) is a force, measured in newton with a spring balance. – page 10

5Free fall (p. 83)നിർബാധ പതനം

A body allowed to fall freely from a height falls only because of the Earth’s gravitational force; such motion is free fall. A body in free fall shows no weight on a balance: weightlessness (ഭാരമില്ലായ്മ). – page 11

★Circular motion (pp. 84–85)വർത്തുളചലനം · അഭികേന്ദ്രബലം

In uniform circular motion (സമവർത്തുളചലനം) the speed is constant but the velocity is not – its direction changes. A force towards the centre, the centripetal force, is needed; without it the body flies off along the tangent (തൊടുവര). For satellites, gravity is the centripetal force. – page 11

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Writing weight in kg. Weight is a force → newton (N). “50 kg” on a spring balance really means 50 kgwt = 490 N.
  • Forgetting to square the distance, or using the height above the ground instead of the distance from the centre. For a satellite at height h use (R + h)2.
  • “Heavier objects fall faster.” Without air they fall together: g = GM/R2 has no m in it.
  • “The heavier body pulls harder.” The force is mutual and equal: the Earth pulls the Moon with F and the Moon pulls the Earth with the same F.
  • “There is no gravity in the space station.” At 400 km g is about 8.7 m/s2 (≈ 89 %); the astronauts float because they are in free fall.
  • “Mass changes on the Moon.” Only the weight changes (to about 1/6); the mass stays the same.
  • Thinking g is a constant like G. g changes from place to place; G is universal.
  • Sign errors for upward throws. Take upwards as positive and use −g (v = u − gt), or use “time up = time down”.
  • Losing units or powers of ten. 10−11 × 1024 = 1013; (6.4 × 106)2 = 40.96 × 1012. Always end with a unit.
