Gravitation ഗുരുത്വാകർഷണം

A coconut drops from a palm. The Moon goes round and round the Earth. Chandrayaan-3 landed gently on the Moon. All three happen because of one force: gravitation. Every object in the universe pulls every other object. In this chapter you will see how big that pull is, why every falling object speeds up by 9.8 m/s every second, why the Moon pulls only about one sixth as hard as the Earth, how mass differs from weight, why astronauts float, and what keeps satellites in orbit. This interactive, animated self-study guide starts with the basics from earlier classes. It then covers every idea of the chapter with Kerala examples and gives animated, exam-style solutions to every textbook question. It ends with a revision sheet and a model test with a fully solved paper.

The chapter in one picture

🌍 One force, two motions

The red arrows are the Earth's pull, called gravity (ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണബലം). It always points towards the centre of the Earth (ഭൂകേന്ദ്രം). The coconut starts at rest, so the pull makes it fall straight down, faster and faster. The Moon is already moving sideways very fast, so the same pull keeps bending its path into a circle around the Earth. You will meet both ideas again on pages 7 and 11.
