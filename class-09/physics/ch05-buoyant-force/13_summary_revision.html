1. The chapter in one picture

🗺️ Buoyant force – concept map

Read it clockwise from the top left: a fluid pushes up on an object in it; we measure the push as the loss of weight on a spring balance; it depends on the density of the fluid and the volume under it; it equals the weight of the fluid displaced (Archimedes); comparing it with the weight tells us whether the object floats or sinks; floating instruments measure relative density; and ships, submarines, life jackets and balloons all use it.

2. The key boxes of the textbook

1Buoyant force (p. 96)പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം · ദ്രവങ്ങൾ

When an object is fully or partly immersed in a fluid, the upward force exerted on it by the fluid is the buoyant force. Liquids and gases together are called fluids. – page 5

2Apparent loss of weight (p. 97)ഭാരനഷ്ടം
FB = W₁ − W₂

The buoyant force on an object in a fluid equals its apparent loss of weight (weight in air − weight in the fluid). – page 5

3Density of the fluid (p. 99)സാന്ദ്രത

The density of the fluid is one factor that influences the buoyant force: the greater the density of the liquid, the greater the buoyant force (glycerine > salt solution > water > kerosene). – page 6

4Two factors (p. 100)സാന്ദ്രത · വ്യാപ്തം

For a fully immersed object the buoyant force depends on (1) the density of the fluid and (2) the volume of the object. Not on the object’s own density, and not on the depth. – page 6

5Archimedes’ principle (p. 101)ആർക്കിമിഡീസ് തത്വം
FB = weight of fluid displaced

When an object is fully or partly immersed in a fluid, the buoyant force on it equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the object. – page 7

6Principle of floatation (p. 102)പ്ലവനതത്വം

When an object floats in a fluid, its weight equals the weight of the fluid it displaces. Sinking: FB < weight. Floating: FB = weight. – page 8

7Relative density (p. 103)ആപേക്ഷികസാന്ദ്രത
RD = density of substance ÷ density of water

It tells how many times the density of water the substance’s density is. It has no unit. Kerosene 0.81, copper 8.9, mercury 13.6. – page 9

8Hydrometer & lactometer (pp. 104–105)ഹൈഡ്രോമീറ്റർ · ലാക്ടോമീറ്റർ

A hydrometer measures the relative density of liquids; its scale values increase downwards and it floats higher in denser liquids (reads 1.00 in water). A lactometer is a hydrometer for milk. – page 9

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Thinking the buoyant force grows as a fully immersed object goes deeper. It doesn’t – the volume displaced stays the same (Let us assess Q6: 30 N at both depths).
  • Thinking a heavier (denser) object of the same volume gets more buoyant force. Equal volume in the same liquid → equal buoyant force.
  • Writing a unit for relative density. It is a ratio of two densities – no unit.
  • Saying a floating object has no weight, or that the buoyant force on a floating object is greater than its weight. It is equal; only the spring balance reads 0.
  • Confusing “weight of displaced water” with “volume of displaced water”. They are linked by density: mass = density × volume, weight = mass × g.
  • Forgetting to convert kgwt ↔ N (1 kgwt = 9.8 N) or g → kg (100 g = 0.1 kg).
  • Saying a hydrometer sinks deeper in a denser liquid. It floats higher.
  • Thinking the buoyant force exists only in water. It acts in every fluid, including air.
