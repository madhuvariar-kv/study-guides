📖 What you need (pages 1–9 of this guide)
  • Weight ഭാരം W = mg, with g = 9.8 m/s². 1 kgwt = 9.8 N (page 1).
  • Buoyant force പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം = upward force of a fluid ദ്രവം on an object in it. Measured as the apparent loss of weight ഭാരക്കുറവ്: FB = W₁ − W₂ (page 5).
  • FB grows with the density of the liquid സാന്ദ്രത and the volume under the liquid വ്യാപ്തം: FB = ρliquid × Vunder × g (page 6).
  • Archimedes' principle ആർക്കിമിഡീസ് തത്വം: FB = weight of the fluid displaced. Floatation പ്ലവനതത്വം: a floating object displaces its own weight of fluid (page 7, page 8).
  • Relative density ആപേക്ഷികസാന്ദ്രത = density of the substance ÷ density of water (1000 kg/m³) – no unit (page 9).

Activity. സുതാര്യമായ ഒരു ബക്കറ്റിൽ മുക്കാൽ ഭാഗത്തോളം ജലം എടുക്കുക. വായ്ഭാഗം അടച്ച ഒഴിഞ്ഞ ഒരു പ്ലാസ്റ്റിക് കുപ്പി ജലോപരിതലത്തിൽ വയ്ക്കുക. എന്താണ് നിരീക്ഷിക്കുന്നത്? ബക്കറ്റിലെ ജലത്തിനടിയിലേക്ക് കുപ്പി കൈകൊണ്ട് താഴ്ത്താൻ ശ്രമിക്കുക.   • ഈ കുപ്പി ജലത്തിൽ പൂർണ്ണമായി താഴ്ത്തിയ ശേഷം കുപ്പിയിൽ നിന്ന് കൈ എടുക്കുക. എന്ത് നിരീക്ഷിക്കുന്നു? Push the bottle fully under the water and then take your hand away. What do you observe?
  • പ്ലാസ്റ്റിക് കുപ്പി ജലോപരിതലത്തിലേക്ക് ഉയർന്നുവരാനുള്ള കാരണം എന്തായിരിക്കും? What could be the reason for the plastic bottle rising to the water surface?
  • ഭൂഗുരുത്വാകർഷണ ബലം ഈ കുപ്പിയിൽ അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്നുണ്ടോ? ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ ഏത് ദിശയിൽ? Does the force of gravity act on this bottle? If so, in which direction?
  • എന്തുകൊണ്ടാണ് കുപ്പി ജലത്തിൽ മുങ്ങിക്കിടക്കാത്തത്? Why does the bottle not stay under the water?

Push the bottle down in the lab below and watch the two arrows. The red arrow (its weight) never changes. What happens to the blue arrow as more of the bottle goes under? Then press “Let go”.

🧪 Try it – push the bottle down, then let go

A 1-litre bottle of mass 30 g (approx.): weight 0.29 N. Arrows are drawn to the same scale (the weight arrow is so short that it is drawn a little longer so you can see it).

• When we take our hand away, the bottle shoots up to the surface and floats again.
• It rises because the water pushes it upwards with a force – the buoyant force പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം. Under water this upward push (about 9.8 N for a 1-litre bottle) is much bigger than the bottle's weight (about 0.3 N).
• Yes, gravity acts on the bottle all the time – vertically downwards. That pull is the bottle's weight ഭാരം.
• It does not stay under water because the upward force of the water is greater than its weight. The net force is upward, so it rises until only a small part is under water – there the upward force just equals the weight and it floats.
⚠️ Common trap

“Under water the bottle has no weight.” Wrong – gravity never switches off. The bottle rises because a second force (the buoyant force) acts on it, upward, and wins.

2 · The bucket from the well and the mug (p 96, Fig 5.2)

കിണറ്റിൽ നിന്ന് ജലം ഉയർത്തുമ്പോൾ ബക്കറ്റ് ജലോപരിതലത്തിൽ എത്തുന്നത് വരെ ഭാരക്കുറവ് അനുഭവപ്പെടാറുണ്ട്. എന്തായിരിക്കും ഈ ഭാരക്കുറവിന് കാരണം? When we draw water from a well, the bucket feels lighter until it reaches the water surface. What could be the reason for this loss of weight?

Activity (Fig 5.2). Take a bucket three-quarters full of water and lift a mug full of water from the bottom of the bucket up to the surface and out of it. ബക്കറ്റിലെ ജലത്തിനുള്ളിലും ജലനിരപ്പിന് മുകളിലും കപ്പിന്റെ ഭാരത്തിൽ എന്ത് വ്യത്യാസമാണ് അനുഭവപ്പെട്ടത്? എന്തായിരിക്കും കാരണം? What difference did you feel in the weight of the mug inside the water and above the water level? What could be the reason?

The felt force is what your hand (or the rope) has to supply: weight minus the upward push of the water. Slide the object up and watch the graph jump as it leaves the water.

⚠ Never lean over a well or play near deep water; do water experiments in a bucket or tub, with an adult nearby.
🧪 Try it – lift it up through the surface

Sample values (approx.): plastic bucket 1 kg + 10 L water; plastic mug 150 g + 1 L water; plastic density 1200 kg/m³. Felt force = W − FB.

Bucket from the well: while the bucket is in the water, the water pushes it upwards (buoyant force), so the rope only has to supply weight − buoyant force – the bucket feels light. As soon as it comes out of the water this upward support is gone and we have to hold up its full weight, so it suddenly feels heavy.
The mug (Fig 5.2): the mug feels lighter inside the water and heavier above the water level. Reason: a liquid exerts an upward force on any object placed in it. Inside the water the mug of water weighs almost nothing (the water in it is supported by the water around it); in air we feel all of it.
💡 Why this matters

That is why the last metre of pulling a bucket out of a well is the hardest. In the graph the felt force for a 10-litre bucket jumps from about 1.6 N to about 108 N – about 66 times – just by leaving the water.

3 · The helium balloon – do gases push up too? (p 96)

ദ്രാവകങ്ങളിൽ മാത്രമാണോ ഈ പ്രത്യേകത അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്നത്? … അന്തരീക്ഷവായുവിൽ സ്വതന്ത്രമാക്കുന്ന ഹീലിയം നിറച്ച ബലൂണിന് എന്ത് സംഭവിക്കും? Is this property seen only in liquids? … What will happen to a helium-filled balloon released in the air?

A balloon in air is just like the bottle in water: there are two forces on it. Which is bigger here?

🎬 Solution animation – a helium balloon let go
The balloon rises up. The helium balloon has weight, but the air pushes it upward with a force greater than its weight, so it rises. So objects in gases വാതകങ്ങൾ also feel an upward force, not only objects in liquids ദ്രാവകങ്ങൾ. Liquids and gases together are called fluids ദ്രവങ്ങൾ.
Two forces act on every object in a fluid: its weight (downward) and the upward force of the fluid. Buoyant force = the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object fully or partly immersed in it.

7 · Egg, ship and the Dead Sea (p 99, science diary)

താഴെക്കൊടുത്ത സന്ദർഭങ്ങൾക്കുള്ള വിശദീകരണം കണ്ടെത്തി സയൻസ് ഡയറിയിൽ രേഖപ്പെടുത്തുക. Find the explanation for the following situations and write it in your science diary.

  • ശുദ്ധജലത്തിൽ കോഴിമുട്ട താഴ്ന്നു പോകുമെങ്കിലും പൂരിത ഉപ്പുലായനിയിൽ പൊങ്ങിക്കിടക്കുന്നു. A hen's egg sinks in pure water but floats in saturated salt solution.
  • കപ്പൽ ശുദ്ധജലതടാകത്തിൽ നിന്ന് കടലിലേക്ക് കടക്കുമ്പോൾ കൂടുതൽ ഉയരുന്നു. A ship rises higher when it moves from a fresh-water lake into the sea.
  • ചാവുകടലിൽ ആരും തന്നെ പൂർണ്ണമായും മുങ്ങിപ്പോകുന്നില്ല. Nobody sinks completely in the Dead Sea ചാവുകടൽ.

In all three the object stays the same – only the liquid changes. A denser liquid gives more buoyant force for the same volume under it.

🎬 Solution animation – egg, ship, Dead Sea
Egg: salt solution is denser than pure water. In pure water the buoyant force on the fully immersed egg is less than its weight, so it sinks. In the denser salt solution the buoyant force is larger and can become equal to the egg's weight, so it floats.
Ship: sea water is denser than fresh water. A floating ship always needs a buoyant force equal to its own weight. In denser sea water a smaller volume under water already displaces that much weight of water, so the ship sits higher.
Dead Sea: its water is extremely salty and so much denser than ordinary water. Its buoyant force equals a person's weight when only part of the body is under water, so nobody sinks completely.
🔎 A note on the numbers

A fresh egg is a little denser than water (about 1030 kg/m³, approx.). Table 5.3 gives 1025 kg/m³ for saturated salt solution, but salt water made with plenty of salt in the kitchen is denser still (around 1200 kg/m³, approx.) – that is why the egg floats in it so easily. The Dead Sea is about 1240 kg/m³ (approx.). In the exam, just compare: the denser the liquid, the larger the buoyant force.

• So No – objects of the same weight do not feel the same buoyant force. For a fully immersed object, the buoyant force depends on (1) the density of the fluid and (2) the volume of the object – not on its weight.

വസ്തുക്കൾ ദ്രാവകങ്ങളിൽ താഴ്ത്തുമ്പോൾ ദ്രാവക നിരപ്പ് ഉയരുന്നുണ്ടല്ലോ. ഇങ്ങനെ ഉയരുന്ന ദ്രാവകത്തിന്റെ അളവും പ്ലവക്ഷമബലവും തമ്മിൽ ബന്ധമുണ്ടാകുമോ? “When objects are lowered into liquids the level rises. Is the amount of liquid that rises related to the buoyant force?” Yes – the bigger iron block pushed more water aside and felt more buoyant force. Exactly how they are related is the next experiment (section 9): the buoyant force equals the weight of the liquid pushed aside.

⚠️ Common trap

“The buoyant force on a floating object is bigger than its weight.” No – once it is floating at rest, the two forces are equal. (It is bigger only while the object is held under water, like the bottle in section 1 – and then the object rises until they become equal.)

11 · The aluminium-foil boat (pp 102–103, Fig 5.7)

ഏകദേശം 15 cm നീളവും 10 cm വീതിയും ഉള്ള ഒരു അലുമിനിയം ഫോയിൽ ബോട്ടിന്റെ ആകൃതിയിലാക്കി ജലോപരിതലത്തിൽ വച്ചുനോക്കൂ. എന്താണ് നിരീക്ഷിക്കാൻ കഴിയുന്നത്? ബോട്ടിനുള്ളിൽ 100 g മാസുള്ള ഒരു തൂക്കക്കട്ടി വയ്ക്കുക. ബോട്ട് മുങ്ങാത്തത് എന്തുകൊണ്ടായിരിക്കും? Fold a piece of aluminium foil about 15 cm long and 10 cm wide into the shape of a boat and place it on the water. What do you observe? Put a 100 g weight inside the boat. Why does the boat not sink?

ഇതേ അലുമിനിയം ഫോയിൽ നന്നായി ചുരുട്ടിയ ശേഷം ജലോപരിതലത്തിൽ വച്ചു നോക്കിയാലോ? What if the same foil is crumpled up tightly and placed on the water?

The foil is the same in both shapes – same mass, same weight. What changes is how much water it can push aside.

🧪 Try it – boat or ball?

Sample values (approx.): kitchen foil 15 cm × 10 cm weighs about 0.65 g; folded with 2 cm sides it makes a boat 11 cm × 6 cm; crumpled tightly, the solid aluminium is only 0.24 cm³.

• The foil boat floats. Even with the 100 g weight inside it still floats – it just sinks a little deeper.
• As a boat, the foil displaces a weight of water equal to its own weight; with the weight inside, it sinks deeper until it displaces a weight of water equal to the total weight of the boat and the 100 g weight (about 100 cm³ of water). Its hollow shape can push aside much more water than that (up to about 130 cm³), so it does not sink.
• Crumpled tightly into a ball, the same foil sinks. Now it displaces only a tiny volume of water (about 0.24 cm³) – the weight of that water is less than the weight of the foil, so the buoyant force is less than the weight.
• The volume of water displaced by the boat is much more than by the crumpled ball – that is why the boat floats.

12 · An iron nail sinks, an iron ship floats (pp 95, 103)

ഇരുമ്പാണി ജലത്തിൽ താഴ്ന്നുപോകുമെങ്കിലും ഇരുമ്പ് കൊണ്ടുണ്ടാക്കിയ കപ്പൽ സമുദ്രജലത്തിൽ പൊങ്ങിക്കിടക്കുന്നതിന് കാരണം വിശദീകരിക്കാമല്ലോ. An iron nail sinks in water, but a ship made of iron floats on the sea. Explain the reason.

This is the foil boat again, but huge. Compare the volume of water the solid nail can push aside with the volume a hollow hull pushes aside.

🎬 Solution animation – same iron, different shapes
The nail is solid iron. The volume of water it displaces is tiny (equal to its own small volume), and since iron is about 7.8 times as dense as water, the weight of that water is far less than the nail's weight – the buoyant force is less than the weight, so it sinks.
A ship is made hollow, with a huge hull full of air. Its hull pushes aside an enormous volume of water. It sinks only until the weight of the sea water displaced equals the ship's own weight (principle of floatation) – long before the water reaches the deck – so it floats. That is how INS Vikrant, built at Cochin Shipyard, floats.
💡 Why this matters

Ships are designed so that a large hollow volume sits below the water line. If water gets into the hull, that space no longer displaces water – that is why a leak can sink a ship, and why ships have watertight compartments.

13 · A boat displaces 6000 kg of water (p 103)

തടാകത്തിൽ സ്ഥിതിചെയ്യുന്ന ഒരു ബോട്ട് 6000 kg ജലം ആദേശം ചെയ്യുന്നു. ബോട്ടിൽ അനുഭവപ്പെടുന്ന പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം എത്രയായിരിക്കും? A boat on a lake displaces 6000 kg of water. What will be the buoyant force on the boat?

Archimedes: buoyant force = weight of the water displaced. You are given its mass – multiply by g.

🎬 Solution in exam layout – Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer
🧪 Try other numbers
Given: mass of water displaced m = 6000 kg; g = 9.8 m/s².
Formula: buoyant force = weight of displaced water = mg.
Substitution: FB = 6000 kg × 9.8 m/s².
Answer: FB = 58 800 N (= 6000 kgwt). Since the boat floats, its own weight is also 6000 kgwt = 58 800 N.

Writing “6000 kg” as the buoyant force. Kilogram is a unit of mass; a force is in newtons (or kgwt). Either write 6000 kgwt or convert: 6000 × 9.8 = 58 800 N.

14 · 0.45 kgwt in air, 0.31 kgwt in water (p 103)

വായുവിൽ 0.45 kgwt ഭാരമുള്ള ഒരു വസ്തു മുഴുവനായും ജലത്തിൽ മുങ്ങിയിരിക്കുമ്പോഴുള്ള ഭാരം 0.31 kgwt ആണ്. എങ്കിൽ താഴെ കൊടുത്തവ കണ്ടെത്തുക. a) വസ്തുവിനുണ്ടാകുന്ന ഭാരനഷ്ടം b) പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം c) ആദേശം ചെയ്ത ജലത്തിന്റെ ഭാരം An object weighs 0.45 kgwt in air and 0.31 kgwt when fully immersed in water. Find: (a) the loss of weight ഭാരനഷ്ടം of the object, (b) the buoyant force, (c) the weight of the water displaced.

(a) W₁ − W₂. (b) The buoyant force equals the loss of weight. (c) Archimedes: it also equals the weight of the displaced water. All three are the same number!

🎬 Solution in exam layout – Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer
🧪 Try other numbers
Given: W₁ = 0.45 kgwt, W₂ = 0.31 kgwt.
Formula: loss of weight = W₁ − W₂; buoyant force = loss of weight; weight of displaced water = buoyant force (Archimedes).
Substitution: 0.45 kgwt − 0.31 kgwt = 0.14 kgwt.

16 · Relative density – kerosene, copper and mercury (pp 103–104)

Relative density ആപേക്ഷികസാന്ദ്രത = density of the substance ÷ density of water. It tells how many times the density of water the substance's density is. It has no unit.

Worked example in the book. ജലത്തിന്റെ സാന്ദ്രത 1000 kg/m³ ഉം മണ്ണെണ്ണയുടെ സാന്ദ്രത 810 kg/m³ ഉം ആണ് എങ്കിൽ മണ്ണെണ്ണയുടെ ആപേക്ഷികസാന്ദ്രത? The density of water is 1000 kg/m³ and that of kerosene is 810 kg/m³. Find the relative density of kerosene.

🎬 Exam layout – relative density of kerosene
Relative density of kerosene = 810 kg/m³ ÷ 1000 kg/m³ = 0.810 (no unit) – kerosene's density is 0.81 times that of water.

ചെമ്പിന്റെ സാന്ദ്രത 8900 kg/m³ ആണെങ്കിൽ ആപേക്ഷിക സാന്ദ്രത എത്രയായിരിക്കും? If the density of copper is 8900 kg/m³, what will be its relative density?

Divide by the density of water, 1000 kg/m³. The units cancel.

🎬 Solution in exam layout – copper
Given: density of copper = 8900 kg/m³; density of water = 1000 kg/m³.
Formula: relative density = density of the substance ÷ density of water.
Substitution: = 8900 kg/m³ ÷ 1000 kg/m³.
Answer: relative density of copper = 8.9 (no unit) – copper is 8.9 times as dense as water.

മെർക്കുറിയുടെ ആപേക്ഷികസാന്ദ്രത 13.6 ആണെങ്കിൽ അതിന്റെ സാന്ദ്രത എത്രയായിരിക്കും? If the relative density of mercury is 13.6, what will be its density?

Turn the formula round: density of the substance = relative density × density of water.

🎬 Solution in exam layout – mercury
Given: relative density of mercury = 13.6; density of water = 1000 kg/m³.
Formula: relative density = density of mercury ÷ density of water ⇒ density of mercury = relative density × density of water.
Substitution: = 13.6 × 1000 kg/m³.
Answer: density of mercury = 13 600 kg/m³.
🧪 Try other numbers – density ↔ relative density
⚠️ Common trap

Writing “8.9 kg/m³”. Relative density is density ÷ density – the units cancel, so it has no unit. And density has a unit: 13 600 kg/m³, not just 13 600.

17 · The hydrometer (p 104, Fig 5.8)

Find the relative density of different liquids with a hydrometer ഹൈഡ്രോമീറ്റർ and answer:

  • ജലത്തിൽ ഹൈഡ്രോമീറ്റർ കാണിക്കുന്ന അങ്കനം എത്രയായിരിക്കും? What reading will the hydrometer show in water?
  • ജലത്തെക്കാൾ സാന്ദ്രതയുള്ള ദ്രാവകത്തിൽ ഹൈഡ്രോമീറ്റർ സൂചിപ്പിക്കുന്ന അങ്കനം, 1 എന്ന അങ്കനത്തിന് മുകളിൽ ആയിരിക്കുമോ? താഴെ ആയിരിക്കുമോ? In a liquid denser than water, will the hydrometer reading be above the mark 1 or below it?

A hydrometer floats, so it always displaces its own weight of liquid. In a denser liquid, does it need more or less volume under the surface?

🧪 Try it – float the hydrometer
• In water the hydrometer reads 1.00 (the relative density of water is 1).
• In a liquid denser than water the reading is more than 1 (for example about 1.03 in sea water). The hydrometer floats higher – it needs less volume under the surface to displace its own weight – so the liquid surface meets the stem at a mark below the 1.00 mark on the stem, where the values are bigger.
• The scale values increase downwards (Fig 5.8 b: 0.50 at the top … 1.00 lower down). The higher the hydrometer floats, the greater the relative density of the liquid.
⚠️ Common trap

“A hydrometer sinks deeper in a denser liquid.” It is the opposite – denser liquid, bigger buoyant force for each cm³ under, so it floats higher.

18 · Water in milk? The lactometer (pp 104–105, Fig 5.9)

പാലിൽ വെള്ളം ചേർത്ത് വിൽക്കുന്നത് കുറ്റകരമാണെന്ന് അറിയാമല്ലോ? പാലിൽ വെള്ളം ചേർന്നിട്ടുണ്ടോ എന്ന് എങ്ങനെ മനസ്സിലാക്കും? അതിനുപയോഗിക്കുന്ന ഉപകരണം ഏതാണ്? You know that adding water to milk and selling it is an offence. How can we find out whether water has been added to milk? Which instrument is used for it?

  • ശുദ്ധമായ പാലിലും ജലം ചേർത്ത പാലിലും ലാക്ടോമീറ്റർ കാണിക്കുന്ന റീഡിങ് തുല്യമായിരിക്കുമോ? എന്തുകൊണ്ട്? Will the lactometer ലാക്ടോമീറ്റർ show the same reading in pure milk and in milk with water added? Why?
  • ശുദ്ധമായ പാലിലാണോ കൊഴുപ്പു മാറ്റിയ പാലിലാണോ ലാക്ടോമീറ്റർ കൂടുതലായി താഴുന്നത്? എന്തായിരിക്കും കാരണം? Does the lactometer sink more in pure milk or in skimmed (fat-removed) milk? What could be the reason?
  • പാലിൽ വെള്ളം ചേർന്നിട്ടുണ്ടോ എന്ന് ലാക്ടോമീറ്റർ ഉപയോഗിച്ച് എങ്ങനെ തിരിച്ചറിയും? How can we identify, using a lactometer, whether water has been added to milk?

Pure milk is a little denser than water (relative density about 1.028–1.032, approx.). Water (1.000) lowers the mixture's density. Milk fat is lighter than water (about 0.9) – so taking the fat out does what to the density?

🧪 Try it – the lactometer at the milk society

Sample values (approx.): pure milk 1.030, skimmed milk 1.035; the mixture's relative density is worked out by volume.

• Water in milk is found by measuring the relative density of the milk with a lactometer (a hydrometer made for milk).
• No, the readings are not the same. Watered milk is less dense than pure milk (water has RD 1), so the lactometer sinks deeper and shows a lower reading.
• It sinks more in pure (whole) milk. Fat is lighter than water (RD about 0.9), so when the fat is removed the milk becomes denser; in the denser skimmed milk the lactometer floats higher (reading about 1.035).
• If the lactometer reading is below the value for pure milk (about 1.028–1.032), water has been added.
⚠️ Why the lactometer alone is not enough

Try it in the lab: skimmed milk (1.035) with about 20 % water reads 1.028 – just like pure milk! A cheat who removes fat and adds water can fool a lactometer. So milk societies also measure the fat %.

Ideas: a lactometer in two glasses of milk (“pure – 1.030 ✔, watered – 1.024 ✘”); “Test before you trust”; the food-safety helpline of your district; simple home tests (a drop of pure milk on a slanted surface flows slowly and leaves a white trail).
