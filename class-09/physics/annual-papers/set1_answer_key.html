1.
The speed of light in a transparent medium is 1.5 × 108 m/s. Its refractive index is (speed of light in vacuum c = 3 × 108 m/s)
(a) 0.5(b) 1.5(c) 2(d) 4.5
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Refractive index (അപവർത്തനാങ്കം) n = c/v.
  2. n = (3 × 108) ÷ (1.5 × 108) = 3 ÷ 1.5 = 2. It is a ratio, so it has no unit.
✅ Answer: (c) 2

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

2.
A car moves along a straight, level road at a steady speed of 60 km/h. The resultant force on the car is
(a) in the direction of motion(b) opposite to the direction of motion(c) equal to the weight of the car(d) zero
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. The car moves in a straight line with uniform velocity – its speed and direction do not change.
  2. By Newton’s first law, an unbalanced external force is needed only to change the state of motion. So the forces on the car are balanced and the resultant force is zero.
✅ Answer: (d) zero

Marking: 1 mark for (d).

3.
Pick the odd one out and give the reason:
(i) dust comes out when a hanging carpet is beaten   (ii) passengers fall backwards when a bus starts suddenly   (iii) an athlete cannot stop at once at the finishing line   (iv) a coin on a card falls into the glass when the card is flicked away
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. In (i), (ii) and (iv) a body at rest tends to stay at rest: inertia of rest (നിശ്ചലജഡത്വം).
  2. In (iii) the running athlete tends to keep moving: inertia of motion (ചലനജഡത്വം). So (iii) is the odd one.
✅ Answer: (iii) – it is due to inertia of motion; the others are due to inertia of rest

4.
A girl of mass 40 kg and her father of mass 80 kg stand 2 m apart. Which statement about the gravitational force between them is correct?
(a) The father pulls the girl with a larger force(b) They pull each other with forces of equal size(c) The girl pulls the father with a larger force(d) Only the father pulls the girl
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. The force F = G m1m2/d2 contains both masses in the same way, so there is only one value of F for the pair.
  2. The father pulls the girl with F and the girl pulls the father with the same F, in the opposite direction (as in Newton’s third law).
✅ Answer: (b) They pull each other with forces of equal size.

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

6.
A wooden block and an iron block of the same volume are held fully under water. The buoyant force is
(a) greater on the iron block(b) greater on the wooden block(c) the same on both(d) zero on the iron block
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Both blocks are fully immersed and have the same volume, so they displace the same volume (and the same weight) of water.
  2. By Archimedes’ principle (ആർക്കിമിഡീസ് തത്വം) the buoyant force equals the weight of water displaced, so it is the same on both. The material of the block does not matter.
✅ Answer: (c) the same on both

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

7.
A box slides along a rough floor and slowly comes to rest. The work done on the box by the force of friction is . (positive / negative / zero)
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Friction acts opposite to the direction in which the box moves.
  2. When the displacement is opposite to the force, the work done is negative (നെഗറ്റീവ് പ്രവൃത്തി).
✅ Answer: negative

8.
A 20 kg box is raised to a platform 2 m high, once by lifting it straight up and once by pushing it up a smooth ramp 5 m long. The work done against gravity (g = 10 m/s²) is
(a) 400 J when lifted, 1000 J along the ramp(b) 400 J in both cases(c) 1000 J in both cases(d) 200 J in both cases

  2. It depends only on the height raised (2 m), not on the path, so it is 400 J both ways. (Not 5 m – the ramp length is not the height.)

11.
The refractive index of a type of glass is 1.6. (a) Find the speed of light in this glass. (Speed of light in vacuum c = 3 × 108 m/s) (b) The refractive index of water is 1.33. In which of the two, this glass or water, does light travel faster?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: n = 1.6, c = 3 × 108 m/s
  2. Formula: n = c/v, so v = c/n
  3. Substitution: v = (3 × 108) ÷ 1.6
  4. Answer: v = 1.875 × 108 m/s
  5. (b) The smaller the refractive index, the greater the speed of light (n = c/v). Water (1.33) has a smaller refractive index than this glass (1.6), so light travels faster in water.
✅ Answer: (a) 1.875 × 108 m/s   (b) in water

Marking: (a) formula ½, substitution ½, answer with unit ½; (b) ½.

12.
The velocity–time graphs of three vehicles P, Q and R are shown. (a) Which vehicle moves with uniform velocity? (b) Which one has uniform acceleration? (c) Which one has uniform retardation? (d) What does the area under a velocity–time graph give?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  4. (d) The area under a velocity–time graph (പ്രവേഗ-സമയ ഗ്രാഫ്) gives the displacement in that time interval.
✅ Answer: (a) P   (b) Q   (c) R   (d) the displacement

13.
Appu pushes a heavy box towards the east with a force of 120 N. At the same time Binu and Chinnu push it towards the west with forces of 50 N and 40 N (figure). Ignore friction. (a) Find the resultant force on the box and its direction. (b) Are the forces on the box balanced or unbalanced? What happens to the box?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: 120 N east; 50 N and 40 N west. Take east as positive, so the westward forces are −50 N and −40 N.
  2. Formula: resultant force (സഫലബലം) = sum of the forces with their signs
  3. Substitution: 120 + (−50) + (−40) = 120 − 90
  4. Answer: resultant = +30 N, i.e. 30 N towards the east.
  5. (b) The resultant is not zero, so the forces are unbalanced. The box starts to move (accelerate) towards the east, the direction of the resultant force.
✅ Answer: (a) 30 N towards the east   (b) unbalanced; the box starts moving towards the east

Marking: (a) working ½, 30 N with direction ½; (b) unbalanced ½, box moves east ½.

14.
Two lead spheres of masses 100 kg and 200 kg are placed with their centres 0.5 m apart. Find the gravitational force between them. (G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m²/kg²)
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m1 = 100 kg, m2 = 200 kg, d = 0.5 m, G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m²/kg²
  2. Formula: F = G m1m2/d2
  3. Substitution: F = 6.67 × 10−11 × (100 × 200) ÷ 0.52 = 6.67 × 10−11 × 20 000 ÷ 0.25 = 6.67 × 10−11 × 80 000
  4. = 6.67 × 10−11 × 8 × 104 = 53.36 × 10−7
  5. Answer: F = 5.336 × 10−6 N (a very small force).
✅ Answer: F = 5.336 × 10−6 N

15.
An object hangs from a spring balance. The balance reads 6 N when the object is in air and 5 N when exactly half of the object is under water. (a) Find the buoyant force on the object when it is half immersed. (b) What will the balance read when the object is fully immersed in water? Give a reason.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: weight in air W1 = 6 N; reading when half immersed W2 = 5 N
  2. Formula: buoyant force (പ്ലവക്ഷമബലം) = apparent loss of weight = W1 − W2
  3. Substitution: 6 − 5
  4. Answer: (a) buoyant force = 1 N
  5. (b) When fully immersed, the object displaces twice the volume of water it displaced when half immersed. By Archimedes’ principle the buoyant force also doubles: 2 × 1 = 2 N.
  6. Reading = 6 − 2 = 4 N.
✅ Answer: (a) 1 N   (b) 4 N

Marking: (a) ½ for W₁ − W₂, ½ for 1 N; (b) reason (twice the volume displaced) ½, 4 N ½.

Four 1.5 V cells are joined in series and connected to a resistor. An ammeter in the circuit reads 0.3 A. (a) What is the emf of this battery? (b) Find the resistance of the resistor. (Take the whole emf to be across the resistor.)

19.
The velocity of a car on a straight stretch of the National Highway was noted every 2 seconds:
Time t (s)0246810
Velocity v (m/s)4812161616
(a) Draw the velocity–time graph (X-axis: 1 cm = 2 s; Y-axis: 1 cm = 4 m/s). (b) Find the acceleration of the car during the first 6 s. (c) Find the total displacement of the car in the 10 s.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Take time on the X-axis and velocity on the Y-axis with the given scales. Plot the six points and join them: a sloping straight line from (0 s, 4 m/s) to (6 s, 16 m/s), then a horizontal line at 16 m/s up to 10 s.

  6. (c) Displacement = area under the graph. From 0 to 6 s (trapezium): ½ × (4 + 16) × 6 = 60 m. (Check: s = ut + ½at² = 4 × 6 + ½ × 2 × 6² = 24 + 36 = 60 m.)
  7. From 6 s to 10 s (rectangle): 16 × 4 = 64 m.
  8. Answer: total displacement = 60 + 64 = 124 m
✅ Answer: (b) 2 m/s²   (c) 124 m

20.

(a) Up to about what height does the ball rise on the other side? (b) What happens to the distance the ball rolls on the other side as the slope is reduced? 22.
At Ernakulam railway station, Rahul lifts his 12 kg suitcase straight up from the platform to a height of 1.5 m. Then, holding it at that height, he walks 8 m along the level platform. (g = 10 m/s²) (a) Find the work he does against gravity while lifting the suitcase. (b) Find the work done by the force of gravity on the suitcase during the lift. Is it positive or negative? (c) How much work does he do against gravity while walking the 8 m? Why?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m = 12 kg, h = 1.5 m, g = 10 m/s²

  6. (c) While walking, the suitcase stays at the same height: there is no displacement in the direction of his upward force. So the work done against gravity is zero.
✅ Answer: (a) 180 J   (b) −180 J (negative)   (c) zero

25.
In the physics lab, Neha places a rectangular glass slab PQRS on a sheet of paper and sends a ray of light AB from a ray box on to its face PS (figure). The speed of light in this glass is 2 × 108 m/s. (a) Find the refractive index of the glass. (c = 3 × 108 m/s) (b) Copy and complete the diagram: show the path of the ray inside the slab and after it comes out, with the normals. (c) At which face does the ray bend away from the normal? Why? (d) How is the ray that comes out related to the ray AB? What is meant by lateral shift? Mark it on your diagram.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Given: c = 3 × 108 m/s, v = 2 × 108 m/s
  2. Formula: n = c/v   Substitution: n = (3 × 108) ÷ (2 × 108)   Answer: n = 1.5 (no unit)
  3. (b) At B the ray goes from air into glass (rarer to denser), so it bends towards the normal: the angle r inside is less than 50° (about 31° for this glass). It meets the opposite face QR at C. There it goes from glass into air and bends away from the normal, coming out along CD at 50° to the normal.
  4. (c) At the face QR (point C), because the ray passes from glass to air – from an optically denser to a rarer medium.
  5. (d) The emergent ray CD is parallel to the incident ray AB but shifted sideways. The perpendicular distance between the incident ray produced (dashed) and the emergent ray is the lateral shift.
✅ Answer: (a) 1.5 (b) bends towards the normal at B, away from the normal at the second face QR (c) at QR – glass to air is denser to rarer (d) parallel to AB; the sideways (perpendicular) distance between AB produced and the emergent ray

Marking: (a) formula ½, answer ½; (b) ray bending towards the normal inside ½, emergent ray bending away and parallel to AB ½, normals drawn ½; (c) ½; (d) parallel ½, lateral shift explained and marked ½.

26.
An aeroplane at Cochin International Airport starts from rest and accelerates uniformly along the runway. It must reach a velocity of 288 km/h to take off, and it covers 2000 m of the runway before it lifts off. (a) Express the take-off velocity in m/s. (b) Find the acceleration of the aeroplane. (c) How long does it run on the runway before taking off? (d) Find its velocity and the distance it has covered 25 s after starting.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) 1 km/h = 5/18 m/s, so 288 km/h = 288 × 5/18 = 80 m/s.
  2. (b) Given: u = 0, v = 80 m/s, s = 2000 m. Formula: v² = u² + 2as. Substitution: 80² = 0 + 2 × a × 2000 → 6400 = 4000a. Answer: a = 6400 ÷ 4000 = 1.6 m/s²
  3. (c) Formula: v = u + at (ചലനസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ). Substitution: 80 = 0 + 1.6 × t → t = 80 ÷ 1.6. Answer: t = 50 s
  4. (Check: s = ut + ½at² = ½ × 1.6 × 50² = 0.8 × 2500 = 2000 m ✓)
  5. (d) Given: u = 0, a = 1.6 m/s², t = 25 s. Velocity v = u + at = 0 + 1.6 × 25 = 40 m/s.
  6. Distance s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 1.6 × 25² = 0.8 × 625 = 500 m. (In half the time it covers only a quarter of the 2000 m, because s grows with t².)
✅ Answer: (a) 80 m/s (b) 1.6 m/s² (c) 50 s (d) 40 m/s; 500 m

Marking: (a) 1; (b) formula ½, answer with unit ½; (c) ½ substitution, ½ answer; (d) velocity ½, distance ½.

27.
In the school lab, Fathima hangs a brass block from a spring balance. In air the balance reads 3.92 N. She lowers the block completely into an overflow jar filled with water up to its spout, and collects the water that overflows in a beaker (figure). The water collected has a mass of 50 g. (g = 9.8 m/s²; density of water = 1000 kg/m³, i.e. 1 g/cm³) (a) Find the weight of the water collected. (b) Using Archimedes’ principle, find the buoyant force on the block and the reading of the spring balance now. (c) Find the volume of the block and its density. (d) She lowers the block a little deeper, still fully under water and not touching the jar. Will the reading change? Why?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Given: mass of water m = 50 g = 0.05 kg, g = 9.8 m/s². Formula: W = mg. Substitution: W = 0.05 × 9.8. Answer: 0.49 N
  2. (b) Archimedes’ principle (ആർക്കിമിഡീസ് തത്വം): buoyant force = weight of water displaced = 0.49 N.
  3. Reading = weight in air − buoyant force = 3.92 − 0.49 = 3.43 N
  4. (c) Volume of the block = volume of water displaced = 50 g ÷ 1 g/cm³ = 50 cm³ = 5 × 10−5 m³.
  5. Mass of the block = 3.92 ÷ 9.8 = 0.4 kg. Formula: density = mass/volume. Substitution: 0.4 ÷ (5 × 10−5). Answer: density = 8000 kg/m³ (8 g/cm³).
  6. (d) No. Once the block is fully immersed, going deeper does not change the volume of water it displaces, so the buoyant force (0.49 N) and the reading (3.43 N) stay the same.
✅ Answer: (a) 0.49 N (b) 0.49 N; 3.43 N (c) 50 cm³; 8000 kg/m³ (d) no – the volume of water displaced does not change

Marking: (a) ½ for W = mg, ½ for 0.49 N; (b) buoyant force ½, reading ½; (c) volume ½, density ½; (d) no ½, reason ½.

28.
Anu has three 1.5 V cells joined in series, a switch and two identical bulbs L1 and L2, each of resistance 9 Ω (take it as constant). She makes circuit (a) with the bulbs in series and circuit (b) with the bulbs in parallel (figure). (a) What is the emf of the battery? (b) For circuit (a), find the effective resistance and the current from the battery. (c) For circuit (b), find the effective resistance, the current from the battery and the current through each bulb. (d) In which circuit do the bulbs glow brighter? If L1 fuses, what happens to L2 in each circuit?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Cells in series: emf (വിദ്യുത്ചാലകബലം) = 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 V.
  2. (b) Series: R = R1 + R2 = 9 + 9 = 18 Ω (സഫലപ്രതിരോധം). I = V/R = 4.5 ÷ 18 = 0.25 A (the same current flows through both bulbs).
  3. (c) Parallel: R = R1R2/(R1 + R2) = (9 × 9) ÷ (9 + 9) = 81 ÷ 18 = 4.5 Ω. I = V/R = 4.5 ÷ 4.5 = 1 A.
  4. Each bulb gets the full 4.5 V, so the current through each = 4.5 ÷ 9 = 0.5 A (0.5 + 0.5 = 1 A ✓).
  5. (d) The bulbs glow brighter in circuit (b): each bulb gets the full 4.5 V and 0.5 A, while in series each gets only 2.25 V and 0.25 A.
  6. If L1 fuses: in (a) the only path is broken, so L2 also goes off; in (b) L2 has its own path, so it keeps glowing.
✅ Answer: (a) 4.5 V (b) 18 Ω, 0.25 A (c) 4.5 Ω, 1 A, 0.5 A each (d) circuit (b); in (a) L₂ goes off, in (b) L₂ keeps glowing

Marking: (a) ½; (b) effective resistance ½, current ½; (c) effective resistance ½, total current ½, current in each bulb ½; (d) brighter circuit ½, what happens to L₂ in both circuits ½.
