1.
A ray of light passes from glass into water. It falls on the boundary obliquely, at an angle of incidence of 30°. In the water, the ray
(a) bends towards the normal(b) bends away from the normal(c) goes straight on without bending(d) is reflected back into the glass
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Glass (refractive index 1.5) is optically denser than water (1.33).
  2. The ray goes from a denser to a rarer medium obliquely, so it bends away from the normal (ലംബത്തിൽ നിന്ന് അകലുന്നു).
✅ Answer: (b) bends away from the normal

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

2.
A ray of light passes obliquely from medium P into medium Q and bends towards the normal. Which statement is correct?
(a) Light travels faster in Q than in P.(b) P is optically denser than Q.(c) Q has a higher refractive index than P.(d) The angle of refraction is greater than the angle of incidence.
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. A ray bends towards the normal when it goes from a rarer to a denser medium. So Q is optically denser than P.
  2. Light is slower in the denser medium Q, and since n = c/v, Q has the higher refractive index.
  3. Also, r is less than i here, so (d) is wrong; (a) and (b) are the opposite of the truth.
✅ Answer: (c) Q has a higher refractive index than P

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

3.
Which of the following moves with uniform velocity?
(a) a stone dropped from a height(b) a bus leaving a bus stop(c) a child on a swing(d) light travelling through vacuum
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Uniform velocity: equal displacements in equal intervals of time, in one direction – neither the speed nor the direction changes.
  2. A falling stone speeds up, a bus leaving a stop speeds up, and a swing keeps changing its speed and direction – all non-uniform.
  3. Light in vacuum goes in a straight line at a constant speed, so its velocity is uniform.
✅ Answer: (d) light travelling through vacuum

Marking: 1 mark for (d).

4.
Pick the odd one out and give the reason:   distance, speed, displacement, time.
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Displacement (സ്ഥാനാന്തരം) has both magnitude and direction, so it is a vector quantity.
  2. Distance, speed and time have only magnitude – they are scalar quantities (അദിശ അളവുകൾ).
✅ Answer: Displacement – it is a vector quantity; the others are scalar quantities.

Marking: ½ mark for “displacement”, ½ mark for the reason.

A bullet of mass 10 g moves with a velocity of 300 m/s. A ball of mass 5 kg rolls with a velocity of 0.5 m/s. (a) Find the momentum of each. (b) Which has more momentum?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  4. Answer: bullet p = 3 kg m/s; ball p = 2.5 kg m/s
  5. (b) The bullet has more momentum. Its mass is very small, but its velocity is very large – momentum depends on both mass and velocity.
✅ Answer: (a) bullet 3 kg m/s, ball 2.5 kg m/s   (b) the bullet

14.
A diver feels weightless for a moment after she leaves the diving board, until she enters the water. (a) What is meant by free fall? (b) Why does she feel weightless during this time, even though the Earth is still pulling her?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) When a body moves only because of the Earth’s gravitational force, with no other force acting, its motion is called free fall (നിർബാധ പതനം).
  2. (b) After leaving the board, the diver moves only under gravity. She and everything with her fall with the same acceleration g.
  3. Nothing supports her and nothing presses on her body. The whole of the gravitational force is used to give her acceleration, so she feels no weight – weightlessness (ഭാരമില്ലായ്മ).
  4. Her real weight mg still acts on her – that is why she accelerates downwards.
✅ Answer: (a) Falling only because of the Earth’s gravitational force. (b) She and everything with her fall with the same acceleration g, so nothing supports or presses on her.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) same acceleration / nothing supporting her ½, whole of gravity used for acceleration ½.

15.
The relative densities of candle wax, aluminium and kerosene are 0.9, 2.7 and 0.81. (a) Which of the two solids, wax or aluminium, will float in water? Why? (b) Will a piece of wax float in kerosene? Give a reason.
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Relative density less than 1 means the substance is less dense than water; more than 1 means denser than water.
  2. When an object is fully under a liquid, the buoyant force equals the weight of the liquid it displaces (Archimedes’ principle). If the object is less dense than the liquid, this buoyant force is more than its weight, so it rises and floats. If it is denser, its weight is more, so it sinks.
  3. (a) Wax floats in water (0.9 < 1). Aluminium is 2.7 times as dense as water, so it sinks.
  4. (b) No. Wax (0.9) is denser than kerosene (0.81), so its weight is more than the buoyant force of kerosene and it sinks in kerosene.
✅ Answer: (a) Wax floats (relative density less than 1); aluminium sinks (2.7 > 1). (b) No – wax (0.9) is denser than kerosene (0.81), so it sinks.

Marking: (a) wax floats with reason ½, aluminium sinks ½; (b) sinks ½, reason ½.

16.
A girl riding a bicycle has a kinetic energy of 625 J. The mass of the girl and the bicycle together is 50 kg. (a) Find her speed. (b) What will the kinetic energy be if she doubles her speed?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: Ek = 625 J, m = 50 kg
  2. Formula: kinetic energy (ഗതികോർജം) Ek = ½mv2
  3. Substitution: 625 = ½ × 50 × v2 = 25 v2  ⇒  v2 = 625 ÷ 25 = 25
  4. Answer: (a) v = √25 = 5 m/s
  5. (b) New speed = 2 × 5 = 10 m/s. Ek = ½ × 50 × 102 = 25 × 100 = 2500 J – four times as much, because Ek depends on v2.
✅ Answer: (a) 5 m/s   (b) 2500 J

Marking: (a) formula and substitution ½, answer with unit ½; (b) 1 mark.

19.
(a) Why do stars twinkle? (b) In the figure, just before sunrise, the Sun S is still below the horizon, yet the observer sees it at S′, above the horizon. Explain why. (c) Name the phenomenon behind both (a) and (b).
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Starlight passes through many layers of the atmosphere. The temperature and pressure of these layers keep changing, so their optical density keeps changing.
  2. So the starlight is refracted irregularly and its path keeps changing. The apparent position and brightness of the star keep changing – the star twinkles.
  3. (b) Air near the Earth is denser; higher up it is rarer. Light from the Sun below the horizon enters the atmosphere and passes into denser and denser layers. At each layer it bends towards the normal, so its path curves down and reaches the observer.
  4. Our eye sees the light as coming straight from the direction in which it enters the eye. So we see the Sun at a higher, apparent position S′, above the horizon, a little before it actually rises.
  5. (c) Atmospheric refraction (അന്തരീക്ഷത്തിലെ അപവർത്തനം).
✅ Answer: (a) and (b) are both due to atmospheric refraction: starlight is refracted irregularly by changing layers of air; sunlight from below the horizon bends down towards us, so the Sun appears higher.

Marking: (a) changing optical density ½, irregular refraction ½, apparent position/brightness change ½; (b) bending towards the normal in denser layers ½, Sun seen at a higher apparent position ½; (c) ½.

20.
The position–time graphs of two cyclists A and B riding along a straight road in the same direction are shown. At t = 0, B is 30 m ahead of A. (a) What kind of velocity does each cyclist have? Give a reason. (b) Find the velocity of A and of B. (c) At what time, and at what position, does A catch up with B?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Both graphs are sloping straight lines: each cyclist has equal displacements in equal intervals of time. So both have uniform velocity (സമപ്രവേഗം).
  2. Given: A: 0 m at 0 s, 50 m at 10 s; B: 30 m at 0 s, 50 m at 10 s
  3. Formula: velocity v = (s2 − s1) ÷ (t2 − t1)
  4. Substitution: A: (50 − 0) ÷ (10 − 0);   B: (50 − 30) ÷ (10 − 0)
  5. Answer: (b) velocity of A = 5 m/s; velocity of B = 2 m/s
  6. (c) A catches B where the two graphs meet: at t = 10 s, at the 50 m position.
  7. Check: A gains 5 − 2 = 3 m on B every second, and B was 30 m ahead, so A needs 30 ÷ 3 = 10 s. Then A is at 5 × 10 = 50 m and B at 30 + 2 × 10 = 50 m ✓.
✅ Answer: (a) uniform velocity (straight-line graphs)   (b) A: 5 m/s, B: 2 m/s   (c) at t = 10 s, at the 50 m position

Marking: (a) uniform ½, reason ½; (b) ½ each; (c) time ½, position ½.

21.
A carpenter’s hammer of mass 0.8 kg hits a nail at 5 m/s and comes to rest in 0.02 s. (Take the direction of the hammer’s motion as positive.) (a) Find the change in momentum of the hammer. (b) Find the average force exerted on the hammer by the nail. What does the negative sign show? (c) If a thick rubber sheet were placed on the nail head, the hammer would stop in 0.1 s. Find the force now. Which way is better for driving the nail in? Why?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m = 0.8 kg, u = 5 m/s, v = 0, t = 0.02 s
  2. Formula: change in momentum = mv − mu;   F = (mv − mu) ÷ t
  3. Substitution: mv − mu = 0.8 × 0 − 0.8 × 5 = −4;   F = −4 ÷ 0.02
  4. Answer: (a) change in momentum = −4 kg m/s (this is also the impulse, −4 N s); (b) F = −200 N
  5. The negative sign shows that the force acts opposite to the direction of motion of the hammer – it stops the hammer. By the third law, the hammer pushes the nail forward with 200 N.
  6. (c) Same change in momentum, longer time: F = −4 ÷ 0.1 = −40 N.
  7. Hitting the nail directly is better: the hammer stops in a very short time, so the force (ആവേഗബലം, impulsive force) is large and drives the nail in. For the same change in momentum, the longer the time, the smaller the force.
✅ Answer: (a) −4 kg m/s   (b) −200 N, opposite to the hammer’s motion   (c) −40 N; hitting the nail directly is better

Marking: (a) formula ½, answer ½; (b) force ½, meaning of the sign ½; (c) force ½, conclusion with reason ½.

22.
A wooden block has a volume of 2000 cm3 and a mass of 1.2 kg. (Density of water = 1000 kg/m3; 1 m3 = 106 cm3.) (a) Find the density and the relative density of the wood. (b) The block is placed in water (figure). What volume of the block is under water when it floats? (c) The block is moved to a saturated salt solution (density 1025 kg/m3). Does it float higher or lower? Find the volume under the solution now.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: V = 2000 cm3 = 2000 ÷ 106 = 0.002 m3, m = 1.2 kg
  2. Formula: density = mass ÷ volume;   relative density = density of wood ÷ density of water
  3. Substitution: density = 1.2 ÷ 0.002;   relative density = 600 ÷ 1000
  4. Answer: (a) density = 600 kg/m3; relative density = 0.6 (no unit)
  5. (b) Its relative density is less than 1, so it floats. By the principle of floatation, the weight of the water displaced = the weight of the block, so the mass of water displaced = 1.2 kg.
  6. Volume of water displaced = mass ÷ density = 1.2 ÷ 1000 = 0.0012 m3 = 1200 cm3. This is the volume under water (the other 800 cm3 is above the water).
  7. (c) The salt solution is denser, so a smaller volume of it has the same weight as the block. The block floats higher.
  8. Volume under the solution = 1.2 ÷ 1025 = 0.001 171 m3 ≈ 1171 cm3.
✅ Answer: (a) 600 kg/m3, relative density 0.6   (b) 1200 cm3   (c) higher; about 1171 cm3

Marking: (a) density ½, relative density ½; (b) principle ½, volume ½; (c) floats higher ½, volume ½.

23.
(a) A body of mass m is at rest. A constant force F acts on it and it moves through a displacement s, reaching a velocity v. Show that the kinetic energy it gains is ½mv2. (b) What kind of energy is stored in (i) a stretched catapult (ii) a coconut hanging on a coconut tree?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Work done by the force: W = F × s.
  2. By the second law of motion, F = ma. So W = ma × s = m(as).
  3. From v2 = u2 + 2as with u = 0 (it starts from rest): v2 = 2as, so as = v2/2.
  4. So W = m × v2/2 = ½mv2. This work is stored in the body as kinetic energy: Ek = ½mv2.
  5. (b) (i) Stretched catapult: potential energy due to configuration (കോൺഫിഗറേഷൻ മൂലമുള്ള സ്ഥിതികോർജം) – its shape has been changed.
  6. (ii) Coconut on the tree: potential energy due to position (സ്ഥാനം മൂലമുള്ള സ്ഥിതികോർജം) – it is at a height.
✅ Answer: (a) W = Fs = mas and as = v2/2, so Ek = ½mv2   (b) (i) potential energy due to configuration (ii) potential energy due to position

Marking: (a) W = Fs and F = ma ½, v² = 2as ½, final result 1; (b) ½ + ½.

25.
An autorickshaw with its driver has a total mass of 400 kg. Starting from rest on a straight road, it is pushed forward by a resultant (unbalanced) force of 600 N for 10 s. (a) Find its acceleration. (b) Find its velocity after 10 s. (c) Find its momentum after 10 s and the rate of change of its momentum. Compare this rate with the force. (d) Two passengers with a total mass of 200 kg get in. If the same resultant force acts, what is the new acceleration? What do you learn from this?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m = 400 kg, F = 600 N, u = 0, t = 10 s
  2. Formula: F = ma  ⇒  a = F/m
  3. Substitution: a = 600 ÷ 400
  4. Answer: (a) a = 1.5 m/s2
  5. (b) v = u + at = 0 + 1.5 × 10 = 15 m/s.
  6. (c) Momentum p = mv = 400 × 15 = 6000 kg m/s. Change in momentum = 6000 − 0 = 6000 kg m/s. Rate of change = 6000 ÷ 10 = 600 kg m/s2.
  7. This is equal to the force, 600 N – the rate of change of momentum equals the resultant force (Newton’s second law).
  8. (d) New mass = 400 + 200 = 600 kg. a = F/m = 600 ÷ 600 = 1 m/s2.
  9. For the same force, a bigger mass gets a smaller acceleration (a = F/m) – the heavier autorickshaw speeds up more slowly.
✅ Answer: (a) 1.5 m/s²   (b) 15 m/s   (c) 6000 kg m/s; 600 kg m/s², equal to the force   (d) 1 m/s²

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark; (c) momentum ½, rate and comparison ½; (d) acceleration ½, conclusion ½.

26.
Chandrayaan-3 placed a rover on the Moon. Suppose a small rover has a mass of 30 kg. (g on the Earth = 9.8 m/s2; g on the Moon = 1.62 m/s2) (a) Find its weight on the Earth. (b) What are its mass and its weight on the Moon? (c) A small tool is dropped from a height of 3.24 m above the Moon’s surface and falls freely. How long does it take to reach the surface? (d) An astronaut on the Moon drops a feather and a hammer together from the same height. Which reaches the ground first? Why?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m = 30 kg, g (Earth) = 9.8 m/s², g (Moon) = 1.62 m/s²
  2. Formula: weight W = mg
  3. Substitution: Earth: W = 30 × 9.8;   Moon: W = 30 × 1.62
  4. Answer: (a) weight on the Earth = 294 N (30 kgwt). (b) On the Moon the mass is still 30 kg (മാസ് does not change); weight = 48.6 N – about 1/6 of its weight on the Earth.
  5. (c) Given: u = 0, s = 3.24 m, a = g = 1.62 m/s2. Formula: s = ut + ½gt2 ⇒ t2 = 2s/g. Substitution: t2 = 2 × 3.24 ÷ 1.62 = 4.
  6. Answer: (c) t = √4 = 2 s
  7. (d) They reach the ground together. The Moon has no air, so there is no air resistance to slow the feather. The acceleration g = GM/R2 does not depend on the mass of the falling body, so both fall with the same acceleration.
✅ Answer: (a) 294 N   (b) mass 30 kg, weight 48.6 N   (c) 2 s   (d) both together – no air, and g is the same for all bodies

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) mass ½, weight ½; (c) formula ½, answer ½; (d) together ½, reason ½.

27.
At an Onam celebration, a child sits on a swing. The total mass of the child and the seat is 25 kg. At the highest point A the seat is 0.8 m above its lowest point B (figure). Ignore air resistance; g = 10 m/s2. (a) Find the potential energy (taking B as the zero level) and the kinetic energy at A, where the swing stops for an instant. (b) Find the kinetic energy and the speed at B. (c) At what height above B will the speed of the seat be 2 m/s? (d) If nobody pushes, the swing slowly comes to rest. Does this mean that energy is destroyed? Explain.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: m = 25 kg, h = 0.8 m, g = 10 m/s²
  2. Formula: Ep = mgh;   Ek = ½mv2
  3. Substitution: Ep at A = 25 × 10 × 0.8
  4. Answer: (a) Ep = 200 J; the swing is at rest for an instant, so Ek = 0 J. Total energy = 200 J.
  5. (b) At B, Ep = 0. By the law of conservation of energy (ഊർജസംരക്ഷണ നിയമം), Ek = 200 J. ½ × 25 × v2 = 200 ⇒ v2 = 400 ÷ 25 = 16 ⇒ v = 4 m/s.
  6. (c) At 2 m/s: Ek = ½ × 25 × 22 = 50 J. So Ep = 200 − 50 = 150 J. h = Ep ÷ mg = 150 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.6 m.
  7. (d) No. Air resistance and friction at the hooks slowly change the mechanical energy into heat (and a little sound). Energy is neither created nor destroyed; it only changes from one form to another.
✅ Answer: (a) PE 200 J, KE 0   (b) KE 200 J, speed 4 m/s   (c) 0.6 m   (d) No – it changes into heat (and sound)

Marking: (a) PE ½, KE ½; (b) KE ½, speed ½; (c) 1 mark; (d) no ½, explanation ½.

28.
On a monsoon evening, lightning strikes a coconut tree 1388 m away from Meena. (Speed of sound in air = 347 m/s; speed of light = 3 × 108 m/s.) (a) After how many seconds does she hear the thunder? (b) She sees the flash almost at once. Why? (c) On a hotter day, would the thunder from the same distance reach her sooner or later? Why? (d) The thunder reaches her as compressions and rarefactions in the air. Do the air particles near the lightning travel all the way to her ear? Explain.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: distance d = 1388 m, speed of sound v = 347 m/s
  2. Formula: time = distance ÷ speed
  3. Substitution: t = 1388 ÷ 347
  4. Answer: (a) t = 4 s
  5. (b) Light travels at 3 × 108 m/s. It takes only 1388 ÷ (3 × 108) ≈ 0.000 004 6 s to reach her – practically no time. Sound in air is nearly a million times slower.
  6. (c) Sooner. The speed of sound increases when the temperature of the medium increases, so in hotter air the sound covers the same distance in less time.
  7. (d) No. Each air particle only vibrates to and fro about its place and pushes the next particles. These pressure changes – compressions (മർദം കൂടിയ മേഖല) and rarefactions (മർദം കുറഞ്ഞ മേഖല) – pass on through the air to her ear. The energy travels; the particles do not.
✅ Answer: (a) 4 s   (b) light is extremely fast (about 0.000 004 6 s for 1388 m)   (c) sooner – sound is faster in hotter air   (d) No – the particles only vibrate; the disturbance travels

Marking: (a) formula ½, answer ½; (b) 1 mark; (c) sooner ½, reason ½; (d) no ½, explanation ½.
