1. The chapter in one picture

🗺️ Equations of motion – concept map

Read it clockwise from the top left: displacement (with direction) divided by time is velocity; the rate of change of velocity is acceleration; both are pictured on graphs (slope and area); uniform acceleration gives the three equations of motion; and the same physics decides how far a vehicle needs to stop – road safety.

2. The key boxes of the textbook

1Displacement (p. 28)സ്ഥാനാന്തരം · സദിശ അളവുകൾ · അദിശ അളവുകൾ

The straight-line distance between two positions, stated with a definite direction. Symbol s, unit metre (m). Vector quantities have magnitude and direction; scalar quantities have only magnitude. – page 5

2When are they equal? (p. 29)ദൂരം · സ്ഥാനാന്തരം

The magnitude of displacement equals the distance travelled only when the object moves along a straight path in one direction. – page 5

3Velocity (p. 31)പ്രവേഗം
v = s/t  (m/s)

Displacement per unit time. A vector, in the direction of the displacement. Displacement does not depend on the path, but the whole time taken must be used. – page 6

4Uniform velocity (p. 33)സമപ്രവേഗം · അസമപ്രവേഗം

An object moving in one direction with equal displacements in equal intervals of time has uniform velocity. If the speed or the direction changes, the velocity is non-uniform. – page 7

5Acceleration (p. 35)ത്വരണം · പ്രവേഗമാറ്റത്തിന്റെ നിരക്ക്
a = (v − u)/t  ·  v = u + at

Change in velocity per unit time – the rate of change of velocity. A vector. Unit m/s2. – page 8

6Retardation (p. 36)മന്ദീകരണം · നെഗറ്റീവ് ത്വരണം

The rate of decrease of velocity = negative acceleration. Unit m/s2. a = −2 m/s2 means a retardation of 2 m/s2. – page 8

7Uniform acceleration (p. 37)സമത്വരണം · അസമത്വരണം

Equal changes in velocity in equal intervals of time; otherwise non-uniform acceleration (Table 2.6: 2, 2, 2 m/s2 – uniform; Table 2.7: −0.5, −1, −1, −0.25 m/s2 – non-uniform). – page 9

8Scale of a graph (p. 43)തോത്

Choose a scale that fits the data on the graph paper. Changing the scale changes the steepness of the line, but not the value of the physical quantity it shows. – page 10

9Area under a v–t graph (p. 44)പ്രവേഗ-സമയ ഗ്രാഫ് · പരപ്പളവ്

In a velocity–time graph, the displacement in a time interval equals the area under the graph for that interval. – page 10

10Equations of motion (p. 45)ചലനസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at2
v2 = u2 + 2as

Valid only for uniform acceleration. – page 11

11km/h to m/s (p. 46)
1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s = 5/18 m/s

km/h × 5/18 = m/s; m/s × 18/5 = km/h. 36 km/h = 10 m/s, 54 km/h = 15 m/s, 72 km/h = 20 m/s, 90 km/h = 25 m/s. – page 11

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Using distance instead of displacement for velocity. In Fig 2.4 the velocity is 72/18 = 4 m/s, not 90/18 = 5 m/s (that is the speed).
  • Forgetting the sign or direction of displacement and velocity. Write “40 m east” or “−40 m (east +)”, not just “40 m”.
  • Not converting km/h to m/s (× 5/18) or minutes to seconds (× 60) before using the equations.
  • Using the equations of motion when the acceleration is not uniform. They work only for uniform acceleration.
  • Writing “retardation = −2 m/s²”. Retardation is already the negative acceleration: a = −2 m/s2, retardation 2 m/s2.
  • Reading the wrong scale on a graph, or thinking that a steeper line drawn with a different scale means a bigger acceleration.
  • In a v–t graph, taking the height (velocity) instead of the area as the displacement.
  • Forgetting that circular motion at constant speed is non-uniform velocity – the direction keeps changing.
  • Train-and-bridge problems: forgetting to add the train’s own length.
