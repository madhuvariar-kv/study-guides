Units, Time and Conversions ഭൗതിക അളവ്

Every answer in this chapter is a number with a unit: 50 s, 20 m/s, 2 m/s². Many questions give speeds in km/h, times in minutes and lengths in cm. You must change these into metres and seconds before you use a formula. This page explains the units of motion, what “per second” really means, and how to change km/h into m/s and back.

📖 What you need
  • Multiplying and dividing by 10, 100, 1000 and 60.
  • Simple fractions such as 5/18 (also revised on page 4).

1. Physical quantities and their SI units

Anything that can be measured and written as a number × a unit is a physical quantity (ഭൗതിക അളവ്). Scientists all over the world use the same set of units, the SI units. For motion we need only metres and seconds, and the units built from them:

⭐ Write units the right way

Always write the unit with the number, with a space between them: 20 m/s, not “20” or “20m/s”. Don't mix up the symbol s (displacement) with the unit s (second): “s = 120 m” means a displacement of 120 metres, but “t = 120 s” means a time of 120 seconds.

2. Bigger and smaller units

Long distances are measured in kilometres (km) and short ones in centimetres (cm) or millimetres (mm). Long times are in minutes (min) or hours (h). Before using any formula of motion, change them into metres and seconds.

🧪 The conversion ladder

Going down the ladder (to a smaller unit) you multiply: there are more of the small units. Going up (to a bigger unit) you divide. The green box is the SI unit you need for the formulas.

🤔 Quick check: does the answer make sense?

A smaller unit always needs a bigger number: 2 km = 2000 m (more metres than kilometres). A bigger unit needs a smaller number: 2 cm = 0.02 m. If your conversion goes the other way, you have multiplied where you should have divided.

3. What “per second” means

“Per” means “for each” or “in every”. A speed of 20 m/s (20 metres per second) means the object covers 20 metres in every second. So in 6 seconds it covers 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 20 × 6 = 120 m. That is why

distance = speed × time    and    speed = distance ÷ time
🧪 Metres in every second: a KSRTC bus

The blue dots show where the bus was after 1 s, 2 s, 3 s … The gaps are all equal, because the bus moves the same number of metres in every second.

The unit of acceleration: m/s²

Acceleration tells how much the velocity changes in every second. If a bus's velocity goes 0 → 3 → 6 → 9 m/s, second after second, its velocity grows by 3 m/s in every second. We write this as 3 (m/s) per second = 3 m/s ÷ s = 3 m/s². The “²” appears because “per second” is used twice: metres per second, per second.

🧪 What does m/s² mean?

Each bar is the velocity at the end of a second. Each bar is taller than the one before by exactly a, the acceleration.

4. Changing km/h into m/s, and back

Speedometers and road signs in Kerala show speeds in km/h (kilometres per hour), but the formulas need m/s. Here is where the magic fraction 5/18 comes from:

1 km/h = 1 km1 h = 1000 m3600 s = 518 m/s

(1000/3600: divide the top and the bottom by 200 to get 5/18.) So:

⭐ The two conversion rules (textbook page 46)

km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18.   90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s.
m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5.   25 m/s = 25 × 18/5 = 90 km/h.
Memory aid: m/s numbers are always smaller than the km/h numbers for the same speed, because 5/18 is less than 1.

🌍 Real life in Kerala

When a Kochi Metro train cruises at 72 km/h, it covers 20 metres every second. An autorickshaw at 36 km/h covers 10 m every second. If the driver looks at a phone for just 2 seconds, the autorickshaw travels 20 m “blind”. That is longer than a zebra crossing is wide! Thinking in m/s shows why road safety rules matter.

Worked example 1 · km/h into m/s

Q. A train reaches a speed of 90 km/h. Express this speed in m/s.

km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18. Simplify 90/18 first: 90 ÷ 18 = 5.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: 90 × 5/18 = 5 × 5 = 25 m/s.

Worked example 2 · Both quantities into SI units

Q. A train starting from rest reaches 72 km/h in 5 minutes. Write its final velocity in m/s and the time in seconds.

Velocity: × 5/18. Time: 1 min = 60 s, so × 60.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: v = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s; t = 5 × 60 = 300 s.

Worked example 3 · m/s into km/h

Q. A KSRTC bus moves at 15 m/s on a straight road. What does its speedometer show, in km/h?

m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5. 15 ÷ 5 = 3 first.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: 15 × 18/5 = 3 × 18 = 54 km/h.
⚠️ Common mistake

Putting km/h or minutes straight into v = u + at or s = ut + ½at² gives a wrong answer. Convert first, then substitute. A train that reaches 90 km/h in 10 minutes has v = 25 m/s and t = 600 s, not 90 and 10.
