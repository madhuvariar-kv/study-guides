Rearranging Formulas, Squares and Areas പരപ്പളവ്

The chapter's numericals need a handful of maths skills. You must turn a formula round to find the quantity you want, put numbers in carefully, square a number and find a square root (√1600 = 40), find the area of a rectangle, a triangle and a trapezium, and handle fractions like 1/24. None of it is hard once you have seen it done slowly. This page does exactly that.

📖 What you need
  • The units m, s, m/s and m/s² (page 2) and the sign rules (page 1).
  • Multiplication tables; a calculator is not needed for the textbook numbers.

1. Turning v = s/t round

Velocity is displacement divided by time: v = s/t. Sometimes the question gives v and t and asks for s, or gives s and v and asks for t. You do not need three formulas to remember, just one and the formula triangle:

v = s ÷ tto find velocity
s = v × tto find displacement
t = s ÷ vto find time
🧪 Formula triangle

Cover the quantity you want. What is left shows the formula: s over v means s ÷ v; v beside t means v × t. (The slider of the unknown quantity is ignored: the answer is calculated from the other two.)

Worked example 1 · How long?

Q. A cyclist rides along a straight road at a uniform velocity of 5 m/s. How long does she take to cover 300 m?

Time is wanted. Cover t in the triangle: s over v, so t = s ÷ v.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: t = 300 ÷ 5 = 60 s.

2. Turning a = (v − u)/t round

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time: a = (v − u)/t, where u is the initial velocity (ആദ്യപ്രവേഗം) and v the final velocity (അന്ത്യപ്രവേഗം). An equation is like a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do the same to the other side, and it stays true:

StepWhat we do to both sidesResult
starta = (v − u) ÷ t
1multiply both sides by tat = v − u
2add u to both sidesv = u + at (the first equation of motion!)
3from at = v − u, subtract at from both sides and add u to both sidesu = v − at
4from at = v − u, divide both sides by at = (v − u) ÷ a
🧪 Find the missing quantity

The graph shows the velocity changing from u to v in t seconds. The slope of the line is the acceleration. A negative a means the line goes down: the object is slowing down.

Worked example 2 · How long to speed up?

Q. A car speeds up from 10 m/s to 30 m/s with a uniform acceleration of 4 m/s². How long does it take?

u = 10 m/s, v = 30 m/s, a = 4 m/s². Time is wanted: t = (v − u) ÷ a.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: t = (30 − 10) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5 s.

3. Substituting values in the right order

When you put numbers into a formula, work in this order (BODMAS): Brackets first, then Orders (squares), then Division and Multiplication, and last Addition and Subtraction. In s = ut + ½at², the little ² belongs to t only: square t first, then multiply by a and by ½.

🧪 s = ut + ½at², one piece at a time

Start: u = 3, a = 2, t = 4 gives 3 × 4 + ½ × 2 × 4² = 12 + ½ × 2 × 16 = 12 + 16 = 28 m. The red line shows the common mistake of squaring a × t together: ½ × (2 × 4)² = 32, which is wrong.

4. Squares and square roots

The square of a number is the number multiplied by itself: 4² = 4 × 4 = 16, 20² = 400. It is the area of a square with that side. The square root √ goes backwards: √400 asks “which number times itself makes 400?”. The answer is 20. In the equation v² = u² + 2as you first find v², then take the square root to get v.

🤔 A shortcut for numbers ending in zeros

√1600 = √(16 × 100) = √16 × √100 = 4 × 10 = 40. In the same way √3600 = 6 × 10 = 60, and 300² = 3² × 100² = 9 × 10,000 = 90,000.

🧪 Squares you can see

Area of the square = n × n = n². Reading it backwards, the side = √(area).

Worked example 3 · Square, then square root

Q. Work out v from v² = u² + 2as when u = 10 m/s, a = 2 m/s² and s = 75 m.

Square u first: 10² = 100. Then 2 × 2 × 75. Add, and finally take the square root.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: v² = 100 + 300 = 400, so v = √400 = 20 m/s.

5. Areas: rectangle, triangle and trapezium

On page 10 you will find the displacement from the area (പരപ്പളവ്) under a velocity–time graph. Only three shapes ever appear:

rectangle = l × blength × breadth
triangle = ½ × b × hhalf of base × height
trapezium = ½ × (a + b) × hhalf the sum of the parallel sides × the distance between them
🧪 Area lab on graph paper

The rectangle uses the left height for its height. The triangle starts from a point at the left and rises to the right height, just like a v–t graph that starts from rest. The trapezium is a rectangle (blue) plus a triangle (purple) on top, which is why its formula works.

Worked example 4 · Area of a trapezium

Q. A trapezium has parallel sides of 10 and 20 units, and they are 6 units apart. Find its area. (On a v–t graph this is a velocity rising from 10 m/s to 20 m/s in 6 s.)

Area = ½ × (a + b) × h with a = 10, b = 20 and h = 6. Or split it: rectangle 10 × 6 plus triangle ½ × 6 × 10.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: ½ × (10 + 20) × 6 = 15 × 6 = 90 square units. Check: 10 × 6 + ½ × 6 × 10 = 60 + 30 = 90 ✓. On a v–t graph this area is a displacement of 90 m.

6. Fractions and decimals

Some answers come out as fractions. To simplify a fraction, divide the top and the bottom by the same number. To change it into a decimal, divide the top by the bottom. Useful ones: ½ = 0.5, ¼ = 0.25, 5/18 ≈ 0.278.

🧪 Fraction simplifier

Try 20 over 300 (you get 1/15, from “Let us assess” question 6) and 5 over 18 (already in its simplest form).

Worked example 5 · A small acceleration

Q. A calculation gives a = 25/600 m/s². Write it as a fraction in its simplest form and as a decimal to three places.

Both 25 and 600 can be divided by 25. Then divide 1 by the bottom number.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: 25/600 = 1/24 m/s² ≈ 0.042 m/s².
🌍 Real life in Kerala

A long passenger train leaving Shoranur Junction speeds up very gently. Its acceleration is often less than 0.1 m/s², a small fraction such as 1/24 m/s². That is why it takes several minutes to reach full speed. An autorickshaw pulling away from a signal accelerates many times faster, but it has a much lower top speed.
