📐 How the ray diagrams are drawn

Rays are red with arrows; the normal (ലംബം) is a dashed line drawn at the point of incidence; every angle is measured from the normal. Media are coloured as in the textbook: air grey, water blue, glass green. The angles in the animations are worked out with the real law of refraction, so they are drawn to scale.

1.
The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed of light in a medium is called the of that medium.
(1 mark)

💡 n = c/v – what is n called?

✅ Answer: refractive index (അപവർത്തനാങ്കം)

Refractive index (അപവർത്തനാങ്കം) n = c/v, where c = 3 × 108 m/s is the speed of light in vacuum (ശൂന്യത) and v its speed in the medium.

Which of these media has the highest optical density?
(a) air(b) water(c) glass(d) diamond
(1 mark)

💡 In which medium is light slowest?

✅ Answer: (d) diamond

Light is slowest in diamond (1.25 × 108 m/s in Table 1.1), so diamond is the optically densest (പ്രകാശികസാന്ദ്രത കൂടിയ മാധ്യമം). Order: air < water < glass < diamond.

3.
The critical angle of glass is 42°. Inside a glass block, total internal reflection takes place when the angle of incidence is
(a) 30°(b) 40°(c) 42°(d) 50°
(1 mark)

💡 It must be greater than – not equal to – the critical angle.

✅ Answer: (d) 50°

Total internal reflection (പൂർണ്ണാന്തര പ്രതിപതനം) needs an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle. Only 50° is greater than 42°. At exactly 42° the ray grazes along the surface (r = 90°).

Marking: 1 mark for (d).

5.
Find the relation and fill in:   air → water : bends towards the normal  ::  water → air :
(1 mark)

💡 Is water → air rarer → denser or denser → rarer?

✅ Answer: bends away from the normal

Rarer → denser: towards the normal; denser → rarer: away from the normal (ലംബത്തിൽ നിന്ന് അകലുന്നു).

Marking: 1 mark.

6.
The twinkling of stars is due to .
(1 mark)

💡 Light from a star passes through many layers of air.

✅ Answer: atmospheric refraction (അന്തരീക്ഷത്തിലെ അപവർത്തനം)

The layers of the atmosphere keep changing in temperature and pressure, so their optical density keeps changing. Starlight is refracted irregularly, and the apparent position and brightness of the star keep changing – it twinkles.

Marking: 1 mark (“refraction” alone: ½ mark).

9.
Light travels through glycerine at a speed of 2.04 × 108 m/s. Find the refractive index of glycerine. (Speed of light in vacuum c = 3 × 108 m/s)
(2 marks)

💡 Divide c by v; the powers of ten cancel.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: 1.47

Given: v = 2.04 × 108 m/s; c = 3 × 108 m/s

Formula: n = c/v

Substitution: n = (3 × 108) ÷ (2.04 × 108) = 3 ÷ 2.04

Answer: n ≈ 1.47 (no unit)

The 108 cancels; the answer matches glycerine in Table 1.5.

Marking: ½ for the formula, ½ for substitution, 1 for the answer (writing a unit: lose ½).

A ray of light passes obliquely from air into water (figure). (a) Complete the diagram showing the refracted ray and mark the angle of incidence i and the angle of refraction r. (b) Which is greater, i or r?

💡 Rarer → denser: towards or away from the normal?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) bends towards the normal   (b) i > r

11.
Write the two conditions necessary for total internal reflection.
(2 marks)

💡 Which way must the light travel, and how big must the angle be?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: denser → rarer; i > critical angle

(1) Light must travel from an optically denser medium to an optically rarer medium (for example glass → air).

(2) The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle (ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോൺ) of the pair of media (42° for glass–air).

Marking: 1 mark for each condition.

12.
A boy fishing with a bow and arrow in a paddy-field canal aims a little below the place where he sees the fish. Why? Explain with the help of the figure.
(2 marks)

💡 Where does the light from the fish bend, and which way?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: the fish appears raised

Light from the fish passes from water (denser) into air (rarer) and bends away from the normal at the surface. The boy’s eye traces the ray back in a straight line, so he sees the fish at a raised position, higher than where it really is. So he must aim below the image he sees.

Marking: 1 mark for “bends away from the normal at the water surface”, 1 mark for “the fish appears higher than its real position”.

14.
The speed of light in three media P, Q and R is given below.
MediumPQR
Speed of light (m/s)2.25 × 1081.25 × 1082 × 108
(a) Find the refractive index of Q (c = 3 × 108 m/s). (b) Which medium is optically the densest? (c) A ray passes obliquely from R into P. Does it bend towards or away from the normal? Why?
(3 marks)

💡 Lower speed = higher refractive index = optically denser.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 2.4   (b) Q   (c) away from the normal

Given: v(Q) = 1.25 × 108 m/s; c = 3 × 108 m/s

Formula: n = c/v

Substitution: (a) n = 3 ÷ 1.25

Answer: (a) n(Q) = 2.4

(b) Light is slowest in Q, so Q is optically the densest (it is like diamond).

(c) n(R) = 3/2 = 1.5 and n(P) = 3/2.25 ≈ 1.33. Light is faster in P, so R → P is denser → rarer: the ray bends away from the normal.

Marking: 1 mark for each part; in (c) ½ for the answer, ½ for the reason.

15.
A ray of light inside a semicircular glass block is aimed at the centre of its flat face at three different angles of incidence: (i) 35° (ii) 42° (iii) 50°. In (i) the angle of refraction in air is 60°. (a) What happens to the ray in (ii)? What is this angle called? (b) What happens in (iii)? Name the phenomenon. (c) Why does the ray in (i) bend away from the normal?
(3 marks)

💡 Compare each angle with 42°.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) grazes along the surface, r = 90°; critical angle   (b) total internal reflection   (c) glass → air is denser → rarer

(a) At i = 42° the refracted ray goes along the surface – the angle of refraction is 90°. This angle of incidence is the critical angle of glass.

(b) At 50° (greater than 42°) the ray is not refracted; it is reflected completely back into the glass at 50° – total internal reflection.

(c) The light goes from glass (denser) to air (rarer), so it bends away from the normal: r (60°) is greater than i (35°).

Marking: 1 mark for each part.

16.
A ray of light PQ falls on a rectangular glass slab ABCD at Q with an angle of incidence of 50° (figure). (a) At which points does refraction take place? (b) Draw the path of the ray through the slab and out of it. (c) How is the emergent ray related to the incident ray PQ?
(3 marks)

💡 Two surfaces – two refractions.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) at Q and at R   (b) towards the normal at Q, away at R   (c) parallel to PQ

(a) At Q (air → glass) and at R, where the ray leaves the slab (glass → air).

(b) At Q it bends towards the normal (about 31° inside the glass); at R it bends away from the normal and leaves along RS.

(c) The emergent ray RS is parallel to the incident ray PQ, but shifted sideways.

Marking: 1 mark for each part; in (b) normals must be drawn at both surfaces.

17.
On a hot summer afternoon the road far ahead appears wet, but there is no water when we reach the spot. (a) Name this phenomenon. (b) Explain how it happens. (c) Why is the image seen upside down?
(3 marks)

💡 Which air is optically rarer – hot or cool?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) mirage (മരീചിക)   (b) refraction through hot air layers + total internal reflection   (c) the rays cross over

(a) A mirage (മരീചിക).

(b) The air near the hot road is hotter and optically rarer; the layers above are cooler and denser. Light from the sky or a distant tree travelling down passes from denser to rarer layers and bends away from the normal more and more. Near the road the angle of incidence becomes greater than the critical angle, and the light is totally internally reflected upwards into our eyes.

(c) The light from the top of the tree arrives at the eye from lower down than the light from the bottom, so the image appears inverted, below the road – just like a reflection in a pool of water.

Marking: ½ for (a), 2 marks for (b) (rarer hot air ½, bending away ½, total internal reflection 1), ½ for (c).

18.
Refractive indices of some media are given below.
Mediumwaterkerosenecrown glassdiamond
Refractive index1.331.441.522.42
(a) In which medium is the speed of light the highest? (b) Find the speed of light in diamond (c = 3 × 108 m/s). (c) A ray passes obliquely from kerosene into water. Does it bend towards or away from the normal? Why? (d) The critical angle of diamond is 24.4° and that of glass is 42°. Which of the two sparkles more? Explain.
(5 marks)

💡 Higher n → slower light → smaller critical angle.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) water   (b) 1.24 × 108 m/s   (c) away from the normal   (d) diamond

(a) Water – it has the lowest refractive index, and v = c/n.

Given: n = 2.42; c = 3 × 108 m/s

Formula: v = c/n

Substitution: v = (3 × 108) ÷ 2.42

Answer: (b) v ≈ 1.24 × 108 m/s

(c) Kerosene (1.44) is denser than water (1.33): denser → rarer, so it bends away from the normal. (Kerosene floats on water – it has a lower mass density – but it is optically denser.)

(d) Diamond. Its critical angle (24.4°) is much smaller, so light inside it meets the faces at angles greater than the critical angle far more often. It is totally internally reflected many times and comes out through the top towards our eyes.

Marking: (a) 1, (b) 1 (formula ½, answer with unit ½), (c) 1 (½ answer, ½ reason), (d) 2 (1 for diamond, 1 for the reason).

19.
Right-angled isosceles glass prisms (critical angle 42°) are used in cycle reflectors and in periscopes. (a) A ray falls normally on the face PQ of a reflector prism (figure). Draw its path and write the angle at which it strikes the other two faces. (b) Why is the ray totally reflected at those faces? (c) In which direction does the ray finally leave the prism, and why is this useful on a cycle? (d) In a prism periscope, through what angle does each prism turn the light? (e) If the prisms of a periscope were hollow and filled with water (critical angle 48.6°), would the periscope work? Why?
(5 marks)

💡 Light falling along the normal is not refracted; compare 45° with 42°.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 45° at both faces   (b) 45° > 42°   (c) back, parallel to the incoming light   (d) 90°   (e) no

(a) The ray enters PQ along the normal (no refraction), strikes the first slanted face at A at 45°, is reflected to the second face at B, again at 45°, is reflected again and leaves through PQ.

(b) The light goes from glass to air (denser → rarer) and 45° is greater than the critical angle 42°, so it is totally internally reflected.

(c) It leaves parallel to the incoming light, going back towards the source – the headlights of the vehicle behind – so its driver sees the cycle at night without any bulb.

(d) Each prism turns the light through 90° (one total internal reflection at the hypotenuse).

(e) No. The light would meet the slanted face at 45°, which is less than the critical angle of water (48.6°). It would refract out instead of being totally reflected, and the image would be very dim.

Marking: (a) 2 (path 1, angles 1), (b) 1, (c) ½ + ½ for the use, (d) ½, (e) ½ with the reason. Total 5.
