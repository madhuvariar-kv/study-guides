Speed, Ratios and Powers of Ten പ്രകാശവേഗം

The textbook tells you that light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in air, slower in water and glass, and that the refractive index is a ratio of two speeds. Then it asks you to work out things like 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4. This page gives you every bit of arithmetic you need: what speed means, how to read and write huge numbers with powers of ten, how to divide them, why a ratio has no unit, how to put decimals like 1.33 and 1.52 in order, and what it means when “one goes up and the other goes down”.

📖 What you need
  • Multiplying and dividing simple numbers, and decimals like 2.5 or 1.33.
  • Nothing else – everything else is built here.

1. Speed = distance ÷ time

Speed tells you how much distance something covers in one unit of time. If you walk 100 m in 50 s, you cover 2 m every second: your speed is 2 metres per second, written 2 m/s.

speed = distance ÷ time   ·   distance = speed × time   ·   time = distance ÷ speed

The unit of speed is a distance unit “per” a time unit: m/s (metres per second) in science, km/h (kilometres per hour) on a vehicle's speedometer. The symbol for speed is usually v.

🧪 Race on the school ground

More distance in the same time → more speed. The same distance in less time → more speed too. Try 100 m in 12.5 s: that is 8 m/s, a good school sprinter.

Worked example 1 · Speed of a sprinter

Q. At the sub-district sports meet, Arjun runs the 100 m race in 12.5 s. Find his average speed.

Speed = distance ÷ time. To divide by 12.5, multiply both numbers by 2: 100 ÷ 12.5 = 200 ÷ 25.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: v = 100 m ÷ 12.5 s = 8 m/s.

2. Powers of ten

Light is so fast that its speed is a very long number. To avoid writing lots of zeros, we use powers of ten. The small raised number (the power) tells how many 10s are multiplied together – which is the same as how many zeros follow the 1:

PowerMeansWritten outIn Indian words
10¹1010ten
10²10 × 10100hundred
10³10 × 10 × 101 000thousand
10⁵five 10s1 00 000one lakh
10⁷seven 10s1 00 00 000one crore
10⁸eight 10s10 00 00 000ten crore
🧪 Powers of ten machine

Each step up in the power makes the number 10 times bigger: the digits shift one place to the left and one more zero appears. Multiplying by 10⁸ moves the decimal point 8 places to the right.

⭐ The speed of light

In a vacuum (ശൂന്യത) – empty space – light travels at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s = 300 000 000 m/s = 30 crore metres every second = 3 lakh kilometres per second. In air it is almost exactly the same. The letter c is always used for this speed.

🌍 How fast is that?

The distance round the Earth is about 40 000 km. In one second, light could go round it about 300 000 ÷ 40 000 = 7.5 times. Sunlight takes 1.5 × 10¹¹ m ÷ 3 × 10⁸ m/s = 500 s (8 minutes 20 seconds) to reach Kerala, and moonlight only about 1.3 s. A KSRTC bus at 60 km/h would need almost 285 years to cover the distance to the Sun!

3. Writing numbers in scientific notation

Scientists write big numbers as a number between 1 and 10, multiplied by a power of ten. This is called scientific notation (or standard form). To change 225 000 000 into this form:

  1. Put a decimal point after the first digit: 2.25000000.
  2. Count how many places the point moved to the left: 8.
  3. Write the number with that power of ten: 2.25 × 10⁸.
🧪 Move the decimal point

Every place the point moves left divides the front number by 10, so the power of ten must go up by 1 to keep the value the same. Stop when exactly one digit (not 0) is in front of the point.

4. Dividing numbers with powers of ten

In this chapter you will divide 3 × 10⁸ by other numbers. There are only two cases:

Case 1 · divide by a plain number

Divide the front numbers; the power of ten stays.

(3 × 10⁸) ÷ 2.4 = (3 ÷ 2.4) × 10⁸ = 1.25 × 10⁸

Case 2 · both have the same power

The powers of ten cancel; divide the front numbers.

(3 × 10⁸) ÷ (2 × 10⁸) = 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5

Dividing by a decimal: multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100) until the divisor is a whole number. 3 ÷ 2.4 = 30 ÷ 24 = 1.25. 3 ÷ 2.25 = 300 ÷ 225 = 4 ÷ 3 = 1.333… ≈ 1.33.

🧪 The 3 × 10⁸ divider

Case 1 gives a speed (it still has × 10⁸ and the unit m/s). Case 2 gives a plain number – a ratio – with no power of ten and no unit. Try b = 2.4, 1.44, 2 and 2.25.

Worked example 2 · Case 1

Q. Work out 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4. (You will meet this sum on page 6, for the speed of light in diamond.)

Keep the 10⁸. Work out 3 ÷ 2.4 by multiplying both by 10: 30 ÷ 24.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: 3 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.4 = (30 ÷ 24) × 10⁸ = 1.25 × 10⁸.

Worked example 3 · Case 2

Q. Light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in air and 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s in water. How many times faster is it in air than in water?

“How many times” means divide. Both speeds have × 10⁸ and m/s, so both cancel. 3 ÷ 2.25 = 300 ÷ 225.

🎬 Exam layout, step by step
Answer: (3 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ (2.25 × 10⁸ m/s) = 300 ÷ 225 = 4/3 ≈ 1.33 times – a plain number with no unit.

5. A ratio has no unit

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by dividing one by the other. Because the units are the same on the top and the bottom, they cancel:

(m/s) ÷ (m/s) → no unit   ·   6 m ÷ 2 m = 3 (not “3 m”)

This is exactly why the refractive index (അപവർത്തനാങ്കം) you will meet on page 6 has no unit: it is the speed of light in vacuum divided by the speed of light in the material.

🧪 How many times faster?
⚠ Common mistake

Writing a unit after a ratio, e.g. “refractive index = 1.5 m/s”. A ratio of two speeds is just a number: 1.5.

6. Putting decimals in order

Later you will compare refractive indices such as 1.33, 1.44, 1.47, 1.52 and 2.42. To compare two decimals, line up their decimal points and compare place by place from the left. Put in zeros at the end if one is shorter: 1.5 = 1.50, so 1.5 is bigger than 1.47 (because 5 tenths beats 4 tenths).

🧪 Decimals on a number line

The top line shows everything; the bottom line zooms in between 1.30 and 1.65, where the liquids and glasses crowd together. Glycerine and sunflower oil both have 1.47 – they are equal.

7. “One goes up, the other goes down” – inverse relation

If you travel 80 km from Kochi to Thrissur at 40 km/h you need 2 hours; at 80 km/h only 1 hour. Doubling the speed halves the time. When one quantity goes up exactly as the other goes down, so that their product stays the same (40 × 2 = 80 × 1 = 80), we say they are inversely proportional.

The same happens with light. In the next pages you will learn that speed in a material = 3 × 10⁸ ÷ (refractive index). A bigger refractive index means a smaller speed.

🧪 Bigger divisor, smaller answer

The curve always falls: as n goes up, 3 × 10⁸ ÷ n comes down. At n = 2 the answer is exactly half of 3 × 10⁸. The product n × speed is always 3 × 10⁸.
