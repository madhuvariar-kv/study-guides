📖 What you need (pages 5–11 of this guide)
  • Angles of incidence പതനകോൺ (i) and refraction അപവർത്തനകോൺ (r) are always measured from the normal ലംബം, never from the surface.
  • Optical density പ്രകാശികസാന്ദ്രത: air < water < glass < diamond. Denser medium → light is slower.
  • Rarer → denser (obliquely): bends towards the normal. Denser → rarer: bends away. Along the normal: no bending.
  • Refractive index അപവർത്തനാങ്കം n = c / v, with c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s; n has no unit.
  • Critical angle ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോൺ: glass 42°, water 48.6°. Total internal reflection പൂർണ്ണാന്തര പ്രതിപതനം needs denser → rarer and i greater than the critical angle.

🎬 Solution animation – Fig 1.6 (a) and (b)
• Before water: light from B reaches the eye through one medium (air) – no change of direction.
• After water: light from B bends at the water surface (water → air, bends away from the normal). The eye traces it straight back and sees B at C, higher up. The dipped part of the straw is seen raised, so the straw looks broken at the water surface – this is refraction അപവർത്തനം.

2 · Table 1.2 – find the refractive index

ചില മാധ്യമങ്ങളിലൂടെയുള്ള പ്രകാശവേഗം പട്ടികയിൽ നല്കിയിരിക്കുന്നു. ഓരോ മാധ്യമത്തിന്റേയും അപവർത്തനാങ്കം കണ്ടെത്തി പട്ടിക പൂർത്തീകരിക്കൂ. The speed of light in some media is given in the table. Find the refractive index of each medium and complete the table.

Medium മാധ്യമംSpeed of light (v) പ്രകാശവേഗംRefractive index (n) അപവർത്തനാങ്കം
Air3 × 10⁸ m/sn = c/v = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ (3 × 10⁸ m/s) = 1
Glass2 × 10⁸ m/s?
Water2.25 × 10⁸ m/s?

n = c / v. Divide the speed in vacuum (3 × 10⁸ m/s) by the speed in the medium. The “× 10⁸” and the “m/s” are the same on top and bottom, so they cancel.

🎬 Solution in exam layout – Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer
🧪 Try it – any speed → refractive index

The slower the light, the bigger n. At 3 × 10⁸ m/s (vacuum/air) n = 1 – the smallest possible value.

Answer: c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s; n = c / v.
Glass: n = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ (2 × 10⁸ m/s) = 3/2 = 1.5.
Water: n = (3 × 10⁸ m/s) ÷ (2.25 × 10⁸ m/s) = 4/3 ≈ 1.33.
(No unit – it is a ratio of two speeds.)
⚠️ Common trap

Writing “1.5 m/s”. The units cancel (m/s ÷ m/s), so a refractive index has no unit. And always divide c by v – never v by c (that would give a number less than 1).

3 · Speed of light in diamond

വജ്രത്തിന്റെ (diamond) അപവർത്തനാങ്കം 2.4 ആണ്. വജ്രത്തിൽ കൂടിയുള്ള പ്രകാശവേഗം എത്രയായിരിക്കും? The refractive index of diamond is 2.4. What will be the speed of light through diamond?

Rearrange n = c / v to get v = c / n, then divide 3 by 2.4 and keep the × 10⁸.

🎬 Solution in exam layout
🧪 Try it – refractive index → speed
Answer: n = 2.4, c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
v = c / n = (3 × 10⁸) ÷ 2.4 = 1.25 × 10⁸ m/s – the same value as in Table 1.1.
💡 Why this matters

Light crawls through diamond at less than half its speed in air. That huge slowing (and the tiny critical angle it causes, about 24°) is why a diamond in a jeweller's shop in Thrissur sparkles so much more than a glass bead.

Write in your science diary how optical density is related to the refractive index.

Look at Table 1.1 and your completed Table 1.2 side by side: as the speed goes down, what happens to n?

📈 The density ladder – one ray through air, water, glass and diamond
The greater the refractive index, the lower the speed of light in the medium (n = c / v: v goes down when n goes up – an inverse relation).
Optical density and refractive index go together: a medium with a higher refractive index is optically denser. So: optical density ↑ ⇔ refractive index ↑ ⇔ speed of light ↓.

🚀 Go further – how much does it bend? (not in the textbook)

Snell's law, n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r, gives the exact angle. Air → water at 30°: sin r = sin 30° ÷ 1.33 → r ≈ 22.1°. Water → air at 30°: sin r = 1.33 × sin 30° → r ≈ 41.7°. The animation is drawn with these values.

🔑 Key box – the rules of refraction (p 14)

① The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence പതനബിന്ദു lie in the same plane. ② Rarer → denser obliquely: the refracted ray bends towards the normal. ③ Denser → rarer obliquely: it bends away from the normal. ④ A ray falling normally on the interface is not refracted.

What is the reason? ചിത്രം 1.13 വിശകലനം ചെയ്ത് വിശദീകരണം സയൻസ് ഡയറിയിൽ രേഖപ്പെടുത്തുക. (Analyse Fig 1.13 and write the explanation in your science diary.)

Going down through the atmosphere, the air gets denser. Which way does light bend going from rarer to denser? Then: where does the eye think the light came from?

🎬 Solution animation – Fig 1.13: real and apparent position of the Sun
This is atmospheric refraction അന്തരീക്ഷത്തിലെ അപവർത്തനം. The layers of the atmosphere become denser closer to the Earth. Light from the Sun, even when the Sun is just below the horizon, enters these layers from rarer to denser air and keeps bending towards the normal, so its path curves down and reaches our eyes. 💡 Why this matters

At Kanyakumari or Kovalam, the red Sun you watch “touching the sea” has, in reality, already slipped below the horizon. Atmospheric refraction also flattens the setting Sun into an oval.

🚀 Go further – where does 42° come from? (not in the textbook)

At the critical angle r = 90°, so Snell's law gives n sin C = 1 × sin 90°, i.e. sin C = 1/n. For glass (n = 1.5): C = sin⁻¹(1/1.5) ≈ 41.8° ≈ 42°. For water (n = 1.333): C ≈ 48.6°. For diamond (2.42): C ≈ 24.4°.

Light from the bottom of the aquarium meets the water surface from below at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle of water (48.6°). It is totally internally reflected and reaches her eye. Her eye traces the light back and sees the bottom above the water surface – the surface acts like a plane mirror സമതല ദർപ്പണം. Yes, as with a plane mirror, the image പ്രതിബിംബം is as far above the surface as the bottom is below it.

14 · The mirage on a summer road (Fig 1.21)

വേനൽക്കാലത്ത് റോഡിൽ അകലെ വെള്ളം കിടക്കുന്നതായി തോന്നുന്നതിനുള്ള കാരണം എന്തായിരിക്കും? In summer, why does the road far ahead seem to have water lying on it? (“Look, water on the road, drive carefully, Amma!” – from the opening page.)

Hot air is optically rarer. Near the road the air is hottest. Follow a ray from the top of a tree downwards through rarer and rarer layers.

🎬 Solution animation – Fig 1.21
The air close to the hot road is the hottest, so it is optically rarer; going up, the air gradually becomes denser. Light from distant objects (trees, the sky) passing down through these layers goes from denser to rarer air again and again, bending away from the normal more and more. Near the road the angle of incidence becomes greater than the critical angle and the light is totally internally reflected upwards to our eyes. We see an inverted image on the road, as if reflected from a water surface – a mirage മരീചിക. That is why the road ahead looks wet.

15 · Reflectors and periscopes (Fig 1.22, 1.23)

ഇവയിൽ ഒരു പ്രിസത്തിലേക്ക് പതിക്കുന്ന പ്രകാശരശ്മി പ്രതിപതിച്ചു വരാൻ കാരണം എന്തായിരിക്കും? ഗ്ലാസിന്റെ ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോണിനെ അടിസ്ഥാനപ്പെടുത്തി വിശദീകരിക്കാം. Reflectors റിഫ്ലക്റ്റർ in vehicle tail lamps contain many small prisms പ്രിസം. Why does a ray falling on one of these prisms come back? Explain using the critical angle of glass.

The ray enters face PQ perpendicularly. What is its angle of incidence on the slanting face at A – and is that more or less than 42°?

🎬 Solution animation – Fig 1.22 (b)
The ray falls perpendicularly on face PQ, so it is not refracted. At A it meets the slanting face with an angle of incidence of 45°, which is greater than the critical angle of glass (42°), so it is totally internally reflected towards B. At B, again i = 45° > 42° – total internal reflection again – and the ray leaves through PQ, going back the way it came, towards the vehicle behind. The other prisms in the reflector work the same way.

ചിത്രത്തിന്റെ അടിസ്ഥാനത്തിൽ പെരിസ്കോപ്പിൽ പ്രിസങ്ങളെ എങ്ങനെ പ്രയോജനപ്പെടുത്തിയിരിക്കുന്നു എന്ന് മനസ്സിലാക്കി സയൻസ് ഡയറിയിൽ രേഖപ്പെടുത്തൂ. From the figure (Fig 1.23), understand how prisms are used in the periscope പെരിസ്കോപ്പ് and record it in your science diary.

🎬 Solution animation – Fig 1.23: the prism periscope
Two right-angled prisms are placed at the top and bottom of the tube. Light from the object enters the top prism perpendicularly, strikes the slanting face at 45° (> 42°) and is totally internally reflected – turned through 90° down the tube. The bottom prism turns it through 90° again in the same way, towards the eye. Because total internal reflection reflects practically all the light, the image is brighter and clearer than with plane mirrors.
💡 Endoscopy (Fig 1.25) – for your class presentation

മെഡിക്കൽ രംഗത്ത് ഇവ എങ്ങനെയാണ് പ്രയോജനപ്പെടുത്തിയിരിക്കുന്നതെന്ന് വിവരശേഖരണം നടത്തി ക്ലാസിൽ അവതരിപ്പിക്കൂ. In an endoscope എൻഡോസ്കോപ്പി, one bundle of optical fibres ഒപ്റ്റിക്കൽ ഫൈബർ carries light into the stomach and another carries the image back out, by repeated total internal reflection – so doctors can see inside the body without surgery. Ask at a hospital near you what endoscopy is used for.
