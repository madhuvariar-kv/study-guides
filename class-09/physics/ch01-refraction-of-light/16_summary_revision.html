1. The chapter in one picture

🗺️ Refraction of light – concept map

Read it clockwise from the top left: light has different speeds in different media (optical density, refractive index n = c/v); at an interface it changes direction – refraction – following four rules; refraction explains the raised coin, the broken straw and the twinkling stars; and from a denser to a rarer medium beyond the critical angle the light is totally reflected – fibres, reflectors, periscopes, diamonds and mirages.

2. The key boxes of the textbook

1Light changes direction (p. 9)പ്രകാശരശ്മി · മാധ്യമം · വിഭജനതലം

Light travels in a straight line within one medium. When a ray passes obliquely from one medium into another, its direction changes at the interface. A ray falling perpendicularly does not change direction. – page 5

2Optical density (p. 10)പ്രകാശികസാന്ദ്രത

The ability of a medium to affect the speed of light through it. Light is slower in an optically denser medium and faster in a rarer one. Optical density is not mass density. Order: air < water < glass < diamond. – page 5

3Refraction (p. 11)അപവർത്തനം

When light passes obliquely from one medium into another of different optical density, its direction changes at the interface. This is refraction. – page 6

4Refractive index (p. 11)അപവർത്തനാങ്കം
n = c/v  ·  c = 3 × 108 m/s

The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium. No unit. – page 6

5The rules of refraction (p. 14)ലംബത്തോട് അടുക്കുന്നു · ലംബത്തിൽ നിന്ന് അകലുന്നു

(i) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal lie in the same plane. (ii) Rarer → denser obliquely: bends towards the normal. (iii) Denser → rarer: bends away from the normal. (iv) A ray falling normally is not refracted. – page 7

6Critical angle & total internal reflection (p. 19)ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോൺ · പൂർണ്ണാന്തര പ്രതിപതനം

From a denser to a rarer medium, the angle of incidence for which the angle of refraction is 90° is the critical angle (glass–air 42°). At a greater angle of incidence the ray is not refracted but reflected completely back – total internal reflection. – page 10

7“Critical angle of glass” (p. 19)

When a critical angle is given for one medium alone, the second medium is air (or vacuum): “critical angle of water 48.6°” means water → air. – page 10

8Nobel Prize 2009 (p. 20)

Charles K. Kao won the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physics for achievements in the transmission of light through optical fibres (ഒപ്റ്റിക്കൽ ഫൈബർ). – page 11

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Measuring the angle of incidence or refraction from the surface instead of from the normal. A ray at 30° to the surface has i = 60°.
  • Thinking optical density = mass density. Kerosene is lighter than water (it floats) but optically denser (n 1.44 > 1.33).
  • Saying light bends at normal incidence. A ray along the normal goes straight through.
  • Mixing up towards and away. Rarer → denser = towards the normal; denser → rarer = away.
  • Thinking total internal reflection can happen from rarer to denser, or at angles smaller than (or equal to) the critical angle.
  • Giving refractive index a unit. It is a ratio of two speeds – no unit.
  • Quoting a critical angle as a property of one medium alone without saying “with air” – the critical angle belongs to a pair of media.
  • Writing “the star moves” instead of “the apparent position of the star changes” when explaining twinkling.
