Refraction of Light പ്രകാശത്തിന്റെ അപവർത്തനം

On a hot summer afternoon, a girl in the car shouts, “Look, water on the road! Drive carefully, Amma!” Her mother smiles: “It is summer – that isn't water. You'll see when we get there.” The textbook opens with this puzzle, and with a straw that looks broken in a glass of lime juice. By the end of this chapter you will explain both, and much more. You will see why light changes direction when it passes from one material into another (refraction), what the refractive index of a material tells you, why stars twinkle, why a pond looks shallower than it is, and how light can be trapped inside a glass thread and carried for kilometres (total internal reflection, the idea behind the internet cables of KFON). This interactive, animated self-study guide starts from the basics – rays, angles, speed and powers of ten. It explains every idea with Kerala examples, answers every textbook question, and ends with a revision sheet and a model test.

The chapter in one picture

🔦 A laser ray bends into water · light trapped in a glass fibre

Left: a laser ray (പ്രകാശരശ്മി) travels in a straight line through the air. Where it meets the water surface – the interface (വിഭജനതലം) – it changes direction. This is refraction (അപവർത്തനം). The dashed line is the normal (ലംബം), drawn at right angles to the surface. The ray bends towards the normal as it goes into water, so the angle in the water (r) is smaller than the angle in the air (i). Watch what happens when the ray falls straight down along the normal: it does not bend at all. Right: light enters one end of a thin glass fibre and strikes its wall at a large angle (60°). That is more than the critical angle (ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോൺ) of glass, about 42°, so none of it escapes: it is reflected completely, again and again. This is total internal reflection (പൂർണ്ണാന്തര പ്രതിപതനം). The light comes out only at the far end. You will learn all of this on pages 5–11.

⚠ Never shine a laser torch into anyone's eyes (or at a mirror towards a face); an adult should handle the laser.

        പ്രകാശത്തിന്റെ അപവർത്തനംRefraction of lightThe chapter title
        അപവർത്തനംRefractionThe change of direction of light when it passes obliquely from one medium into another of different optical density
        പ്രകാശരശ്മി · പ്രകാശപാതRay of light · path of lightA line with an arrow showing the direction light travels · the route the light follows
        മാധ്യമം · ശൂന്യതMedium · vacuumA material light travels through (air, water, glass…) · empty space with no material at all
        വിഭജനതലംInterfaceThe boundary surface between two media, e.g. the surface of the water
        ലംബം · ലംബമായി പതിക്കുന്നNormal · falling perpendicularlyA line at 90° to the surface at the point where the ray hits it · a ray that falls along the normal (it does not bend)
        ദിശാവ്യതിയാനംChange of direction (deviation)How much a ray turns from its original direction
        പ്രകാശവേഗംSpeed of light3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum (almost the same in air); slower in water, glass and diamond
        പ്രകാശികസാന്ദ്രതOptical densityThe ability of a medium to affect the speed of light through it
        പ്രകാശികസാന്ദ്രത കൂടിയ / കുറഞ്ഞ മാധ്യമംOptically denser / rarer mediumA medium in which light is slower / faster
        പദാർഥ സാന്ദ്രതMass densityMass per unit volume – not the same as optical density (kerosene floats on water but is optically denser)
        അപവർത്തനാങ്കംRefractive indexn = speed of light in vacuum ÷ speed in the medium; a ratio with no unit
        പതനരശ്മി · അപവർത്തനരശ്മിIncident ray · refracted rayThe ray arriving at the interface · the ray after it has crossed into the second medium
        പതനകോൺ · അപവർത്തനകോൺAngle of incidence (i) · angle of refraction (r)The angles the incident and refracted rays make with the normal (not with the surface)
        പതനബിന്ദുPoint of incidenceThe point where the incident ray meets the interface
        ലംബത്തോട് അടുക്കുന്നു / ലംബത്തിൽ നിന്ന് അകലുന്നുBends towards / away from the normalRarer → denser: towards; denser → rarer: away
        ഗ്ലാസ് സ്ലാബ് · പ്രിസംGlass slab · prismA rectangular block of glass with parallel faces · a block of glass with flat faces at an angle (triangular)
        അന്തരീക്ഷത്തിലെ അപവർത്തനംAtmospheric refractionBending of light by the layers of air of changing optical density
        ചക്രവാളംHorizonThe line where the sky seems to meet the earth or the sea
        നക്ഷത്രങ്ങളുടെ മിന്നിത്തിളക്കംTwinkling of starsThe flickering of starlight caused by irregular refraction in the atmosphere
        പ്രതിപതനം · സമതല ദർപ്പണം · പ്രതിബിംബംReflection · plane mirror · imageLight bouncing back from a surface · a flat mirror · what you see in a mirror or through a lens
        പൂർണ്ണാന്തര പ്രതിപതനംTotal internal reflectionDenser → rarer at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle: all the light is reflected back into the same medium
        ക്രിട്ടിക്കൽ കോൺCritical angleThe angle of incidence (denser → rarer) for which the angle of refraction is 90°; glass–air ≈ 42°
        മരീചികMirageThe “water on the road” seen on hot days, caused by total internal reflection in layers of hot air
        റിഫ്ലക്റ്റർ · പെരിസ്കോപ്പ്Reflector · periscopePrisms that send light back towards a vehicle behind · a tube with two prisms for seeing over obstacles
        ഒപ്റ്റിക്കൽ ഫൈബർOptical fibreA thin glass thread that carries light by repeated total internal reflection
        ആന്തരകോർ / ക്ലാഡിങ് / ബാഹ്യകവചംCore / cladding / outer jacketThe three layers of an optical fibre cable, from the inside out
        എൻഡോസ്കോപ്പിEndoscopyLooking inside the body with optical fibres
        പ്രവർത്തനം · സയൻസ് ഡയറിActivity · science diaryThings to do, and the notebook where you write what you observed
        വിലയിരുത്താം · തുടർപ്രവർത്തനങ്ങൾLet us assess · extended activitiesThe end-of-chapter questions and projects (solved on pages 13–14)
