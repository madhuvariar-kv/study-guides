2. The key boxes of the textbook

1Homogeneous mixtures (p. 114)ഏകാത്മക മിശ്രിതങ്ങൾ

Mixtures that show the same properties in all parts are homogeneous mixtures. A solution is a homogeneous mixture of solute and solvent. – page 3

2Concentration (p. 117)ലായനിയുടെ ഗാഢത · നേർത്ത ലായനി · ഗാഢലായനി

The amount of solute dissolved in a definite amount of solution. Little solute – a dilute solution; more solute – a concentrated solution. – page 4

3Saturated solution (p. 119)പൂരിത ലായനി

A solution in which the maximum amount of solute has dissolved at a given temperature. – page 5

4Unsaturated solution (p. 119)അപൂരിത ലായനി

A solution with less solute than is needed to saturate it at that temperature. – page 5

5Supersaturated solution (p. 120)അതിപൂരിത ലായനി

A solution with more solute dissolved than is needed to saturate it at that temperature – unstable. – page 5

6Solubility (p. 121)ലേയത്വം

The amount of solute in grams needed to saturate 100 g of the solvent at a given temperature. – page 6

Box916 gold (p. 115)ഖരലായനി

916 g of pure gold in 1000 g of ornament, the rest silver and copper – a solid solution (916 = 22 carat). – page 3

BoxHow salt dissolves (p. 116)ജലയോജിത അയോണുകൾ · അയോണിക ലായനി

Water molecules surround Na+ (O side, δ−, towards it) and Cl− (H side, δ+, towards it): hydrated ions – an ionic solution. – page 3

BoxOther measures of concentration (p. 118)പാർട്സ് പെർ മില്ല്യൺ (ppm)

ppm = parts per 106 parts: chlorine in drinking water 4 ppm; oxygen in sea water 5.8 ppm; CO2 in air 270 ppm → 414 ppm. Also volume %, molarity, molality. – page 4

BoxGlauber’s salt (p. 123)ഗ്ലോബേഴ്സ് സാൾട്ട് · അസാധാരണ ലേയത്വം

3. Facts & rules

📐 Facts & rules card

solution = solute + solvent (a homogeneous mixture)
mass percentage = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 100
solubility = grams of solute that saturate 100 g of solvent at a given temperature

Solute, solvent, solution ലീനം · ലായകം · ലായനി

The component in the smaller amount is the solute, the one in the larger amount the solvent; in an aqueous solution (ജലീയ ലായനി) water is always the solvent. The solution usually has the state of its solvent: solid solutions (ഖരലായനി) – brass (zinc 34 % in copper 66 %), 916 gold; liquid solutions (ദ്രാവകലായനി) – salt water, soda water, alcohol in water; gaseous solutions (വാതകലായനി) – air (nitrogen 78 % is the solvent). The components do not react chemically.

Like dissolves like (സമാനമായത് സമാനമായതിനെ ലയിപ്പിക്കും): polar (പോളാർ) and ionic solutes dissolve in polar solvents – salt in water; non-polar (നോൺപോളാർ) substances – enamel paint, grease, kerosene – in non-polar organic solvents (ഓർഗാനിക് ലായകങ്ങൾ) such as turpentine. Water dissolves so many substances that it is called the universal solvent (സാർവിക ലായകം).

Mass percentage മാസ് പെർസെന്റേജ്

  • Forward: 10 g salt + 90 g water → 10 ÷ 100 × 100 = 10 %; 2 g + 18 g water → 2 ÷ 20 × 100 = 10 %.
  • Meaning: a 15 % salt solution has 15 g of salt in every 100 g of solution.
  • Reverse: solute = % × solution ÷ 100; solution = solute × 100 ÷ %; then water = solution − solute (1 mL of water ≈ 1 g).

Saturated, unsaturated, supersaturated പൂരിത · അപൂരിത · അതിപൂരിത ലായനി

dissolved < solubilityunsaturated – more solute can still dissolve
dissolved = solubilitysaturated – no more dissolves at that temperature
dissolved > solubilitysupersaturated – made by cooling a hot saturated solution slowly; unstable: a seed crystal makes the extra crystallise (പരലീകരണം)

Test: drop in one crystal of the solute. It dissolves → unsaturated; it stays → saturated; crystals grow on it → supersaturated.

Factors of solubility ലേയത്വം · ലേയത്വ കർവ്

FactorWhat the book shows
Nature of the solutein 50 mL of water much more NaCl dissolves than NaHCO3
Nature of the solventNaCl dissolves well in water, little in alcohol, not in kerosene
Temperaturemost solids dissolve more when hot (KNO3 steeply); NaCl changes very little; calcium sulphate decreases slightly; Glauber’s salt rises to 32.4 °C and then falls. Gases dissolve less when hot.
Pressure (gases)more gas dissolves at higher pressure – soda is made under high pressure and fizzes when opened

Drinks and food additives കൃത്രിമ പാനീയങ്ങൾ · സ്റ്റെബിലൈസറുകൾ · പ്രിസർവേറ്റീവുകൾ

Natural drinks – pure water, buttermilk, lemon water, tender coconut water, fresh fruit juices – keep only for a short time. Stabilisers stop particles settling in artificial drinks; preservatives keep food from spoiling (vinegar, salt). Table 6.5: tartrazine – yellow; erythrosine – red; indigo carmine – blue; vanillin – flavour; phosphoric acid – sourness in soft drinks; allyl hexanoate – pineapple flavour; sodium benzoate – sauces, salads, soft drinks, against yeast and moulds; sorbic acid – cheese, cake, salad, against moulds.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Dividing by the mass of the solvent. Mass percentage uses the mass of the solution (solute + solvent): 12 g in 68 g water is 12 ÷ 80, not 12 ÷ 68.
  • Saying the solvent is always a liquid. In brass the solvent is copper (a solid); in air it is nitrogen (a gas).
  • Calling a saturated solution “concentrated” and an unsaturated one “dilute”. Saturation compares with the solubility at a stated temperature; a saturated solution of a poorly soluble salt is still dilute.
  • Saying gases dissolve more when water is warmed. They dissolve less – warm soda goes flat, fish suffer in warm water.
  • Saying every salt dissolves more when heated. NaCl barely changes; calcium sulphate, and Glauber’s salt above 32.4 °C, become less soluble.
  • Thinking colloid particles settle or can be filtered. They do not – suspension particles do.
  • Mixing up dissolving and melting. Sugar in tea dissolves (spreads into the solvent); ice on a plate melts (solid → liquid on heating).
  • Thinking every clear liquid is a true solution. Some colloids look clear but show the Tyndall effect in a torch beam.
