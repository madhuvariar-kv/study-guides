Question 1 · Four definitions

1. താഴെപ്പറയുന്നവ എന്തെന്ന് നിർവചിക്കുന്നതിനോടൊപ്പം അനുബന്ധ ചോദ്യത്തിന് ഉത്തരമെഴുതുക. Define each of the following and answer the related question.

  1. Solute ലീനം – സോഡാജലത്തിലെ ലീനം ഏതായിരിക്കും? What is the solute in soda water?
  2. Solvent ലായകം – കറിയുപ്പ് ലയിക്കുന്ന ലായകം ഏത്? Which solvent dissolves common salt?
  3. Solution ലായനി – ഗാഢ ലായനി, നേർപ്പിച്ച ലായനി ഇവ തമ്മിലുള്ള വ്യത്യാസം കണ്ടെത്തുക. Find the difference between a concentrated solution and a dilute solution.
  4. Solubility ലേയത്വം – 25°C ൽ കോപ്പർ സൾഫേറ്റിന്റെ ലേയത്വം എന്നതുകൊണ്ട് അർത്ഥമാക്കുന്നത് എന്ത്? What is meant by “the solubility of copper sulphate at 25 °C”?
🔍 See each idea
a) The solute ലീനം is the substance that dissolves (usually the component present in the smaller amount). In soda water the solute is carbon dioxide (a gas).
b) The solvent ലായകം is the substance in which the solute dissolves (usually the larger amount). Common salt dissolves in water.
c) A solution ലായനി is a homogeneous mixture of a solute and a solvent. A concentrated solution ഗാഢലായനി has more solute dissolved in a given amount of solution; a dilute solution നേർത്ത ലായനി has less solute in the same amount of solution.
d) Solubility ലേയത്വം is the amount of solute in grams needed to saturate 100 g of the solvent at a given temperature. So “the solubility of copper sulphate at 25 °C” means the mass in grams of copper sulphate needed to saturate 100 g of water at 25 °C.

Question 2 · How much water for a 20 % solution?

2. 200 g പൊട്ടാസിയം നൈട്രേറ്റിനെ എത്ര mL ജലത്തിൽ ലയിപ്പിച്ചാലാണ് 20 ശതമാനം ഗാഢതയുള്ള ലായനി തയ്യാറാക്കാനാകുന്നത്? (താപനില സ്ഥിരമാണ്) In how many mL of water should 200 g of potassium nitrate be dissolved to make a solution of 20 % concentration? (The temperature is constant.)

20 % means 20 g of solute in every 100 g of solution, not in 100 g of water. Find the mass of the solution first, then subtract the solute.

🎬 Step by step – solution first, then water
Mass percentage = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 100
20 = (200 ÷ mass of solution) × 100 → mass of solution = 200 × 100 ÷ 20 = 1000 g
Mass of water = mass of solution − mass of solute = 1000 − 200 = 800 g
1 mL of water has a mass of 1 g, so the water needed is 800 mL.
Check: 200 ÷ (200 + 800) × 100 = 20 % ✔  (A common mistake is 1000 mL: that would give 200 ÷ 1200 × 100 ≈ 16.7 %.)

Question 3 · Sodium and sodium chloride “disappear” in water

3. സോഡിയം എന്ന ലോഹം ജലത്തിൽ ഇട്ടാലും സോഡിയം ക്ലോറൈഡ് എന്ന ലവണം ജലത്തിൽ ഇട്ടാലും അപ്രത്യക്ഷമാകുന്നത് നിങ്ങൾ കണ്ടിരിക്കും? രണ്ടും ഒരേ തരത്തിൽ സംഭവിക്കുന്ന മാറ്റമാണോ? നിങ്ങളുടെ ഉത്തരത്തിന് ന്യായീകരണം എഴുതുക. You may have seen that both the metal sodium and the salt sodium chloride disappear when put in water. Are both the same kind of change? Justify your answer.

🧪 Two beakers, two kinds of change
⚠ Sodium reacts violently with water and the hydrogen can catch fire. This is a teacher demonstration only, with a piece smaller than a rice grain, behind a safety screen. Never at home.
No, they are different changes.
Sodium in water is a chemical change. Sodium reacts with water: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑. A new substance, sodium hydroxide, forms and hydrogen gas is given off (fizzing, heat). If the water is evaporated we get sodium hydroxide, not sodium. The sodium cannot be got back by a simple physical method.
Salt in water is a physical change (dissolving). The salt only breaks up into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions that spread through the water as hydrated ions ജലയോജിത അയോണുകൾ; no new substance forms. On evaporating the water the same salt is recovered. So salt water is a solution ലായനി, while sodium in water is a reaction.

Question 4 · Saturated or unsaturated?

4. സോഡിയം ക്ലോറൈഡിന്റെ ഒരു ലായനി തന്നിരിക്കുന്നു. ഇത് പൂരിതമാണോ, അപൂരിതമാണോ എന്ന് തെളിയിക്കാൻ ലഘുവായ ഒരു മാർഗം നിർദേശിക്കുക. A solution of sodium chloride is given. Suggest a simple way to find out whether it is saturated or unsaturated.

🧂 Test two mystery bottles (same temperature)
Both bottles look the same: clear and colourless. Test each one.
Add a little more sodium chloride to the solution at the same temperature and stir.
• If it dissolves, the solution could take more solute: it was unsaturated അപൂരിത ലായനി.
• If it does not dissolve and stays at the bottom, the solution already holds the maximum amount: it is saturated പൂരിത ലായനി.
(Do not warm it while testing: warming changes how much can dissolve.)

Question 5 · Make a saturated solution unsaturated

5. ഒരു പൂരിത ലായനിയെ അപൂരിത ലായനിയായി മാറ്റണമെന്നിരിക്കട്ടെ. ഇതിനായി തെരഞ്ഞെടുക്കുന്ന രണ്ട് മാർഗങ്ങൾ നിർദേശിക്കുക. Suppose a saturated solution has to be made unsaturated. Suggest two ways to do it.

⚖️ Ammonium chloride: dissolved vs what the water can hold

Solubility of NH₄Cl from the table in Question 6: 37 g per 100 g water at 20 °C and 41 g at 40 °C.

1. Add more solvent (water). The same amount of solute is now in more water, which can hold more, so the solution becomes unsaturated.
2. Raise the temperature (warm it). For most solids, such as NH₄Cl, KNO₃ and CuSO₄, solubility increases with temperature, so the warm solution can dissolve more.
go further Warming does not work for a substance whose solubility falls as the temperature rises (calcium sulphate, or a gas); adding solvent always works.

Question 7 · “Shake well before use”

7. ചില മരുന്നുകുപ്പികളിൽ Shake well before use എന്നു രേഖപ്പെടുത്തിയിട്ടുള്ളത് ശ്രദ്ധിച്ചിട്ടില്ലേ. Have you noticed “Shake well before use” written on some medicine bottles?

  1. ഇത്തരം മരുന്നുകൾ താഴെപ്പറയുന്നവയിൽ ഏതു വിഭാഗത്തിൽപ്പെട്ടവയായിരിക്കാം? (കൊളോയിഡ്, സസ്പെൻഷൻ, ലായനി) To which of these groups do such medicines belong? (colloid, suspension, solution)
  2. ലേബലിൽ കാണിച്ചിട്ടുള്ള നിർദേശത്തിന് കാരണമെന്തായിരിക്കും? What could be the reason for the instruction on the label?
🍼 A suspension medicine on the shelf

illustrative The particles are drawn far larger than real ones.

a) Such medicines are suspensions സസ്പെൻഷൻ.
b) The particles of the medicine are large (more than 1000 nm) and settle at the bottom when the bottle stands. Shaking spreads them evenly through the liquid again, so that every dose contains the correct amount of medicine. Without shaking, the first doses would have too little medicine and the last ones too much.

Reasons: in a true solution the particles are 0.01–1 nm, cannot be seen even with a microscope, do not settle, pass through filter paper and the path of light is not visible. Question 9 · Gases dissolved in water

📉 The textbook graph, redrawn – drag the temperature cursor

Values are read from the textbook graph (approximate). Note the break in the vertical axis: CO₂ is plotted on a scale about 35 times coarser than O₂ and N₂.

a) Carbon dioxide (about 0.32 g per 100 g water at 0 °C, far more than oxygen or nitrogen).
b) About 0.004 g per 100 g of water (read from the graph).
c) The solubility of gases decreases as the temperature increases (all three curves fall).
d) Increase the pressure of CO₂ above the water (as in making soda), or lower the temperature (cool the water).
e) Note: for most solid solutes (KNO₃, NH₄Cl, CuSO₄) solubility increases with temperature, though for some (NaCl) it hardly changes. For gases the solubility decreases as the water gets warmer, and gases dissolve much more under high pressure, while pressure has almost no effect on solids. Gases are also dissolved in very small amounts (oxygen only about 0.004 g in 100 g of water at 20 °C). This is why a warm soda goes flat quickly and why bubbles appear in water as it is heated.
f) Note: aquatic animals such as fish breathe the oxygen dissolved in water. If more oxygen dissolved, fish and other water life would get more oxygen and could live and grow well, and the breakdown of waste in water by microbes that use oxygen would be quicker, keeping water cleaner. go further The opposite happens in warm or polluted water: it holds less oxygen, so fish may gasp at the surface or die.

Question 10 · Two seed crystals overnight

10. കോപ്പർസൾഫേറ്റിന്റെ അതിപൂരിത ലായനിയിലും അപൂരിത ലായനിയിലും കോപ്പർസൾഫേറ്റിന്റെ ചെറുക്രിസ്റ്റൽ തൂക്കിയിട്ടിരിക്കുന്നു. അടുത്ത ദിവസം എന്തായിരിക്കും നിരീക്ഷണം? A small crystal of copper sulphate is hung in a supersaturated solution of copper sulphate and another in an unsaturated solution. What will be observed the next day?

💎 Hang the seed crystals and wait
Evening: two equal seed crystals are hung in the two solutions.
• In the supersaturated solution അതിപൂരിത ലായനി the seed crystal grows bigger. The solution holds more solute than it can at that temperature, so it is unstable; the extra copper sulphate comes out of the solution and builds up on the seed crystal (crystallization പരലീകരണം).
• In the unsaturated solution അപൂരിത ലായനി the crystal becomes smaller or dissolves completely, because the solution can still dissolve more copper sulphate.

Stabilisers സ്റ്റെബിലൈസറുകൾ are added to artificial drinks കൃത്രിമ പാനീയങ്ങൾ to stop the particles in them from settling, so that the drink stays uniform (stable) and looks the same all through, for a long time. Examples given in the textbook: brominated vegetable oil, sucrose acetate isobutyrate, glyceryl ester of rosin.
⚠️ Four traps in these questions
  • In mass percentage divide by the mass of the solution (solute + solvent), not by the solvent alone (Q2).
  • Dissolving is a physical change; sodium in water is a chemical change (Q3).
  • Gases become less soluble in warm water and more soluble under pressure (Q9).
  • Colloid particles do not settle and pass through filter paper; suspension particles settle and are filtered (Q7, Q8).

Extended activity 1 · A crystal garden

1. ഒരു ടഫിൽ സോഡിയം സിലിക്കേറ്റിന്റെ (Na₂SiO₃) അതിപൂരിത ലായനി തയ്യാറാക്കുക. ചൂടുവെള്ളത്തിൽ അത് എളുപ്പം ലയിക്കും. സാധാരണ താപനിലയിലേക്ക് എത്തി കഴിഞ്ഞാൽ അതിലേക്ക് വിവിധ രാസവസ്തുക്കളുടെ ക്രിസ്റ്റലുകൾ (seed crystals) ഇടുക. കോപ്പർ സൾഫേറ്റ്, ഫെറസ് സൾഫേറ്റ്, ഫെറിക് ക്ലോറൈഡ്, കൊബാൾട്ട് ക്ലോറൈഡ്, നിക്കൽ സൾഫേറ്റ് തുടങ്ങി ലഭ്യമായവയൊക്കെ ഇടാം. ഇനി ടഫ് അനക്കാതെ വയ്ക്കുക. കുറച്ചു ദിവസങ്ങൾക്കുശേഷം ക്രിസ്റ്റലുകളിൽനിന്ന് മുകളിലേക്ക് ശിഖരങ്ങൾ പോലെ രാസവസ്തുക്കൾ വളർന്നിരിക്കുന്നത് കാണാം. ഉൽപന്നങ്ങളുടെ ഫോട്ടോകൾ ശേഖരിച്ച് ഡിജിറ്റൽ ആൽബം തയ്യാറാക്കുക. Make a supersaturated solution of sodium silicate (Na₂SiO₃) in a glass trough; it dissolves easily in hot water. When it has cooled to room temperature, drop in seed crystals of different chemicals: copper sulphate, ferrous sulphate, ferric chloride, cobalt chloride, nickel sulphate or whatever is available. Leave the trough without disturbing it. After a few days, branch-like growths rise up from the crystals. Collect photos of the results and make a digital album.

🛑 Safety – a teacher-led activity

Do the real crystal garden only with your teacher. Sodium silicate solution is strongly alkaline and irritates skin and eyes. Cobalt chloride and nickel sulphate are harmful (they can cause allergies and are toxic); copper sulphate and ferric chloride are also harmful if swallowed. Wear gloves and goggles, use forceps to drop the crystals, never touch or taste the chemicals, do not take the garden home, and let the teacher dispose of it. Extended activity 2 · Making tea as a note

2. അടുക്കളയിൽ ചായ ഉണ്ടാക്കുന്ന പ്രക്രിയ പലപ്പോഴായി നിങ്ങൾ കണ്ടിരിക്കും. എന്തൊക്കെ പദാർഥങ്ങളാണ് ചായ നിർമ്മിക്കാൻ ഉപയോഗിക്കുന്നത്? ചായ നിർമ്മിക്കുന്ന പ്രക്രിയ ഒരു കുറിപ്പിലൂടെ അവതരിപ്പിക്കൂ. ലീനം, ലായകം, ലായനി, ലയനം, ലയിക്കാത്തവ, അരിച്ചെടുക്കൽ, അവക്ഷിപ്തം തുടങ്ങിയ പദങ്ങൾ കടന്നുവരുന്ന രീതിയിലായിരിക്കണം അവതരണം. You have often seen tea being made in the kitchen. What substances are used to make tea? Present the process of making tea as a note, using words such as solute, solvent, solution, dissolving, insoluble, filtering and precipitate.

🫖 Step by step – tea in the words of this chapter
How tea is made
Tea is made from water, tea powder (dried tea leaves), sugar and, if we like, milk. First, water is heated in a pan. Water is the solvent ലായകം. When tea powder is added to the boiling water, the colour, taste and flavour substances in the leaves dissolve ലയനം in the hot water: they are solutes ലീനം. Sugar is added and stirred; it also dissolves and becomes a solute. The leaf pieces themselves do not dissolve: they are insoluble ലയിക്കാത്തവ. When the pan is left for a moment the heavy leaf pieces sink and collect at the bottom as a solid deposit (in this note the word precipitate അവക്ഷിപ്തം is used for this solid left behind). The tea is then poured through a strainer: this filtering അരിച്ചെടുക്കൽ holds back the insoluble leaves and lets the liquid pass. The clear, evenly coloured, sweet black tea in the cup is a solution ലായനി.
go further When milk is added, the tea becomes a colloid, because milk itself is a colloid (an emulsion).
✔ solute✔ solvent✔ solution✔ dissolving✔ insoluble✔ filtering✔ precipitate

Strictly, in chemistry a precipitate is a solid that separates out of a solution; ask your teacher how they would like this word used in your note.

⚠ Boiling water and a hot pan scald. Make tea only with an adult, keep the pan handle turned inwards and let the strainer cool before you touch it.

Extended activity 4 · A food-safety skit

Interest in different food styles and in finding and trying new dishes has grown a lot among us, and we also hear news about food poisoning. 🎭 Skit outline: “Mayonnaise and Moru” (about 5 minutes, 5 actors)

Characters: Anu and Rahul (Class 9 students), Ammachi (grandmother), a shop owner, Sneha (the narrator, a science-club member).

Scene 1 · After school at a juice shop. Rahul orders a bright red soft drink and a shawarma with mayonnaise. Anu picks the bottle up and reads the label aloud: “carbonated water, sugar, acidity regulator, colour…”.

Anu: Rahul, see this: added colour and preservatives. Our chemistry book lists colours like erythrosine (red) and preservatives like sodium benzoate.

Rahul: But it tastes so good!

Scene 2 · The mayonnaise question. Sneha (to the audience): Mayonnaise is a colloid made with oil, vinegar and egg white or milk. Our textbook warns that it must be made and stored carefully, or it can spoil and cause food poisoning. Ask: was it fresh? Was it kept cold?

Scene 3 · At home. Ammachi serves moru (buttermilk), lemon water and tender coconut water.

Ammachi: In my time we drank these. Fresh and natural, but they do not keep for long, so we made them fresh every day.

Anu: The book says soft drinks are mostly sugar, colour and flavour, with little nutrition, and some studies say too many of them can lead to obesity and tooth decay.

Scene 4 · The pledge. All five face the audience and say one line each: “Read the label.” “Check the FSSAI number and the date.” “Choose fresh and natural drinks.” “Eat freshly cooked food and keep it covered.” “Wash your hands before eating.”

Collage tips: cut out newspaper reports on food poisoning and food-safety raids, labels showing ingredients and additives, and pictures of natural drinks; group them under “Danger signs”, “Read the label” and “Healthy choices”. Write one sentence from this chapter under each group.

Keep health statements as the textbook gives them; this skit is for awareness, not medical advice.

🔑 The ideas that answered everything on this page

Mass % = solute ÷ solution × 100. A saturated solution holds the most solute it can at that temperature; add solvent or warm it to make it unsaturated; a seed crystal grows in a supersaturated solution. Solubility of most solids rises with temperature (NaCl barely changes); gases are less soluble when warm and more under pressure. Particle size sorts mixtures: true solution < 1 nm, colloid 1–1000 nm (Tyndall effect), suspension > 1000 nm (settles, “shake well”).
