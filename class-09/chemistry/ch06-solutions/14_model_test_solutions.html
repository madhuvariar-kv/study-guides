📐 How the solutions are drawn

In a solution bar the orange part is the solute and the blue part the solvent; the whole bar is the solution, so mass percentage = orange ÷ whole bar × 100. On a solubility curve the dashed red line marks the temperature you read at. In the torch-beam pictures a yellow band inside a beaker means the path of the light is seen; dashed edges mean the light passes but its path is not seen.

Three questions decide almost every answer: How much solute is in how much solution? (mass %) · How much can 100 g of solvent hold at this temperature? (solubility, saturation) · How big are the particles? (true solution, colloid, suspension).

1.
Which of the following is a solid solution?
(a) soda water(b) air(c) brass(d) tincture of iodine
(1 mark)

💡 Which one has a solid as its solvent?

✅ Answer: (c) brass

Brass is an alloy (ലോഹസങ്കരം) of zinc (solute) dissolved in copper (solvent) – both solids, so it is a solid solution (ഖരലായനി). Soda water and tincture of iodine are liquid solutions; air is a gaseous solution.

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

2.
A solution in which the maximum amount of solute has dissolved at a given temperature is called a solution.
(1 mark)

💡 Can any more solute dissolve in it at that temperature?

✅ Answer: saturated (പൂരിത ലായനി)

At that temperature no more solute can dissolve: extra solute stays at the bottom. That is a saturated solution.

3.

(1 mark)

💡 What happens to muddy water when it is left to stand?

✅ Answer: suspension (സസ്പെൻഷൻ)

In muddy water the particles can be seen, they settle when it stands and they are left on filter paper – a suspension, a heterogeneous mixture. Sugar solution is a homogeneous true solution.

4.
The size of the particles in a colloid is
(a) 0.01 nm – 1 nm(b) 1 nm – 1000 nm(c) more than 1000 nm(d) less than 0.01 nm
(1 mark)

💡 Colloids lie between true solutions and suspensions.

✅ Answer: (b) 1 nm – 1000 nm

True solution 0.01–1 nm; colloid (കൊളോയിഡുകൾ) 1–1000 nm; suspension more than 1000 nm (1 nm = 10−9 m).

5.
The scattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles, which makes the path of the light visible, is called the .
(1 mark)

💡 Think of the beam of a cinema projector.

✅ Answer: Tyndall effect (ടിൻഡൽ പ്രഭാവം)

Colloidal particles are big enough to scatter light, so the path of the beam is seen – the Tyndall effect, like a cinema projector beam in a dusty hall.

6.
Which of these will make more carbon dioxide dissolve in water?
(a) warming the water(b) increasing the pressure(c) decreasing the pressure(d) leaving the bottle open
(1 mark)

💡 How is soda water made in the factory?

✅ Answer: (b) increasing the pressure

The solubility of a gas increases with pressure – soda water is made by dissolving CO2 under high pressure. Warming the water, lowering the pressure or opening the bottle makes the gas escape.

7.
The solubility of potassium nitrate in water at 20 °C is 32 g. This means
(a) 32 g of potassium nitrate dissolves in 100 g of solution(b) 32 g of potassium nitrate is needed to saturate 100 g of water at 20 °C(c) 32 g of potassium nitrate dissolves in 100 g of water at any temperature(d) the saturated solution at 20 °C is a 32 % solution
(1 mark)

💡 Solubility is always “per 100 g of solvent”.

✅ Answer: (b) 32 g of potassium nitrate is needed to saturate 100 g of water at 20 °C

Solubility (ലേയത്വം) is the mass of solute in grams needed to saturate 100 g of solvent at a given temperature. (The saturated solution is 24.24 %, because its mass is 32 + 100 = 132 g: 32 ÷ 132 × 100 = 24.24. So (d) is wrong.)

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

SECTION BAnswer all · 5 × 2 = 10 marks
9.
12 g of sugar is dissolved in 68 g of water. (a) What is the mass of the solution?
(b) Find the mass percentage of sugar in the solution.
(2 marks)

💡 Divide by the mass of the solution, not by the mass of the water.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 80 g   (b) 15 %

(a) Mass of solution = mass of solute + mass of solvent = 12 + 68 = 80 g.

(b) Mass percentage (മാസ് പെർസെന്റേജ്) = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 100 = (12 ÷ 80) × 100 = 15 %.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) formula ½, answer ½.

10.
To make kalkandam (sugar candy), Ammini’s grandfather dissolves as much sugar as he can in hot water and lets the syrup cool slowly without disturbing it. The cool syrup holds more sugar than water at room temperature can normally dissolve. (a) What is such a solution called?
(b) He hangs a thread with a few small sugar crystals into the syrup. After some days big crystals form on the thread. Why? Name the process.
(2 marks)

💡 Is a supersaturated solution stable?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) supersaturated solution   (b) the extra sugar comes out on the seed crystals – crystallization

(a) A solution with more solute dissolved than is needed to saturate it at that temperature is a supersaturated solution (അതിപൂരിത ലായനി).

(b) A supersaturated solution is not stable. The small crystals act as seed crystals: the extra dissolved sugar comes out of the solution and deposits on them, so they grow into big crystals. Crystals forming from a supersaturated solution in this way is crystallization (പരലീകരണം).

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) reason ½, name ½.

11.
At a petty shop a bottle of soda is opened with a “pop” and it fizzes. A bottle left open in the afternoon sun goes flat much faster than one kept cold. (a) Which factor is used in the factory to dissolve a large amount of carbon dioxide in soda water?
(b) Explain why the soda fizzes when it is opened, and why the warm one goes flat faster.
(2 marks)

💡 Two factors decide how much gas stays dissolved.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: pressure; on opening the pressure falls – CO₂ escapes; a warm liquid dissolves less gas

(a) Pressure: CO2 is dissolved in water under high pressure and the bottle is sealed. The solubility of a gas increases with pressure.

(b) On opening, the pressure falls, so the water can hold less CO2; the extra gas escapes as bubbles. The solubility of a gas decreases as the temperature rises, so a warm drink holds even less CO2 and goes flat faster.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) pressure ½, temperature ½.

13.
For each colloid, write the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium, and name the type of colloid: (a) mist in the high ranges of Idukki
(b) soap lather.
(2 marks)

💡 Which is spread out in which?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) liquid in gas – liquid aerosol   (b) gas in liquid – foam

(a) Mist: tiny water droplets (liquid – dispersed phase, ഡിസ്പേർസ്ഡ് ഫേസ്) spread in air (gas – dispersion medium, ഡിസ്പേർഷൻ മീഡിയം) – a liquid aerosol.

(b) Soap lather: air bubbles (gas) spread in soap solution (liquid) – a foam.

SECTION CAnswer all · 4 × 3 = 12 marks
14.
(a) How much salt and how much water are needed to make 400 g of a 5 % salt solution?
(b) A bottle contains 500 g of bleaching solution with 3.62 % sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl). What mass of NaOCl is in it?
(c) How much water must be added to 30 g of sugar to make a 12 % sugar solution?
(3 marks)

💡 Write “x % = x g of solute in 100 g of solution” first.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 20 g salt, 380 g water   (b) 18.1 g   (c) 220 g (= 220 mL) of water

(a) 5 % means 5 g of salt in every 100 g of solution. Salt = 5 × 400 ÷ 100 = 20 g; water = 400 − 20 = 380 g.

(b) NaOCl = 3.62 × 500 ÷ 100 = 18.1 g.

(c) 30 g must be 12 % of the solution: mass of solution = 30 × 100 ÷ 12 = 250 g; water = 250 − 30 = 220 g, that is 220 mL (1 mL of water has a mass of about 1 g).

Marking: (a) salt ½, water ½; (b) 1 mark; (c) mass of solution ½, water ½.

15.
The table and the graph give the solubility of three salts in g per 100 g of water.
Temperature (°C)0305070
Potassium nitrate (KNO3)134685138
Potassium chloride (KCl)28374348
Sodium chloride (NaCl)36363738
(a) Whose solubility changes the least with temperature, and whose the most?
(b) How much potassium chloride is needed to saturate 200 g of water at 30 °C?
(c) A saturated solution of potassium nitrate in 100 g of water at 70 °C is cooled to 30 °C. What mass of crystals separates? Name the process.
(3 marks)

💡 Read both numbers from the same row of the table.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) least – sodium chloride; most – potassium nitrate   (b) 74 g   (c) 92 g – crystallization

16.
The solubility of potassium chloride at 30 °C is 37 g. Three beakers each have 100 g of water at 30 °C. In P, 25 g of potassium chloride is dissolved; in Q, 37 g; in R, 42 g (R was made at 50 °C and cooled carefully; it is clear). (a) Name the kind of solution in P, Q and R.
(b) A small crystal of potassium chloride is dropped into each beaker. What will you see in each?
(c) How much more potassium chloride can P dissolve at the same temperature?
(3 marks)

💡 Compare each mass with the solubility.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) P unsaturated, Q saturated, R supersaturated   (b) P – it dissolves; Q – no change; R – crystals grow (5 g separates)   (c) 12 g

(a) Compare each with the solubility, 37 g: P 25 g < 37 → unsaturated (അപൂരിത ലായനി); Q 37 g = 37 → saturated (പൂരിത ലായനി); R 42 g > 37 → supersaturated (അതിപൂരിത ലായനി).

(b) P: the crystal dissolves. Q: the crystal stays as it is (no more can dissolve). R: the crystal acts as a seed – more crystals form on it and the extra 42 − 37 = 5 g separates, leaving a saturated solution.

(c) 37 − 25 = 12 g.

Marking: (a) 1 mark (½ if only two are right); (b) 1 mark (½ if only two are right); (c) 1 mark.

17.
Give the scientific reason. (a) On hot summer afternoons fish in a temple pond come up to the surface and gulp air.
(b) A mechanic first rubs a little kerosene on his greasy hands before washing them with soap and water.
(c) On a misty night on the Thamarassery ghat road the beams of the headlights are clearly seen, but on a clear night they are not.
(3 marks)

💡 Temperature – polarity – particle size.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) less oxygen dissolves in warm water   (b) like dissolves like   (c) Tyndall effect – mist is a colloid

(a) The solubility of gases decreases as the temperature rises. The warm water of the pond holds less dissolved oxygen, so the fish come up for air.

(b) Like dissolves like (സമാനമായത് സമാനമായതിനെ ലയിപ്പിക്കും): grease is non-polar (നോൺപോളാർ) and dissolves in non-polar kerosene, not in polar (പോളാർ) water.

(c) Mist is a colloid (a liquid aerosol). Its droplets scatter the light, so the path of the beam is seen – the Tyndall effect (ടിൻഡൽ പ്രഭാവം). Clear air has no such particles.

Marking: 1 mark each (fact ½, reason ½).

SECTION DAnswer all · 2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
Shameer runs a juice shop near Kozhikode beach. For one glass of kulukki sarbath he puts into a tall shaker: crushed ice, a spoonful of soaked basil seeds (kasakasa), a pinch of salt, the juice of half a lime with the lime pieces dropped in, a slit green chilli and some sugar syrup. He tops it up with cold soda water, covers the shaker with a glass and shakes it hard. He makes the sugar syrup every morning by dissolving 600 g of sugar in 400 g of hot water. On his shelf there are also bottles of a lemon-flavoured soft drink. Its label reads: Ingredients: carbonated water, sugar, lemon flavour, stabiliser (glyceryl ester of rosin), preservative (sodium benzoate), colour (tartrazine). Best before 6 months from packing.
(a) Name two substances in the sarbath that dissolve in water and two that do not.
(b) Find the mass percentage of sugar in Shameer’s syrup.
(c) For a less sweet drink he mixes 100 g of the syrup with 500 g of water. What is the mass percentage of sugar now?
(d) Why are a stabiliser and a preservative added to the bottled drink?
(e) The sarbath must be drunk soon after it is made, but the bottled drink lasts for months. Which is the better choice for health? Give one reason.
(5 marks)

💡 Underline every number and every ingredient in the passage first.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) sugar, salt – basil seeds, lime pieces   (b) 60 %   (c) 10 %   (d) stabiliser – stops settling; preservative – stops spoiling   (e) the fresh sarbath

(a) Dissolve: sugar, salt (also the lime juice and the CO2 of the soda). Do not dissolve: basil seeds, lime pieces (also the chilli). So the sarbath is a mixture of a solution and things that stay undissolved.

(b) Mass of syrup = 600 + 400 = 1000 g; mass percentage = (600 ÷ 1000) × 100 = 60 %.

(c) 100 g of syrup has 60 × 100 ÷ 100 = 60 g of sugar. New mass of solution = 100 + 500 = 600 g. Mass percentage = (60 ÷ 600) × 100 = 10 % – a more dilute solution (നേർത്ത ലായനി).

(d) A stabiliser (സ്റ്റെബിലൈസറുകൾ) keeps the particles of flavour and colour from settling, so the drink stays uniform. A preservative (പ്രിസർവേറ്റീവുകൾ) – here sodium benzoate, which acts against yeast and moulds – keeps the drink from spoiling for a long time.

(e) The fresh sarbath: it is made from natural things (lime, salt, sugar, basil seeds) with no added chemicals, and fresh lime juice has nutritional value. Artificial drinks (കൃത്രിമ പാനീയങ്ങൾ) are mostly sugar, colour and flavour with little nutrition, and the textbook warns of harmful effects of some additives. (Even the sarbath has a lot of sugar – drink it in moderation.)

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((a) ½ + ½; (b), (c) working ½, answer ½; (d) ½ + ½).

19.
Study the experiment and answer the questions.
In a dark room a torch is shone from the side through three glass beakers kept in a row: A – copper sulphate dissolved in water (clear blue); B – water with a few drops of milk stirred in; C – water with chalk powder stirred in.
(a) In which beakers is the path of the light seen? What is this effect called?
(b) The beakers are left undisturbed for an hour and then the contents are filtered. In which beaker do the particles settle, and in which is a residue left on the filter paper?
(c) Classify A, B and C as true solution, colloid or suspension, and give the range of particle size of each.
(d) Neither A nor B settles. Why is the beam seen in B and not in A?
(e) Beaker A is replaced by a clear solution of sodium thiosulphate to which 2–3 drops of dilute hydrochloric acid are added. What will you see in the beam over the next few minutes? Why?
(5 marks)

💡 Size decides everything: seen, settles, filtered, scatters light.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) B and C – Tyndall effect   (b) C, C   (c) A true solution (0.01–1 nm), B colloid (1–1000 nm), C suspension (> 1000 nm)   (d) particle size   (e) the beam becomes visible, then yellow sulphur settles

(a) In B and C. In B the colloidal particles of milk scatter the light – the Tyndall effect (ടിൻഡൽ പ്രഭാവം). (In C the large chalk particles also scatter light.)

(b) C – the chalk particles settle under gravity, and they are left as a residue on the filter paper. The particles of A and B pass through the filter paper.

(c) A – true solution (യഥാർഥ ലായനി), 0.01–1 nm; B – colloid (കൊളോയിഡുകൾ), 1–1000 nm; C – suspension (സസ്പെൻഷൻ), more than 1000 nm.

(d) The particles in A are far too small (less than 1 nm) to scatter light; the colloidal particles in B (1–1000 nm) are big enough to scatter it, though still too small to settle or be filtered.

(e) The clear solution slowly turns milky (cloudy) and the path of the beam becomes visible: very fine particles of sulphur form, of colloidal size – the Tyndall effect. Later the particles grow bigger and yellowish sulphur settles – it has become a suspension.

Marking: 1 mark for each part (½ + ½ where there are two answers; (c) classes ½, sizes ½).
