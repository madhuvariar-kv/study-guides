Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment and the Neutron റഥർഫോർഡിന്റെ ഗോൾഡ് ഫോയിൽ പരീക്ഷണം · ന്യൂട്രോൺ

Fire a cannon shell at a sheet of tissue paper – and it comes straight back at you! That is how Ernest Rutherford described the most surprising result of his life. On this page you fire α particles at a gold foil a few hundred atoms thick, count where they land, zoom in to see why a few bounce back, build Rutherford's "solar system" atom, watch why it should have collapsed, and finally weigh the nucleus to discover the missing particle – the neutron.

📖 What you need (pages 2, 3 and 5 of this guide)
  • α particles (ആൽഫ (α) കിരണങ്ങൾ) from radioactive substances are positive and have mass; they move very fast (page 5).
  • Like charges repel (വികർഷിക്കപ്പെടുക) – the closer they are, the harder the push (page 2).
  • Thomson's model: positive charge spread through the whole atom, electrons embedded in it (page 5).

1. The gold foil experiment (Fig 1.6)

In 1911, scientists led by Ernest Rutherford – Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden – aimed α particles at a very thin gold foil (സ്വർണ്ണത്തകിട്). These experiments made the structure of the atom much clearer. Positive α particles coming from a radioactive substance were sent through the thin gold foil, and Rutherford tried to find the changes in their paths. The α particles coming out of the foil were recorded on a circular photographic film (ഫോട്ടോഗ്രാഫിക് ഫിലിം) placed all round it (Fig 1.6).

☢ The α source is radioactive. In the real experiment it sat in a lead block inside a sealed, air-free box and was handled only by trained scientists – this is never a school experiment.
🧪 Virtual lab: fire α particles at the gold foil (Fig 1.6, seen from above)

Scaled: to see the rare events within a minute, the deflected and bounced-back α particles are made about 100 times more common here than in the real experiment. In Rutherford's experiment only about 1 in 20 000 bounced back. (Go further: the real detector was a zinc sulphide screen that gave a tiny flash for each α particle, watched through a microscope in a dark room; the textbook calls it a photographic film.)

Rutherford made these observations (page 11):

  • ഭൂരിഭാഗം ആൽഫാകണങ്ങളും സ്വർണ്ണത്തകിടിലൂടെ യാതൊരു വ്യതിയാനവും ഇല്ലാതെ കടന്നുപോയി. – Most α particles passed straight through the gold foil without any deflection.
  • ചില ആൽഫാകണങ്ങൾ സ്വർണ്ണത്തകിടിൽ തട്ടിയപ്പോൾ നേർരേഖയിൽ നിന്ന് ചെറിയ കോണളവിൽ വ്യതിചലിച്ച് സഞ്ചരിച്ചു. – Some α particles were deflected (വ്യതിചലിക്കുക) from the straight line by small angles.
  • വളരെ കുറച്ച് ആൽഫാകണങ്ങൾ മാത്രം (ഏകദേശം 20000-ൽ 1) 180° കോണളവിൽ വ്യതിചലിച്ച് തിരിച്ചുവന്നു. – Very few (about 1 in 20 000) were turned through 180° and came back.

From observations to inferences

ഈ നിരീക്ഷണങ്ങളിൽ നിന്ന് അദ്ദേഹം ചില അനുമാനങ്ങളിൽ എത്തിച്ചേർന്നു. – From these observations he reached some inferences. Match each observation to its inference.

1. Most α particles went straight through, without any deflection.
2. Some α particles were deflected by small angles.
3. About 1 in 20 000 came back at 180°.

• ആറ്റത്തിന്റെ ഭൂരിഭാഗവും ശൂന്യമായതിനാലാണ് ഭൂരിപക്ഷം ആൽഫാകണങ്ങളും വ്യതിയാനം കൂടാതെ കടന്നുപോയത്. – Most α particles went straight through because most of the atom is empty space.

• പോസിറ്റീവ് ചാർജ് ഉള്ള ആൽഫാകണങ്ങളിൽ ചിലത് ആറ്റത്തിനുള്ളിലെ പോസിറ്റീവ് ചാർജ് ഉള്ള ഭാഗത്തിന് സമീപത്ത് കൂടി കടന്നുപോയപ്പോൾ വികർഷിക്കപ്പെട്ടതിനാലാണ് അവ ചെറിയ കോണളവിൽ വ്യതിചലിച്ചത്. – Some positive α particles passed near the positive part of the atom and were repelled, so they were deflected by small angles.

• ആറ്റത്തിലെ മുഴുവൻ പോസിറ്റീവ് ചാർജും കേന്ദ്രീകരിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നത് ആറ്റത്തിന്റെ മധ്യഭാഗത്തുള്ള ഒരു ചെറിയ വ്യാപ്തത്തിലാണ്. ... ഈ കേന്ദ്രത്തെ അദ്ദേഹം ന്യൂക്ലിയസ് എന്ന് വിളിച്ചു. – All the positive charge of the atom is concentrated in a very small region at the centre, very small compared with the size of the atom. The α particles that came straight at it bounced back at 180°. He called this centre the nucleus (ന്യൂക്ലിയസ്).

💡 Why couldn't Thomson's atom do this?

In Thomson's atom the positive charge is spread thinly through the whole atom, and the electrons sit all through it, so the charges nearly cancel everywhere. A fast, heavy α particle would feel only a gentle push and go almost straight – none could ever come back (try "Thomson's atom" in both labs). To turn an α particle round, the positive charge must be packed into a tiny, heavy centre.

2. Rutherford's atom model – the solar system model

⭐ Key idea (textbook page 12)
  • ആറ്റത്തിന് ന്യൂക്ലിയസ് എന്ന ഒരു കേന്ദ്രഭാഗമുണ്ട്.
  • ആറ്റത്തിന്റെ വലിപ്പവുമായി താരതമ്യം ചെയ്യുമ്പോൾ ന്യൂക്ലിയസിന് വലിപ്പം വളരെ കുറവാണ്.
  • ആറ്റത്തിന്റെ മുഴുവൻ പോസിറ്റീവ് ചാർജും, മാസ് ഏകദേശം പൂർണമായും ന്യൂക്ലിയസിൽ കേന്ദ്രീകരിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നു.
  • ഇലക്ട്രോണുകൾ ന്യൂക്ലിയസിനുചുറ്റും വൃത്താകൃതിയിലുള്ള ഓർബിറ്റിൽ വളരെ വേഗത്തിൽ പ്രദക്ഷിണം ചെയ്യുന്നു.
  • The atom has a central part called the nucleus (ന്യൂക്ലിയസ്).
  • The nucleus is very small compared with the size of the atom.
  • All the positive charge and almost all the mass of the atom are concentrated in the nucleus.
  • Electrons revolve around the nucleus very fast in circular orbits.

This model is known as the solar system model (സൗരയൂഥ മാതൃക) – the nucleus is like the Sun and the electrons like the planets.

3. Limitations of Rutherford's model (page 12)

വൈദ്യുതകാന്തിക സിദ്ധാന്തപ്രകാരം, ചലിക്കുന്ന ചാർജുള്ള കണങ്ങൾ തുടർച്ചയായി ഊർജം പുറത്തുവിടേണ്ടതാണ്. – According to electromagnetic theory (വൈദ്യുതകാന്തിക സിദ്ധാന്തം), a charged particle moving in a circle must continuously give out energy. So, in Rutherford's model, the negative electrons revolving round the nucleus should keep losing energy and finally fall into the nucleus. But this does not happen. So Rutherford's model could not explain the stability (സ്ഥിരത) of the atom – this is its main limitation (പരിമിതികൾ).

🎬 What the theory predicted vs. what really happens

Hugely slowed down. By the old theory the collapse would take far less than a millionth of a second – every atom in the universe would have collapsed long ago. Yet atoms are stable. Niels Bohr solved this puzzle in 1913 (next page).

4. The neutron (page 12)

The real mass of the nucleus was found to be much more than the mass Rutherford calculated from the number of protons in it. But he could not prove the reason by experiment. Later, in 1932, James Chadwick found that the nucleus also contains some neutral particles (നിർവീര്യ കണങ്ങൾ), each with a mass about equal to that of a hydrogen atom. Because they have no charge, they were named neutrons (ന്യൂട്രോൺ).

🧭 Summary
  • Gold foil experiment (Rutherford, Geiger and Marsden, 1911): most α particles went straight (atom mostly empty space), some deflected by small angles (repelled near the positive part), about 1 in 20 000 came back at 180° (all the positive charge in a tiny centre – the nucleus).
  • Solar system model: tiny nucleus with all the positive charge and almost all the mass; electrons revolve very fast round it in circular orbits.
  • Limitation: by electromagnetic theory a revolving electron should lose energy and fall into the nucleus – the model could not explain the stability of the atom.
  • Neutron (Chadwick, 1932): neutral particle in the nucleus, mass about that of a hydrogen atom; explains why nuclei are heavier than their protons alone.
