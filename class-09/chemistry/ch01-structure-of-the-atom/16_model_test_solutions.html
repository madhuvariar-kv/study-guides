📐 How the figures are drawn

As in the textbook, the nucleus (ന്യൂക്ലിയസ്) is a dark-red disc showing the numbers of protons and neutrons (for example 12p / 14n), the shells (ഷെല്ലുകൾ) K, L, M, N are orange rings, and the electrons are green dots. The drawings are not to scale – a real nucleus is far, far smaller than the atom. Every number in the animations is worked out with Z = p = e, A = p + n, n = A − Z and 2n².

1.
The subatomic particle that has no charge is the
(a) electron(b) proton(c) neutron(d) α particle
(1 mark)

💡 Which particle did Chadwick call “neutral”?

✅ Answer: (c) neutron (ന്യൂട്രോൺ)

The neutron is a neutral particle (നിർവീര്യ കണം) in the nucleus, with a mass of about 1 u. The electron has charge −1, the proton +1, and the α particle is positive. James Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932.

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

3.
The maximum number of electrons that the M shell can hold is
(a) 2(b) 8(c) 18(d) 32
(1 mark)

💡 M is shell number 3. Use 2n².

✅ Answer: (c) 18

M is the third shell, so n = 3 and the maximum is 2n² = 2 × 3² = 2 × 9 = 18. (K: 2, L: 8, N: 32.)

5.
How many neutrons are there in an atom of 3717Cl?
(1 mark)

💡 The top number is A, the bottom number is Z.

✅ Answer: 20

Number of neutrons = mass number − atomic number = A − Z = 37 − 17 = 20. (This chlorine atom has 17 protons and 17 electrons.)

The charge of an electron, measured by Robert Millikan’s oil-drop experiment, is

SECTION BAnswer all · 5 × 2 = 10 marks
9.
(a) Using 2n², find the maximum number of electrons that the N shell can hold. (b) The electron configuration of calcium (Z = 20) is 2, 8, 8, 2, although the M shell can hold 18 electrons. Why is it not written as 2, 8, 10?
(2 marks)

💡 Rule 3 of electron configuration.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 32   (b) the outermost shell can hold at most 8 electrons

Given: N is the fourth shell, n = 4

Formula: maximum number of electrons = 2n²

Substitution: 2 × 4² = 2 × 16

Answer: (a) 32 electrons

(b) A shell can hold 2n² electrons, but the outermost shell of an atom can hold at most 8. In 2, 8, 10 the outermost shell M would have 10. So when M has 8, the last 2 electrons go into the next shell, N: 2, 8, 8, 2 (textbook Fig 1.12).

Marking: (a) formula ½, answer ½; (b) 1 mark for “the outermost shell can have at most 8 electrons”.

10.
Ammu wrote these electron configurations in her science diary. Find the mistake in each and write the correct configuration.
(i) sodium (Z = 11): 2, 9    (ii) magnesium (Z = 12): 8, 4
(2 marks)

💡 How many electrons can K and L hold?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (i) 2, 8, 1   (ii) 2, 8, 2

(i) The L shell (n = 2) can hold at most 2 × 2² = 8 electrons, not 9. Sodium: 2, 8, 1.

(ii) The K shell (n = 1) can hold at most 2 × 1² = 2 electrons, not 8 – and it must be filled first. Magnesium: 2, 8, 2.

Check: 2 + 8 + 1 = 11 and 2 + 8 + 2 = 12 electrons.

Marking: For each: ½ mark for the mistake, ½ mark for the correct configuration.

12.
J. J. Thomson proposed the plum pudding model of the atom. (a) Describe this model. (b) According to this model, why is the atom electrically neutral?
(2 marks)

💡 Plums in a pudding – what is the pudding, what are the plums?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) electrons embedded in a positive sphere   (b) equal positive and negative charges

(a) The atom is a positively charged sphere in which the negatively charged electrons are embedded, like the fruit pieces in a plum pudding (പ്ലം പുഡിങ് മാതൃക).

(b) The total positive charge of the sphere is equal to the total negative charge of the electrons, so they cancel and the atom is electrically neutral.

The model could not explain many experimental results (for example the gold foil experiment), so it was set aside.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark.

13.
Rutherford’s atom model could not explain the stability of the atom. (a) Why? (b) How did Niels Bohr’s model overcome this limitation?
(2 marks)

💡 A moving charge gives out energy …

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) a revolving electron would lose energy and fall into the nucleus   (b) fixed orbits with fixed energy

(a) According to electromagnetic theory (വൈദ്യുതകാന്തിക സിദ്ധാന്തം) a moving charged particle continuously gives out energy. So an electron revolving round the nucleus should lose energy, move closer and closer and finally fall into the nucleus. That does not happen – atoms are stable.

(b) Bohr said that electrons revolve only in definite orbits with a definite energy; while an electron stays in an orbit its energy does not change (stationary energy levels, സ്ഥിരോർജനിലകൾ). So it does not spiral into the nucleus. Energy is absorbed or given out only when it jumps from one orbit to another.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark.

SECTION CAnswer all · 4 × 3 = 12 marks
14.
The figure shows Rutherford’s gold foil experiment. Write the inference Rutherford drew from each observation.
ObservationInference
(i) Most of the α particles passed straight through the gold foil without any deflection.……………
(ii) Some α particles were deflected by small angles.……………
(iii) Very few α particles (about 1 in 20 000) bounced back at 180°.……………
(3 marks)

💡 Empty space → straight; near a positive part → a little bend; head-on with a tiny positive core → back.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (i) mostly empty space   (ii) a positive part repels   (iii) a tiny central nucleus holds all the positive charge

(i) Most of the space in an atom is empty.

(ii) The positive α particles that passed near the positive part of an atom were repelled a little – the atom has a positively charged part.

(iii) All the positive charge is concentrated in a very small central region; the α particles that hit it head-on were pushed straight back. Rutherford called it the nucleus (ന്യൂക്ലിയസ്). (Key box 2: almost all the mass is there too.)

Marking: 1 mark for each inference.

💡 Number of electrons = number of protons.

17.
The numbers of protons and neutrons in the nuclei of three atoms P, Q and R are given.
P: 16 protons, 16 neutrons  ·  Q: 16 protons, 20 neutrons  ·  R: 18 protons, 18 neutrons
(a) Write the mass number of each atom. (b) Which two are isotopes? Why? (c) Which two are isobars? Why?
(3 marks)

💡 Isotopes: same Z. Isobars: same A.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 32, 36, 36   (b) P and Q   (c) Q and R

(a) A = protons + neutrons: P = 16 + 16 = 32; Q = 16 + 20 = 36; R = 18 + 18 = 36.

(b) P and Q are isotopes (ഐസോടോപ്പുകൾ): same atomic number (16 – both are sulphur) but different mass numbers (32 and 36).

(c) Q and R are isobars (ഐസോബാറുകൾ): same mass number (36) but different atomic numbers (16 and 18 – sulphur and argon).

Marking: (a) 1 mark (all three; two correct ½); (b) ½ for the pair, ½ for the reason; (c) ½ + ½.

18.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
At the Regional Cancer Centre (RCC), Thiruvananthapuram, the radiation from cobalt-60 (6027Co) is used to treat cancer. Doctors use iodine-131 to study and treat diseases of the thyroid gland. Archaeologists find the age of fossils with carbon-14. In nuclear power stations uranium-235 is used as the fuel, and heavy water is also used there. Cobalt-60, iodine-131, carbon-14 and uranium-235 are all isotopes.
(a) What are isotopes? (b) Cobalt has atomic number 27. How many protons and neutrons are there in a cobalt-60 atom? (c) Which isotope of carbon is used to study life processes in plants and animals? Which isotope of carbon is the most abundant? (d) 146C and 147N have the same mass number. What are such atoms called? How many neutrons does each have? (e) Heavy water is the oxide of which isotope of hydrogen? How many neutrons are there in one atom of that isotope?
(5 marks)

💡 n = A − Z; isotopes share Z, isobars share A.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) same Z, different A   (b) 27 p, 33 n   (c) carbon-13; carbon-12   (d) isobars; 8 and 7   (e) deuterium; 1

(a) Isotopes are different atoms of the same element with the same atomic number and different mass numbers.

Given: Z = 27, A = 60

Formula: protons = Z; neutrons = A − Z

Substitution: 60 − 27

Answer: (b) 27 protons, 33 neutrons

(c) Carbon-13 (only about 1.1 % of all carbon) is used to study life processes. Carbon-12 is the most abundant and the most stable. (Carbon-14 is the radioactive one used for dating.)

(d) They are isobars. Neutrons: 146C → 14 − 6 = 8; 147N → 14 − 7 = 7.

(e) Heavy water (ഘനജലം, D2O) is the oxide of deuterium, 21H; one deuterium atom has 1 neutron (and 1 proton).

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((b), (c), (d), (e): ½ + ½).

19.
The orbit diagram of an atom X is given. (a) Write the atomic number and mass number of X. (b) Write its electron configuration. How many electrons are there in its outermost shell? (c) Which shell of X has the highest energy? (d) An electron of X jumps from the L shell to the M shell. Does it absorb energy or give out energy? (e) Using 2n², find the maximum number of electrons the M shell can hold. Why does X have only 4 electrons in it?
(5 marks)

💡 Read the nucleus first; count the green dots ring by ring.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) Z = 14, A = 28   (b) 2, 8, 4; four   (c) M   (d) absorbs   (e) 18; X has only 14 electrons

(a) The nucleus shows 14p and 14n: Z = 14, A = 14 + 14 = 28. (X is silicon.)

(b) 2, 8, 4; the outermost shell (M) has 4 electrons.

(c) The M shell – the energy of the shells increases with distance from the nucleus, and M is the farthest shell of X.

(d) It absorbs energy: moving from a lower energy level (L) to a higher one (M) needs energy. (Coming back from M to L it would give out energy.)

(e) 2n² = 2 × 3² = 18. But X has only 14 electrons: K takes 2 and L takes 8, so only 14 − 10 = 4 are left for M.

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((a), (b), (e): ½ + ½).
