Every question of the model paper, in the same order, with the full answer, the marking scheme, an animated step-by-step solution for the longer questions, and a link to the page where the idea is taught. Try each one again before you open the answer, and mark your own paper honestly.

📐 How the solutions are drawn

Atoms have fixed colours: carbon grey, oxygen red, nitrogen blue, chlorine green, hydrogen white. Under every equation an atom count lists each element on the left and the right: a ✔ in every row means the equation is balanced. In the bar charts, heat = mass × calorific value.

Three questions decide almost every answer: Which gas, and what is its test? (pop – flare – milky – bleach) · Are the atoms equal on both sides? · How are the atoms joined? (four bonds or three, single or triple).

Which of these is the test for oxygen?
(a) clear lime water turns milky(b) the gas burns with a “pop” at a burning splinter(c) a glowing splinter bursts into flame(d) a moist coloured cloth loses its colour
(1 mark)

💡 Which gas helps things burn?

✅ Answer: (c) a glowing splinter bursts into flame

Oxygen does not burn itself but helps burning – it is a supporter of combustion (ജ്വലനസഹായി), so a glowing splinter bursts into flame. (a) is the test for carbon dioxide, (b) for hydrogen, (d) is the bleaching action of chlorine.

10.
Hydrogen is made in industry by passing electricity through water. (a) Write the balanced equation for the electrolysis of water.
(b) Give one use of each gas formed.

💡 Water splits into its two elements.

🎬 Step by step

11.
(a) How does the ozone layer in the stratosphere protect living things?
(b) How do CFCs damage it? Name one substitute used in new refrigerators.
(2 marks)

💡 What does UV do to living cells, and what does chlorine do to ozone?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) it absorbs harmful UV rays   (b) CFCs release chlorine which destroys ozone; substitute – HFCs (or liquid hydrocarbons, ammonia, solid CO₂)

(a) Ozone absorbs the harmful ultraviolet radiations (അൾട്രാവയലറ്റ് വികിരണങ്ങൾ) of the Sun. In the stratosphere UV splits O2 (O2 UV 2O) and the atoms join O2 to form ozone (O2 + O → O3); ozone absorbs UV and changes back to oxygen. This cycle keeps the ozone steady and stops the UV.

(b) CFCs reach the stratosphere, break up and release chlorine, which destroys ozone – ozone layer depletion (ഓസോൺ പാളിയുടെ ശോഷണം). Substitutes: HFCs, liquid hydrocarbons, ammonia, solid carbon dioxide.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) chlorine ½, substitute ½.

(b) How do these products finally help the paddy fields?
(2 marks)

💡 Follow the nitrogen from the air to the roots.

🎬 Step by step

13.
Give reasons: (a) Hydrogen-filled balloons at a temple festival rise up, but sellers must keep them away from flames.
(b) Nitrogen in air is said to help control combustion.
(2 marks)

💡 Density – and what each gas does to a flame.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) hydrogen is less dense than air; it burns explosively   (b) it does not support burning and dilutes the oxygen

(a) Hydrogen is less dense than air, so the balloons float up. Hydrogen burns explosively with air, so a flame near the balloons is dangerous.

SECTION CAnswer all · 4 × 3 = 12 marks
14.
The graph shows the calorific values (kJ/g) of some fuels (Fig 7.2). (a) Which fuel releases the most heat per gram, and which the least?
(b) How much heat is released when 2 g of hydrogen and 2 g of methane burn completely? How much more does hydrogen give?
(c) A scooter needs 480 kJ for a short trip. How many grams of petrol would give this heat? How many grams of ethanol?
(3 marks)

💡 Heat = mass × calorific value; mass = heat ÷ calorific value.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) most – hydrogen; least – carbon monoxide   (b) 286 kJ and 112 kJ; 174 kJ more   (c) 10 g of petrol, 16 g of ethanol

Calorific value (കലോറിക മൂല്യം) = heat energy released when unit mass (1 g) of a fuel burns completely. So heat = mass × calorific value.

(a) Most: hydrogen, 143 kJ/g. Least: carbon monoxide, 10 kJ/g.

(b) Hydrogen: 2 × 143 = 286 kJ. Methane: 2 × 56 = 112 kJ. Difference: 286 − 112 = 174 kJ more from hydrogen.

(c) Petrol: 480 ÷ 48 = 10 g. Ethanol: 480 ÷ 30 = 16 g – more fuel is needed because its calorific value is lower.

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) ½ for both heats, ½ for the difference; (c) ½ + ½.

15.
Figures A and B show how the carbon atoms are joined in two allotropes of carbon. (a) Identify A and B. To how many other carbon atoms is each carbon joined in each?
(b) Explain from the structure why diamond is used to cut glass but graphite is used as a lubricant.
(c) Equal masses of diamond and graphite, burnt in oxygen, give only carbon dioxide. What does this show? Write the equation.
(3 marks)

💡 Count the bonds around one atom in each picture.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) A – diamond, 4; B – graphite, 3   (b) diamond: rigid 3-D network of strong bonds; graphite: layers held by weak forces slide   (c) both are pure carbon – allotropes; C + O2 → CO2

(a) A – diamond (വജ്രം): each carbon is covalently bonded to 4 other carbon atoms in a 3-D network. B – graphite (ഗ്രാഫൈറ്റ്): each carbon is bonded to 3 others in flat layers of hexagons.

(b) In diamond every atom is held by four strong covalent bonds in all directions, so it is the hardest natural substance and can cut glass. In graphite the layers are held together only by weak forces, so they slide over one another – it is soft and slippery, a lubricant (സ്നേഹകം).

(c) Both contain only carbon atoms – they are allotropes of the same element, differing only in how the atoms are arranged: C + O2 → CO2.

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((a) names ½, numbers ½).

17.
(a) A family slept in a closed room with a charcoal stove burning. Name the poisonous gas that may form, write the equation for its formation, and say why it is dangerous.
(b) Which two properties of carbon dioxide make it useful in fire extinguishers?
(c) The solution of a white powder X gives a white precipitate with barium chloride solution; the precipitate dissolves in dilute hydrochloric acid with effervescence. What kind of salt is X? Give one example used at home.
(3 marks)

💡 Too little oxygen gives which oxide of carbon?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) carbon monoxide; 2C + O2 → 2CO; forms carboxyhaemoglobin   (b) does not support combustion; denser than air   (c) a carbonate – washing soda

(a) With little oxygen the carbon burns incompletely to carbon monoxide: 2C + O2 → 2CO. CO joins haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin (കാർബോക്സീഹീമോഗ്ലോബിൻ), so the blood carries less oxygen – it can cause death. Never burn charcoal in a closed room.

(b) Carbon dioxide does not support combustion and is denser than air, so it settles over the fire like a blanket and cuts off the oxygen.

(c) The white precipitate (barium carbonate) dissolving with effervescence (CO2) in dilute HCl shows that X is a carbonate (കാർബണേറ്റുകൾ) – e.g. washing soda, Na2CO3·10H2O.

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((a) gas and equation ½, danger ½).

SECTION DAnswer all · 2 × 5 = 10 marks
18.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
After the August 2018 floods, the wells of many homes near Chengannur and Aluva were filled with muddy flood water. Health workers asked families to clean out the wells and then add bleaching powder. Near the treated wells there was a strong, pungent smell. A coloured cloth soaked in a strong bleaching-powder solution slowly lost its colour. At the school laboratory the teacher added silver nitrate solution to a sample of the well water.
(a) Bleaching powder is made by passing dry chlorine over dry slaked lime. Write the balanced equation.
(b) Name the gas that gives the smell. Write two of its physical properties.
(c) Write the equations that show how chlorine and water give nascent oxygen. How does nascent oxygen remove the colour of the cloth?
(d) The silver nitrate gave a curdy white precipitate that dissolved in ammonium hydroxide. What does this show? Write the equation, taking sodium chloride as the salt.
(e) In the laboratory, chlorine from potassium permanganate and concentrated hydrochloric acid is collected by upward displacement of air, inside a fume cupboard. Give a reason for each.
(5 marks)

💡 Chlorine and water give HOCl, and HOCl gives nascent oxygen.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 → Ca(OCl)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O   (b) chlorine – greenish-yellow, pungent smell, denser than air   (c) H2O + Cl2 → HCl + HOCl; HOCl → HCl + [O]; [O] oxidises the colour   (d) a chloride is present; NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl + NaNO3   (e) chlorine is denser than air; it is poisonous

(a) 2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 → Ca(OCl)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O. Ca 2 = 1 + 1, O 4 = 2 + 2, H 4 = 4, Cl 4 = 2 + 2. The product Ca(OCl)2 is bleaching powder (ബ്ലീച്ചിങ് പൗഡർ).

(b) Chlorine. It is a greenish-yellow gas with a pungent smell and it is denser than air (any two).

(c) H2O + Cl2 → HCl + HOCl, then HOCl → HCl + [O]. The hypochlorous acid (ഹൈപ്പോക്ലോറസ് ആസിഡ്) gives nascent oxygen (നവജാത ഓക്സിജൻ), [O]. It oxidises the coloured substances to colourless ones – the cloth fades. The same oxidising action kills germs, so bleaching powder is a disinfectant (അണുനാശിനി). That is why moisture is needed: dry chlorine does not bleach.

(d) A chloride is present: the curdy white precipitate is silver chloride, and it dissolves in NH4OH. NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl + NaNO3.

(e) Upward displacement of air – chlorine is denser than air, so it sinks to the bottom of the jar and pushes the air out at the top. Fume cupboard – chlorine is poisonous (toxic); the fumes must not be breathed. This is a teacher’s demonstration only.

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((c) equations ½, explanation ½; (d) conclusion ½, equation ½; (e) ½ + ½).

19.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
FACT (The Fertilisers and Chemicals Travancore Ltd) at Udyogamandal near Kochi makes ammonia by combining nitrogen with hydrogen, and from it nitrogenous fertilisers for Kerala’s farms. Ramettan, a paddy farmer in Palakkad, uses this fertiliser and also cow-dung manure, and after the harvest he sows payar (green gram) in the same field. His son works at a tyre shop that fills tyres with nitrogen, and the snack packets in the shop are also filled with nitrogen.
(a) Write the balanced equation for making ammonia. From where does the factory get its nitrogen?
(b) Nitrogen needs special conditions in the factory before it reacts. Why does it not react easily?
(c) How does growing payar add nitrogen to the soil of the field?
(d) Give one merit and one limitation each of organic manure and chemical fertiliser. What is the best practice for Ramettan?
(e) Why is nitrogen used to fill tyres and snack packets?
(5 marks)

💡 Air → factory → ammonia → fertiliser; air → Rhizobium → soil.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3; from air (78 % nitrogen)   (b) strong triple bond N≡N   (c) Rhizobium in root nodules fixes nitrogen   (d) Table 7.4; balanced, scientific use   (e) inert – does not support burning; keeps out oxygen

(a) N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3. N 2 = 2, H 6 = 6. Nitrogen comes from air, which is 78 % nitrogen; hydrogen can be made by the electrolysis of water.

(b) The two nitrogen atoms are held by a very strong triple bond (N≡N), which is hard to break, so nitrogen is inert (നിഷ്ക്രിയം) at ordinary conditions.

(c) Rhizobium bacteria in the root nodules of pulse plants take nitrogen from the air and change it into compounds the plants can absorb – nitrogen fixation (നൈട്രജൻ സ്ഥിരീകരണം). When the plants decay, these compounds enrich the soil for the next paddy crop.

(d) Organic manure (ജൈവവളം): merit – eco-friendly, keeps the natural character of the soil, less pollution; limitation – gives nutrients slowly, needed in large amounts. Chemical fertiliser (രാസവളം): merit – more yield, gives N, P, K quickly; limitation – increases soil acidity, harms soil life, pollutes. Best practice: a scientific, balanced use of both – manure for soil health plus measured amounts of fertiliser.

(e) Nitrogen is inert: it does not support burning and does not react with the rubber. In snack packets it keeps out oxygen, so the oily snacks do not spoil.

Marking: 1 mark for each part ((a) equation ½, source ½; (d) ½ for the two merits/limitations, ½ for the best practice).
