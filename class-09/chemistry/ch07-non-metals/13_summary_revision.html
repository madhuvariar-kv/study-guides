2. Facts & rules card – one per non-metal

H, C, N and O are in periods 1 and 2; chlorine (2, 8, 7) is a halogen in group 17. Non-metals are dull (except graphite and iodine), brittle when solid, and poor conductors of heat and electricity (except graphite). Many of them exist as diatomic molecules (ദ്വയാറ്റോമിക തന്മാത്ര): H2, N2, O2, Cl2.

H Hydrogen ഹൈഡ്രജൻ

Facts
Z = 1; 1 electron, 1 proton, 0 neutrons (protium). Isotopes (ഐസോടോപ്പുകൾ): protium, deuterium, tritium. Most abundant element in the universe; very little free in air. Discovered by Henry Cavendish in 1766 – “inflammable air” (കത്തുന്ന വായു).
Lab
Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2 (zinc + dilute HCl, Fig 7.1)
Industry
2H2O electrolysis 2H2 + O2
Test
a burning splinter at the mouth – the gas burns with a “pop” (പോപ്പ് ശബ്ദം)
Properties
colourless, odourless, does not dissolve in water, less dense than air, diatomic, burns explosively
Reactions
2H2 + O2 → 2H2O + heat; H2 + Cl2 sunlight 2HCl – both combination reactions
Uses
making ammonia and methanol (CH3OH); fuel; saturated oils such as vanaspati (പൂരിത എണ്ണകൾ); reducing agent (നിരോക്സീകാരി) to get metals from oxides

C Carbon കാർബൺ

Facts
Z = 6; 2, 4; group 14, period 2; valency 4.
Allotropes
Crystalline (ക്രിസ്റ്റലീയ): diamond, graphite, graphene, fullerene. Amorphous (അമോർഫസ്): charcoal, coke, lamp black, coal – see the table below.
Burning
C + O2 → CO2 (enough oxygen); 2C + O2 → 2CO (excess carbon / too little oxygen)

CO₂ Carbon dioxide & carbon monoxide

Lab
CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2 (marble chips + dilute HCl, thistle funnel തിസിൽ ഫണൽ, Fig 7.8)
Test
clear lime water (ചുണ്ണാമ്പുവെള്ളം) turns milky
Properties
colourless, odourless, denser than air, does not support combustion, its solution in water is acidic (carbonic acid കാർബോണിക് ആസിഡ്, H2CO3)
Uses
fire extinguishers (അഗ്നിശമനികൾ); soda water and soft drinks; washing soda Na2CO3·10H2O and baking soda NaHCO3; urea; carbogen (O2 95 % + CO2 5 %); dry ice (ഡ്രൈ ഐസ്) for stage “smoke”
CO
poisonous: with haemoglobin it forms carboxyhaemoglobin (കാർബോക്സീഹീമോഗ്ലോബിൻ) and blood carries less oxygen. Uses: gaseous fuel, water gas (CO + H2), producer gas (CO + N2), reducing agent.
Carbonate test
salt solution + BaCl2 → white precipitate; + dilute HCl → it dissolves with effervescence (CO2) → a carbonate

N Nitrogen നൈട്രജൻ

Facts
78 % of air – the largest share; in all living things; essential for plant growth. Inert (നിഷ്ക്രിയം) because of the strong triple bond N≡N. It dilutes oxygen and so helps control combustion.
Lightning
N2 + O2 lightning 2NO; then 2NO + O2 → 2NO2; then 4NO2 + 2H2O + O2 → 4HNO3 → nitrates in the soil
Fixation
Rhizobium bacteria in the root nodules of pulse plants (payar) – nitrogen fixation (നൈട്രജൻ സ്ഥിരീകരണം)
Properties
colourless, odourless, very slightly soluble in water, hard to liquefy
Uses
ammonia (N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3) and nitrogenous fertilisers; liquid nitrogen as a coolant (ശീതീകാരി); filling tyres; food packets (keeps out oxygen)

O Oxygen & ozone പ്രാണവായു

Facts
Most abundant element in the Earth’s crust; the “life gas”. Discovered by Joseph Priestley in 1774; named by Lavoisier (“acid-producer”).
Lab
2KMnO4 heat K2MnO4 + MnO2 + O2 (Fig 7.9, teacher only)
Test
a glowing splinter bursts into flame – a supporter of combustion (ജ്വലനസഹായി)
Properties
colourless, odourless, slightly soluble in water (enough for fish), does not burn but helps burning; forms oxides with metals and non-metals – C + O2 → CO2, 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O; metals slowly lose their shine
Uses
supports combustion; oxidiser in rocket fuels; artificial respiration; oxy-acetylene welding
Ozone
O3, mostly in the stratosphere: O2 UV 2O, O2 + O → O3; overall 3O2 UV⇌ 2O3. It absorbs harmful UV (അൾട്രാവയലറ്റ് വികിരണങ്ങൾ); it is also a greenhouse gas (ഗ്രീൻ ഹൗസ് വാതകം). CFCs (ക്ലോറോഫ്ലൂറോകാർബണുകൾ) release chlorine that destroys it – ozone layer depletion. Substitutes: HFCs, liquid hydrocarbons, ammonia, solid CO2. Ozone Day: 16 September.

Cl Chlorine ക്ലോറിൻ

Facts
Table 7.6: Z = 17; 2, 8, 7; mass number 35; 17 electrons, 18 neutrons; valency 1; a halogen. Very reactive – never found free.
Lab
2KMnO4 + 16HCl → 2KCl + 2MnCl2 + 8H2O + 5Cl2 (KMnO4 + concentrated HCl, Fig 7.10 – teacher only, fume cupboard). Water removes HCl vapour; concentrated H2SO4 removes water vapour; collected by upward displacement of air.
Properties
greenish-yellow, pungent smell, denser than air, poisonous
Bleaching
dry cloth – no change; moist cloth – colour fades: H2O + Cl2 → HCl + HOCl, HOCl → HCl + [O]. Nascent oxygen (നവജാത ഓക്സിജൻ) oxidises coloured substances to colourless ones.
Bleaching powder
2Ca(OH)2 + 2Cl2 → Ca(OCl)2 + CaCl2 + 2H2O (ബ്ലീച്ചിങ് പൗഡർ)
Chloride test
NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl + NaNO3 – curdy white AgCl; it dissolves in NH4OH → chloride
Uses
bleaching; antiseptics and pesticides (കീടനാശിനി); disinfectant (അണുനാശിനി) for drinking water and wells

4. Calorific value ഇന്ധനം · കലോറിക മൂല്യം

The calorific value of a fuel is the heat energy released when unit mass (1 g) of it burns completely – in kJ/g. So heat = mass × calorific value. Hydrogen is the best by far, and burning it gives only water – but it burns explosively and is hard to store and transport.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Testing hydrogen with a glowing splinter. That is the oxygen test. Hydrogen burns with a “pop” at a burning splinter.
  • Saying carbon dioxide supports burning. It puts fires out – that is why it is in fire extinguishers.
  • Saying diamond conducts electricity because it is carbon, or that graphite does not. Diamond has no free electrons; graphite has one free electron per atom and conducts.
  • Mixing up washing soda (Na2CO3·10H2O) and baking soda (NaHCO3), or water gas (CO + H2) and producer gas (CO + N2).
  • Saying nitrogen is inert because it is “light”. It is inert because of the strong triple bond N≡N.
  • Thinking dry chlorine bleaches. Moisture is needed: chlorine + water → HOCl → nascent oxygen, which bleaches.
  • Writing O3 as an allotrope of carbon, or charcoal as crystalline. Ozone is an allotrope of oxygen; charcoal is amorphous carbon.
  • Thinking all ozone is harmful. Ozone in the stratosphere protects us by absorbing UV; the CFCs are the problem.
  • Balancing by changing a formula (writing H2O2 to “balance” oxygen). Only change the numbers in front.
