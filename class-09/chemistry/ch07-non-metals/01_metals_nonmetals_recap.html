1. Metals, non-metals and metalloids

About three quarters of all elements are metals: iron, copper, aluminium, gold, sodium. They are shiny, can be beaten into sheets and drawn into wires, and carry heat and electricity well. Non-metals are fewer, but they make up nearly all of the air (nitrogen, oxygen, argon, carbon dioxide) and most of your body (oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen). Solid non-metals such as sulphur are dull and brittle: they break instead of bending. A few elements, the metalloids, have some properties of each. Silicon looks shiny like a metal but is brittle, and it conducts electricity only a little. That is why it is used in computer chips.

🤔 The rules have exceptions

Graphite, a form of carbon, is a non-metal that conducts electricity. That is why the centre rod of a dry cell is graphite (page 5). Diamond, another form of the same carbon, is the hardest natural substance and does not conduct at all. Mercury is a metal but a liquid. Sodium is a metal soft enough to cut with a knife. So decide by looking at several properties together, and above all by the position in the periodic table.

2. Where they sit in the periodic table

The periodic table sorts the three kinds neatly. Metals fill the left side and the middle: groups 1 and 2, all the transition elements (groups 3–12), and the lower parts of groups 13–16. Non-metals sit at the top right. Metalloids form a thin diagonal band between them along the staircase line: boron (B), silicon (Si), germanium (Ge), arsenic (As), antimony (Sb) and tellurium (Te). Hydrogen is the odd one out. It sits at the top of group 1 because it has one outer electron, but it is a non-metal gas, not an alkali metal.

3. Electron configurations of H, C, N, O and Cl

In Chapter 2 you wrote the electron configuration (ഇലക്ട്രോൺ വിന്യാസം) of an atom by filling its shells: at most 2 electrons in the first shell and 8 in the second; in the first 20 elements the third shell also takes up to 8. The number of shells gives the period. For these main-group elements the outermost electrons give the group: 1 or 2 outer electrons → group 1 or 2; 3 to 8 outer electrons → group 10 + that number. Elements of a group form a family (മൂലക കുടുംബങ്ങൾ) with similar properties.

📌 Go further: valency and outer electrons

Valency (സംയോജകത) is the combining power of an atom. For these non-metals it is the number of electrons the atom still needs to fill its outer shell: hydrogen needs 1 (to reach 2, like helium), carbon 4, nitrogen 3, oxygen 2 and chlorine 1 (to reach 8). That is why water is H₂O, ammonia NH₃ and methane CH₄.

4. Covalent bonds and diatomic molecules (a recap of Chapter 3)

Non-metal atoms reach a full outer shell by sharing electrons. A shared pair of electrons holds the two atoms together: a covalent bond (സഹസംയോജകബന്ധനം). One shared pair is a single bond (ഏകബന്ധനം), two pairs a double bond (ദ്വിബന്ധനം) and three pairs a triple bond (ത്രിബന്ധനം). Hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen and chlorine gases are each made of molecules with two atoms: they are diatomic molecules (ദ്വയാറ്റോമിക തന്മാത്ര).

5. A first look at allotropes

The same atoms can be arranged in different ways. Carbon atoms joined to four neighbours in a 3-D network make diamond. Carbon atoms joined to three neighbours in flat sheets make graphite, the “lead” of your pencil. Sixty carbon atoms closed into a hollow ball make a fullerene. These are not mixtures or compounds: each is pure carbon. Different forms of the same element with different physical properties are called allotropes (രൂപാന്തരങ്ങൾ), and the existence of such forms is allotropy (രൂപാന്തരത്വം). Oxygen has allotropes too: ordinary oxygen O₂ and ozone O₃ (page 8). Page 5 explores the allotropes of carbon in detail.

⭐ Key ideas
  • Metals (ലോഹങ്ങൾ): shiny, malleable, ductile, good conductors. Non-metals (അലോഹങ്ങൾ): mostly dull, brittle when solid, poor conductors. Metalloids (ഉപലോഹങ്ങൾ): in between.
  • Non-metals are at the top right of the periodic table (plus hydrogen at the top left); metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te) lie along the staircase.
  • H 1 · C 2,4 · N 2,5 · O 2,6 · Cl 2,8,7. Families: carbon family, nitrogen family, oxygen family, halogens (ഹാലൊജനുകൾ).
  • Non-metals share electron pairs: H–H, Cl–Cl (single), O=O (double), N≡N (triple). H₂, N₂, O₂, Cl₂ are diatomic.
  • Allotropes: different forms of the same element with different physical properties (diamond, graphite, fullerene; O₂ and O₃).
⚠️ Common mistakes
  • “Hydrogen is in group 1, so it is an alkali metal” ✗ – it is a non-metal gas.
  • “Non-metals never conduct electricity” ✗ – graphite does.
  • “Allotropes are isotopes” ✗ – isotopes are atoms with different numbers of neutrons; allotropes are different arrangements of the same atoms.
  • Writing chlorine as 2,7 ✗ – it has 17 electrons: 2,8,7.
