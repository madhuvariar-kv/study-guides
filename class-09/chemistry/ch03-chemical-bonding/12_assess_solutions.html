Let Us Assess & Extended Activities – Solutions വിലയിരുത്താം

All twelve end-of-chapter questions with model answers. Every dot diagram and orbit model the questions ask for is drawn step by step: you can watch the electrons move from the metal to the non-metal in ionic bonds, and watch the shared pairs appear in covalent bonds. Fill in the tables yourself first, then check. After the questions come the four extended activities തുടർപ്രവർത്തനങ്ങൾ: magnesium nitride, the bonds in ethane, ethene and ethyne, a virtual conductivity experiment, and printable chart cards for your bulletin board.

📖 What you need (pages 3–10 of this guide)
  • Atoms combine to get 8 electrons in the outermost shell, the octet അഷ്ടകവിന്യാസം (hydrogen: 2, the duplet രണ്ട് ഇലക്ട്രോൺ സംവിധാനം) (page 3).
  • Ionic bond അയോണിക ബന്ധനം: a metal atom gives electrons and becomes a cation കാറ്റയോൺ (+); a non-metal atom takes them and becomes an anion ആനയോൺ (−). The protons never change (pages 4, 5).
  • Covalent bond സഹസംയോജകബന്ധനം: atoms share electron pairs. 1 pair = single bond ഏകബന്ധനം, 2 pairs = double bond ദ്വിബന്ധനം, 3 pairs = triple bond ത്രിബന്ധനം (pages 6, 7).
  • Electronegativity ഇലക്ട്രോനെഗറ്റിവിറ്റി difference ≥ 1.7 → generally ionic; less than 1.7 → covalent (page 8).
  • Valency സംയോജകത = electrons given, taken or shared. Formula: write the symbols, cross over the valencies as subscripts പാദാങ്കം, simplify, leave out 1 (pages 9, 10).

How the diagrams are coloured: in an electron dot diagram ഇലക്ട്രോൺ ഡോട്ട് ഡയഗ്രം only the outermost electrons are drawn. The electrons of the two kinds of atom are drawn differently, so you can see where every electron came from: • blue dots for one atom, × red crosses for the other (in ionic compounds the crosses belong to the metal). In the orbit models the electrons that move are red. Ions are written in square brackets with the charge at the top right. Parts marked go further are beyond the textbook.

Question 4 · Ionic bonds in four compounds

4. ചുവടെ നൽകിയിരിക്കുന്ന അയോണിക സംയുക്തങ്ങളിലെ അയോണിക ബന്ധന രൂപീകരണം ഇലക്ട്രോൺ ഡോട്ട് ഡയഗ്രം, ഓർബിറ്റ് മാതൃക എന്നിവയിലൂടെ ചിത്രീകരിക്കുക. Show the formation of the ionic bond in the ionic compounds അയോണിക സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ below with an electron dot diagram and an orbit model.

  1. Sodium fluoride സോഡിയം ഫ്ലൂറൈഡ് (NaF)
  2. Sodium oxide സോഡിയം ഓക്സൈഡ് (Na₂O)
  3. Magnesium fluoride മഗ്നീഷ്യം ഫ്ലൂറൈഡ് (MgF₂)
  4. Calcium oxide കാൽസ്യം ഓക്സൈഡ് (CaO)

For each compound: write both configurations, decide who gives and who takes, and count how many atoms of each you need so that every atom reaches an octet. Na₂O needs two sodium atoms for one oxygen; MgF₂ needs two fluorine atoms for one magnesium.

4 a) Sodium fluoride, NaF

🎬 NaF – orbit model, then dot diagram
Na (2, 8, 1) gives its 1 outermost electron to F (2, 7). Na → Na⁺ + 1e⁻ (2, 8); F + 1e⁻ → F⁻ (2, 8). Dot diagram: Na× + F (7 •) → [Na]⁺ [F (7 • + 1 ×)]⁻. The oppositely charged ions attract: ionic bond. Formula NaF.

4 b) Sodium oxide, Na₂O

🎬 Na₂O – two sodium atoms, one oxygen atom
O (2, 6) needs 2 electrons, but each Na (2, 8, 1) has only 1 to give, so two Na atoms are needed. 2Na → 2Na⁺ + 2e⁻; O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻ (2, 8). Dot diagram: Na× + O (6 •) + ×Na → [Na]⁺ [O (6 • + 2 ×)]²⁻ [Na]⁺. Charges: 2 × (+1) + (−2) = 0. Formula Na₂O.

4 c) Magnesium fluoride, MgF₂

🎬 MgF₂ – one magnesium atom, two fluorine atoms
Mg (2, 8, 2) must give away 2 electrons, but each F (2, 7) takes only 1, so two F atoms are needed. Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ (2, 8); 2F + 2e⁻ → 2F⁻ (2, 8 each). Dot diagram: F (7 •) + ×Mg× + F (7 •) → [F]⁻ [Mg]²⁺ [F]⁻, each F⁻ with 7 dots and 1 cross. Charges: +2 + 2 × (−1) = 0. Formula MgF₂.

4 d) Calcium oxide, CaO

🎬 CaO – two electrons move together
Ca (2, 8, 8, 2) gives its 2 outermost electrons to O (2, 6). Ca → Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻ (2, 8, 8, the argon octet); O + 2e⁻ → O²⁻ (2, 8). Dot diagram: ××Ca + O (6 •) → [Ca]²⁺ [O (6 • + 2 ×)]²⁻. Formula CaO. (Check with electronegativity: 3.44 − 1.00 = 2.44, well above 1.7, so ionic.)
⚠️ How to draw it in the exam
  • Orbit model: nucleus with protons and neutrons (11p 12n), circles with the electrons on them. After the transfer the metal ion has one shell less.
  • Dot diagram: only outermost electrons. Use crosses for the metal and dots for the non-metal, so the examiner sees which electron moved.
  • Ions go in square brackets with the charge at the top right: [Na]⁺, [O]²⁻. Write the configuration of each ion below it.
  • Never change the number of protons. Never write Na⁻ or F⁺.

1. ചൂടാക്കിയ മഗ്നീഷ്യത്തിന് മുകളിലൂടെ നൈട്രജൻ കടത്തിവിട്ടാൽ മഗ്നീഷ്യം നൈട്രൈഡ് ലഭിക്കും. ഈ രാസപ്രവർത്തനത്തിന്റെ രാസസമവാക്യം എഴുതുക. ഈ യൂണിറ്റിൽ നൽകിയിട്ടുള്ള ഇലക്ട്രോനെഗറ്റിവിറ്റി സ്കെയിൽ ഉപയോഗിച്ച് സംയുക്തം അയോണികമാണോ സഹസംയോജകമാണോ എന്ന് കണ്ടെത്തുക. (സൂചന - സംയോജകത : നൈട്രജൻ - 3, മഗ്നീഷ്യം - 2) When nitrogen is passed over heated magnesium, magnesium nitride is formed. Write the chemical equation for this reaction. Using the electronegativity scale ഇലക്ട്രോനെഗറ്റിവിറ്റി സ്കെയിൽ of this unit, find whether the compound is ionic or covalent (hint: valency of nitrogen 3, magnesium 2).

🎬 Formula, equation and the electronegativity test
🎬 Three magnesium atoms, two nitrogen atoms go further
Formula: Mg (valency 2), N (valency 3). Cross over → Mg₃N₂.
Equation: 3Mg + N₂ → Mg₃N₂ (left: 3 Mg, 2 N; right: 3 Mg, 2 N, balanced).
Electronegativity (Fig 3.12): N 3.04, Mg 1.31. Difference = 3.04 − 1.31 = 1.73. This is ≥ 1.7, so magnesium nitride is ionic. It is only just above the line, a borderline case, but by the rule of the textbook it is ionic. It contains Mg²⁺ and N³⁻ (nitride) ions: 3 × (+2) + 2 × (−3) = 0.

Show the chemical bonds in ethane (C₂H₆), ethene (C₂H₄) and ethyne (C₂H₂) with electron dot diagrams. Find whether these three compounds are ionic or covalent. Find the total number of bonds in each.

🔬 Pick a molecule, then count its bonds

Carbon (2, 4) has 4 outermost electrons and needs 4 more; each hydrogen has 1 and needs 1 more (duplet). The two carbon atoms are coloured differently so you can see that the C–C pairs come from both of them.

All three are covalent: they are made of non-metals only, and the electronegativity differences are small (C–H: 2.55 − 2.20 = 0.35; C–C: 0), far below 1.7.

3. ചിത്രങ്ങളിൽ കാണിച്ചിരിക്കുന്നത് പോലെ ഉപകരണങ്ങൾ ക്രമീകരിച്ച് പരീക്ഷണം ചെയ്യുക. നിരീക്ഷണം രേഖപ്പെടുത്തുക. നിരീക്ഷണത്തിന്റെ അടിസ്ഥാനത്തിൽ കറിയുപ്പ്, ഗ്ലൂക്കോസ് എന്നിവ ഏത് തരം സംയുക്തമാണെന്ന് തിരിച്ചറിയുക. ⚠ Use only low-voltage dry cells (one or two 1.5 V cells, or a 9 V battery) for this experiment. Never use mains electricity or a phone charger plugged into the wall. Keep your hands and the table dry, do not taste the solutions, and wash your hands afterwards.
Observation: with common-salt solution the galvanometer needle deflects; with glucose solution it does not deflect.
Inference: salt solution conducts electricity, because dissolved common salt gives free ions (Na⁺ and Cl⁻) that move to the electrodes. Common salt is an ionic compound. Glucose solution does not conduct, because glucose dissolves as whole molecules with no charge. Glucose is a covalent compound.
This matches the properties in the textbook: ionic compounds conduct when molten or dissolved; covalent compounds generally do not conduct electricity.
🔎 Why not dry salt? go further

If you put the electrodes into dry salt crystals, the needle does not move. In the solid the ions are locked in the crystal and cannot travel. Dissolving or melting sets them free. That is why the textbook says ionic compounds conduct only when molten or in solution.

Extended activity 4 · A bulletin board of bonds

4. വ്യത്യസ്ത സംയുക്തങ്ങളിലെ രാസബന്ധനം ചിത്രീകരിച്ച് ബുള്ളറ്റിൻ ബോർഡിൽ പ്രദർശിപ്പിക്കുക. Draw the chemical bonds in different compounds and display them on the bulletin board.

🛠️ How to do it as a class
  1. Divide the board into two halves: ionic (transfer) and covalent (sharing).
  2. Each group takes two or three substances and draws the electron dot diagram on a chart card, large and neat. Use two colours, or dots and crosses, for the two kinds of atom.
  3. Under each diagram write: the formula, the configurations, "transfer" or "sharing", the electronegativity difference, and one property or use.
  4. Add a strip across the top: "difference ≥ 1.7 → ionic · less than 1.7 → covalent".
  5. Or print the cards below, colour them and pin them up.

🔑 The ideas that answered everything on this page

Transfer → ions → ionic bond (metal + non-metal). Sharing pairs → covalent bond (non-metals). Count shared pairs for single, double, triple. Electronegativity difference ≥ 1.7 → ionic. Valency = electrons given, taken or shared; cross over the valencies to write the formula.
