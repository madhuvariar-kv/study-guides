4.
Pick the odd one out and give the reason:   scandium (2, 8, 9, 2),  titanium (2, 8, 10, 2),  calcium (2, 8, 8, 2),  vanadium (2, 8, 11, 2).

Step-by-step solution

  2. In scandium, titanium and vanadium the new electrons go into the shell just inside the outermost shell (9, 10, 11) – they are transition elements (സംക്രമണ മൂലകങ്ങൾ).

5.
Aluminium burns in oxygen:   4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3. Which statement is correct?
(a) Aluminium atoms gain electrons.(b) Oxygen is oxidised.(c) Aluminium is the reducing agent.(d) Oxygen is the reducing agent.
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Each aluminium atom loses 3 electrons: Al → Al3+ + 3e−. Losing electrons is oxidation, so aluminium is oxidised.
  2. Each oxygen atom gains 2 electrons: O + 2e− → O2−, so oxygen is reduced.
  3. Aluminium gives the electrons that reduce oxygen, so aluminium is the reducing agent (നിരോക്സീകാരി); oxygen is the oxidising agent.
✅ Answer: (c) Aluminium is the reducing agent.

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

6.
When reactants are heated, the reaction usually becomes faster. The main reason is that
(a) the threshold energy of the reaction becomes lower(b) more reactant particles get a kinetic energy equal to or greater than the threshold energy(c) the number of reactant particles in the vessel increases(d) the reactant particles become larger
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Heating increases the kinetic energy of the particles.
  2. So more particles reach the threshold energy (ത്രെഷോൾഡ് എനർജി) and there are more effective collisions every second – the rate increases.
  3. Heating does not lower the threshold energy, and it does not change the number or size of the particles.
✅ Answer: (b) more reactant particles get a kinetic energy equal to or greater than the threshold energy

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

12.
The electron configurations of four elements are given (the letters are not their real symbols):   P 2, 8, 8  ·  Q 2, 8, 8, 2  ·  R 2, 7  ·  S 2, 8, 1. Find the group number of each and name the family to which it belongs.

  5. S: 1 outermost electron → group 1 – alkali metals (ആൽക്കലി ലോഹങ്ങൾ).
✅ Answer: P – group 18, noble gases; Q – group 2, alkaline earth metals; R – group 17, halogens; S – group 1, alkali metals

Marking: ½ mark for each element (group and family both correct).

16.
Ammu adds sugar to a glass of lime juice and stirs. After a while, however much she stirs, some sugar stays at the bottom; the temperature does not change. (a) What kind of solution is the lime juice now? Why? (b) How can she make the remaining sugar dissolve without adding any more water or juice? (c) She does this, and then lets the glass cool slowly without disturbing it. No sugar separates. What kind of solution is it now?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Saturated solution (പൂരിത ലായനി) – the maximum amount of sugar that can dissolve at this temperature has dissolved, so no more dissolves.
  2. (b) Warm the juice. The solubility of a solid like sugar generally increases with temperature, so the extra sugar dissolves.
  3. (c) It now holds more sugar than is needed to saturate it at room temperature – a supersaturated solution (അതിപൂരിത ലായനി). It is unstable: a small crystal or a shake will make the extra sugar separate.
✅ Answer: (a) saturated (b) warm it (c) supersaturated

Marking: (a) saturated with reason 1; (b) ½; (c) ½.

22.
Boxes P and Q have the same volume and are at the same temperature. They contain the particles of two gases that react with each other. (a) Which box has the higher concentration of reactants? What is meant by concentration here? (b) In which box will the reaction be faster? Explain using collision theory. (c) Not every collision between the particles gives the products. Which collisions do? What is threshold energy?
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Q. Concentration (ഗാഢത) is the number of reactant particles (molecules) per unit volume; Q has 14 particles and P only 6 in the same volume.
  2. (b) Q. By collision theory (കൊളിഷൻ സിദ്ധാന്തം) a reaction takes place when reactant particles collide. With more particles in the same space, there are more collisions every second and so more effective collisions – the rate is higher.

✅ Answer: (a) Q (b) Q – more particles per unit volume → more collisions → more effective collisions (c) effective collisions, with at least the threshold energy

25.
In the science club, Anu compares water (H2O) and carbon dioxide (CO2). Electronegativity values (Fig 3.12): H 2.20, C 2.55, O 3.44. (a) Find the electronegativity difference of the O–H bond and of the C=O bond. Are these bonds ionic or covalent? (b) In the O–H bond, which atom gets the partial negative charge (δ−)? (d) Water has a low molecular mass, but it is a liquid at room temperature. Which attraction between water molecules is responsible?

  3. Substitution: O–H: 3.44 − 2.20   C=O: 3.44 − 2.55

  6. (c) In O=C=O the two oxygen atoms pull equally in opposite directions, so the pulls cancel – because of its shape the CO2 molecule is non-polar. In the water molecule the pulls do not cancel, so water is polar.
  7. (d) Hydrogen bonding (ഹൈഡ്രജൻ ബന്ധനം): the δ+ hydrogen of one water molecule is attracted by the δ− oxygen of another. These attractions hold the molecules together, so water is a liquid.

26.
Bordeaux mixture is sprayed on coconut palms and arecanut trees. Rajan dissolved copper sulphate in water in an old iron bucket and left it overnight. Next morning the blue colour had become paler and a reddish-brown coating of copper had formed on the inside of the bucket:   Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu. (c) Name the oxidising agent and the reducing agent. (d) Why did the blue colour become paler? Why do farmers prepare copper sulphate solution in plastic vessels, not in iron ones?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Iron atom: Fe → Fe2+ + 2e−.   Copper ion: Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu. (The sulphate ion, SO42−, does not change.)
  2. (b) Iron loses electrons → iron is oxidised (ഓക്സീകരിക്കപ്പെടുന്നു). The copper ion gains electrons → it is reduced. (Oxidation numbers: Fe 0 → +2, increase; Cu +2 → 0, decrease.)
  3. (c) Oxidising agent (ഓക്സീകാരി): Cu2+ (copper sulphate) – it takes electrons from iron and is itself reduced. Reducing agent (നിരോക്സീകാരി): iron – it gives electrons and is itself oxidised.
  4. (d) The blue copper sulphate is used up: its copper ions change into copper metal (the brown coating), and pale-green iron sulphate forms, so the blue fades. In an iron vessel the iron itself reacts – the bucket is eaten away and the copper sulphate needed for spraying is wasted. Plastic does not react.
✅ Answer: (a) Fe → Fe2+ + 2e−; Cu2+ + 2e− → Cu (b) iron oxidised, copper ion reduced (c) oxidising agent Cu2+ (copper sulphate); reducing agent Fe (d) copper sulphate is used up; iron reacts and the bucket is damaged

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) oxidised ½, reduced ½ (with the electron reason); (c) ½ + ½; (d) ½ + ½.

For the school science fair, Nithya studies the solubility of three salts in water. The table and the graph give the solubility in grams per 100 g of water.
Temperature (°C)1020406080
KNO₃213262106167

(a) Which salt is more soluble at 20 °C: potassium nitrate or ammonium chloride? Which is more soluble at 60 °C? (b) What is the lowest temperature at which 62 g of potassium nitrate can dissolve completely in 100 g of water? (c) 55 g of ammonium chloride is dissolved in 100 g of water at 60 °C. Is the solution saturated or unsaturated? If it is heated to 80 °C, how much more ammonium chloride can it dissolve? (d) Which salt’s solubility changes the least with temperature? If a saturated solution of it in 100 g of water is cooled from 80 °C to 20 °C, about how much salt separates?
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) At 20 °C: KNO3 32 g, NH4Cl 37 g → ammonium chloride is more soluble. At 60 °C: KNO3 106 g, NH4Cl 55 g → potassium nitrate. (The curves cross between 20 °C and 40 °C.)
  2. (b) The solubility of KNO3 reaches 62 g at 40 °C. Below 40 °C less than 62 g dissolves in 100 g of water.
  3. (c) At 60 °C the solubility of NH4Cl is 55 g, so 55 g in 100 g of water is a saturated solution (പൂരിത ലായനി). At 80 °C the solubility is 66 g, so it can dissolve 66 − 55 = 11 g more.
  4. (d) Sodium chloride – its curve is almost flat (36–37 g). Saturated at 80 °C: 37 g; at 20 °C only 36 g can stay dissolved, so 37 − 36 = about 1 g separates as crystals (പരലീകരണം).
✅ Answer: (a) 20 °C: ammonium chloride (37 g > 32 g); 60 °C: potassium nitrate (106 g > 55 g) (b) 40 °C (c) saturated; 11 g more (d) sodium chloride; about 1 g

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) 1 mark; (c) saturated ½, 11 g ½; (d) NaCl ½, 1 g ½.

In a chemistry class in Kochi, the teacher draws two hydrocarbons, P and Q. (a) Write the molecular formula of P and of Q. What do you notice? (b) Write the IUPAC names of P and Q. (c) Q belongs to a homologous series. Name the series and write its general formula. Draw the structural formula of the member that comes just before Q. (d) Is P an alicyclic or an aromatic compound? Give the reason.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) P: 5 carbons; hydrogens 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8 → C5H8. Q: 5 carbons; hydrogens 1 + 0 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 8 → C5H8. Both have the same molecular formula, but one is a ring and the other an open chain.
  2. (b) P: a ring (വലയസംയുക്തങ്ങൾ) of 5 carbons with one double bond → cyclo + pentene = cyclopentene. Q: 5 carbons (pent) with a triple bond (yne) → pentyne.
  3. (c) Q is an alkyne (ആൽക്കൈനുകൾ); general formula (പൊതുവാക്യം) CnH2n−2. In a homologous series (ഹോമലോഗസ് ശ്രേണി) neighbours differ by –CH2–, so the member just before C5H8 is C4H6, butyne:
  4. (d) Alicyclic (ആലിസൈക്ലിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ): P is a ring compound that is like an open-chain alkene closed into a ring. It is not a benzene-type ring with a characteristic smell (aromatic).
✅ Answer: (a) both C5H8 (b) P cyclopentene, Q pentyne (c) alkynes, CnH2n−2; butyne C4H6 (d) alicyclic
