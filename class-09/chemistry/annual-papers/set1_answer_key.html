1.
In Rutherford’s gold foil experiment, most of the α particles passed straight through the gold foil without being deflected. From this observation Rutherford concluded that
(a) the nucleus is positively charged(b) most of the space inside an atom is empty(c) electrons revolve only in fixed orbits(d) the atom is a positive sphere with electrons embedded in it
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. If the α particles met nothing on their way, there must be nothing in most of the atom to stop or turn them. So most of the atom is empty space (ഗോൾഡ് ഫോയിൽ പരീക്ഷണം).
  2. The positive nucleus (a) was concluded from the few particles that were deflected or bounced back; (c) is Bohr’s idea; (d) is Thomson’s plum pudding model.
✅ Answer: (b) most of the space inside an atom is empty

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

2.
When a magnesium atom (2, 8, 2) changes into a magnesium ion, which of these does not change?
(a) the number of electrons(b) the number of protons(c) the charge(d) the electron configuration
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. The atom gives away its 2 outermost electrons: Mg → Mg2+ + 2e−.
  2. The number of electrons (12 → 10), the charge (0 → +2) and the configuration (2, 8, 2 → 2, 8) all change. Only electrons move; the nucleus is untouched, so the number of protons stays 12.
✅ Answer: (b) the number of protons

4.
In the reaction 4Na + O2 → 2Na2O, the substance that is reduced is
(a) sodium(b) oxygen(c) sodium oxide(d) both sodium and oxygen
[1]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Sodium atoms lose electrons: Na → Na+ + e− (oxidation number 0 → +1). Sodium is oxidised.
  2. Oxygen atoms gain electrons: O + 2e− → O2− (0 → −2). Gain of electrons is reduction (നിരോക്സീകരണം), so oxygen is reduced.
✅ Answer: (b) oxygen

12.
(a) State Mendeleev’s periodic law and the modern periodic law. (b) Chlorine has two isotopes, 35
17Cl and 37
17Cl. Why would they be a problem in a table arranged by atomic mass, but not in the modern periodic table?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Mendeleev’s periodic law (പീരിയോഡിക് നിയമം, 1869): the properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses.
  2. Modern periodic law (ആധുനിക പീരിയോഡിക് നിയമം, Moseley): the chemical and physical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.
  3. (b) The two isotopes have different mass numbers (35 and 37), so by atomic mass they would need two different places – Mendeleev’s table had no separate places for isotopes.
  4. Both have the same atomic number, 17. The modern table is arranged by atomic number, so both share the one place of chlorine.
✅ Answer: (a) properties are periodic functions of atomic masses (Mendeleev) / of atomic numbers (modern) (b) different atomic masses but the same atomic number

Marking: (a) each law ½; (b) reason 1 (½ for the problem by atomic mass, ½ for the same atomic number).

14.
Aluminium powder burns in oxygen:   4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3. Using the atomic masses Al = 27 u and O = 16 u, find the total mass of the reactants and the total mass of the products. Which law does the result show?
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: Al = 27 u, O = 16 u; 4 Al atoms, 3 O2 molecules → 2 Al2O3
  2. Formula: total mass = sum of (number of particles × mass of one particle)
  3. Substitution: reactants = 4 × 27 + 3 × (2 × 16) = 108 + 96; products = 2 × (2 × 27 + 3 × 16) = 2 × (54 + 48) = 2 × 102
  4. Answer: reactants = 204 u, products = 204 u
  5. The masses are equal: the law of conservation of mass (മാസ് സംരക്ഷണനിയമം) – atoms are only rearranged.
✅ Answer: reactants 204 u, products 204 u – the law of conservation of mass

Marking: Mass of reactants with working 1, mass of products with working ½, law ½.

15.
A, B, C and D stand for atoms or ions. Name the type of chemical reaction shown by each pattern.
(i) A + B → AB    (ii) AB → A + B    (iii) A + BC → AC + B    (iv) AB + CD → AD + CB
[2]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Two substances join to give one product: combination (സംയോജന രാസപ്രവർത്തനം), e.g. CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2.
  2. (ii) One compound breaks into two substances: decomposition (വിഘടന രാസപ്രവർത്തനം), e.g. CaCO3 → CaO + CO2↑.
  3. (iii) Element A takes the place of B in compound BC: displacement (ആദേശ രാസപ്രവർത്തനം), e.g. Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2↑.
  4. (iv) Two compounds exchange their ions: double decomposition (ദ്വിവിഘടന രാസപ്രവർത്തനം), e.g. NaCl + AgNO3 → AgCl↓ + NaNO3.
✅ Answer: (i) combination (ii) decomposition (iii) displacement (iv) double decomposition

Marking: ½ mark for each.

19.
The atomic numbers of three elements P, Q and R are 5, 12 and 16 (the letters are not the real symbols). Write the electron configuration of each, and find its period number and group number. Give the reasons.
[3]

Step-by-step solution

  1. Rules: period number = number of shells. Group 1 and 2: group number = number of outermost electrons. Groups 13–18: group number = outermost electrons + 10 (the ten groups 3–12 come in between).
  2. P (Z = 5): 2, 3 → 2 shells → period 2; 3 outermost electrons → 3 + 10 = group 13.
  3. Q (Z = 12): 2, 8, 2 → 3 shells → period 3; 2 outermost electrons → group 2.
  4. R (Z = 16): 2, 8, 6 → 3 shells → period 3; 6 outermost electrons → 6 + 10 = group 16.
  5. (P is boron, Q is magnesium, R is sulphur.)
✅ Answer: P 2, 3 – period 2, group 13 · Q 2, 8, 2 – period 3, group 2 · R 2, 8, 6 – period 3, group 16

Marking: For each element: configuration ½, period and group with the reason ½ (3 × 1).

25.
Read the situation and answer. On a visit to the old house of her grandmother, Meera found a CRT television – a cathode ray tube. Her teacher showed the class this picture of the discharge tube used by scientists such as William Crookes.
(a) Name the electrodes P and Q. (b) When the tube contains air at normal pressure, no current flows through it even at a very high voltage. Why? What is done with R to make the discharge take place? (c) The glow at the ZnS coating stays the same whatever gas is taken in the tube and whatever metal the electrodes are made of. What did scientists conclude from this? Name the particles. (d) Goldstein used a cathode with holes in it in a similar tube and found rays coming out behind the cathode. Name these rays and give one way in which they differ from the rays from P.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) P is joined to the negative terminal – the cathode (കാഥോഡ്). Q is joined to the positive terminal – the anode, here a perforated anode (സുഷിരങ്ങളുള്ള ആനോഡ്).
  2. (b) Air at normal pressure is an insulator (വൈദ്യുതരോധി), so no current flows. The air is pumped out through R with a vacuum pump (നിർവാത പമ്പ്) step by step until the pressure is very low; then the electric discharge takes place.
  3. (c) The cathode rays (കാഥോഡ് രശ്മികൾ) do not depend on the gas or the metal, so the particles in them must be present in all matter. These particles are electrons.
  4. (d) Canal rays (കനാൽ രശ്മികൾ), also called anode rays. Difference (any one): canal rays are positively charged while cathode rays are negative; the nature of canal rays depends on the gas (hydrogen gives the lightest particles – protons), but cathode rays do not.
✅ Answer: (a) P cathode, Q anode (perforated) (b) air at normal pressure is an insulator; the air is pumped out through R to a very low pressure (c) the particles are present in all matter – electrons (d) canal (anode) rays; they are positively charged / their nature depends on the gas

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) insulator ½, reduce the pressure through R ½; (c) present in all matter ½, electrons ½; (d) name ½, difference ½.

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Protons = 20 → atomic number 20. Mass number = 20 + 20 = 40. Electrons in the shells: 2, 8, 8, 2. (X is calcium.)
  2. (b) 4 shells → period 4 (period number = number of shells). 2 outermost electrons → group 2 (for groups 1 and 2, group number = number of outermost electrons).
  3. (c) Group 2 is the family of alkaline earth metals (ആൽക്കലൈൻ എർത്ത് ലോഹങ്ങൾ). Magnesium (2, 8, 2) or beryllium (2, 2) – they also have 2 outermost electrons (ബാഹ്യതമ ഇലക്ട്രോണുകൾ), and chemical properties depend on the outermost electrons.
  4. (d) Y has 19 protons → 19 electrons → 2, 8, 8, 1 (potassium). Y also has 4 shells, so X and Y are in the same period (period 4); Y is in group 1, X in group 2.
✅ Answer: (a) Z = 20, A = 40, 2, 8, 8, 2 (b) period 4, group 2 (c) alkaline earth metals; magnesium (or beryllium) – same number (2) of outermost electrons (d) 2, 8, 8, 1; the same period (4)

Marking: (a) Z and A ½, configuration ½; (b) period with reason ½, group with reason ½; (c) family ½, element with reason ½; (d) configuration ½, same period ½.

27.
Read the passage and answer. Raghavan, a coconut farmer, sprays Bordeaux mixture on his palms to protect them from മഹാളി (mahali). Bordeaux mixture is made from copper sulphate solution and lime. The lime is made by strongly heating sea shells (calcium carbonate) to get quicklime, and then adding water to the quicklime, which becomes very hot. The agricultural officer warns him not to keep copper sulphate solution in an iron bucket.
(a) CaCO3 → CaO + CO2↑ – name the type of this reaction. (b) CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 – name the type of reaction and write one sign that shows a chemical change. (c) Write the equation for the reaction of iron with copper sulphate solution. Name its type and say what Raghavan would see in the bucket. (d) When the two are mixed: CuSO4 + Ca(OH)2 → Cu(OH)2↓ + CaSO4. Name the type of this reaction and give the reason.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) One compound breaks into two substances: decomposition – heating is used, so it is a thermal decomposition (താപീയ വിഘടനം).
  2. (b) Two substances combine to give one compound: combination. Sign: the mixture becomes very hot – heat is given out (an exothermic reaction, താപമോചക പ്രവർത്തനം).
  3. (c) Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu – iron replaces copper in the compound: displacement. A reddish-brown coat of copper appears on the iron and the blue colour of the solution fades. (The bucket is eaten away and the copper sulphate is wasted.)
  4. (d) Double decomposition (ദ്വിവിഘടന രാസപ്രവർത്തനം): the two compounds exchange their ions – Cu2+ joins OH−, and Ca2+ joins SO42−, forming two new compounds.
✅ Answer: (a) decomposition (b) combination; it becomes very hot (c) Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu – displacement; a reddish-brown coat of copper forms and the blue colour fades (d) double decomposition – the two compounds exchange ions

Marking: Each part 1 mark: (a) type 1; (b) type ½, sign ½; (c) equation and type ½, observation ½; (d) type ½, reason ½.

A school science club in Kochi is comparing fuels for a project on cleaner transport. The bar chart (textbook Fig 7.2) gives the calorific values of some fuels.
(a) What is meant by calorific value? Which fuel in the chart has the highest calorific value? (b) Find the heat released when 5 g of hydrogen burns completely. (c) What mass of carbon monoxide must burn completely to give the same amount of heat? (d) Write one merit and one limitation of hydrogen as a fuel.
[4]

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Calorific value (കലോറിക മൂല്യം) is the heat energy released when unit mass of a fuel burns completely (kJ/g). Hydrogen has the highest value, 143 kJ/g.
  2. Given: hydrogen 143 kJ/g, carbon monoxide 10 kJ/g; mass of hydrogen 5 g
  3. Formula: heat released = mass × calorific value; mass = heat ÷ calorific value
  4. Substitution: (b) heat = 5 × 143; (c) mass of CO = 715 ÷ 10
  5. Answer: (b) 715 kJ; (c) 71.5 g of carbon monoxide – more than 14 times the mass of hydrogen.
  6. (d) Merit: very high calorific value, and burning it gives only water (2H2 + O2 → 2H2O), so there is little pollution. Limitation: it burns explosively, and it is difficult to store and transport.
✅ Answer: (a) heat energy released when unit mass of a fuel burns completely; hydrogen (b) 715 kJ (c) 71.5 g (d) merit: high calorific value / only water forms; limitation: burns explosively / hard to store and transport

Marking: (a) definition ½, hydrogen ½; (b) 1; (c) 1; (d) merit ½, limitation ½.
