Balancing Chemical Equations രാസസമവാക്യ സമീകരണം

Atoms are never made or destroyed in a reaction – they only change partners. So a chemical equation must have the same number of each kind of atom on both sides. Here you balance the equation for water step by step, exactly as the book does (the number that changes is shown in red), work through five more examples, and then balance any equation yourself in the equation-balancer lab.

📖 What you need
  • Reading an equation: reactants, products, subscripts and coefficients, counting atoms in a formula (page 1).
  • The law of conservation of mass മാസ് സംരക്ഷണനിയമം: total mass of reactants = total mass of products (page 3).

1. Why must an equation be balanced?

പ്രതീകങ്ങളും, രാസസൂത്രങ്ങളും ഉപയോഗിച്ച് രാസപ്രവർത്തനങ്ങളെ ശാസ്ത്രീയമായി സൂചിപ്പിക്കുന്ന രീതിയാണ് രാസസമവാക്യം (Chemical equation). മാസ് സംരക്ഷണനിയമം പാലിച്ച് രാസസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ എഴുതുമ്പോൾ അഭികാരകങ്ങളുടെ ആകെ മാസും ഉൽപന്നങ്ങളുടെ ആകെ മാസും തുല്യമായിരിക്കണമല്ലോ. രാസസമവാക്യങ്ങളിൽ അഭികാരകങ്ങളിലെയും ഉൽപന്നങ്ങളിലെയും ഒരേ ഇനത്തിൽപ്പെട്ട ആറ്റങ്ങളുടെ എണ്ണം തുല്യമാക്കി ഇത് സാധ്യമാക്കാം.

A chemical equation രാസസമവാക്യം shows a chemical reaction scientifically, using symbols and formulae. To obey the law of conservation of mass, the total mass of the reactants അഭികാരകങ്ങൾ must equal the total mass of the products ഉൽപന്നങ്ങൾ. We make this happen by making the number of each kind of atom equal on the reactant side and the product side.

🧠 Two kinds of numbers in an equation

In 5H2O the big 5 in front is the coefficient – it tells how many molecules. The small 2 below is the subscript – it tells how many H atoms are inside one molecule. To balance an equation you may change only the coefficients. Changing a subscript changes the substance itself (H2O is water, H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide!).

Write 2 in front of H2: 2H2 = 2 molecules × 2 atoms = 4 hydrogen atoms. Oxygen is untouched (still 2 = 2), so now everything balances.

⭐ Key idea (textbook page 74)

ഒരു രാസപ്രവർത്തനത്തിലെ അഭികാരകങ്ങളിലെയും ഉൽപന്നങ്ങളിലെയും ഒരേയിനത്തിൽപ്പെട്ട ആറ്റങ്ങളുടെ എണ്ണം തുല്യമാക്കുന്ന പ്രക്രിയയാണ് രാസസമീകരണം. ഇങ്ങനെ കിട്ടുന്ന സമവാക്യം, സമീകൃത രാസസമവാക്യം (Balanced chemical equation) എന്നറിയപ്പെടുന്നു.

The process of making the number of each kind of atom in the reactants equal to that in the products is called balancing രാസസമവാക്യ സമീകരണം. The equation obtained is called a balanced chemical equation സമീകൃത രാസസമവാക്യം.

✍ A method that always works
  1. Write the correct formulae of all reactants and products (O2, H2, N2, Cl2 are diatomic). Never change them afterwards.
  2. Count each kind of atom on both sides – make a small table.
  3. Pick an atom that is not equal and put a coefficient in front of a formula that contains it.
  4. Count again; repeat until every kind of atom is equal.
  5. Check that the coefficients are the smallest whole numbers (4H2 + 2O2 → 4H2O is balanced but should be divided by 2).

🧠 Why 3 and 2 here?

Oxygen comes in pairs on the left (O2) but in threes on the right (O3 inside Al2O3). The smallest number that both 2 and 3 go into is 6: 3 × O2 = 6 O and 2 × Al2O3 = 6 O. Then aluminium: 2 × Al2 = 4 Al, so 4Al on the left.

a)H2 + I2 → HI
b)Na + H2O → NaOH + H2
c)Mg + HCl → MgCl2 + H2
Load in the balancer:
a) I: 2 on the left, 1 on the right → put 2 in front of HI. H is then 2 = 2. H2 + I2 → 2HI
b) H: 2 on the left but 1 + 2 = 3 on the right – an odd number, so double the water: 2H2O (4 H, 2 O). To match O, make it 2NaOH (now H on the right = 2 + 2 = 4 ✔). Na is now 2 on the right → 2Na. 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2 (Na 2 = 2, O 2 = 2, H 4 = 4).

📋 How chemical equations are written രാസസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ എഴുതുന്ന രീതി (side box, page 75)

രാസസമവാക്യങ്ങൾ എഴുതുമ്പോൾ അഭികാരകങ്ങളുടെയും ഉൽപന്നങ്ങളുടെയും ഭൗതികാവസ്ഥ, ഊർജമാറ്റം, ഉൽപ്രേരകങ്ങളുടെ സാന്നിദ്ധ്യം മുതലായവ വ്യക്തമാക്കി എഴുതുന്ന രീതിയാണ് പൊതുവേ അവലംബിച്ചു വരുന്നത്. – When writing chemical equations it is usual to show the physical state ഭൗതികാവസ്ഥ of the reactants and products, the energy change ഊർജമാറ്റം and the presence of a catalyst ഉൽപ്രേരകം. Example:

N2(g) + 3H2(g) Fe⟶ 2NH3(g) + heat താപം

(s) solid – Zn(s), Mg(s)
(l) liquid – H2O(l)
(g) gas – O2(g), NH3(g)
(aq) dissolved in water – HCl(aq), NaCl(aq)
Fe over the arrow – a catalyst: it speeds up the reaction and is not used up
+ heat on the right – heat is given out

So the zinc–acid reaction of page 3 can be written Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl2(aq) + H2(g). The state symbols and the catalyst do not change the balancing – count only the atoms.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Changing a subscript to balance ✗ H2 + O2 → H2O2 – that is a different substance. ✔ Change only coefficients: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O.
  • Writing a number in the middle of a formula ✗ H22O – the coefficient always goes in front.
  • Multiplying only the first atom ✗ “2H2O has 4 H and 1 O” – the coefficient multiplies every atom: 4 H and 2 O.
  • Writing single atoms for gases ✗ H + O → H2O – hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and chlorine are diatomic: H2, O2, N2, Cl2.
  • Not using the smallest numbers ✗ 4H2 + 2O2 → 4H2O – divide by the common factor.

6. Check yourself

🧭 Summary
  • A balanced equation has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides – that is the law of conservation of mass in symbols.
  • Change only the coefficients (numbers in front), never the subscripts.
  • Count, fix one kind of atom at a time, count again; finish with the smallest whole numbers.
  • 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O · 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO · H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl · Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2 · 4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3 · N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3.
  • States (s), (l), (g), (aq), a catalyst over the arrow and “+ heat” give extra information; they do not change the balancing.
