Every question of the model paper, in the same order, with the full answer, the marking scheme, an animated step-by-step solution for the longer questions, and a link to the page where the idea is taught. Try each one again before you open the answer, and mark your own paper honestly.

📐 How the solutions are drawn

In equations a coefficient that has just been changed is red, as in the textbook. The atom tally under an equation turns green when an atom is equal on both sides. Mass bars show the masses in u side by side – when the reactant bar and the product bar end at the same place, mass is conserved. Oxidation numbers are written above the symbols; a blue arrow above the equation shows an increase (oxidation) and a red arrow below it a decrease (reduction).

Rules used everywhere: total mass of reactants = total mass of products; balance with coefficients only; loss of electrons = oxidation = oxidation number up; gain = reduction = down; the oxidising agent is reduced and the reducing agent oxidised; free elements are 0, the sum in a compound is 0 (H +1, O −2, Na and K +1, Mg and Ca +2, Al +3).

1.
A 2.4 g piece of magnesium ribbon burns completely in air and forms 4.0 g of magnesium oxide. The mass of oxygen that combined with the magnesium is
(a) 6.4 g(b) 1.6 g(c) 2.4 g(d) 4.0 g
(1 mark)

💡 Total mass of reactants = total mass of products.

✅ Answer: (b) 1.6 g

Law of conservation of mass (മാസ് സംരക്ഷണനിയമം): mass of magnesium + mass of oxygen = mass of magnesium oxide → 2.4 g + ? = 4.0 g → oxygen = 4.0 − 2.4 = 1.6 g. (Check with 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO: 48 u of Mg combine with 32 u of O2; 48 : 32 = 2.4 : 1.6.)

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

2.
How many atoms in all are there in 3Ca(OH)2?
(1 mark)

💡 First count the atoms in one unit, then multiply by the coefficient.

✅ Answer: 15 atoms

One Ca(OH)2 has 1 Ca + 2 O + 2 H = 5 atoms (the 2 outside the bracket multiplies both O and H). The coefficient 3 means three units: 3 × 5 = 15 atoms (Ca 3, O 6, H 6).

Marking: 1 mark.

3.
The oxidation number of phosphorus in a P4 molecule is
(a) +4(b) −3(c) 0(d) +5
(1 mark)

💡 No other element is present – who could take the electrons?

✅ Answer: (c) 0

P4 is a molecule of an element – phosphorus is in the elemental state (മൂലകാവസ്ഥ). The oxidation number of an element in the elemental state is 0, however many atoms the molecule has (like H2, O2, Cl2).

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

4.
Find the relation and fill in:   Na → Na+ + e− : oxidation  ::  Zn2+ + 2e− → Zn :
(1 mark)

💡 Are electrons given out or taken in?

✅ Answer: reduction (നിരോക്സീകരണം)

Sodium loses an electron – oxidation. The zinc ion gains two electrons and becomes a zinc atom – gain of electrons is reduction (positive ions that gain electrons are reduced, like Ag+ + e− → Ag).

Marking: 1 mark.

5.
In the reaction CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, the reducing agent is
(a) CuO(b) H2(c) Cu(d) H2O
(1 mark)

💡 The reducing agent is itself oxidised.

✅ Answer: (b) H2

Cu+2O−2 + H02 → Cu0 + H+12O−2. Hydrogen: 0 → +1 – it is oxidised, so H2 is the reducing agent (നിരോക്സീകാരി). Copper: +2 → 0 – CuO is reduced; it is the oxidising agent.

Marking: 1 mark for (b).

6.
The oxidation number of nitrogen in ammonia, NH3, is .
(1 mark)

💡 H is +1; the total is zero.

✅ Answer: −3

Nitrogen is more electronegative than hydrogen, so H is +1. Sum of oxidation numbers = 0: x + 3(+1) = 0 → x + 3 = 0 → x = −3. So N is −3.

Marking: 1 mark (the sign is needed).

7.
Which of these is not a redox reaction?
(a) 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO(b) 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2(c) KOH + HNO3 → KNO3 + H2O(d) 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
(1 mark)

💡 Look for an element whose oxidation number changes.

✅ Answer: (c) KOH + HNO3 → KNO3 + H2O

K+1O−2H+1 + H+1N+5O−23 → K+1N+5O−23 + H+12O−2: K +1, O −2, H +1, N +5 – no oxidation number changes, so it is not a redox reaction (it is a neutralisation). In the other three a metal or hydrogen goes from 0 to a positive value (oxidation) while another element is reduced.

Marking: 1 mark for (c).

8.
Write the missing coefficient so that the equation is balanced:   2KClO3 → 2KCl +  O2
(1 mark)

💡 Count the oxygen atoms on the left.

✅ Answer: 3

Left: 2 × 3 = 6 oxygen atoms. Right: each O2 has 2, so 6 ÷ 2 = 3. 2KClO3 → 2KCl + 3O2 – K 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2, O 6 = 6.

SECTION BAnswer all · 5 × 2 = 10 marks
9.
Sea shells, which are mostly calcium carbonate, are heated in kilns to make lime:   CaCO3 → CaO + CO2. Using the atomic masses Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16, show that this equation obeys the law of conservation of mass.
(2 marks)

💡 Add the masses of all the atoms in each formula.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: reactants 100 u = products 100 u

Reactant: CaCO3 = 40 + 12 + 3 × 16 = 100 u.

Products: CaO = 40 + 16 = 56 u; CO2 = 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 u; total 56 + 44 = 100 u.

Total mass of reactants = total mass of products (ആകെ മാസ്) = 100 u, so the law of conservation of mass is obeyed. The atoms are only rearranged.

Marking: reactant mass 1 mark; product masses and conclusion 1 mark.

11.
Potassium reacts with bromine:   2K + Br2 → 2KBr. (a) Write the equations showing the change that happens to a potassium atom and to a bromine atom. (b) Which of these changes is oxidation and which is reduction? Give the reason.
(2 marks)

💡 Metals lose electrons; non-metals gain them.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) K → K+ + e−; Br + e− → Br−   (b) potassium: oxidation; bromine: reduction

(a) Potassium (2, 8, 8, 1), a metal, gives away its outermost electron: K → K+ + e−. Bromine, a non-metal like chlorine, takes one electron: Br + e− → Br−. The ions form KBr.

(b) Potassium loses an electron → oxidation (ഓക്സീകരണം). Bromine gains an electron → reduction (നിരോക്സീകരണം). The 2 electrons lost by two potassium atoms are gained by the two bromine atoms of one Br2 molecule.

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) ½ + ½ (with the reason).

12.
Find the oxidation number of (a) chlorine in sodium hypochlorite, NaClO (used in bleaching liquids)   (b) phosphorus in phosphoric acid, H3PO4. Show the working.
(2 marks)

💡 Write x for the unknown and make the total zero.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) Cl +1   (b) P +5

(a) Na = +1, O = −2, Cl = x: (+1) + x + (−2) = 0 → 1 + x − 2 = 0 → x = +1. Na+1Cl+1O−2

(b) H = +1, O = −2, P = x: 3(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0 → 3 + x − 8 = 0 → x = +5. H+13P+5O−24

Chlorine is −1 only when it is the more electronegative partner (NaCl, HCl); here oxygen is −2 and chlorine takes the value that makes the sum zero.

Marking: 1 mark each (working ½, answer ½).

13.
In the thermite reaction, used to weld railway tracks, aluminium reacts with iron oxide:   2Al + Fe2O3 → Al2O3 + 2Fe. Using oxidation numbers, find (a) the element oxidised and the element reduced (b) the oxidising agent and the reducing agent.
(2 marks)

💡 Write the oxidation number above every atom first.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) Al oxidised (0 → +3), Fe reduced (+3 → 0)   (b) oxidising agent Fe2O3, reducing agent Al

2Al0 + Fe+32O−23 → Al+32O−23 + 2Fe0

(a) Aluminium: 0 → +3 – the oxidation number increases → aluminium is oxidised. Iron: +3 → 0 – it decreases → iron is reduced. Oxygen stays −2.

(b) Fe2O3 makes aluminium’s oxidation number rise and is itself reduced → oxidising agent (ഓക്സീകാരി). Aluminium is itself oxidised → reducing agent (നിരോക്സീകാരി).

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) ½ + ½.

15.
Find the oxidation number of (a) manganese in potassium manganate, K2MnO4  
(b) nitrogen in dinitrogen trioxide, N2O3  
(c) sulphur in aluminium sulphate, Al2(SO4)3.
(3 marks)

💡 In (c) the bracket multiplies: 3 sulphur atoms and 12 oxygen atoms.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) +6   (b) +3   (c) +6

(a) K = +1, O = −2, Mn = x: 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0 → 2 + x − 8 = 0 → x = +6   K+12Mn+6O−24

(b) O = −2, N = x: 2x + 3(−2) = 0 → 2x − 6 = 0 → 2x = 6 → x = +3   N+32O−23

(c) Al = +3, O = −2; there are 3 S and 3 × 4 = 12 O atoms: 2(+3) + 3x + 12(−2) = 0 → 6 + 3x − 24 = 0 → 3x = 18 → x = +6   Al+32(S+6O−24)3

Always multiply each oxidation number by the number of atoms of that element.

Marking: 1 mark each (working ½, answer with sign ½).

16.
Write the oxidation number of every atom above its symbol and find out whether each reaction is a redox reaction. (a) CuO + 2HCl → CuCl2 + H2O  
(b) 2FeCl2 + Cl2 → 2FeCl3
(c) For the redox reaction, name the oxidising agent and the reducing agent.
(3 marks)

💡 Elements in the free state are 0.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) not redox   (b) redox   (c) oxidising agent Cl2, reducing agent FeCl2

(a) Cu+2O−2 + 2H+1Cl−1 → Cu+2Cl−12 + H+12O−2

Cu +2 → +2, O −2 → −2, H +1 → +1, Cl −1 → −1. No oxidation number changes → not a redox reaction.

(b) 2Fe+2Cl−12 + Cl02 → 2Fe+3Cl−13

Iron: +2 → +3 (increases → oxidised). Chlorine of Cl2: 0 → −1 (decreases → reduced). Oxidation and reduction happen together → a redox reaction.

(c) Cl2 is reduced → oxidising agent; FeCl2 (its iron is oxidised) → reducing agent.

Marking: (a) 1 mark; (b) 1 mark; (c) ½ + ½.

17.
Anu puts about 20 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid in a conical flask. She places a few pieces of magnesium ribbon in a balloon, ties the balloon over the mouth of the flask without letting the magnesium fall in, and keeps the flask on an electronic balance. The reading is 86.40 g. She then lifts the balloon so that the magnesium falls into the acid. (a) Write two observations.
(b) Write the balanced equation of the reaction. What will the balance show after the reaction? Why?
(c) When she repeats the experiment in an open flask without the balloon, the reading becomes less. Does this go against the law of conservation of mass? Explain.
(3 marks)

💡 Is anything able to get out of the flask?

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) bubbles, balloon swells   (b) Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2; still 86.40 g   (c) no – hydrogen escapes

(a) Bubbles of gas form (effervescence) and the balloon swells; the magnesium pieces slowly disappear and the flask becomes warm.

(b) Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2↑. The reading stays 86.40 g: the flask is closed by the balloon, so the hydrogen formed stays inside – nothing enters or leaves. The total mass of the products equals the total mass of the reactants.

(c) No. In the open flask the hydrogen gas escapes into the air, so the balance does not weigh it. If the escaping gas were collected and weighed, the total mass would still be the same – just as burning wood seems to lose mass only because CO2 and water vapour escape.

Marking: (a) ½ + ½; (b) equation ½, reading with the reason ½; (c) 1 mark.

18.
Burning magnesium keeps burning even in carbon dioxide, forming white magnesium oxide and black specks of carbon:   Mg + CO2 → MgO + C. (a) Balance the equation.
(b) Using the atomic masses Mg = 24, C = 12, O = 16, show that mass is conserved.
(c) Write the oxidation numbers of Mg, C and O before and after the reaction.
(d) Name the element oxidised, the element reduced, the oxidising agent and the reducing agent.
(e) Why should a carbon dioxide fire extinguisher not be used on burning magnesium?
(5 marks)

💡 Do (a) first – parts (b) to (d) all use the balanced equation.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 2Mg + CO2 → 2MgO + C   (b) 92 u = 92 u   (c) Mg 0 → +2, C +4 → 0, O −2 → −2   (d) Mg oxidised, C reduced; oxidising agent CO2, reducing agent Mg   (e) Mg takes the oxygen of CO2

(a) O: 2 in CO2 → 2MgO, then 2Mg: 2Mg + CO2 → 2MgO + C

Mg22
C11
O22

(b) Reactants: 2Mg = 2 × 24 = 48 u; CO2 = 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 u; total 92 u. Products: 2MgO = 2 × (24 + 16) = 80 u; C = 12 u; total 92 u. Equal.

(c) 2Mg0 + C+4O−22 → 2Mg+2O−2 + C0

Mg: 0 → +2; C: +4 → 0 (in CO2: x + 2(−2) = 0 → x = +4); O: −2 → −2 (no change).

(d) Magnesium (0 → +2) is oxidised; carbon (+4 → 0) is reduced. CO2 is the oxidising agent; magnesium is the reducing agent.

19.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
At Neendakara fishing harbour in Kollam, Rafeeq’s family owns a fishing boat. Its engine frame and anchor are made of iron. In the moist, salty sea air the iron parts are soon covered with a reddish-brown layer of rust, and the rusted anchor becomes heavier than when it was new. Rust forms when iron reacts with the oxygen and water of the air; in this question take rust as iron(III) hydroxide, Fe(OH)3. Rafeeq’s father paints the iron parts before every monsoon. The roof of their house is made of galvanised iron (GI) sheets – iron sheets coated with zinc. The zinc slowly forms a thin, white oxide layer (ഓക്സൈഡ് ആവരണം), and the sheets do not rust for many years.
(a) Balance the equation for rusting:   Fe + O2 + H2O → Fe(OH)3.
(b) Find the oxidation number of iron before and after rusting. Write the equation for the change of an iron atom. Is iron oxidised or reduced?
(c) Which substance is the oxidising agent? Write the equation for the change of an oxygen atom.
(d) Does the increase in mass of the rusted anchor go against the law of conservation of mass? Explain.
(e) On the GI sheet, zinc reacts with oxygen: 2Zn + O2 → 2ZnO. Write the change in the oxidation number of zinc and explain how the zinc coating protects the iron.
(5 marks)

💡 Underline the substances in the passage; write the oxidation numbers above them.

🎬 Step by step
✅ Answer: (a) 4Fe + 3O2 + 6H2O → 4Fe(OH)3   (b) 0 → +3; Fe → Fe3+ + 3e−; oxidised   (c) O2; O + 2e− → O2−   (d) no   (e) 0 → +2; zinc is oxidised instead of iron

(a) Start with H (2 in H2O, 3 in Fe(OH)3): 2Fe(OH)3 has 6 H → 3H2O. Its 6 O atoms = 3 from water + 3 → 1½O2. Double everything to remove the half: 4Fe + 3O2 + 6H2O → 4Fe(OH)3

AtomReactantsProducts
Fe44
O1212
H1212

4Fe0 + 3O02 + 6H+12O−2 → 4Fe+3(O−2H+1)3

(b) Iron is 0 in the free metal. In Fe(OH)3: x + 3(−2) + 3(+1) = 0 → x − 6 + 3 = 0 → x = +3. So 0 → +3: Fe → Fe3+ + 3e− – loss of electrons, an increase in oxidation number → iron is oxidised.

(c) Oxygen of O2: 0 → −2, it is reduced, so O2 is the oxidising agent: O + 2e− → O2−. (Hydrogen stays +1.)

(d) No. The anchor gains the mass of the oxygen and water from the air that combined with the iron. Mass of iron + oxygen + water used = mass of rust formed – the total mass does not change. In u: 4Fe = 4 × 56 = 224 u; 3O2 = 3 × (2 × 16) = 96 u; 6H2O = 6 × (2 × 1 + 16) = 108 u; total 428 u = 4Fe(OH)3 = 4 × (56 + 3 × 16 + 3 × 1) = 428 u.

(e) 2Zn0 + O02 → 2Zn+2O−2: zinc 0 → +2 – zinc is oxidised. Zinc is oxidised more readily than iron, so the air and moisture react with the zinc instead of the iron, and the thin zinc oxide layer it forms covers the sheet and keeps air and water away from the iron (paint does the same job on the boat).

Marking: 1 mark for each part (½ + ½ where there are two answers).
