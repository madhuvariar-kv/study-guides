Redox Reactions റിഡോക്സ് പ്രവർത്തനങ്ങൾ

The chapter opens in the school laboratory. A tiny piece of sodium is dropped into water with a little phenolphthalein: it darts about, fizzes and the water turns pink. Something new has formed. But has anything been lost? Two experiments on an electronic balance show that the total mass does not change in a chemical reaction: this is Lavoisier's law of conservation of mass. Next you will learn to balance chemical equations, so that the same atoms appear on both sides. Then comes the main idea of the chapter. When sodium meets chlorine, sodium loses an electron (oxidation) and chlorine gains it (reduction). The two always happen together, so the reaction is a redox reaction. You will meet oxidising and reducing agents, learn to work out the oxidation number of any atom in a compound, and use it to decide whether a reaction is redox. Rusting, burning, respiration and batteries are all redox reactions. This self-study guide starts from the basics, explains every idea step by step with animations and virtual labs, answers every textbook question, and ends with a revision sheet and a model test.

The chapter in one picture

⚖️ Mass is conserved · ⚡ electrons are transferred

Part 1 is the law of conservation of mass (മാസ് സംരക്ഷണനിയമം): the hydrogen gas is caught in the balloon, so nothing leaves and the reading stays the same. Part 2 is a redox reaction (റിഡോക്സ് പ്രവർത്തനം): sodium loses an electron (oxidation, ഓക്സീകരണം) and chlorine gains it (reduction, നിരോക്സീകരണം). The small red numbers above the symbols are oxidation numbers (page 7).

⚠ Safety in this chapter: several activities in the book are dangerous – sodium in water, burning, heating iron with sulphur, carbon disulphide, calcium carbide with water, aluminium with iodine. In this guide they are virtual labs or teacher demonstrations only. Never try them at home. In the school lab wear goggles, use tiny amounts, use the fume cupboard when told to, and never taste or smell chemicals.
