IUPAC Naming of Hydrocarbons ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകളുടെ നാമകരണം

Chemists in Kochi, Tokyo and Zurich all call CH₃–CH₂–CH₃ by the same name: propane. That is possible because one international body sets the naming rules. The good news: for the hydrocarbons in this chapter, a name is just two pieces glued together – a word root പദമൂലം that counts the carbons, and a suffix പ്രത്യയം that tells the kind of bond. Learn ten roots and three suffixes and you can name thirty compounds. Then use the name machine to go both ways: structure → name and name → structure.

📖 What you need (pages 4–6 of this guide)
  • Structural formula ഘടനാവാക്യം, condensed formula കണ്ടൻസ്ഡ് ഫോർമുല and molecular formula തന്മാത്രാസൂത്രം of the alkanes, alkenes and alkynes (page 4, page 5).
  • General formulas: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, alkenes CₙH₂ₙ, alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂; neighbours differ by –CH₂– (page 6).

1. Who decides the names? നാമകരണം

മൂലകങ്ങളുടെയും സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെയും നാമകരണം International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) യുടെ നേതൃത്വത്തിലാണ് തീരുമാനിക്കപ്പെടുന്നത്. കാർബണിക സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെ നാമകരണത്തിന് IUPAC ചില നിയമങ്ങൾ ആവിഷ്കരിച്ചിട്ടുണ്ട്. The naming നാമകരണം of elements and compounds is decided under the leadership of the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC). IUPAC has framed rules for naming organic compounds. Let us learn some of them.

🌍 IUPAC (page 161)

IUPAC – the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry – is the international organisation that works to keep chemistry moving with new developments around the world and to make chemistry's contribution to humanity's progress. It was formed in 1919, and its headquarters is at Zurich, Switzerland (as printed in the textbook).

The naming of elements and compounds, the standard values of atomic masses and physical constants, and the acceptance of new terms are all decided under IUPAC's leadership.

Go further: IUPAC is registered in Zurich, while its secretariat (day-to-day office) works from North Carolina, USA. For the examination, write what the textbook says.

Q (page 162). ഒരു ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണിന്റെ നാമകരണത്തിന് എന്തെല്ലാമാണ് പ്രധാനമായും പരിഗണിക്കേണ്ടത്? What must mainly be considered when naming a hydrocarbon?

Two things:
  • the number of carbon atoms കാർബൺ ആറ്റങ്ങളുടെ എണ്ണം → gives the word root;
  • the nature of the bonds between the carbon atoms കാർബൺ ആറ്റങ്ങൾക്കിടയിലുള്ള രാസബന്ധനങ്ങളുടെ സ്വഭാവം (single, double or triple) → gives the suffix.

2. Word roots: counting the carbons പദമൂലം

കാർബൺ ആറ്റങ്ങളുടെ എണ്ണം സൂചിപ്പിക്കുന്ന സംഖ്യകളുടെ അടിസ്ഥാനത്തിൽ പദമൂലങ്ങൾ (Word root) സ്വീകരിക്കുന്നു. Word roots are chosen according to the number of carbon atoms.

🧠 How to remember them

1 to 4 are old names – learn them with a sentence: "My Elephant Prefers Bananas" (meth, eth, prop, but).
5 to 10 are counting words you already know from shapes and words: pentagon (5 sides), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octopus (8 arms), nonagon (9), decade (10 years).

⭐ The naming rule in one line (pages 162–164)

IUPAC name = word root (number of carbons) + suffix (kind of bond)
single bonds only → -ane (alkanes) · a C=C double bond → -ene (alkenes) · a C≡C triple bond → -yne (alkynes)

🔭 Go further – where is the double bond?

From 4 carbons on, the double or triple bond can sit in different places: CH₂=CH–CH₂–CH₃ and CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ are both C₄H₈. Full IUPAC names add a number for the first carbon of the multiple bond, counting from the end nearer to it: but-1-ene and but-2-ene; CH≡C–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃ is pent-1-yne. In this class, names without numbers (butene, pentyne) are accepted, and all the chains on this page have the multiple bond at the first carbon.

⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Calling CH₂=CH–CH₃ "propane" ✗ – check the bond first: -ane single, -ene double, -yne triple. It is propene.
  • Mixing up hex (6) and hept (7) – think hexagon and heptagon.
  • "Methene" or "methyne" ✗ – with one carbon there is no carbon–carbon bond, so there is only methane.
  • Counting the hydrogens to choose the root ✗ – the root counts carbons only.

🧭 Summary
  • IUPAC (International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry, formed 1919, headquarters Zurich, Switzerland as printed) decides the names.
  • Naming depends on the number of carbons (word root: meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex, hept, oct, non, dec) and the kind of bond (suffix).
  • Alkanes: root + ane (methane … decane, Table 8.6). Alkenes: root + ene (ethene, propene … decene, Table 8.7). Alkynes: root + yne (ethyne, propyne … decyne, Table 8.8).
