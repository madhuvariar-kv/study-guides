Organic Chemistry കാർബണിക രസതന്ത്രം

The chapter opens with rice, wheat, sugar and sugarcane. Food, medicines, plastic bottles, the cooking gas in an LPG cylinder and the petrol in a scooter are all made of carbon compounds. Carbon atoms share electrons and join each other into chains, branches and rings, so there are about ten times as many carbon compounds as compounds of all the other elements together. You will start with the simplest of them, the hydrocarbons, made of only carbon and hydrogen. You will sort them into alkanes, alkenes and alkynes, find their general formulas, give them their proper IUPAC names and meet ring compounds such as benzene and the naphthalene of moth balls. Then you will follow crude oil through the fractionating tower at a refinery like the one at Kochi and compare coal and natural gas (CNG, LNG). Last comes the fuel question of our time: global warming, and what all of us can do about it. This self-study guide starts from the basics and builds every structure with animations and virtual labs. It answers every textbook question and ends with a revision sheet and a model test.

The chapter at a glance

🔗 Carbon compounds and hydrocarbons

  1. Organic compounds (ഓർഗാനിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ): carbon compounds, except CO, CO₂, carbonates and bicarbonates · Berzelius, the vital force (ജീവശക്തി) and Wöhler's urea
  2. Hydrocarbons (ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകൾ): only C and H · single, double and triple bonds
  3. Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (saturated) · alkenes CₙH₂ₙ and alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (unsaturated)
  4. Homologous series (ഹോമലോഗസ് ശ്രേണി): neighbours differ by –CH₂–
  5. IUPAC names: word root (meth, eth, prop, but …) + ane / ene / yne

💍 Rings, fuels and the climate

  1. Ring compounds: alicyclic (cyclohexane C₆H₁₂) and aromatic (benzene C₆H₆, naphthalene C₁₀H₈) · catenation
  2. Petroleum (പെട്രോളിയം): a fossil fuel separated by fractional distillation into gases, petrol, kerosene, diesel … bitumen
  3. Coal (കൽക്കരി) from carbonisation: anthracite 94 % C, bituminous 83 %, lignite 67 %, peat 57 %
  4. Natural gas (പ്രകൃതിവാതകം): mainly methane · CNG and LNG
  5. Global warming (ആഗോളതാപനം): greenhouse gases CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, CFCs · what we can do

        Three scientists you will meet

        🌿

        Jöns Jacob Berzelius

        1779–1848 · Swedish chemist
        • Gave the branch of chemistry about carbon compounds its name, organic chemistry (കാർബണിക രസതന്ത്രം).
        • The name came from the belief that organic compounds could be made only by living things, with the help of a vital force (ജീവശക്തി).
        • Read his side box on page 3 (textbook page 154).
        ⚗️

        Friedrich Wöhler

        1800–1882 · German chemist
        • In 1828 he heated the inorganic substance ammonium cyanate and got urea, a compound found in urine: NH₄CNO → H₂N–CO–NH₂.
        • That was the first blow to the vital-force theory. Kolbe made acetic acid (1845) and Berthelot made methane (1856) from inorganic substances, and the theory was dropped.
        • The name “organic” stayed: today it simply means carbon compounds.
        💍

        August Kekulé

        1829–1896 · German chemist
        • Michael Faraday discovered benzene in 1825, but nobody knew how its six carbons and six hydrogens were joined.
        • In 1865 Kekulé proposed a ring: a hexagon of carbon atoms with alternating single and double bonds.
        • He said the idea came to him in a dream of a snake biting its own tail. Read the box on page 8 (textbook page 166).

        Safety first

        ⚠️ Fuels and some organic compounds are dangerous – read before any activity
        • Petrol, kerosene, diesel, turpentine and LPG are highly flammable. Never play with them, never store them near a flame or a stove, and never use petrol to light a fire.
        • If you smell LPG at home (a smell is added to it on purpose), do not light anything or switch on lights or fans; close the regulator, open the doors and windows and call an adult.
        • Naphthalene (moth balls, പാറ്റാഗുളിക) and benzene are toxic. Keep moth balls out of reach of small children, never chew or taste them, and wash your hands after touching them.
        • Acetone (nail-polish remover) and other solvents give off vapours that catch fire easily; use them only with good air flow and away from flames.
        • For the model-making activities use clay, beads, sticks or a model kit – never real chemicals.
