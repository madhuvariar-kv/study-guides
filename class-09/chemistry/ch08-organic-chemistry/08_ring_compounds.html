Ring Compounds, Benzene and Catenation വലയസംയുക്തങ്ങൾ

So far every carbon chain had two loose ends. But a chain can also bend round and bite its own tail, closing into a ring. Moth balls (paattagulika) in a cupboard, the smell of some paints and glues, and many medicines all contain rings of carbon atoms. On this page you will close a chain into a ring with your own hands, build benzene the way Kekulé imagined it, and find out why carbon makes more compounds than all the other elements put together.

📖 What you need (pages 1, 6 and 7 of this guide)
  • Every carbon atom forms 4 bonds (valency 4) – single, double or triple (page 1).
  • Alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, alkenes CₙH₂ₙ, alkynes CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ (page 6).
  • IUPAC names: word root + ane / ene / yne – butane, butene, pentene, hexane … (page 7).

1. Carbon chains that close into rings (Fig 8.7)

കാർബൺ ആറ്റങ്ങളുടെ ഏക ബന്ധനമുള്ളതും, ദ്വിബന്ധനമുള്ളതും, ത്രിബന്ധനമുള്ളതുമായ ധാരാളം ഓപ്പൺ ചെയിൻ (open chain) സംയുക്തങ്ങളെ പരിചയപ്പെട്ടുവല്ലോ. You have met many open-chain ഓപ്പൺ ചെയിൻ compounds with single, double and triple bonds between carbon atoms. ചുവടെ നൽകിയിരിക്കുന്ന ഏതാനും കാർബൺ സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെ ഘടന ശ്രദ്ധിക്കൂ. Now look at the structures of these carbon compounds.

🔵 Fig 8.7 – three carbon compounds

Q (page 164). ഈ കാർബൺ സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെ ഘടനയിലുള്ള പ്രത്യേകത എന്ത്? What is special about the structures of these carbon compounds?

The carbon atoms are joined in a closed ring: there is no "first" or "last" carbon – start at any carbon, follow the bonds, and you come back to where you started. Such compounds are called cyclic or ring compounds വലയസംയുക്തങ്ങൾ.

കാർബൺ ആറ്റങ്ങൾക്ക് പരസ്പരം സംയോജിച്ച് വലയരൂപത്തിലുള്ള സംയുക്തങ്ങളുണ്ടാക്കാനുമുള്ള കഴിവുണ്ട്. വലയ ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബൺ സംയുക്തങ്ങളെ ആലിസൈക്ലിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ, അരോമാറ്റിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ എന്നിങ്ങനെ രണ്ടായി തരംതിരിക്കാം. Carbon atoms can also join one another to form compounds in the shape of a ring. Ring hydrocarbons are classified into two groups: alicyclic compounds ആലിസൈക്ലിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ and aromatic compounds അരോമാറ്റിക് ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകൾ.

2. Alicyclic compounds (Fig 8.8) ആലിസൈക്ലിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ

ആൽക്കെയ്ൻ, ആൽക്കീൻ, ആൽക്കൈൻ എന്നീ ഓപ്പൺ ചെയിൻ ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകളോട് സമാനമായ വലയഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകളാണ് ഇവ. These are ring hydrocarbons that are similar to the open-chain alkanes, alkenes and alkynes. A ring with only single bonds is like an alkane; a ring with a double bond is like an alkene.

Q (page 165). ആലിസൈക്ലിക് ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകൾക്ക് പേര് നൽകിയിരിക്കുന്നവിധം കണ്ടെത്തൂ. Find out how the alicyclic hydrocarbons have been named.

⭐ Naming ring compounds (page 165)
ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണിന്റെ IUPAC നാമത്തിന്റെ മുൻപ്രത്യയമായി 'സൈക്ലോ' എന്ന പദം ചേർത്തെഴുതിയാൽ മതി.

Just write the word "cyclo" as a prefix മുൻപ്രത്യയം before the IUPAC name of the hydrocarbon: cyclo + propane → cyclopropane, cyclo + hexane → cyclohexane, cyclo + butene → cyclobutene.

🔭 Go further – a ring "costs" two hydrogens

Closing a chain into a ring uses one more C–C bond, so the two end carbons each need one H less. That is why cyclobutane is C₄H₈, not C₄H₁₀, and cyclohexane C₆H₁₂: a ring with only single bonds fits CₙH₂ₙ – the same formula as an alkene. So a formula like C₅H₁₀ can be pentene or cyclopentane. When a question just says "C₅H₁₀ – which family?", the textbook answer is alkene.

4. Aromatic hydrocarbons and benzene അരോമാറ്റിക് ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണുകൾ

തനതായ ഗന്ധമുള്ള വലയസംയുക്തങ്ങളാണ് അരോമാറ്റിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ. വ്യാവസായിക പ്രാധാന്യമുള്ള ഒരു അരോമാറ്റിക് സംയുക്തമാണ് ബെൻസീൻ (Benzene). Aromatic compounds are ring compounds with a characteristic smell ("aroma"). Benzene ബെൻസീൻ is an aromatic compound of great industrial importance. Look at its structure (Fig 8.9) – or build it below.

⬡ Build benzene (Fig 8.9) the way Kekulé did

Q (page 165). ബെൻസീനിന്റെ തന്മാത്രാസൂത്രം എഴുതിനോക്കൂ. Write the molecular formula of benzene.

🧪 The structure of benzene (page 166) ബെൻസീനിന്റെ ഘടന

Benzene has been produced industrially since the nineteenth century. It was discovered by Michael Faraday in 1825 through his experiments. For many years the chemists of the time could not explain its structure.

August Kekulé (1829–1896), who studied the structures of chemical compounds, tried to work it out. It is said that a dream led Kekulé to the structure of benzene (the story goes that he dreamt of a snake biting its own tail). In 1865 he published his research paper on the structure of benzene, the discovery that made him famous worldwide. He gave benzene a hexagonal (six-sided) structure in which six carbon atoms and six hydrogen atoms are held together with alternating double bonds.

🔭 Go further – the two Kekulé pictures

The double bonds can be drawn in two ways (shifted by one position). Kekulé suggested that benzene keeps switching between them. Today we know all six C–C bonds in benzene are exactly the same – halfway between single and double – so chemists often draw a hexagon with a circle inside. In this class, draw the Kekulé structure with three alternating double bonds, as in Fig 8.9.

Naphthalene – moth balls നാഫ്തലീൻ · പാറ്റാഗുളിക

പ്രത്യേക ഗന്ധമുള്ളതും വെളുത്ത ക്രിസ്റ്റലാകൃതിയുള്ളതുമായ മറ്റൊരു അരോമാറ്റിക് ഹൈഡ്രോകാർബണാണ് നാഫ്തലീൻ. ഇതിന്റെ ഘടനയിൽ രണ്ട് ബെൻസീൻ വലയങ്ങൾ ഒരുമിച്ച് ചേർന്നിരിക്കുന്നു. പാറ്റാഗുളിക (moth ball) യിലെ പ്രധാന ഘടകമാണിത്. Naphthalene is another aromatic hydrocarbon. It forms white crystals with a characteristic smell. In its structure two benzene rings are joined together (they share one side). It is the main component of moth balls പാറ്റാഗുളിക (Fig 8.10). Choose "naphthalene" in the ring gallery above to count its atoms.

🔵 Fig 8.10 – naphthalene, two benzene rings joined

Q (page 165). നാഫ്തലീനിന്റെ തന്മാത്രാസൂത്രം എഴുതുക. Write the molecular formula of naphthalene.

Moth balls are poisonous if swallowed – keep them away from small children (they can look like sweets), use them only in closed cupboards and bags, and wash your hands after touching them.

5. Compare compound A and compound B

Q (page 166). രണ്ട് ഓർഗാനിക് സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെ ഘടന ചുവടെ നൽകിയിരിക്കുന്നു. ഇവ താരതമ്യം ചെയ്യുക. The structures of two organic compounds are given. Compare them.

Alike: both are ring compounds (hydrocarbons) with 6 carbon atoms in a hexagon.
Different: A (benzene, C₆H₆) has alternating double and single bonds, one H on each carbon, is unsaturated and aromatic (characteristic smell). B (cyclohexane, C₆H₁₂) has only single bonds, two H on each carbon, is saturated and alicyclic (a ring version of hexane). B has 6 more hydrogen atoms than A.

6. Catenation – why there are so many carbon compounds കാറ്റിനേഷൻ

⭐ Catenation (page 166)
ഒരേ മൂലകത്തിന്റെ ആറ്റങ്ങൾക്ക് പരസ്പരം സംയോജിക്കാനുള്ള കഴിവാണ് കാറ്റിനേഷൻ. കാർബണിന് കാറ്റിനേഷനുള്ള കഴിവ് കൂടുതലാണ്. കാർബണിന്റെ ഈ കഴിവാണ് പ്രകൃതിയിൽ ഇത്രയധികം കാർബൺ സംയുക്തങ്ങൾ കാണപ്പെടുന്നതിനുള്ള ഒരു കാരണം.

Catenation is the ability of atoms of the same element to join with each other. Carbon has the greatest ability for catenation. This is one reason why so many carbon compounds are found in nature.

🔗 Catenation – carbon atoms holding hands

Try 4 carbons: the straight chain and the branched chain have the same formula C₄H₁₀ but different structures (go further: such compounds are called isomers). The ring is C₄H₈. More shapes → more compounds.

Q (page 166). കാർബൺ സംയുക്തങ്ങളുടെ എണ്ണം വളരെ കൂടുതലാണെന്ന് മനസ്സിലായല്ലോ. ഇതിനുള്ള കാരണങ്ങൾ ലിസ്റ്റ് ചെയ്യുക. You have understood that the number of carbon compounds is very large. List the reasons for this.

First a quick check: which of these is not a reason?

  1. Catenation കാറ്റിനേഷൻ: carbon atoms join one another to form very long chains – the strongest catenation of all elements.
  2. Valency 4: each carbon can bond to four other atoms, so chains can grow in many directions.
  3. Carbon forms single, double and triple bonds (alkanes, alkenes, alkynes).
  4. Carbon chains can be straight, branched or closed into rings (open-chain, alicyclic and aromatic compounds).
  5. Carbon also bonds strongly with other elements – hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, sulphur, chlorine and other halogens – giving alcohols, acids, sugars, proteins and many more.
  6. (Go further) The same molecular formula can have different structures – isomers – such as butene and cyclobutane, both C₄H₈.
⚠️ Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the cyclo prefix: a ring of 4 carbons with single bonds is cyclobutane, not butane (butane is C₄H₁₀, cyclobutane C₄H₈).
  • Thinking benzene and cyclohexane have the same formula ✗ – benzene is C₆H₆, cyclohexane C₆H₁₂.
  • Drawing benzene with 2 H on each carbon or with all double bonds ✗ – each carbon has 1 H, one double and one single ring bond: 4 bonds.
  • Writing naphthalene as C₁₂H₁₂ ✗ – the two rings share two carbons: C₁₀H₈.
  • "Catenation = carbon joining with hydrogen" ✗ – it is atoms of the same element joining each other.

🧭 Summary
  • Ring (cyclic) compounds വലയസംയുക്തങ്ങൾ: carbon atoms joined in a closed ring (Fig 8.7). Two kinds: alicyclic and aromatic.
  • Alicyclic: rings like the open-chain alkanes/alkenes – cyclopropane C₃H₆, cyclobutane C₄H₈, cyclobutene C₄H₆, cyclopentene C₅H₈, cyclohexane C₆H₁₂ (Fig 8.8). Name = cyclo + IUPAC name.
  • Aromatic: ring compounds with a characteristic smell – benzene C₆H₆ (Faraday 1825; Kekulé 1865: hexagon with alternating double bonds) and naphthalene C₁₀H₈ (two fused benzene rings, white crystals, moth balls).
  • CH₂=CH–CH₂–CH₃ is C₄H₈; the ring with the same formula is cyclobutane. Benzene (C₆H₆, unsaturated, aromatic) ≠ cyclohexane (C₆H₁₂, saturated, alicyclic).
  • Catenation: atoms of the same element joining each other; carbon has it most. With valency 4, multiple bonds, chains/branches/rings and bonds to other elements, it explains the huge number of carbon compounds.
