19.
Read the passage and answer the questions.
Ammu lives in Thrissur with her grandmother, who still cooks on a firewood hearth. In the evening grandmother grinds soaked rice and urad dal into a batter for the next morning’s idli and dosa. In the hot days of April the batter rises and smells sour by morning; in the cool, rainy days of July it takes much longer; and batter kept in the refrigerator hardly changes at all. The batter rises (ferments) because tiny microbes in it produce enzymes. To light the hearth quickly, grandmother never puts in a whole log – she splits it into thin sticks. When the fire is weak she blows air into it through a metal blowpipe, and the flames at once become bright.
(a) Burning of firewood is a chemical change. Write two observations that show this.
(b) Name the factor that makes the batter rise faster in April. Where should grandmother keep the batter on a cool July evening so that it is ready by morning?
(c) What are enzymes? Name one enzyme in your body and the change it speeds up.
(d) Why does a split log catch fire faster than a whole log? Explain using collisions.
(e) Why do the flames become bright when air is blown into the fire? Name the factor.
